**The §5 reply "Global evidential constancy is not at issue" mistakes GEC's definition for its rationale; Meehan and Zhang's two-handed Dana (pp. 320–23, n. 29) shows the paper's policy reproduces meta-conditionalization on her, and the paper must confront that.**

*What is misread.* §5 notes that f_log and f_Br are functions of the proposition learned, so non-contrastiveness is untouched. True of the rules, but M&Z motivate the constraint with a slogan (p. 309): same prior over S, same proposition learned, same posterior, whatever one believes about how one learns (Noah and Dana, p. 312; the Litmus caveat, p. 310, exempts only differences that show up in the prior or in what is learned). The dominance of f_log holds only relative to Dana's knowing that her learning is two-sided. So the paper's theory gives Dana-right (1, 0, 0) and Dana-left (0.25, 0.75, 0) at 15: same prior, state and proposition {15, 16}. The rule is constant. The choice of rule is not.

M&Z's sequential case (pp. 320–21) makes this vivid: left hand at t1, right at t2. The paper's policy takes her from one-quarter to certainty in 15 on re-learning {15, 16}. That is the pattern M&Z charge against META-COND ("one-fourth confident … at t1, but certain … at t2"). §3 separates the paper's rules from Schoenfield's because they "do not" respond to the learning situation. The policy that selects them does. One fixed rule for both hands is not dominance either: with all-proposition Brier, f_log gains 0.90 on the right (−0.29 against −1.19) and loses 1.80 on the left (−2.4 against −0.6, M&Z's figures, p. 328).

*What the paper ignores.*

1. Footnote 29 (p. 321) anticipates the paper's gloss, "knowing that you cannot tell neighbouring cases apart is information about where … the truth lies", and denies it: Dana knows the biconditional but does not learn that this is what she learns. The paper should say its dominance is no inference by Dana, only a fact about scores across states she leaves open, and then face pp. 322–23: she cannot tell what such a plan tells her to do.
2. Dana's "total" is not a population of strangers. The left hand is hers too, at another time. That is a stronger answer to "whose accuracy?" than n. 35 suggests, and the "Uncertain margins" paragraph concedes the structure without crediting it.
