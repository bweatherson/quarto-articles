**On E = K, which §2 uses to get two-sidedness, the pure dominating rules are not options for a two-sided knower: what they make her certain of would be knowledge.**

*Where.* §2 ("If what one learns is what one comes to know…"); §3, first and third remarks; §5 "Absurdity"; §6.

*The problem.* Two-sidedness comes from E = K, so E(s) is everything the agent knows, not a sensory input upstream of updating. Meehan and Zhang's Williamsonian option says the same: one learns the same proposition at two states just in case one knows the same thing at them (p. 317). For Williamson, discrimination talk "is just shorthand for talk of how much the subject can know" (2014, 980 n. 2).

The pure rules recommend certainty in int E(s). By your Observation that is true wherever recommended, so an agent updating this way is never certain of a falsehood, at s or any neighbour. Safety gives no reason to deny this is knowledge. But if it is, then by E = K Olivia's evidence at 17 is {17}, and she can tell 17 from 16. Then her learning is not two-sided, and conditionalizing on it gives what f_log gives. On a connected scale, the pure rules leave what one knows unchanged only when nothing is learned.

So the Proposition compares conditionalization with rules no two-sided knower could follow and stay two-sided. Your first remark misses this asymmetry, which concerns stability, not luminosity: conditionalization makes one certain of exactly what one knows, and the pure rules make one certain of more. §5 blames "the model of evidence"; the fault is the model combined with the rule. (Your reply on uncertain margins is fine: Williamson's variable margins (2014, §5) leave the Observation intact.)

*What to do.*
1. State this objection in §5, in place of most of "Absurdity".
2. Rest the dominance on the conservative mixtures. Choose ε so that nothing stronger than E(s) gets credence high enough to count as belief. Then knowledge, hence E, stays put, and concavity still gives strict dominance. Say what belief requires. On the clock, ε = 0.3 gives 0.8 to the true position and never to a false one, so a Lockean threshold of 0.8 brings the objection back. Present "certain of 17" in the third remark as the unstable case.
3. In §6, do not say "the externalist Bayesian wanted" the accuracy explanation. Meehan and Zhang report that for Williamson accuracy-based considerations are "neither here nor there" (p. 288). Horn (i) is his home; dominance by a rule that merely leans toward the interior is a price he can pay. Aim the trilemma at accuracy-first externalists.
