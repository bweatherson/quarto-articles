**Make horn (ii) the paper's own resolution, stated fairly: it is no retreat, the paper already concedes it, and it absorbs horn (iii).**

*The jibe is unfair.* "Little left for it to survive" (§6) describes the guess model in the coarse position space. Put the guess into the state, as Salow does, and learning is partitional, so Greaves-Wallace and Briggs-Pettigrew apply as stated. Conditionalizing on "my guess is g", then marginalizing to positions, yields the likelihood-weighted posterior. Easwaran and Nielsen (§4) report that Isaacs and Russell apply the plan to a "guess". So the authors you file under (iii) are guess-theorists, and (ii) and (iii) differ only over which proposition counts as "what is learned". With Gallow-style error (80/10/10), conditionalizing on the guess gives 0.1, 0.8, 0.1. That is what Remark 2 reaches with an arbitrary ε = 0.3, and §5 calls it the only non-absurd output. On (iii) ε is free; on (ii) it is the error profile.

*The paper already concedes it.* §4 shows that every conditionalization-friendly clock is edge-allowing. §5 says "the culprit is the model of evidence, as Salow suggests". Salow 2018 is not relevant only "for a different purpose" (§1): his popularity clock, where truth-centred evidence yields predictable good news and guess-centred evidence does not, is the informal ancestor of your Proposition. M&Z are not hostile either: n. 7 (p. 295) lists Salow among those arguing against nontransparency "as conceived here".

*The §2 classification is muddled.* You put the Williamson and Weatherson 2013 models, with knowledge [a−(e+m), a+(e+m)], among margins around the truth, while the other family is margins around "a best guess or an appearance". But that interval is centred on the reading a. (As I recall, Weatherson's own label is "appearance centred"; check.) The families differ in half-width, not centre: e + m with m > 0, versus e. Salow identifies margins for error with "the maximum distance between my best guess and the actual hand" (draft p. 23), so m = 0. State the Proposition's hypothesis as a margin in addition to the tracking error. The dispute is then explicit: is there an extra m?

*Name the real cost.* Williamson's Response (*Inquiry* 56: 77-96; Salow n. 31 cites pp. 80-83) presses (from memory; check) that guesses are no more luminous than evidence, so opacity returns at the guess. The paper cites only pp. 1-14 and never states this.
