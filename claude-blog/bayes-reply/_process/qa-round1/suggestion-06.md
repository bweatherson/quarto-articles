**Recast §5 around scale-blind indifference, the premise a rule-consequentialist defence of Hi-TEA needs, and stop granting the population reading.**

**Where.** §5, "Grant that"; §4, footnote 2.

**Fairness.** The teachability gloss is partial. Hooker's criterion is the expected value of a code's internalization across the overall population (from memory; check). His anti-collapse reply rests on internalization costs, so the draft is right that it lapses for idealized agents. But the expectational core goes unmentioned. Footnote 37 (p. 333) does not compare M&Z's proposal to rule consequentialism. It calls the objection to them "analogous to the collapse objection", and their reply is "relatively flat-footed" (p. 334). The draft's Pettigrew quotation also drops the preceding sentence, that the result "relies on you thinking each evidence function is equally likely." That caveat is the premise at issue.

**The gap.** By Lemma 1 (§4), the community optimum is conditionalization only if λ<sub>p</sub>(s) is constant on p. Table 1 (p. 302) implies Dana's prior is (0.2, 0.6, 0.2), which gives conditionalization (0.25, 0.75, 0) on {15, 16}. If left- and right-hand learners are equally common, the optimum is (0.4, 0.6, 0). M&Z concede the likelihood-weighted verdict to the non-Bayesian (p. 337) and call it "a fact that we have to live with" (p. 338). Hi-TEA's counting weights are extensional because they are invariant under permutations of states (fn. 2). Their domain includes E(15) = {15, 17}, which no two-sided learner occupies.

**The better defence.** The RC defender's strongest move is a veil over learning situations: ex ante, equal chance of each. That is what "irrespective of their uncertainties about their evidential situations" (p. 330 n. 35) describes. A Harsanyi-style veil over persons yields frequency weights, and so, generically, the non-Bayesian verdict (from memory; check). The draft's "domain of evaluation" reply asserts the agent's-possibilities principle that the veil denies. It does not argue for it, so it does not refute the veil.

**What to do.**
1. Replace "Grant that" with that dilemma: persons give likelihood weights, situations give counting weights, and Bayes follows only through scale-blind indifference.
2. Answer the situations-veil on its own terms. Its chooser must be ignorant of her learning situation, which Dana is stipulated to know (p. 333). A code chosen behind it costs those who know their type, which is the structure M&Z invoke with "do not jaywalk" (p. 289).
3. Fix cites: M&Z cite Hooker 2002, chap. 4, for collapse. The −2.08 is Table 1's dogmatic score at 15, not Fact 3.
