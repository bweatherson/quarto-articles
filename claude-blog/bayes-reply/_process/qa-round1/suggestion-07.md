**By higher-order lights the "Absurdity" reply (§5) has things backwards: the conservative rules fail Dorst's Trust just as conditionalization does, and at a two-step margin every rule you offer produces Dorst's effacing evidence.**

*Where:* §3, Dorst/Das paragraph ("neutral about whether margin models are right"); §5, "The conservative versions … are not absurd."

*The problem.* Dorst's (2020) ban on Effacing Evidence constrains a rule within a model, not margin models as such, so it applies to yours. Use a 60-position clock, uniform prior.

(1) *Margin one.* Conditionalization effaces "the hand is at an even minute": credence 1/3 when it is, 2/3 when it isn't. That is Dorst's case. Your pure rule is certain of the true position, so it satisfies Trust. Here it is meta-conditionalization, the only kind of plan that is both transparent and reflective (Das 2022, Prop. 1) and so immune to diachronic books. The mixtures with ε < 3/4 do not efface. But they fail positive access, which Trust requires: at 17 with ε = 0.3, she is certain of [16, 18] yet 0.2 confident she shouldn't be. Generally, a conservative rule gives positive credence to the rim states of *E*(*s*), and at a rim state it is not certain of *E*(*s*). So the rule you call implausible is the one that is coherent at the higher order, and the rules you retreat to inherit conditionalization's failure.

(2) *Margin two*, which §2 allows. Now both pure rules are uniform over the three central positions. That is Dorst's effacing model, one step down. Credence in "even" is 1/3 when true and 2/3 when false. On an even-money bet on "even", your agent takes the losing side at every position, while a conditionalizer (0.6 versus 0.4) wins at every position. Conditionalization effaces "multiple of 3" (0.2 versus 0.4), and so does every mixture with 0 < ε ≤ 1. Nothing in your family escapes.

The Proposition survives, but the gloss does not. Dominance does not give evidence the guiding role that Dorst derives from Good's theorem, and §4 invokes that theorem itself.

*What to do.*
(a) Rewrite the Dorst sentences. On his clock your rules cure effacing; at wider margins they only move it. Drop "neutral".
(b) Rebuild the Absurdity reply as a choice. Either argue that dominance over whole credal states outranks Trust and Value (explaining away Dorst's betting argument), or concede that the Proposition plus Dorst yields Salow's verdict that margins around the truth are the culprit, which makes the third horn unstable.
(c) Note that by Das's Proposition 1 the mixtures are as exposed to variant books as conditionalization is.
