**Make the dominance result explicitly sharp-margin and add the graded case: conditionalization still loses, but pointwise dominance does not survive.**

**Where it bites.** The abstract; §2's margin principle and Observation; §3's Proposition and its remark that it "holds for every two-sided situation"; §5's absurdity objection.

**Where perception departs.** First, indiscriminability is criterion-relative and graded. "Cannot discriminate neighbours" is a threshold at a chosen performance level, and observers discriminate neighbours above chance, though not reliably (Green and Swets 1966; from memory; check). Margins need the non-transitivity that Luce's semiorders capture (Luce 1956; from memory; check), but the sharp E(s) is a stipulated criterion. Second, reports are stochastic. What matters is λ_p(s), the probability that a report delivers p at s, and psychometric functions slope. Meehan and Zhang never model noise. Olivia's "nothing more precise" (p. 294) is a stipulation.

**A check (my calculation, not in the paper).** Twelve states on a circle, uniform prior, Gaussian noise (sd 0.5 units, truncated to |ε| < 1.5 to keep learning factive), a reading k delivering {k−1, k, k+1}. Then λ is 0.685 at the centre and 0.158 at each neighbour. Ex-ante expected log scores: conditionalization −1.10; the likelihood-weighted rule (credences 0.16, 0.68, 0.16) −0.84; the edge-discounting rule −∞, since rim states occur with probability 0.16. Pointwise, the weighted rule loses to conditionalization at the rim by 0.75 nats. The sd and truncation are illustrative. Only the claim that λ is not flat over the rim matters.

**What survives.** By Lemma 1 and extensionality, conditionalization is optimal only if λ_p is constant on p, and a sloped psychometric function fails that. The trilemma can be restated without rims: conditionalization is optimal only where the likelihood of learning p is flat.

**What lapses.** The Observation, pointwise dominance and "never at its edge" all require rim learning to have probability zero. The replacement is the expected-accuracy result under the learning process. The §5 absurdity (certainty about 17) is also a sharp-model artefact: the graded optimum gives 0.68 to the best guess, which makes the conservative mixtures the natural rule.

1. Scope the Proposition and the abstract to sharp, factive learning, and say that pointwise dominance lapses under graded learning.
2. Add the graded case as a remark after the Proposition (about 100 words).
3. Restate the trilemma's middle horn in terms of flat likelihoods.
4. Make room by trimming §4's anomalies paragraph (about 150 words), the least load-bearing part of that section.
