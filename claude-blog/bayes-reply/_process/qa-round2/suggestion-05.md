**§7's second horn misdescribes the signal theorists. What they drop is flat likelihood, not slack, and none of them makes the signal luminous.**

*Where:* §7, option two ("If the signal goes into the state space, learning it is partitional ... vindicated by the original arguments"); §4 ("which is to say there is no slack"); "Graded margins".

**1. The cited authors do not take this option.**
- E&N say that "propositions about the signal ... are not part of the algebra F that we represent the agent as having credences over ... (It is only as if one had this certainty.)" (p. 1119).
- Their L is "objective probability", and p × L "a mixture of subjective and objective probabilities" (pp. 1119–20). That is a new argument, not Greaves–Wallace.
- Gallow's agent may misfire, and she need not become certain which update she made (E&N n. 26).
- The partitional version is Salow's best guess. Its cost is luminous signals, not lost margins.

**2. Slack is not the variable.** Coarsening at random requires that there be no slack, but no slack does not secure it.
- E&N's second clock (pp. 1120–21) has likelihoods 1/4, 1/2, 1/4. The truth lies at an edge of the signal's support half the time. Yet "the optimal update rule disagrees with conditionalization on this proposition, or on any proposition in F".
- The cosine dial cited in "Graded margins" is likewise slack-free and centred on the signal.
- So graded discrimination defeats conditionalization on worldly propositions on *both* horns.

**3. An overlap you do not credit.** On Williamson's clock with a flat prior, §6's mixtures give (ε/3, 1−2ε/3, ε/3).
- At ε = 0.3 (n. 4) this is Ahmed and Salow's "Third Objection" credence. It is also Gallow's misfire posterior (2018 draft, p. 27).
- At ε = 3/4 it is E&N's 1/4–1/2–1/4 update.
- Your margins-plus-accuracy credences are the signal theorists' slack-free credences.

**What to do:**
- **Restate option two:** keep accuracy and update by prior × likelihood on signals one responds to but need not know one has. E&N: "We don't commit ourselves to any claims about how these signals relate to knowledge" (p. 1122).
  - This gives up credence as evidential probability, and conditionalization on worldly propositions except under coarsening at random.
  - It need not give up margins.
  - Salow's guess is the special case that the original arguments cover.
- **Fix §4:** write "which requires, but is not secured by, the absence of slack".
  - Credit Isaacs and Russell's Theorem 4, which E&N generalize.
  - p. 1120 is E&N's clock, not "their dial".
- **Credit** Gallow, Ahmed and Salow, and E&N in §6.
