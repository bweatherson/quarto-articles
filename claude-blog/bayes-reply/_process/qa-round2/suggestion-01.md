**§5's claim that idempotence is "untouched" is false by Meehan and Zhang's own Fact 6, and your both-hands Dana shows it. Own the contrastiveness, and swap the veil dilemma for the reply a real Hi-TEA defender gives.**

In §5 you say the complaint is not about "evidential constancy in Meehan and Zhang's formal sense", since each dominating rule is globally evidentially constant (GEC). But noncontrastiveness and idempotence are cross-problem conditions on *the* rational rule (pp. 309, 320), and Fact 6 makes them equivalent. Your theory says each agent should adopt the GEC rule that is best for the learning she knows she faces. As a map from updating problems to plans, that is contrastive. Every candidate rule is GEC; the function that selects among them is META-COND laundered through rule choice (compare p. 327).

Your case shows it. Both-hands Dana weighs with her left hand at t1 and her right at t2, using the single rule with the best expected accuracy over both occasions. By Lemma 1, {15, 16} gets weight 2 at 15 and 1 at 16, so at t1 she should be 0.4 confident of 15 (I checked). Left-only Dana, with the same prior, hand and reading, should be at 0.25: her learning is transparent. The only difference is an intention to weigh again; cancelling it should lower her credence on no new evidence.

Re-optimizing at each time fails in the reverse order. Weighing right then left, she is certain of 15 at t1, or after §6's retreat well above 0.25. At t2, on relearning what she knew, she either drops to 0.25 or violates accumulation. That is how Meehan and Zhang convict META-COND (n. 30).

The sophisticated escape is to say the rule belongs to the prior space, "irrespective of their uncertainties about their evidential situations" (n. 35). Nobody chooses behind either veil. The sum is Fact 10's decomposition, which grades each response to *p* by the prior's expectation given *p*. Extensionality becomes GEC's evaluative twin (parity): if the output may depend only on (π, *p*), so may its grading. The cost: right-handed Dana can see that conditionalizing is dominated *for her* by a rule meeting all of Meehan and Zhang's constraints, and she must conditionalize anyway. Accuracy vindicates a support function, not any self-aware learner's choice: the rule-consequentialist's old problem (n. 37).

So:
1. Delete "idempotence is untouched", concede the contrastiveness, and defend plan-dependence or the failure of accumulation as its price.
2. Answer the parity argument where the veil dilemma now sits.
3. Restate §7's first horn as "conditionalization with only a rule-level vindication". The push in §6 exists only at the agent level.
