**Section 6's belief-level claim fails below a threshold of 2/3, so restate it with its threshold and cut most of footnote 4.**

As a generalist I can follow the paper through Section 5 on the clock and Dana's hand. Footnote 4 is where I lose it, and checking the one sweeping sentence in that paragraph shows it is false as written.

**Where.** Section 6, the paragraph beginning "Suppose, then, that we restrict attention to stable rules." The problem sentence is "With ε large enough, such a mixture produces no high credence in anything stronger than what she knows, on any threshold for belief short of certainty." Footnote 4 supports it.

**What is wrong.** The certainty-level claim is right. For 0 < ε < 1, the mixture (1 − ε)f + εb is never certain of anything stronger than the learned proposition p, since that would require b to be certain of it too. The belief-level claim is not right. Take any rim state r of p. The interior of p lies in p∖{r}, so f gives p∖{r} credence 1, while conditionalization gives it 1 − π(r|p). The mixture gives it 1 − επ(r|p). Raising ε lowers this, but never below 1 − π(r|p).

Olivia's case shows the failure. Her prior is uniform, she learns [16, 18] at 17, and the mixture gives {16, 17} credence 1 − ε/3. Conditionalization gives 2/3, and no ε takes the mixture below 2/3. So for a belief threshold t, the mixture avoids belief in {16, 17} only if t > 2/3, and then only with ε > 3(1 − t). I checked the footnote's own numbers: its 0.8 (credence in 17 at ε = 0.3) is right, and the credence in {16, 17} at that ε is exactly 0.9, which sits on a 0.9 threshold.

**Why it matters.** The sentence "So even among stable rules, conditionalization is dominated" leans on the belief-level claim. The certainty-level version is enough. Certainty-stable mixtures are at least as accurate as conditionalization wherever f is, so conditionalization gets no accuracy support even among rules that never make her certain of more than she knows. That is what the first horn of the dilemma needs, and it does not need a threshold.

**What to do.** Delete the "any threshold for belief" sentence and rest the stable-rules result on certainty. If you want the belief version, state its condition: it can hold only for thresholds above one minus the smallest conditional probability of a rim state. Then cut most of footnote 4. Its "effacing" and "positive access" claims, and the claim that with two-step margins every rule in the family effaces some proposition, are asserted rather than argued, and a reader outside formal epistemology cannot check them. If the higher-order reply matters, state it in one plain sentence in the main text. That frees about 100 words. Spend them on saying which horn the author takes, which the conclusion currently leaves open.
