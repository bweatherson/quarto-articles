**Rewrite §5 around what M&Z say at pp. 320–23 and 337–39: the paper's own policy is contrastive and fails their idempotence test, their likeliest reply.**

*Misrepresentation.* §5 says M&Z meet the weighting complaint by calling extensionality "bedrock" (p. 337), then answers their high/low-level remark by saying the high level "is the rationality of learners without margins". Neither matches the text. On p. 337 they say that the dispute is "close to the bedrock of normative explanations": Bayesians "could dig in their heels", non-Bayesians may reply that "how likely it is for one to learn p at s" matters, and then "that is just a fact that we have to live with" (p. 338). So coarsening at random is not a hidden premise the paper exposes. It is their stated hinge, and Lemma 1 is the likelihood-weighted rule. Their high level "abstracts away" "environmental conditions" (p. 338) and is not confined to margin-free agents; p. 295 takes BCOND to be the rule for Olivia-type cases. The paper's real contribution answers their closing question (p. 339): margin externalists cannot accept extensionality. Say so.

Smaller slips: "Williamson's own gloss" (p. 294) is M&Z's wording; "M&Z's own examples" (§1) include the partitional left hand; n. 21 argues Monty Hall is compatible with noncontrastiveness once S is fine-grained, which §5 omits.

*Unconsidered reply.* §5 says idempotence "is untouched" because f_A is a function of p. But the paper recommends f_A to agents who know their learning is two-sided and something else to the rest. That is a map from learning situations to plans, an updating rule in M&Z's sense, and by Fact 6 it is idempotent only if noncontrastive. It is not: right-handed Dana is told to be certain of 15 on {15,16}; left-handed Dana (a partition, so Greaves–Wallace makes conditionalization uniquely best) is told 1/4.

Take n. 30's order, right hand at t1 and left at t2. She knows which hand she uses (n. 29), so the paper's "uncertainty about which hand delivers what" changes the case. The policy gives certainty at t1. The joint evidence equals the left hand's, so it gives 1/4 at t2, though the second reading adds nothing at 15. Keep the certainty and accumulation fails. This is M&Z's charge against meta-conditionalization. Their pp. 322–23 reply, that plans are ex ante and ex post fidelity is not required, also blunts §6's "cannot be followed" argument.
