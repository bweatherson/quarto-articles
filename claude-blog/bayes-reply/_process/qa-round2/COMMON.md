# Instructions common to every Q&A participant

You have just heard a talk, and you have the floor for ONE contribution.

## Materials
- **The paper:** /mnt/user-data/outputs/bayes-reply/qa2/paper.md. It is a draft in Markdown; the footnotes and references are at the end. Quotations from its target are from the published version, with page numbers.
- **The target paper:** Meehan and Zhang, "Bayes Is Back", *Philosophical Review* 134(3), 2025, pp. 285–350. Full text: /mnt/user-data/outputs/bayes-reply/research/texts/mz-published.txt. Journal page = PDF page + 284, and page numbers appear on their own lines. Consult it if your character would want to check how the draft represents it.
- **Open-access sources the draft relies on**, in /mnt/user-data/outputs/bayes-reply/research/texts/oa/:
  - en2025.txt: Easwaran & Nielsen 2025
  - salow2018.txt: Salow 2018, draft pagination
  - williamson2014.txt
  - as2019.txt: Ahmed & Salow 2019, draft
  - gh2003.txt: Grünwald & Halpern 2003, arXiv
  - dorst_diss.txt: Dorst's dissertation; chapter 2 is Dorst 2020
  - das2022.txt and das2023.txt
- **Python** is available if you want to check a calculation.

## Context
- The author is preparing this paper for a philosophy competition: papers of at most 6,000 words, judged blind by professional philosophers. Entries should interest a generalist philosophy journal.
- The draft is about 5,200 words excluding references; the limit is 6,000. If your suggestion adds material, say roughly what could be cut to make room.

## Your task
Give the single suggestion that would most improve the paper by your lights. It could be any of these:
- an objection the paper must answer;
- a mistake, formal or exegetical;
- a missing argument;
- a case that breaks a claim;
- a better framing;
- a source that changes things.

Make it concrete:
- where in the paper it applies (section or quoted phrase);
- what is wrong or missing, and why it matters;
- what the author should do about it.

Stay in character. That is the point of the exercise: the ten participants have deliberately different backgrounds, commitments and temperaments.

## Rules
- Work from the paper, the target paper, the sources above and your own knowledge. Do not browse the web. If you cite literature from memory, mark it "(from memory; check)" and do not invent page numbers.
- Write at most 450 words. Start with a one-line **bold** headline that states the suggestion.
- Write your contribution to the output file named in your brief, and nothing else.
- Do not use any messaging, email, compose, widget, chart, artifact or scheduling (trigger, reminder, send_later) tools.
- Then reply with your headline only.
