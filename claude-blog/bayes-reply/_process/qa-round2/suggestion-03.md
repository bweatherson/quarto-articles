**§6 misapplies safety. By Williamson's lights the margin makes the dominating certainty unsafe, so nothing becomes unstable. Recast §6 as accuracy rewarding certainty that is not knowledge.**

*Where.* §6, first paragraph: "It is also safe, since she would form it only where it is true … Nothing in Williamson's account of knowledge seems to deny it the status of knowledge."

*What is wrong.* Something in that account denies it exactly: the margin principle you state in §2. For Williamson it is derived from safety plus limited discrimination, and it constrains knowledge however acquired, rule-following included. Talk of discrimination is "just shorthand for talk of how much the subject can know" (Williamson 2014, 980 n. 2). Cases within the margin are those she cannot discriminate from the actual one, so safety tests a belief formed at 17 against 18, where "it weighs 17" is false. Your test checks the belief only at states where the rule delivers it. That assumes her beliefs track *E*(*s*) more finely than the margin allows, which is the discrimination two-sidedness denies.

So discharge the contradiction the other way. Olivia can comply with the dominating rule and stay two-sided. Her certainty is true but unsafe, and so not knowledge. Parity confirms this: complying with conditionalization also varies her credences state by state, and if that counted as discrimination, conditionalization would destabilize margins too.

On Williamson's clock the verdict is starker. *R*(*w*) is known only at *w*, so certainty of *w* is certainty that she knows *R*(*w*). She knows, to every iteration, that this has evidential probability at most 1/(2*h*+1) (2014, 980–81). That is the KK principle returning as credence.

It also clashes with §3, remark 1: followability "does not separate" the rules there, yet here they "cannot be followed".

*Why it matters.* The dilemma rests on §6. The corrected version is stronger, because it gives a principled asymmetry. Conditionalization's certainties (*R*(*w*)) are true throughout the margin, while the dominating rules' certainties (int *R*(*w*)) are false at its edge. Safety registers that difference and accuracy does not. This is the truth-rule objection, now applied to a globally evidentially constant rule.
