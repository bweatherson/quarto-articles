**Stop dodging idempotence in §5. In Meehan and Zhang's own ordering your view violates it, accuracy endorses the violation, and that is your §6 thesis, so claim it.**

1. *The "route" reply fails in their ordering.* You use n. 30's order, right hand then left. Use p. 320's: left then right. At 15 the left hand delivers {15, 16}; conditionalizing gives 1/4 on 15. The right hand then delivers {15, 16} at 15 and *S* at 16. If credence "responds to what each hand delivered", *f*<sub>*A*</sub> takes the right hand's {15, 16} and makes her certain of 15. So she goes from 1/4 to certainty on learning the same proposition again. That is their exact charge against meta-conditionalization (p. 320). It also violates idempotence\*, which they call "perhaps a more faithful" regimentation (n. 27). And accuracy *endorses* the violation: δ15, (¼, ¾, 0), δ17 at 15, 16, 17 weakly dominates staying put (Brier 0 against −2.25 at 15). To save the intuition, she must update the right hand's reading against what she already knows, which means ignoring an informative reading and responding to the conjunction after all.

2. *Your own Remark 1 refutes "coarsening at random … in normative dress".* *f*<sub>*A*</sub> is route-independent, and it is optimal exactly where coarsening at random fails. Coarsening at random is a condition on weightings, and it enters only with extensionality.

3. *Two misreadings.* "A rule that responds only to {15, 16} does the work": give Dana both hands and that rule makes her certain of 15 when her left hand delivers {15, 16} at 16 (Brier −4, log −∞). What does the work is a rule indexed to the hand. And n. 21 concedes nothing. Meehan and Zhang deny that the standard Monty Hall is a counterexample, and "less obvious that the standard solution still applies" leans *toward* conditionalization for opaque versions.

*How a defender escapes.* Treat idempotence as what "same evidence, same posterior" means (p. 309), and file the Proposition under the radical response (p. 334). *The cost:* idempotence then has no accuracy credentials for two-sided learners. What selects conditionalization is the requirement that credence be a function of what one has learned, taken together. That is evidential probability, not accuracy.

*What to do.* Cut from "But consider what drives it" to "does the work" (about 225 words). Replace it with about 150 words giving the calculation in p. 320's order, plus one claim: the violation is diachronic certainty without knowledge, since at *t*<sub>2</sub> she knows only {15, 16} yet is certain of 15. The support-function paragraph then follows from it. In §3, Remark 1, say that *f*<sub>*A*</sub> is non-contrastive but the view you recommend, one rule per route, is not.
