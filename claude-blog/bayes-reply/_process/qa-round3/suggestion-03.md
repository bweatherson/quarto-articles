**Section 6 polices the wrong knowledge norm: by Williamson's own result the agent knows her evidence makes the exact position improbable, and the norm that respects this leaves accuracy nothing to choose on the clock.**

*Where.* §6 from "On Williamson's clock"; §7's "rewards certainty that is not knowledge".

*The problem.* You cite Williamson (2014, 980) for Pr_w(KR(w)) = 1/(2h+1) but stop before his next step (980–81). Pr_x(KR(w)) ≤ 1/(2h+1) at every world, so the bound is known, to every iteration. The starker verdict's certainty is therefore not merely short of knowledge: its object is something the agent knows her evidence makes improbable. §6 then drops this charge. "Never be certain of what is not known" is not her norm. Your mixtures satisfy it while giving KR(w) credence 1 − 2ε/3: 0.8 at Gallow's ε = 0.3, 0.5 at Easwaran and Nielsen's ε = 3/4, against a known ceiling of 1/3. By her lights that is certainty's fault, diluted.

Her norm has two parts:
- (a) credence 1 in exactly what one knows;
- (b) no credence in *q* above a known upper bound on *q*'s evidential probability.

Both follow, by factivity, from conformity to evidential probability, and both are weaker. Neither needs the reflection principle that Williamson's appendix shows failing.

On Williamson's clock with a uniform prior, (a) and (b) leave exactly conditionalization, for every *h*. Each of the 2h+1 open positions has a known ceiling of 1/(2h+1). Dana at 15 is the same (ceilings 0.25 and 0.75). (Checked by linear programming over every proposition.) Elsewhere slack can remain: with states 0–4, prior (.01, .3, .3, .3, .09) and {1,2,3} learned at 2, credence in 2 may range from 1/3 to .49 and accuracy pushes inward again.

*What to do.*
1. State the knowledge-first constraint as (a) plus (b). §6's conclusion then sharpens: on the paradigm case, accuracy has nothing left to choose.
2. Move the signal-theorist sentence to §7's accuracy-first option. It is what accuracy yields when only certainty is policed, not a resting place for the knowledge-first theorist.
3. In §7, give Williamson's diagnosis instead. Accuracy scores credence at the actual world, so it rewards confidence in truths about one's own epistemic state, such as KR(w). Anti-luminosity keeps those truths out of one's evidence, and the evidence marks them as improbable.
4. Small fixes:
   - Williamson's "cannot discriminate" means epistemic accessibility (980 n. 2). Any certainty stronger than *E*(*s*) is then false at an indiscriminable state, so "next to the rim" is unnecessary.
   - The 1/(2h+1) sentence concerns the single-situation verdict. For h > 1 the cross-situation rule is certain only of the 2h − 1 interior positions.

*Cost.* About 80 words net. Cut §5's second "smaller point".
