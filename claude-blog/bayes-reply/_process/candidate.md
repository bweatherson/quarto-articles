# Candidate reply to Meehan & Zhang (M&Z), "Bayes Is Back", Phil Review 134(3): 285–350 (2025)

## Thesis (one sentence)
Learning with a margin for error, the kind of learning that makes evidence opaque, is never "coarsening at random"; since conditionalization is the accuracy-best evidence-only rule exactly when learning is coarsening at random, M&Z's total-expected-accuracy vindication of conditionalization works only because their measure over learning situations gives most of its weight to learning in which the evidence is unsafe (the truth lies within the margin of the evidence's boundary). Restricted to margin-respecting learning, their own bridge principle selects a different, globally evidentially constant rule: conditionalize on the *interior* of the evidence (the states at which the evidence holds with a margin to spare), or a conservative softening of that rule.

## Formal core (all checked numerically: code/checks.py, code/margins_fast.py)
Notation as in M&Z: finite S, regular prior π, factive learning situations E (s ∈ E(s)), weights λ over learning situations, λ_p(s) = Σ_{E: E(s)=p} λ(E). GEC = globally evidentially constant (posterior depends only on (S, π, p)).

1. **Reduction.** For a GEC rule f, λ-weighted total expected accuracy equals (Σλ times) ordinary expected accuracy in an auxiliary problem on S × {learning situations}, prior π(s)λ(E)/Σλ, in which the agent's evidence is the cell "the proposition I learned is p". That auxiliary problem is *transparent* (the cells partition it). The optimal GEC rule is therefore conditionalization in the auxiliary problem: π(s | λ_p) ∝ π(s) λ_p(s) (M&Z's Lemma 1), i.e., likelihood-weighted updating with "likelihood" λ_p(s). It equals π(· | p) iff λ_p is constant on p (M&Z's "extensionality"), which is the statisticians' condition of coarsening at random (CAR: Heitjan & Rubin 1991; Gill, van der Laan & Robins 1997; Grünwald & Halpern 2003). Same-shape results in philosophy: Isaacs & Russell 2023 (their Theorem 4, reported by Easwaran & Nielsen 2025, p. 1120), Easwaran & Nielsen 2025 §4.4 (stochastic "signals"; optimal plan ∝ prior × likelihood), Gallow 2021. Kenny Easwaran made the informal equivalence point about M&Z in a comment on Richard Pettigrew's Substack post (20 Dec 2025). So M&Z's Facts 10–12 are: the uniform measure is CAR; conditionalization is optimal iff CAR; a single deterministic learning situation is CAR iff partitional.
2. **Why the uniform measure is CAR.** Any weighting invariant under permutations of S is CAR (transposition argument). M&Z's counting measure is permutation-invariant: it treats S as an unstructured set.
3. **Margins ⇒ not CAR (new, as far as I know).** Let R be a reflexive closeness relation on S ("within the margin for error"). Margin principle (Williamson; evidence = knowledge + safety): R(s) ⊆ E(s). If λ gives weight only to margin-respecting learning situations, then λ_p(s) = 0 whenever R(s) ⊄ p. So λ is CAR only if every proposition ever learned is closed under R. If S is R-connected (as any magnitude scale is: the sorites chain), the only R-closed propositions are ∅ and S. Hence: **margin-respecting learning is CAR only if nothing is learned.** Consequently, for every margin-respecting weighting with any nontrivial learning, conditionalization does not maximize λ-total expected accuracy among GEC rules (by M&Z's own Fact 11).
4. **The interior rule.** Under the uniform weighting over all margin-respecting learning situations, λ_p(s) ∝ 1[R(s) ⊆ p]·N(s)⁻¹ (N(s) = number of propositions containing R(s)), so the optimal GEC rule conditionalizes (with a boundary weight) on int(p) = {s : R(s) ⊆ p}. Dana (M&Z's example): learn {15,16} → certainty in 15; learn {16,17} → certainty in 17; learn S → (1/8, 3/4, 1/8). That is M&Z's own "one-shot" foil on the opaque evidence. Olivia: learn [16,18] → certainty in 17. Brier and log total expected accuracy both favour it over conditionalization. Because M&Z's own scoring rules are concave, conservative mixtures (1−ε)·interior + ε·conditionalization also beat conditionalization, so a conservativity requirement does not save it.
5. **What the uniform measure is doing.** Under M&Z's measure over all factive situations on a 6-point scale with margin 1, only 1/3 of (state, evidence) pairs respect the margin (→ 1/4 as the scale grows; 4^{-m} for margin m). Conditionalization wins because unsafe-evidence situations (the truth near the edge of what is "learned") are weighted so as to exactly cancel the interior bias of margin-respecting ones. Mixed domains (transparent + margin learning, any positive margin weight) are not CAR.
6. **GEC does not block meta-conditionalization's verdicts in margin cases.** The interior rule is GEC and reaches meta-cond's verdicts from p alone, because under margins the shape of the evidence carries information about where the truth lies. So non-contrastiveness rules out using one's *particular* learning situation, not the informativeness of having learned p.

## Dialectic and upshot
- M&Z concede (§5.3) that bcond's optimality is equivalent to extensionality and call it "bedrock", defending it as cutting epistemology at its joints; non-Bayesians reply that likelihood matters. The new claim is that, in the very cases that create the problem, extensionality is not merely contestable but *incompatible with the source of opacity* (margins), unless weight goes to unsafe-evidence learning, which E=K externalists deem impossible.
- Readings of λ: frequency (M&Z's jaywalking analogy suggests it) → margin learning → not bcond; agent's credence (Carr 2021) → Dana's point mass → meta-cond; "importance" → extensionality must be defended as impartiality across p-states, but restricted to margin-respecting situations no nontrivial impartial weighting exists (Theorem).
- The externalist Bayesian must give up one of: (a) margins as the source of opacity, (b) an accuracy-based (rule-level) explanation of conditionalization, (c) conditionalization for inexact evidence. Honest options: primitive evidential probability (Williamson; Das 2022), or interior-weighted updating (close to Easwaran & Nielsen's likelihood updating with centre-peaked likelihoods).
- Illustration with the appearance-centred measurement model (Williamson 2013 "Gettier cases in epistemic logic"; Weatherson 2013 "Margins and Errors"): evidence [a−(e+m), a+(e+m)] with truth a±e; given the evidence interval the truth is one of two points.

## Main risks
- Novelty: the CAR/likelihood reduction is partly anticipated (Easwaran comment; Easwaran & Nielsen; Isaacs & Russell Thm 4; Gallow). The margin theorem is elementary; is it already in Salow 2018, Das 2023, Williamson 2011/2014, Dorst 2019/2020, Gallow 2021, Schultheis 2025?
- M&Z might say total expected accuracy is by definition over all factive situations of the prior space; restricting the domain is changing the subject.
- The interior rule's certainty verdicts (e.g., exact clock position) look absurd; the paper must present this as a reductio of the accuracy route for margin learning, or adopt a softened version.
