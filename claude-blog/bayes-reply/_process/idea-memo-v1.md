# Idea memo v1: reply to Meehan & Zhang, "Bayes Is Back" (Phil Review 134(3): 285–350, 2025)

Working title: **Inexact Learning Is Never Random**

## 1. The problem (stated without reference to the recent exchange)
Conditionalization says: on learning p, adopt your prior conditional on p. The best-known justification is accuracy-based: among plans that respond only to what you learn, conditionalization maximizes expected accuracy (Greaves & Wallace 2006). That justification assumes you always know what you have learned. Externalists say you sometimes don't. Williamson's margin-for-error argument is the standard route: inexact knowledge of a magnitude never has a sharp edge, so what you know varies with the true value in ways you cannot track. When learning is opaque in this way, the expected-accuracy maximizer among evidence-dependent plans conditionalizes on the fact that one has learned p, not on p (Schoenfield 2017). So: can accuracy justify conditionalization for agents who can learn without knowing what they have learned?

## 2. Meehan & Zhang's answer
(i) Global evidential constancy (GEC): the posterior may depend only on the prior over S and the evidence proposition p, not on the learning situation (non-contrastiveness, defended through idempotence). (ii) Hi-TEA: evaluate GEC rules by total expected accuracy, summed with equal weight over every factive learning situation for the prior space. Conditionalization uniquely maximizes it (Fact 10). In §5.3 they concede that with weights λ over learning situations, conditionalization is optimal iff λ is "extensional" (Fact 11). They call this "bedrock" (p. 337): Bayesians may insist that extensionality "cuts epistemology at its joints"; non-Bayesians may reply that how likely one is to learn p at s also matters.

## 3. Thesis
In the cases that create the problem, extensionality is not merely contestable; it is incompatible with the structure that makes learning opaque. Margin-for-error learning is never coarsening at random unless nothing is learned. On every weighting confined to margin-respecting learning, M&Z's own bridge principle selects a non-Bayesian GEC rule that gives no credence to the rim of the evidence (the evidence states within a margin of its boundary): "interior conditionalization", or a conservative softening of it. M&Z's equal-weight measure vindicates conditionalization only because it counts learning situations as if the learner had no margin for error. Under that measure most (state, evidence) pairs have the truth within a margin of the evidence's edge, i.e. "evidence" that could not be safe knowledge. Accuracy and conditionalization therefore part company exactly where externalism begins. The externalist Bayesian must ground conditionalization for inexact evidence in something other than accuracy (e.g., Williamson's evidential probability taken as primitive). Alternatively, she can give up conditionalization for inexact evidence.

## 4. Formal results (checked numerically: code/)
R1 (reduction; partly anticipated by Easwaran's blog comment and by Easwaran & Nielsen 2025; cf. Isaacs & Russell 2023 Thm 4, Gallow 2021). λ-total expected accuracy of a GEC rule = (Σλ) × expected accuracy in a transparent auxiliary problem, with "likelihood" λ_p(s). The optimum is π(s)λ_p(s), renormalized. It equals conditionalization iff λ_p is constant on p: coarsening at random (Gill, van der Laan & Robins 1997; Grünwald & Halpern 2003, Thm 3.1, Prop 4.1 ≈ M&Z Fact 12).
R2. Every permutation-invariant λ is CAR. M&Z's counting measure is permutation-invariant, so it treats S as structureless. It is also exactly the uniform measure over learning situations that respect a margin of zero (factivity = margin principle with R = identity).
R3 (margins; new?). If every learning situation in λ's support satisfies R(s) ⊆ E(s) for a reflexive R, then CAR ⇒ every learned proposition is R-closed. On an R-connected space (any magnitude scale), CAR ⇒ only the trivial proposition is ever learned. Corollaries:
- any margin-respecting weighting with nontrivial learning makes conditionalization strictly suboptimal (M&Z Fact 11 / Lemma 2);
- adding any positive-margin learner to M&Z's uniform population breaks CAR;
- the optimum always gives zero credence to rim(p) = {s ∈ p : R(s) ⊄ p}.
R4. Uniform margin-respecting weighting: the optimum is π(s)·|{q : R(s) ⊆ q}|⁻¹ restricted to int(p). Dana: {15,16} → certainty in 15 (= M&Z's one-shot foil on these inputs). Olivia: [16,18] → 17. TEA (Brier, log) favours it over conditionalization. Concave scoring ⇒ conservative mixtures also beat conditionalization.
R5. Under M&Z's uniform measure on an n-point scale with margin m, the share of (state, evidence) pairs that respect the margin is about 4^{−m} for interior states (1/3 for n = 6, m = 1).
R6 (side). The good-case/bad-case asymmetry (Isaacs & Russell's friend/stranger) is also not CAR unless the good case is never recognized.

## 5. Objections and replies (to develop)
O1. TEA is defined over all factive situations; restricting it changes the subject. Reply: any weighting is a choice. Equal counting over situations that are impossible for inexact knowledge is not "robustness", and M&Z's own jaywalking analogy evaluates rules over the situations in which they would actually be applied. Conditionalization's optimality is knife-edge in λ.
O2. The weights measure importance, not frequency. Reply: R3 holds for any interpretation of λ supported on margin-respecting learning.
O3. GEC means the rule must serve agents with different margins who share a prior space (Dana, Noah, perfect discriminators). Reply: the aggregate over such agents is non-CAR unless the only learners are perfect discriminators (margin 0) or learn nothing.
O4. The interior rule is absurd (certainty about the exact clock position). Reply: then accuracy-first rule evaluation is the wrong foundation for updating on inexact evidence, which is the conclusion. M&Z's own §3.3 shows that followability does not separate conditionalization from such rules (luminosity). Softened versions are not absurd: down-weighting the rim of one's evidence is plausible for real measurement.
O5. Evidence need not be knowledge or safe. Reply: then opacity needs another source; M&Z's own examples (Olivia, Dana) are margin-structured (R = ±1 / adjacency).
O6. Refine S to include appearances. Reply: if appearances are luminous, learning becomes transparent, and the externalist problem is abandoned rather than solved.

## 7. Unifying the two ways of modelling what the agent responds to (added 13:55)
- Horn 1 (M&Z, Schoenfield): the update is a function of the evidence proposition p. Weighted TEA is expected accuracy with "signal" = p and likelihood λ_p(s). Under margins, the optimum puts zero credence on rim(p) (R3).
- Horn 2 (Gallow 2021; Isaacs & Russell 2023; Easwaran & Nielsen 2025): the update is a function of a signal σ the agent can respond to, with likelihoods L(σ|s). The optimum is π(s)L(σ|s), renormalized. Suppose the knowledge σ delivers is a proposition K(σ) (needed for "conditionalize on what you know" to be an available plan at all). Then factivity requires L(σ|s) > 0 ⇒ s ∈ K(σ), and the margin principle requires L(σ|s) > 0 ⇒ R(s) ⊆ K(σ). So L(σ|·) vanishes on rim(K(σ)), and the optimum equals conditionalization on K(σ) only if rim(K(σ)) = ∅. On a connected space, that means K(σ) = S. (E&N's uniform-within-margin dial case is conditionalization on a ball that is NOT safe knowledge: the truth can sit at its edge.)
- Unified claim: **when knowledge has a margin for error, accuracy never recommends conditionalizing on what one knows, except when one knows nothing.** The truth lies at least a margin inside safe knowledge. Conditionalization is accuracy-optimal only when the truth is as likely to lie at the edge of the conditioned proposition as anywhere else in it.
- Thesis framing for the paper: the problem (can accuracy justify conditionalization for externalists?) gets a negative answer for inexact knowledge, the source of opacity. M&Z's Hi-TEA escapes only because its counting measure is the uniform measure for a learner with no margin for error.
