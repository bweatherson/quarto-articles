# Referee report: "Constant's Key: Principles, Harm and Pretended Laws"

## Summary judgement

The contribution is on Constant's side: reading the example through his 1797 theory of principles, and the 1815 note on Rousseau's civil religion, which may be new. The Kant quotations from 8:425–430 are accurate as far as they go, but the Kant side is too thin for the claims made about it:
- The paper says Kant "accepts the framework", but 8:428–430 reject it.
- It never states or applies Kant's test for telling a closer determination from an exception.
- Its conclusion, that what remains is the correlation of duty with right, runs together Constant's general correlation thesis and the restriction Kant actually calls the πρῶτον ψεῦδος. It misses that Kant keeps a correlate (humanity) and denies anyone a juridical right to another's truthfulness.
- It ignores where the 1815 key really collides with Kant: his public right.
- The *Religion* comparison contains factual errors.

## Major issues

**1. Kant does not "accept the framework", and the exception criterion is never applied.**
- Kant amends Constant's maxim with "a priori anerkannter, mithin apodiktischer" (8:429).
- He says principles bear that name only because of the *Allgemeinheit* that exceptions destroy (8:430). That is strict universality, "so daß gar keine Ausnahme als möglich verstattet wird" (KrV B4). Constant says the essence of a principle is "pas d'être général … mais d'être fixe", and defines a principle as a "general result of … particular facts", which is the comparative universality of B3–4.
- He limits intermediate principles to applying right "nach Regeln der Politik" (8:430). For truthfulness, "hier auch wirklich keiner einzuschieben ist" (8:428).
- The 8:275 line ("not enough theory") is about the empirical arts. Kant then says that for theory "auf dem Pflichtbegriff gegründet", "Alles verloren ist, wenn die empirischen … Bedingungen der Ausführung des Gesetzes zu Bedingungen des Gesetzes selbst gemacht" are made (8:276–277). That is an objection to both keys.
- The paper asserts that a duty restricted by persons need not be an exception. Kant denies exactly this at 8:429 ("keinen Unterschied zwischen Personen"), and the paper gives no argument against him.

*Fix:* State what *nähere Bestimmung* means. I read it as specifying how a principle is realised (representation) without shrinking the cases it covers. Then argue whether the 1815 key qualifies.

**2. The conclusion misidentifies what remains.**
- (a) Kant targets [4], a duty "*only* towards someone who has a right to the truth" (8:425). That is Constant's "donc", not his general thesis. The abstract and the final paragraph drop "only". Kant says the duty is owed "gegen jeden" (8:426).
- (b) Kant accepts correlativity for duties of right: "nicht aller Pflicht correspondiren Rechte eines Anderen … sondern diese heißen besonders Rechtspflichten" (MS 6:383). The essay concerns a *Rechtspflicht* (8:426n), and it supplies a correlate: the wrong is done to "Menschheit überhaupt" (8:426), "formaliter, obgleich nicht materialiter" (8:429). Timmermann says as much: "the bearer of this right is humanity at large" (¶29–30). Kant moves the correlate; he does not deny it.
- (c) The 1815 key agrees with Kant only about the verdict. On the reading Timmermann takes from J. Weinrib (2008), Kant's concession does not depend on the questioner's intentions. Nobody has a juridical right to my truthfulness: *falsiloquium* falls within innate right "weil es bloß auf ihnen beruht, ob sie ihm glauben wollen" (MS 6:238 and note), and "ein Recht auf die Wahrheit" is "ein Wort ohne Sinn" (8:426). The paper's reductio, that the stranger asking the way would have no right to the truth, is Kant's own juridical position.
- (d) Kant objects to *any* key. Making the duty "zu einer bedingten" is a formal wrong "viel schlimmer" than injustice to an individual, because it presupposes a principle in the subject (8:429). The agent who first considers possible exceptions is "schon ein Lügner (in potentia)" (8:430). So Constant's insistence that his modification is a principle aggravates the wrong.

*Fix:* Rewrite §5 to keep four questions apart: whether there can be a formal wrong with no individual wronged; whether anyone has a claim-right to sincerity; whether a duty may be conditioned by principle; and the ethical duty to oneself (8:426n; MS 6:429–430).

**3. The paper omits the collision that matters for 1815: Kant's public right.**
Constant's chapter IX asserts a "reciprocity of duties" between people and government. Kant denies each step:
- the ruler has "lauter Rechte und keine (Zwangs=)Pflichten" (MS 6:319);
- a subject who judges the ruler wants to be judge "in seiner eigenen Sache" (6:320);
- the ban on resistance holds even if the ruler has made himself "verlustig" of his right to legislate (8:299);
- one must obey "ohne vorhergehende Forschung dem *angemaßten* Rechte" (6:371). Note the echo of "prétendues lois".

Kant's carve-out ("was nicht dem inneren Moralischen widerstreitet", 6:371; Religion 6:99n) permits refusing to profess, not lying. Constant's own 1815 "force d'inertie" (Laboulaye, 14) is an obvious point of contact that the paper misses. Kantian judgement subsumes cases under rules; it does not adjudicate titles. So the later key's reliance on "each man is judge … at his own risk" (92) is not "closer to Kant".

*Fix:* Add this material. Then test the claim that "nothing about the questioner's standing reaches" Kant against three things:
- Varden (2010), on barbaric versus rightful conditions;
- Wood's reading of *Aussagen* as declarations (2008, ch. 14);
- Religion 6:4, where the duty of avowal applies when it is "rechtmäßig abgefordert". This is the passage Kloocke actually cites, on the Timmermann page the paper itself cites.

**4. The *Religion* comparison cannot bear the weight.**
- (a) Kant condemns "Unwahrhaftigkeit im Glauben (oder dessen selbst bloß innerem Bekenntnisse)" (6:187). That is ethical and non-relational. "Verdammlich" and "crime" are different registers: Kant could deny that a crime is committed (MS 6:238) and still call the profession damnable. The passage therefore cannot show that the difference is "whether truthfulness is owed to the one who asks". If anything, it supports Timmermann's duty-to-oneself reading.
- (b) The claim that neither invokes "consequences" is false. Constant cites family and exile. Kant rejects the maxim of avoiding "die nachtheiligen Folgen … aus dem Nichtbekennen" (6:189).
- (c) They do not agree about the compeller's guilt. Constant's "vous, vous seul" makes it exclusive. Kant holds the compeller answerable for misuse but the professor's untruthfulness damnable "an sich"; compare MS 6:431, "Allerdings auch auf den letzteren".
- (d) In "bei Verlust *ihres* Standes", the "ihres" cannot refer to *das Volk*, which Kant treats as neuter singular ("es"). Given the Woellner context, the sanction probably falls on the clergy.

**5. The use of Timmermann, and literature the paper does not engage.**
The Timmermann quotations are accurate, though his "not talking about the state" (n. 60) concerns disciplines (morals versus politics), not who the assassins are. The real problem is that the paper under-credits him. He already rejects [1] and [3] and identifies [4] as the crux (¶24), and he notes that the two "concur" that the murderer is not wronged (¶30). His ¶30 argument (point 2c above) goes unanswered.

The paper must also engage the following (details unverified, no page numbers given):
- Korsgaard (1986): her deceiving questioner is close to the paper's later key.
- Varden (2010).
- J. Weinrib (2008).
- Wood (2008, ch. 14).
- Mahon (2003, 2006), on reticence and duties to others.
- Schapiro (2003, 2006), on complicity and nonideal conditions.
- Paton (1954).
- Ripstein (2009), on innate right. I am unsure how far he treats the 1797 case.
- Geismann and Oberer (eds., 1986).
- Benton (1982): it is in the bibliography and uncited, yet "not expediency" alludes to it.

## Minor issues

- "Wohldenkend" (8:427) means "right-minded", not "well thought".
- The quotation from 8:426 omits several things:
  - "so viel an mir ist";
  - the clause about contracts and the *Rechtsquelle*;
  - Kant's rejection of the jurists' definition, which requires harm ("in praeiudicium alterius").

  Kant's own argument also appeals to harm ("schadet jederzeit … der Menschheit überhaupt"). Does §3's critique of keying the duty to harm rebound on Kant?
- The paper never notes that the duty covers only "Aussagen, die man nicht umgehen kann" (8:426, 8:428). This matters for the Rousseau case, where refusal is possible, and for the *Offenherzigkeit*/*Aufrichtigkeit* distinction (6:190n).
- 6:190n reads "fordern *können*". The "können" has been dropped.
- At 8:428 the truthful speaker "eigentlich nicht schaden" does; chance does the harm. The *nocet*/*laedit* distinction concerns imputation, and the circularity of the argument is well known.
- "Nul homme n'a droit à la vérité qui nuit à autrui" arguably lets the relative clause attach to *homme*. The paper should acknowledge this before insisting that the clause excludes forfeiture.
- "A test of whether a strict duty yields to catastrophe" caricatures current readings.

## Recommendation

**Major revision.** The Constant material deserves publication. The Kant side should be rebuilt around 8:428–430, MS 6:238/6:383 and public right. The conclusion should be restated as a disagreement about formal wrong, claim-rights to sincerity and principled conditioning, not about "correlation".
