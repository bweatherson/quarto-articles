# Canon check: T1 (what a principle is) and T2 (proscription reading)

Checked 28 Oct 2026; open-access sources only; searches in EN, FR, DE, PT, ES, IT, RU and SL; page numbers come from the PDFs.

## T1: verdict PARTLY ANTICIPATED

### Closest items

1. **Zupančič 1993 (Slovene). This is the strongest anticipation of "not an exception".**
   - The *principe intermédiaire* "natančneje določa, ki precizira aplikacijo splošnih načel" ("more closely determines, makes precise, the application of general principles") (p. 175).
   - Kant "vidi poskus napraviti pravilo iz izjeme od pravila" ("sees an attempt to make a rule out of an exception to the rule") and fights a "fiktivnim nasprotnikom" ("fictitious opponent") (p. 178).
   - "Constant nikjer ne uporabi besede izjema" ("Constant nowhere uses the word exception") (p. 179).
   - Circumstances "zožujejo okvir splošnega ... pravila, ne da bi to krnilo njegovo splošnost" ("narrow the scope of the general rule without impairing its generality") (p. 180).
   - Constant's "relativna" generality "ni v smislu, da lahko ... naredim izjemo" ("is not in the sense that I may make an exception") (p. 180).

   Her route is the legal theory of self-defence and the Lacanian "not-all"; she does not use "fixe" or universality/generality. https://ojs.zrc-sazu.si/filozofski-vestnik/article/view/3852

2. **Loparic 2006.**
   - He quotes the definition: a first principle is general only "de uma maneira relativa", and its "essência ... não é a de ele ser geral ... mas de ser fixo" (p. 63).
   - He contrasts this with Kant: "Em Kant, os princípios ... são estabelecidos ... a priori" (p. 65).
   - He quotes 8:430, that exceptions "negam a universalidade" (p. 71).
   - But he reads Constant's restriction as "a especificação de casos de exceção" (p. 65), and his contrast is empirical versus a priori, not fixity versus universality.

   https://periodicos.sbu.unicamp.br/ojs/index.php/kant/article/view/8672466

3. **Blinov 2019a.**
   - It is "сложно найти определение принципов, более далекое от кантовского" ("hard to find a definition of principles further from Kant's").
   - Hence the conception "делает изначально бессмысленной любую дискуссию Констана и Канта" ("makes any Constant–Kant discussion senseless from the outset") (p. 199).

   T1 reverses his conclusion. His translation (2019b) drops "fixe". https://philosophy.hse.ru/article/view/9810

4. **Feldhaus 2011 and 2018.**
   - 2011: "quando existem exceções, as mesmas também devem se constituir em princípios, que ele chama de intermediários"; principles are "não ser gerais ... mas apenas fixos" (p. 127).
   - 2018: the disagreement concerns "qual a função das regras de aplicação" (p. 13).
   - He still reads the intermediate principles as ones "que regulam as exceções" (2018, p. 9).

5. **Hare 1993/1997.** Kant's principles "do not have to be as simple and general as Kant seems to have thought"; "they can still be universal all the same" (*Sorting Out Ethics*, ch. 8). He does not mention Constant.

   Beck (1960, pp. 79–80, quoted by Pavão 2011, p. 80 n. 13) blames the essay on Kant's failure to separate "rule" from "principle".

6. **Timmermann 2025, ch. V.** The summary asks "what are Constant's 'intermediate principles', which he introduces to defuse the problem case?" (paywalled, unread).

7. **Lalatta Costerbosa 2014.** Her abstract finds a shared moral and political refusal of arbitrariness. This anticipates the "two anti-arbitrariness theories" payoff.

8. **Kapustin 2008.** He takes the standard exception reading, "исключениях из общего правила" ("exceptions to the general rule"). He calls correlativity the "азы теории естественного права" ("ABC of natural-law theory").

### What remains new
- Making the definitional opening the reason the restriction is no exception. Two items count: "fixe, not general" and "secondary principles are just as immutable". Loparic and Feldhaus quote the definition but still read exceptions.
- Reading 8:430 together with the inserted "a priori anerkannter, mithin apodiktischer" (8:429) as a rival definition. I found no discussion of the insertion.
- "Keinen Unterschied zwischen Personen" ("no distinction between persons") read as presupposing generality.
- Hare as a lens, specificationism, and codifiability against particularism, none of them applied to Constant.
- The point that Kant quotes nothing from Cramer's definitional pages.

### Read before claiming novelty

- Zupančič, *Ethics of the Real* (2000), ch. 3.

## T2: verdict PARTLY ANTICIPATED (substantially)

### Closest items
1. **Holmes 1984, as reported by Wood.** Constant's position is "the outcome of his experiences during the French Revolution, where the line separating police officials from murderers was not necessarily well-defined". Wood adds: "the real issue is the duty to speak truthfully in declarations in political contexts" (Wood 2011, unpaginated HTML). https://rcientificas.uninorte.edu.co/index.php/eidos/article/download/3671/html_217?inline=1

2. **Troitsky 2016.**
   - The dilemma is "не ... «человек – человек», а «государство – человек»" ("not person–person but state–person").
   - Lying would be "восстанием против власти" ("rebellion against authority").
   - The murderer may be a "представитель правящей партии якобинцев, который врывается в розыске жирондиста" ("representative of the ruling Jacobin party breaking in in search of a Girondin") (p. 88 and n. 56).
   - He links the lying ban to Kant's ban on resistance.

   https://et.iphras.ru/article/download/2666/2001

3. **Solovyov 2008.** Kant ignores the problem field of "публичной исповеди, дознания, допроса" ("public confession, inquest, interrogation") after Jacobin rule (p. 24).

   **Apresyan 2008.** Kant "не мог не понимать о каком преследовании ... шла речь" ("could not have failed to understand what persecution was meant") (p. 7). https://iphras.ru/uplfile/ethics/biblio/logos_5_2008/03.pdf and …/01.pdf

4. **Rousselière 2018.** The debate concerns "the responsibility of individual agents toward political principles in post-revolutionary times": Constant's "principled pragmatism" against Kant's "political legalism" (abstract only).

5. **Albiac 2012.** He places the text after the Terror and the Law of 22 Prairial against "enemigos del pueblo" ("enemies of the people").

6. **Blinov 2019a.** He reads ch. VIII as a middle path "между террором и реакцией" ("between terror and reaction").

### Not found
- Staël and Narbonne.
- "Visites domiciliaires" or "hors la loi".
- The right to truth read as limited sovereignty.
- The 1815 note (see check 1 below).

### What remains new
- The 1815 note as Constant's own confirmation.
- Outlawry as the concrete form of forfeiture.
- Limited sovereignty.
- Narbonne as an illustration, with its dating caveat.

The political, state-questioner reading and its link to resistance are not new.

### Read before claiming novelty
- Holmes 1984 (locate the passage); Rousselière (full text).
- Benton 1982; Fenves 1998/99 (not checked).
- Timmermann chs III and V. Ch. V's keywords include "politics".
- Boituzat 1993.

**(2) Over-breadth (court witness) or harm-keyed clause: NOT FOUND as an objection to Constant.**

Queries run:
- EN: Constant "no one has a right to the truth that harms others" objection witness court; "right to the truth" "harms another" would license lying to courts.
- FR: "nul homme n'a droit à la vérité qui nuit à autrui" témoin tribunal coupable; "droit à la vérité" "qui nuit à autrui" critique trop large témoin.
- DE: "Recht auf eine Wahrheit, die anderen schadet" Zeuge.

Nearest items, after grepping the downloaded texts:
- Kant himself, 8:428: Constant confused *nocet* (harm) with *laedit* (wrong). This is the harm-versus-wrong contrast, but applied to the truth-teller.
- Timmermann's ch. VI summary reframes Constant as forfeiture: the murderer "has forfeited his right to be told the truth".
- Apresyan (2008, p. 7) distinguishes testifying "на справедливом суде" ("before a just court") from testifying before a court "подчиненном произволу правителя" ("subject to the ruler's arbitrariness"). Both of these are said against Kant, not against Constant's clause.

**(3) Kant's "schwankend und unnütz" (8:427) answering to Constant's fixity/"utilité" (pp. 67–68) and "tout est vacillant" (p. 78): NOT FOUND.**

Queries run:
- Kant "schwankend und unnütz" Constant fixity "vacillant";
- Kant reply Constant own standard "uncertain and useless" … "by his own lights";
- FR: "chancelante et inutile" OR "vacillante et inutile" principe fixe arbitraire.

Nearest items, after grepping the downloaded texts:
- Loparic (2006, p. 70) notes Kant's "ironia condescendente" about Constant's "contradição", using his maxim that a principle must never be abandoned (8:428). This is an internal critique, but on *abandonment*, not on fixity.
- Lalatta Costerbosa 2014 identifies a shared anti-arbitrariness (abstract only).
- Zupančič (p. 180) attacks Constant's claim to exclude "vsaka arbitrarnost" ("all arbitrariness"), but from outside.

The fixity/"schwankend" match appears new.
