# Advisor's report on draft v2, "The Principle Constant Needed"

28 Oct 2026. Read myself: Constant 1797 chs. VIII–IX to p. 91 (full), I and VII (skim); AA 8:425–430, 8:275, 3:29, 6:237–238, 6:319–320, 6:429–431; 1815 *Principes* (Laboulaye) ch. I p. 9, ch. XI pp. 92–95, ch. XVII pp. 128–30; Timmermann chs. V–VI (full), III ¶11, IV ¶17, VII ¶30, VIII ¶¶36, 41–43, IX ¶47.

**Bottom line.** The ch. VIII reading is careful and the 1815 note is a real find. But the four headline claims (the note "states the principle the 1797 case needed"; it "meets both Constant's standard and Kant's distinction"; it "shows, against the leading recent study, that the case concerns the state"; what remains is a dispute about authority) are all overstated, and the second and fourth are unsound as argued. Finish the paper, on a narrower thesis (H).

## A. Textual accuracy

Correct as quoted: ch. VIII pp. 66–68 and 72–77; pp. 80, 81, 82, 90–91; ch. I p. 6; ch. VII pp. 50, 56–57; 1815 pp. 9, 94–95, 128–29 (the note reads the same in the Institut Coppet text, principes.txt ll. 5870–81); Kant 8:425–430, 8:275, B3–4, 6:319–320. Timmermann's wording matches; his page numbers can't be checked from the .txt files.

Errors and over-readings:
1. **p. 78** (ll. 2565–86) ends ch. VIII, not ch. IX. It describes what follows from *rejecting principles altogether*. "That is Constant's definition of the arbitrary" (§3) is wrong: the definition is at p. 80. The "point d'appui" is the one "les réclamations" can't find (l. 2572), i.e. a footing for protest against power, not for interlocutors' reliance; "It offers no fulcrum" changes the image's job.
2. **"The word 'exception' does not appear"** is false: "exceptions individuelles" (p. 64, l. 2079); "exceptions sans nombre, en faveur de vos propres intérêts" (p. 62, l. 2000). Limit the claim to the example, then use both passages; they support your fixity reading.
3. **"Comparatively universal in Kant's sense, and he says so."** Constant's "général que d'une manière relative" (p. 66) means relative to scope ("par rapport aux combinaisons desquelles il résulte"). It is not Kant's inductive universality, which tolerates exceptions; Constant calls secondary principles "immuables" (p. 67). He affirms inductive *origin*, which is another point.
4. **p. 73 omits a clause.** Morality is further advanced because its direction "n'étoit pas faussée par les intérêts personnels des dépositaires, ou des usurpateurs du pouvoir" (ll. 2375–77). Timmermann relies on it (ch. V ¶23), so leaving it out makes the reply to him look selective. Quote it, with pp. 83–84 ("c'est donc spécialement dans la politique que l'arbitraire s'est réfugié"), which helps you.
5. **"Its decrees had the form of law without the substance"** is your gloss. Constant stresses the *absence* of forms: "aucune apparence d'institution … aucune forme protectrice, aucune loi fixe" (pp. 89–90).
6. **"The text the note annotates"**: the note is attached to the Rousseau quotation (p. 128, l. 8089). The "juridiction naturelle" sentence is the main text that follows (p. 129).
7. **"Falls under the note's wording directly"** is false. "N'exigent de nous la vérité que pour nous proscrire" is first-person: the person questioned is the one proscribed. At the door, he isn't.
8. **"Kant names the missing key."** At 8:428 Kant uses *nocet/laedit* to class the truthful answer to the murderer as harm *without* wrong ("bloß ein Zufall (casus)"). Timmermann ch. VI n. 11 already ties the Annen/Kettner over-breadth and Campagna's repair to this distinction (¶47), so credit him. He writes "*limited* negative Utilitarianism".
9. **"First monograph on the exchange"**: check Boituzat 1993 (PUF).
10. **Benton 1982** is cast as the expediency reading on the strength of its title alone. Timmermann (VI n. 7) cites him (pp. 142–44) for the application/exception distinction you make central. Read Benton first.
11. **Not verifiable here**: the signature "Hérivaux, 10 germinal an V" isn't in the OCR (the date conversion is right); Hare 1981: 41 is second-hand.

## B. The central claim

It is not fair as stated.

**(i) The disanalogy is deeper than the "two cautions" admit.**
- The 1815 demand is self-regarding. It asks for the truth about one's *own* beliefs, so as to proscribe the speaker ("*nous* proscrire"), in a freedom-of-conscience setting; the 1797 preface mentions Constant's family as "expatriée pour cause de religion". Its nearest analogues are self-incrimination and Huguenot debates over coerced profession.
- The door case concerns a fact about a third party, demanded by people with no claim to law.
- So three extensions are needed: self to third party, belief to fact, pretended law to no law. The last is not an *a fortiori* without argument.
- The note is also hedged: "Je dirai, du reste, que, dans cette circonstance, ce mensonge me paraît loin d'être un crime."

**(ii) The note has two conditions, and the draft runs them together.** One is title ("prétendues lois"); the other is purpose ("ne … que pour nous proscrire").
- At the door, the jurisdiction criterion taken from ch. I (individual independence) does not put "is your friend here?" outside jurisdiction: a legitimate authority may ask that.
- What disqualifies the assassins is their purpose. Applied to the door, the principle becomes "those who demand truth only in order to wrong are not owed it".
- That is Timmermann's own paraphrase of Constant, with "wrong" in place of "harm": "those who intend to do harm lose their right to be told the truth" (ch. III ¶11); "those who intend to murder others do not deserve to learn…" (ch. V ¶23). The contrast with his forfeiture reading largely dissolves.

**(iii) The Weinrib convergence (¶30) is overstated.**
- Weinrib's right does not depend on "the behaviour, character or intentions of the person who asks". The note's purpose clause does.
- On a strict title reading, Constant's "là où il n'y a pas de droits, il n'y a pas de devoirs" releases us from truthfulness towards *anyone* without juridical title, the tourist asking directions included. That over-breadth is worse than the witness case.
- Either way, the convergence is on the point Kant already concedes (8:426).

**(iv) "Meets Constant's standard" is not shown.** The draft says the relevant features of the demand are things "everyone can know in advance". But purpose has to be inferred, and whether a law is *prétendue* is exactly what is in dispute. Constant concedes this in 1815: "Vous ne pouvez pas … indiquer avec précision chaque circonstance où l'obéissance cesse d'être un devoir" (p. 95); and "Pour se délivrer de toute incertitude, l'homme devrait cesser d'être un être moral" (p. 93). §5 quotes the first, which undercuts §4.

**Is the jurisdiction reading imported?** Half.
- For the civil-religion case it is supported: "prétendues lois", "étrangère à sa juridiction naturelle" (p. 129), and ch. I (p. 9).
- "Title" and "standing to demand an answer" are your reconstruction. Label them as such.
- Better support is unused, in 1797 itself. Ch. IX: "En recourant à l'arbitraire, les gouvernemens donnent les mêmes droits qu'ils prennent … toute l'autorité légitime d'un gouvernement tombe … il n'est plus gouvernement" and "Un peuple et un gouvernement sont toujours en réciprocité de devoirs" (pp. 86–87); "il n'y a d'existant en institution que ce qui existe de droit" (p. 85).
- These passages put a title-based idea in the pamphlet itself, mirroring ch. VIII's duty–right correlation. That takes weight off an 1815 footnote.

## C. The disagreement with Timmermann

The draft's reply is partly unfair and not yet persuasive.
- His n. 59 targets Wood and Loriaux (truthfulness of politicians in official declarations).
- His n. 60 is about what "société" means in "rendroit toute société impossible". There Constant sides with him: moral principles disorder "relations sociales", political ones "relations civiles" (p. 73).
- The draft concedes the point ("true of what chapter VIII says") and then moves to what the case "needed". That shows a different text can be applied; it does not show Timmermann "mistaken".

He would reply as follows.
1. The refuge is part of the stock case. His likely source is Schaumann 1796: 194 (ch. III ¶11): someone "in a fury" pursues an innocent man hiding in my house. The draft never mentions Schaumann.
2. Constant's own changes ("assassins" in the plural; the "ami") point, within this pamphlet, to the reaction's extra-legal bands ("excitoient à l'assassinat … rendoient la vengeance nationale illégale", p. 6), not to the state.
3. In 1797 Constant tells republicans to rally "au gouvernement, lorsqu'il modère l'opinion" (p. 57). The Directory *protects* the proscribed.
4. The "unjust compulsion" is a threat of violence (¶20).
5. Your principle, once applied, is his commonplace (B(ii)).
6. Kant's answer doesn't depend on whether the questioner is the state.

Reframe: Timmermann is right about how ch. VIII frames the case. What he misses is that Constant later took up a demand for truth made by (pretended) law and answered it with a different key. Delete the abstract's "shows … that the case concerns the state".

## D. The fixity frame and the harm clause

The diagnosis is right, but most of it is not new:
- over-breadth: Annen and Kettner (credited);
- harm versus wrong, linked to over-breadth: Timmermann VI n. 11, ¶47 (not credited);
- forecasting: Michaelis via ¶47 (credited);
- instability: Kant's "schwankend und unnütz" (8:427), which Timmermann traces to Michaelis (¶36);
- no private judges of exceptions: Mertens via ¶17.

What survives is "fails by Constant's own standard", and Annen gestures even at that (Constant "proud of the precision").

"Fixity" equivocates between three senses:
- (a) invariance: "toujours pareil, toutes les fois que les combinaisons sont les mêmes" (p. 66);
- (b) other people's reliance (p. 82);
- (c) ex ante decidability by the agent.

The harm clause satisfies (a) ("truth that harms others" is a fixed type). It fails (c), and fails (b) only through (c). The p. 78 passage proves too much: every principle with empirical conditions requires a judgement of circumstances (8:275). The defensible claim is narrower: the trouble is a key that requires forecasting *other people's free choices*. Then you must show that the 1815 key escapes the same charge (B(iv)).

Add Constant's letter to Charrière of 16 Dec. 1794 (Timmermann ch. III ¶11). In his translation: if happiness is the touchstone of duty, "it is impossible to determine what it consists of … Duty is thus ground to bits in accordance with … each individual head … a duty that turns into calculations of happiness is no longer a duty." Quote the French from OCBC; I have only the English. It is Constant condemning consequence-keyed duty in his own words, and with it §3 can lose about 300 words.

## E. "What remains"

Not sound as argued.
1. **Kant's remaining ground doesn't depend on authority.** The undirected wrong (8:426, 8:429) is asserted against a *private* murderer, so "the reason lies in his theory of authority" is a non sequitur. Timmermann argues that the formal-wrong argument fails and that Kant's real ground is the ethical duty to oneself (6:429–431; ¶¶41–43), which the questioner's title cannot touch. Engage this.
2. **"A pretended law is not a source of right" equivocates.** Kant's *Rechtsquelle* is trust in declarations as the source of contractual rights (¶36), not law. What you give Constant is Hennings's relevance objection (¶41), which covers private assassins too and doesn't need "pretended law".
3. **MS 6:319–320 concerns active resistance.** Kant allows refusal of commands contrary to inner morality (MS 6:371; Religion 6:99n; both from memory, so verify). Timmermann V n. 45 doubts that Kant leaves no room for passive resistance. "No category of pretended laws for a subject to identify" overstates.
4. **A better comparator for 1815** is Kant on coerced professions of faith (Religion IV §4, c. 6:186–190, from memory) and MS 6:430 (lying belief in a future judge). Like Constant, Kant blames those who compel, but he still forbids the false profession. This is tighter and fresher than resistance, which Troitsky has already linked to lying.
5. **Ch. XI is the section's best material, but it shows Constant giving up the 1797 standard by 1815.** Make that the conclusion.

## F. Three most serious referee objections

1. **Kant scholar: "passes Kant's distinction" begs the question.**
   - Kant classes this very case as *casus* (8:428).
   - "Lends himself to a wrong" is an unargued complicity claim, and that ground is already worked (Timmermann V nn. 44, 47: Stewart, Schapiro, Grünewald).
   - The 1815 principle bears only on the addressee's right, which Kant concedes.

   *Pre-empt:* concede that it doesn't answer Kant's undirected or self-regarding duty, and either argue complicity explicitly or drop it.
2. **Constant scholar / historian: context.**
   - The case is a stock German example; the pursuers are extra-legal bands; Constant sided with the Directory in 1797; the note is an 1815 remark on conscience.
   - The note's provenance is unchecked. Is it already in the 1806 manuscript, or an 1818–20 *Cours* addition? Check OCBC IX and Hofmann.
   - Missing literature: Rousselière 2018, Loparic 2006, Feldhaus, Lalatta Costerbosa 2014, Boituzat 1993.

   *Pre-empt:* concede that the political reading is anticipated (Holmes, Troitsky, Rousselière). Present the note as Constant's later key, not as a decoding of 1797, and anchor the title idea in ch. IX pp. 85–87.
3. **Both: the asymmetry between §§3 and 4 collapses.** Judging intentions or legitimacy is as much "chacun juge des circonstances" as forecasting harm, and Constant concedes it (pp. 93–95).

   *Pre-empt:* argue that judging the title or purpose of a *present* demand, by public criteria and with juries to check it afterwards, differs in kind from forecasting others' free choices. Admit that this is a weaker standard than the one Constant set in 1797.

## H. Verdict and priorities

**Worth submitting after substantial revision, not as is.** A Kant referee would reject §§4–5, and a Constant referee the contextual argument.

A defensible thesis, in five steps:
1. Constant's example belongs to a theory of principles as fixed rules against the arbitrary.
2. His harm-keyed clause fails that theory by his own lights (Charrière 1794; pp. 78–82).
3. The pamphlet (pp. 85–87) and a neglected 1815 note show him moving to a key based on the illegitimacy and proscriptive purpose of a demand.
4. That key avoids the over-breadth and agrees with Kant that the questioner is not wronged. But it doesn't escape judgement (as Constant conceded in 1815), and it doesn't reach Kant's real ground, a duty not owed to the questioner at all.
5. What remains between them is the correlation of duty with right, which Kant calls the πρῶτον ψεῦδος (8:425), not authority.

Revisions, in order of priority:
1. Reframe the abstract, §1 and the conclusion as above.
2. Treat the note's two conditions and its first-person structure explicitly; revise the Weinrib convergence; answer the "no title, no duty" over-breadth.
3. Repair *nocet/laedit*: acknowledge *casus* (8:428), then argue complicity or drop it.
4. Rebuild §5 around Kant's undirected or self-regarding duty, coerced professions of faith, and Constant's 1815 concession; demote resistance.
5. Correct and strengthen the context: Schaumann, ch. I, p. 57, p. 73's clause, ch. IX pp. 85–87.
6. Tighten §3: separate the three senses of fixity, add Charrière, and credit Timmermann n. 11 and ¶¶47, 17, 36.
7. Make the fixes listed in A.
8. Literature and provenance: Rousselière, Loparic, Feldhaus, Boituzat, Lalatta Costerbosa; check the note in OCBC IX, Hofmann (1806) and the 1815 first edition.
