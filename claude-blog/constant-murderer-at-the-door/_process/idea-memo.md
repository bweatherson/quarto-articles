# Idea memo (step 1 output): Constant paper

Draft 28 Oct, about 09:45. This draft is written before the canon check returns and before the Timmermann and Rousselière PDFs arrive, so its novelty claims are provisional.

## Working title
"The Principle Constant Needed: Truthfulness, Fixity and Proscription in *Des réactions politiques*"

Alternatives:
- "Constant's Murderer"
- "Prétendues lois"

## Thesis (one paragraph)
Constant introduced the lie to the assassins as a test case for a theory of what principles are. On that theory a principle's essence is fixity: like combinations yield like results, so a principle is a rule people "doivent pouvoir compter mutuellement" on. Its generality is only relative. Arbitrariness is defined as the absence of rules, limits and definitions, a condition in which "chacun est juge des circonstances".

Read within that theory, Kant's reply is not absolutism against expediency. It is an internal critique. Kant:
- accepts the method (8:427, "wohldenkend und zugleich richtig"; 8:430, "nähere Bestimmung" allowed);
- concedes that the unjust questioner is not wronged (8:426);
- objects that an exception makes the law "schwankend und unnütz", which is Constant's own pair of criteria (fixity is where "toute son utilité" lies; the arbitrary is where "tout est vacillant").

Constant's 1797 intermediate principle, "nul homme n'a droit à la vérité qui nuit à autrui", is keyed to *harm*. It therefore makes every speaker judge of circumstances, and it is over-broad: it releases the court witness whose truthful testimony convicts the guilty. By Constant's own standard it fails.

The pamphlet itself shows the case Constant had in mind:
- the friend pursued to his asylum is the pamphlet's *proscrit*, one of the men "dont on veut … faire une race proscrite … qui n'ait d'asyle sur la terre" (ch. VII);
- the questioners belong with the Terror's agents, who "avoient mis hors la loi" under a government with "aucune loi fixe" (ch. IX).

In 1815 Constant stated the principle that case needed. It is keyed not to harm but to the wrongful, extra-jurisdictional character of the demand: "Quand de prétendues lois n'exigent de nous la vérité que pour nous proscrire, nous ne leur devons pas la vérité." It comes in a note on Rousseau's civil religion, and it concerns a "mensonge devant les lois", which is exactly the coerced juridical declaration Kant's essay treats as the hard case (8:426, "wozu ihn ein ungerechter Zwang nöthigt").

This principle:
- is fixed by Constant's standard;
- survives *nocet/laedit*;
- moves the residual disagreement from the theory of principles to the theory of authority, namely whether a demand by "prétendues lois" belongs to the "Rechtsquelle" at all.

So centring Constant changes the question: the murderer case is about whether truthfulness is owed to power that has exceeded its jurisdiction.

## What is (provisionally) new
1. Kant's reply is read as an internal critique by Constant's own criteria of fixity and utility (the "schwankend und unnütz" parallel). Loparic 2006 sets fixity against "no exceptions" but, as far as the pretest saw, does not argue this.
2. The harm-keyed clause fails Constant's own standard: both the "juge des circonstances" point and the over-breadth of the court-witness case. Pretest item (b) has the first point. The court-witness point is mine and needs a literature check.
3. **The 1815 note as the principle Constant needed.** It is a juridical declaration case, keyed to "prétendues lois" and proscription. "Étrangère à sa juridiction naturelle" is in the same passage. No open-web hits on the sentence so far. *This is the load-bearing novelty claim. Check it against Timmermann chs. III, V, VI and VIII, Rousselière 2018, and the canon check.*
4. The context of chs. VII and IX (proscrit, asyle, hors la loi, "aucune loi fixe") anchors the example. Rousselière 2018 may have some of this.

## What is NOT claimed
- That Constant read Kant's reply, or that the 1815 note answers Kant. There is no evidence either way that I have seen, so the claim is structural only.
- That the example has a specific historical referent, such as the Narbonne episode. Staël's account postdates the essay. Mention it at most as illustration, if at all.
- That Hare's distinction is Constant's. Use the period vocabulary instead: comparative versus strict universality (KrV B3–4), and *nähere Bestimmung* versus *Ausnahme*. Hare appears in at most one sentence or note.

## Outline (target 5,600 words including notes)
1. Introduction (550). The example is Constant's, but Constant is read through Kant. The standard picture: forfeiture, consequentialism, expediency. The claim in brief.
2. Constant on principles (1,000). Ch. VIII: definition, relative generality, fixity, "un seul anneau", discovery by definition, "idée peut-être neuve". The political model of representation, which Kant approves. Ch. IX: the arbitrary as the absence of rules; morality as rules people count on. Comparative versus strict universality.
3. The example inside the theory (700). The two horns (p. 75); duty correlated with right; the harm clause; "exclut tout arbitraire". What the case is: plural assassins, asylum, no forced yes or no, no forfeiture claim.
4. Kant's reply as internal critique (1,100). Method accepted; murderer not wronged; "schwankend und unnütz"; *nocet/laedit* aimed at the harm key; the court-witness case; "juge des circonstances".
5. The principle the case needed (1,300). Chs. VII and IX context; the 1815 note (*mensonge devant les lois*, *prétendues lois*, *que pour nous proscrire*, *juridiction naturelle*). Why it is fixed and keyed to wrong. Why it meets Kant on his own declarations ground.
6. What remains (750). Kant 8:426 on unjust coercion and the *Rechtsquelle*; the dispute relocated to authority and resistance; the "who judges?" problem; Constant 1815 ch. XI on passive obedience ("il reste toujours … quelque chose de discrétionnaire"), with residual judgment handed to institutions. Conclusion.
