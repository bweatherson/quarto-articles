## B. The central claim
**Accepted:**
- The note's three disanalogies (self-regarding, belief rather than fact, pretended law rather than no law) are stated explicitly.
- Its two conditions, title and purpose, are separated.
- At the door only purpose works. I concede that the result is close to Timmermann's paraphrase with "wrong" for "harm", and frame it as the principle Constant came to later.
- "No title, no duty" over-breadth (the tourist): stated and rejected. The note's structure is a default claim with two defeaters.
- "Meets Constant's standard": weakened. The paper now says it is better on decidability, and that Constant in 1815 abandoned the 1797 aspiration. New evidence for this is from Laboulaye 1815 ch. XI:
  - p. 92: "il en est nécessairement le seul juge";
  - pp. 92–93 n.: legality of an order equals its title, and "un innocent peut être arrêté très-légalement";
  - p. 93: "Pour se délivrer de toute incertitude, l'homme devrait cesser d'être un être moral", which reverses 1797's "exclut tout arbitraire et toute incertitude".
