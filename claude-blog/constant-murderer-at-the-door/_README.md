## Why it stopped
The QA round found that almost every part of the v3 thesis was already in print or contestable:
- **Already in print:**
  - Benton 1982 has fixity and the duty–right correlation as the crux.
  - Rousselière 2018 has anti-arbitrariness.
  - Kettner 1992 and Annen 1997, via Timmermann, have the defects of the harm clause.
  - Hofmann 1980 connects the civil-religion note to the Kant exchange. The note is in the 1806 MS (Book VIII, Note A; Liberty Fund p. 147, n. 19).
  - Freller 2020/2023 has the retreat from 1797's "no uncertainty" to individual judgement.
- **Contestable:**
  - Applied to the door, the note collapses into forfeiture.
  - Kant's 1770s lectures have the same extortion exception.

## Leads worth keeping
1. **1806 *Principes*, Book XVIII, ch. 2** (Liberty Fund pp. 401–404). Constant published it in the *Cours* "Additions et notes", Note V (Laboulaye 1872, I, 354–55): "Toute loi qui ordonne la délation, la dénonciation, n'est pas une loi ; toute loi portant atteinte à ce penchant qui commande à l'homme de donner un refuge à quiconque lui demande asile, n'est pas une loi." This is the door case with the state as pursuer. Freller's thesis reportedly covers Book XVIII, so check it first.
2. **"Nuire à autrui".** In 1789 art. 4 and Year III (Droits, art. 2: "ce qui ne nuit pas aux droits d'autrui") the phrase is rights-laden. If Constant's 1797 clause ("nul homme n'a droit à la vérité qui nuit à autrui") is read in that idiom, it keys the duty to violation of rights, not to bare harm. Kant's *nocet/laedit* charge and the witness objection would then rest on Cramer's "schadet". No literature check has been done; check Boituzat 1993 and Hofmann's thesis (I, Part 1, ch. 2, p. 143).
3. **Kant MS 6:371** (Anhang 1798): obedience "ohne vorhergehende Forschung dem angemaßten Rechte"; inquiring into the ruler's "Titel" is already punishable. Set this against Constant's "prétendues lois" (1806/1815) and his claim that each agent is "nécessairement le seul juge" (1815 ch. XI; Laboulaye 92).
