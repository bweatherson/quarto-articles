## (A) Closest existing works

**Directly computational, population ethics**

1. Parent, X. & Benzmüller, C., "Conditional normative reasoning as a fragment of HOL", *Journal of Applied Logics – IfCoLog* (2024); arXiv:2308.10686; AFP entry `CondNormReasHOL` (9 Mar 2024, theory files `DDLcube`, `mere_addition_opt`, `mere_addition_lewis`, `mere_addition_max`). https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10686 ; https://www.isa-afp.org/entries/CondNormReasHOL.html — **Verified.** The only proof-assistant treatment of anything in population ethics I could find. Encodes Parfit's *Mere Addition Paradox* (three premises P0: A ≻ B, P1: A+ ≽ A, P2: B ≻ A+) in Åqvist's dyadic deontic logic E (and F, G) via shallow embedding in Isabelle/HOL; uses Sledgehammer and Nitpick. Result: under the "max" rule with *finite* models, (quasi-)transitivity makes P0–P2 inconsistent; consistency returns with infinite increasing chains; acyclicity/quasi-transitivity suffice to block the paradox while interval-order does not (their Prop. 5.1, Table 4). **Does not cite Arrhenius; none of the six theorems or the Egalitarian Dominance / Non-Elitism / Non-Sadism family is encoded.** It is a 3-premise toy, not an impossibility theorem with Skolem witnesses.

## (B) Verdict

**(a) Formal verification: not done.** No proof-assistant formalisation of any of Arrhenius's six theorems (or of the Ng 1989 / Blackorby–Donaldson 1991 precursors) exists in the AFP, Lean mathlib/Zulip, Coq, or arXiv. The sole mechanised item in the area (Parent & Benzmüller 2024) covers only Parfit's 3-premise Mere Addition Paradox inside a deontic logic. **(b) Automated minimisation / theorem discovery: not done.** No search for minimal inconsistent subsets or new impossibility theorems over finite populations. **(c) Other computational treatment: none**, beyond hand-proved bounded-domain possibility results (Arrhenius–Budolfson–Spears 2021). The field is entirely paper-and-pencil; the one closest relative is Thomas's 2016 note, which already exposed an error in Theorem 6 that machine checking would have caught. What remains is therefore everything: a verified formalisation; a finitisation argument; SAT-based discovery.

## (C) Technical notes on finite structure and SAT-encodability

- **Welfare levels.** Arrhenius assumes *Discreteness* (finitely many levels between any two) and argues it is WLOG under Denseness by passing to a discrete sub-set L1; levels are indexed by integers W…, W−1, W0, W1, …; "slightly higher" = consecutive levels; welfare ranges R(x,y) need ≥3 levels. Thomas's reconstruction uses a *finite* linear order of integers Z < … < 0 < 1 < 2 < 3 < … < A < A+1 < A+2. The proofs use specific named levels: Z (very negative), −1, 1, 2, 3, A, A+1, A+2 — at most ~8 distinct levels per theorem (Theorem 5 uses A+2; Theorem 2 uses A+1, 1, 2, 3). So the welfare *alphabet* is small and finite, with the proviso that Q, IA, NE, GNE, GNEP iterate over *every* level between two others (chains of length A−3 or x−y), so A must be a concrete parameter.
- **Populations.** Anonymous welfarism lets populations be multisets, i.e. distributions W → ℕ (Thomas) or disjoint sets with sizes (Arrhenius). The relation ≽ is a quasi-order (reflexive, transitive, *not* assumed complete).
- **Logical form.** Every condition is first-order over (levels, sizes, distributions) with the pattern ∀(levels, m) ∃(witness size) ∀(background population P): e.g. IA: ∀x>y>z ∀m ∃C: m·x + C·z ≼ m·y + C·y; GNEP: ∀z ∃G(z) ∀x≥A ∀0<y≤3 ∀P: P+z+G·y ≼ P+(z−1)+G·x; WNS: ∃D ∀x>0 ∀m ∀P: P+D·Z ≼ P+m·x. The conclusions (RC, VRC, RA, VRA) are likewise ∀m ∃M. The proofs are Skolem-function bookkeeping: define Q0 = Q(A,m), Qi = Q(A−i, Qi−1), etc., then a 3–5-step transitivity chain.
- **Consequence for SAT.** The theorems are *not* finite in the Geist–Endriss sense. The existential witnesses range over all of ℕ, and Arrhenius–Budolfson–Spears prove that if you bound population size the conjunction becomes *satisfiable* (total utilitarianism inside the bound). A naive encoding "all distributions of ≤N people over k levels, one Boolean per ordered pair, transitivity, conditions" will be SAT for every N unless the witnesses are constrained. Two remedies: (i) *parametrised finitisation*: fix the witness functions as finite tables (or bound them by a constant K) and ask for the least N at which UNSAT appears — Arrhenius's own constructions give explicit upper bounds (e.g. T2: N = m + C(A+1,2,1,m); T4: N = n + C + F − 2D with F > 2D); (ii) *small-model + induction*, à la Tang–Lin / Geist–Endriss: prove inconsistency among the handful of distributions in the proof chain, then a preservation lemma. The ∀P background-population quantifier is the second blow-up: #distributions over k levels with ≤N people is C(N+k, k) (k=5, N=10 → 3003; transitivity clauses cubic), so direct encodings are limited to roughly N ≤ 8–10 or require lazy/incremental transitivity (Brandt–Peters style) or SMT with uninterpreted ≽. Incompleteness of ≽ must be kept (no integer value-function trick) since the theorems assume only a quasi-order.
- **Other obstacles.** "Very high"/"very low"/"very many" are eliminated in the exact formulations (Arrhenius 2011 says so explicitly) via unfixed ranges R(1,y), R(u,v) — fine for encoding. Thomas's note flags that in some informal GNEP statements G depends on P, which breaks the proofs — formalisation must fix the quantifier order. The lexical/incomplete escapes (Thomas 2018) violate Finite Fine-Grainedness, so testing them at finite n is coherent only for the bounded versions of the conditions.

## (D) Sharpest achievable new results

1. **Machine-checked Theorems 1–6** (Isabelle/HOL or Lean 4), following Thomas's reconstruction; verify that Theorem 6 yields only VRA*, not VRA, and check Arrhenius's 2011 Lemma 1.4 (11 populations, 22 steps) directly. Low risk, publishable as the first mechanised result in population axiology; an AFP/mathlib entry.
2. **Threshold theorems.** For each theorem, compute by SAT/SMT the minimal population bound N*(A, |Z|, witness bounds) at which the bounded-domain conjunction becomes UNSAT, and compare with the bounds implicit in Arrhenius's constructions. This quantifies Arrhenius–Budolfson–Spears's possibility result: how large must the policy domain be before the impossibility bites?
3. **Minimal unsatisfiable subsets / new theorems.** Over a parametrised finite fragment (k levels, N people, witnesses ≤ K), enumerate MUSes of {ED, Q, DA, IA, NS, WNS, NE, GNE, NEP, GNEP, Mere Addition, Non-Anti-Egalitarianism, Transitivity, Completeness, ¬RC variants}. Candidates for genuinely new results: theorems using weaker-than-Arrhenius conditions (e.g. restricted background populations, bounded witness sizes, or dropping ED in favour of same-number Pareto), and a proof that certain triples are *consistent* at all finite N (possibility results with explicit witness axiologies read off from SAT models).
4. **Verified escape maps.** For critical-level/critical-band utilitarianism, Total Lexic Utilitarianism, RDCLU, number-dampened and variable-value views, mechanically certify which (bounded) conditions hold for all populations up to size N and k levels, producing a condition × view matrix with certificates and explicit minimal counterexamples. 
5. **Shorter human-readable proofs.** Use SAT-extracted MUSes to find chains shorter than Arrhenius's (Thomas's are already 3–5 steps; the target is minimal step count and minimal number of distinct welfare levels per theorem, e.g. whether A+2 is needed in T5).
