# Literature check: REU/RDU vs. dynamic risk measures and the Kupper–Schachermayer time-consistency theorem

Method and caveats. Google Scholar, PhilPapers and PhilArchive search pages were blocked (robots.txt / Cloudflare), and OpenAlex's free budget was exhausted, so the "cited-by" check was done through the Semantic Scholar API (152 citing records for Kupper & Schachermayer 2009) plus many targeted web searches. Where I say "verified" I read the abstract or PDF; where I say "inferred" I did not. One Springer page (Dominiak & Lefort 2011) was rate-limited, but I got its abstract from IDEAS/RePEc.

## A. Closest existing works

**Mathematical finance / actuarial science (the "rigidity" results)**

1. Kupper, M. & Schachermayer, W. (2009), "Representation results for law invariant time consistent functions", *Math. Financ. Econ.* 2:189–210. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11579-009-0019-9 (preprint: https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~schachermayer/pubs/preprnts/prpr0138.pdf). Verified from the PDF. Two theorems matter. **Thm 1.4** (certainty equivalents, no cash-invariance): on a standard atomless filtered space, a normalized, strictly monotone, ‖·‖∞-continuous, law-invariant, time-consistent (c₀(X)=c₀(c_t(X))) certainty equivalent satisfying a mild continuity condition (C) must be u⁻¹∘E[u(X)] — i.e. *expected utility with an arbitrary u*. **Thm 1.10** adds cash invariance (monetary risk measure) and gets the entropic family ρ_t(X)=γ⁻¹ ln E[e^{−γX}|F_t], γ∈[0,∞] (γ=0 expectation, γ=∞ worst case). They describe Thm 1.4 as extending Nagumo (1930), Kolmogorov (1930) and de Finetti (1931) on quasi-arithmetic means; they cite Gerber's iterative premium principles and Weber 2006, but no decision theory beyond vNM.
2. Weber, S. (2006), "Distribution-invariant risk measures, information, and dynamic consistency", *Math. Finance* 16:419–441. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9965.2006.00277.x. Verified (PDF). With a *weaker* (acceptance/rejection) consistency notion, law invariance forces utility-based shortfall risk (EU-based acceptance). Shows explicitly that AVaR/Expected Shortfall has a non-convex acceptance set and fails.
3. Shapiro, A. (2012), "Time consistency of dynamic risk measures", *Oper. Res. Lett.* 40:436–439. https://optimization-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/3306.pdf. Verified. Finite trees: compositions of law-invariant coherent one-step measures are law invariant only for expectation or max; AVaR cannot be so composed; cites K&S for the coherent case.
4. Pflug, G. & Pichler, A. (2016), "Time-consistent decisions and temporal decomposition of coherent risk functionals", *Math. Oper. Res.* 41:682–699. https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/moor.2015.0747. Not read (paywalled); inferred from title, Shapiro, and S2 listing: the only "decomposable" law-invariant coherent (distortion) functionals are expectation and ess sup.
5. Delbaen, F. (2021), "Commonotonicity and time-consistency for Lebesgue-continuous monetary utility functions", *Finance & Stochastics* 25. arXiv: https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1904.04522. Verified (arXiv). Comonotonic additivity (the Yaari/Choquet/distortion property) + time consistency + relevance ⇒ expectation under a single measure. **No law invariance needed.** Cites Yaari, Schmeidler, Denneberg — the closest a finance paper comes to saying "Yaari's dual theory cannot be time-consistent".
6. Kaluszka, M. & Krzeszowiec, M. (2013a), "On iterative premium calculation principles under Cumulative Prospect Theory", *Insurance: Math. Econ.* 52:435–440. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016766871300022X. Verified abstract: the zero-utility premium under CPT (RDU with two distortions) is iterative (Gerber's tower property = time consistency) **iff the value function is linear or exponential and the distortions are identities.** This is literally "the K&S entropic theorem for RDU/CPT" in actuarial clothing. Companion: Kaluszka & Krzeszowiec (2013b), "An iterativity condition for the mean-value principle under CPT", *ASTIN Bulletin* 43:61–71 (https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/astin-bulletin-journal-of-the-iaa/article/an-iterativity-condition-for-the-meanvalue-principle-under-cumulative-prospect-theory/E1FD7C7BDCFBBDC4CDF2E65CF361B69C): the certainty-equivalent version is iterative for exactly six trivial distortion pairs (identity, ess inf, ess sup combinations).
7. Fasen & Švejda (2012), "Time consistency of multi-period distortion measures", *Stat. & Risk Modeling* 29; Bielecki, Cialenco & Liu (2023/24), "Time consistency of dynamic risk measures ... generated by distortion functions", *Stochastic Models* (https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02570). Verified abstracts: conditional Choquet/distortion measures are only sub-martingale consistent, not strongly or weakly-acceptance consistent.
8. Beiglböck, Pesenti & Sylvestre (2026), "Adapted law invariance and time-consistent dynamic risk measures", arXiv:2607.04392. Verified (HTML). Argues K&S's terminal-law invariance "does not distinguish risks with the same distribution but different times of resolution"; proposes *adapted* law invariance (depends on distribution + information revelation) and shows time-consistent + adapted-law-invariant = backward composition of one-step conditional lifts of static law-invariant measures. This is the finance-side rediscovery of recursive (Kreps–Porteus/Epstein–Zin) evaluation.
9. Hu, Jin & Zhou (2021), "Consistent investment of sophisticated rank-dependent utility agents in continuous time", *Math. Finance* 31:1056–1095 (https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/mathfi/v31y2021i3p1056-1095.html); He, Strub & Zariphopoulou (2021), "Forward rank-dependent performance criteria", *Math. Finance*; He, Jin & Zhou (2015), "Dynamic portfolio choice when risk is measured by weighted VaR", *MOR*. Verified abstract of Hu–Jin–Zhou: RDU "is inherently time inconsistent due to the underlying probability weighting", and they study Strotz-style sophisticated intra-personal equilibrium — the finance literature has independently reproduced the sophisticated/resolute (precommitment) dichotomy for RDU.
10. Bamberg & Krapp (2016), "Is time consistency compatible with risk aversion?", *Rev. Managerial Sci.* 10:195–211. Verified abstract: within EU, CARA (exponential utility) is what guarantees time consistency for terminal wealth.

**Economics / decision theory**

11. Segal (1990), "Two-stage lotteries without the reduction axiom", *Econometrica* 58:349–377. Verified (PDF): RCLA + compound independence (+ continuity) ⇒ EU; each alone is compatible with any preference over simple lotteries; Quiggin's anticipated utility is explicitly characterized under CIA (Thm 9).
12. Chew & Epstein (1989), "The structure of preferences and attitudes towards the timing of the resolution of uncertainty", *IER* 30:103–117. Verified (PDF): recursivity + timing indifference ⇒ EU at each stage (Thm 1).
13. Sarin & Wakker (1998), "Dynamic choice and nonexpected utility", *JRU* 17:87–119. Verified abstract: with dynamic consistency, consequentialism and "sequential consistency", multiple priors survive, but "rank-dependent and betweenness models can only be used in a restrictive manner, where deviation from expected utility is allowed in at most one stage."
14. Eichberger, Grant & Kelsey (2005), "CEU preferences and dynamic consistency", *Math. Soc. Sci.* 49:143–151; Dominiak & Lefort (2011), "Unambiguous events and dynamic Choquet preferences", *Econ. Theory* 46:401–425; Dominiak (2013), "Iterated Choquet expectations: a possibility result", *Econ. Letters* 120:155–159; Lapied & Toquebeuf (2009, HAL). Verified abstracts: the Choquet analogue of rectangularity exists but is degenerate — dynamic consistency on a fixed filtration iff the capacity is additive (unambiguous) on the final stage / additively separable across the partition with Bayesian conditioning. For risk (objective probabilities, a single distortion applied at every node) this means no distortion at the nodes where consistency is required.
15. Epstein & Zin (1990), "First-order risk aversion and the equity premium puzzle", *JME* 26:387–407 (https://people.bu.edu/lepstein/files-research/Epstein_Zin_JME90.pdf). Verified: puts a Yaari/RDU certainty equivalent inside Kreps–Porteus recursive utility — the "recursive REU" that economists already use (dynamically consistent, but not law-invariant over the terminal distribution).
16. Bommier, Kochov & Le Grand (2017), "On monotone recursive preferences", *Econometrica* 85:1433–1466. Verified abstract: recursive + monotone preferences over temporal lotteries are only Uzawa–Epstein or *risk-sensitive* (exponential/entropic CE, Hansen–Sargent); excludes several Epstein–Zin/Weil variants. Another route by which entropic CEs are singled out.
17. Hammond & Zank (2014), "Rationality and dynamic consistency under risk and uncertainty", *Handbook of the Economics of Risk and Uncertainty* vol. 1 (https://web.stanford.edu/~hammond/hamZankWP.pdf). Verified: standard survey; no risk measures, no RDU-specific treatment.

## B. Verdict: is the connection already made?

**In philosophy: no.** None of the 152 Semantic-Scholar-indexed papers citing Kupper & Schachermayer is in a philosophy venue; they are all in mathematical finance, OR, stochastic programming, actuarial science and (increasingly) risk-sensitive RL. Web searches pairing Buchak/REU with "risk measure", "time consistency", "Artzner", "Riedel", "Kupper", "entropic", "Epstein–Zin", "Kreps–Porteus" returned nothing in philosophy. The philosophy dynamic-choice debate (Buchak, Briggs, Thoma, Weisberg, Steele, Joyce) runs entirely through Hammond/McClennen/Machina/Seidenfeld. So far as I can tell the "entropic rigidity" result is unknown in the philosophical literature on REU.

**In economics/finance: the pieces exist but the sentence has not been written.** (i) The decision-theoretic content of K&S Thm 1.4 is essentially known since Segal 1990 and Chew–Epstein 1989 (reduction/timing-indifference + recursivity ⇒ EU), and Sarin–Wakker 1998 state the RDU-specific moral (deviation from EU in at most one stage). (ii) The actuarial papers of Kaluszka & Krzeszowiec 2013 prove the exact RDU/CPT version of the entropic theorem (iterativity ⇒ linear/exponential value function and undistorted probabilities) but do not cite K&S, Buchak, or the dynamic-risk-measure literature. (iii) Delbaen 2021 shows comonotonic (Yaari-type) + time-consistent ⇒ expectation, citing Yaari, but again no connection to RDU as a *normative* theory. (iv) Beiglböck–Pesenti–Sylvestre 2026 note explicitly that K&S's law invariance ignores timing of resolution — i.e. the Segal/Strzalecki point — but do not mention RDU or decision theory. Nobody, in either field, has said: "REU/RDU as a theory of rational risk attitude is refuted/constrained by the law-invariant time-consistency theorems, and the only risk attitude that survives is exponential EU."

## C. What K&S adds beyond Machina/Hammond-style results

The classical philosophy results (Hammond 1988; Machina 1989; McClennen) concern a *single* atemporal non-EU preference confronted with a decision tree, and they are escapable by resolute choice (Buchak's actual reply) or by denying consequentialism/separability. K&S is a different kind of theorem, and in three respects stronger or at least differently targeted:

1. *It is a representation theorem for the family of conditional evaluations, not a tree argument.* It asks: suppose you want, at every date, a conditional evaluation c_t of the terminal prospect such that (a) c_t depends only on the (conditional) distribution and (b) evaluations fold back consistently (c₀ = c₀∘c_t). Then c₀ is EU. Resolute choice does not help here, because resolute choice means abandoning (b) for the later selves; and law invariance of the ex-ante evaluation is precisely what REU insists on (REU is a functional of the distribution of outcomes). So K&S formalizes the dilemma as: REU + recursivity ⇒ r = id. This is the content Weatherson's Appendix E and Sarin–Wakker 1998 reach in small trees, but K&S delivers it on an atomless L∞ space with arbitrary filtrations, with uniqueness of u up to affine transformations.
2. *The cash-invariant version adds something genuinely new relative to the philosophy literature:* if the evaluation is also translation-equivariant (certainty equivalents move one-for-one with sure additions — the natural "risk measure" normalization), the only survivor is the entropic family, i.e. EU with constant absolute risk aversion. This is the dynamic descendant of Pfanzagl's consistency axiom and of Gerber's 1974 result that additive + iterative premium principles are exponential. Philosophers discussing REU have not noticed that time consistency + law invariance + cash invariance leaves *exactly one* parameter of risk attitude, and that it is one EU already represents.
3. *It also identifies the escape route precisely.* Dropping law invariance of the ex-ante functional but keeping recursivity gives Epstein–Zin/Chew–Epstein recursive utility with an RDU certainty equivalent (Epstein & Zin 1990 did exactly this) — equivalently Beiglböck et al.'s "adapted law invariance". The cost is that the agent is no longer indifferent to the timing of resolution of uncertainty (Chew–Epstein 1989, Strzalecki 2013), violates reduction of compound lotteries (Segal 1990), and — by Bommier–Kochov–Le Grand 2017 — generally violates monotonicity with respect to dominance of continuation utilities unless the CE is risk-sensitive (entropic). So the "recursive REU" escape is available but gives up what Buchak uses REU to capture: an attitude to the *distribution* of final outcomes.

Caveat: K&S's time consistency c₀(X)=c₀(c_t(X)) presupposes that the intermediate certainty equivalent c_t is itself the object substituted — i.e. a "folding-back"/substitution principle (Nagumo–Kolmogorov associativity). A Buchakian could reject substitution of certainty equivalents at interior nodes as question-begging, just as she rejects Hammond's separability. The theorem's force is that it shows the two things REU wants — law invariance and a well-defined conditional evaluation that agrees with the ex-ante one — jointly force EU, with no third option.

## D. Sharp open questions

1. **Direct theorem for REU.** State and prove, in Buchak's own finite-outcome setting with objective probabilities, the K&S/Kaluszka–Krzeszowiec result: if REU_{r,u} is applied at every node of every finite tree and the node values fold back consistently, then r = id; if additionally the REU certainty equivalent is translation-equivariant, u is linear or exponential. (Kaluszka–Krzeszowiec 2013a essentially do this for CPT premiums; a clean decision-theoretic statement with REU axioms does not exist.)
2. **Is there any non-degenerate "rectangularity" for risk-weighting?** For multiple priors, Epstein–Schneider rectangularity delivers consistency with non-trivial ambiguity aversion. The Choquet results (EGK 2005; Dominiak 2013) say the capacity must be additive on the conditioning partition. Under *risk* with a fixed distortion this seems to leave nothing — but one could ask whether a *state-dependent* or *filtration-dependent* r (an "adapted" risk function, in the Beiglböck–Pesenti–Sylvestre sense) can rationalize the intuitions Buchak cites (Allais) while remaining time-consistent, and whether such an r still deserves the name "attitude to risk".
3. **What exactly does the resolute REU agent give up?** K&S makes precise that she gives up having conditional evaluations that are themselves REU with the same r (Sarin–Wakker's "sequential consistency"). Is that a cost Buchak's holistic-agency view can absorb, given that her argument for REU over EU is that risk attitudes should be a *stable, global* feature of the agent?
4. **Entropic uniqueness as a normative argument for CARA?** K&S Thm 1.10 + Bommier–Kochov–Le Grand suggest exponential/entropic evaluation is the unique "risk attitude" compatible with recursivity under two different mild side conditions. Whether this is a reason for philosophers to take CARA/entropic risk seriously as *the* rational risk-averse attitude (as Hansen–Sargent multiplier preferences do for robustness) appears unexplored.
