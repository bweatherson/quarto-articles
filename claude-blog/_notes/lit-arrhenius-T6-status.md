# Literature status: Arrhenius's sixth impossibility theorem and the Weak Quality Addition Condition

Date of check: 2 Oct 2026. "Verified" = I read the text (PDF downloaded and grepped, or fetched page). "Inferred" = from catalogue metadata, abstracts, or absence of hits.

## Bottom line

1. The gap you identify in Lemma 1.3 step (12) is real and has been flagged in print twice (Thomas, *Mind* 2018, fn. 4; Thornley, *Phil Studies* 2021, fns. 7 and 14), both deriving from Thomas's 2016 note. Both say the theorem "requires" a strengthened Weak Quality Addition (WQA) with the quantifiers swapped, so that the negative level and the number of negative lives are chosen independently of X.
2. Nobody, as far as I can find, has shown that the theorem *as originally stated* is false — i.e., produced a complete, transitive axiology obeying Discreteness/Finite Fine-Grainedness that satisfies all five original conditions. Thomas hedges ("As far as I know, one has to slightly strengthen the premisses"); Thornley asserts the stronger condition is "actually required" without proof. The only published countermodels to the sixth theorem (Thomas 2018, Carlson 2022, Thornley 2021's Lexical Totalism) work by denying Finite Fine-Grainedness and also satisfy the *strengthened* WQA, so they do not separate VRA from VRA*. A countermodel within Arrhenius's own discrete framework would be new.
3. Arrhenius's book is still cited as "forthcoming" by Arrhenius himself in work published 2022–2025. I found no OUP catalogue record, ISBN, or publication year. I could not access any manuscript to check whether the book's statement of WQA has been reformulated.

## 2. Thomas on VRA/VRA*

- **Thomas 2016 note** (verified, https://users.ox.ac.uk/~mert2060/webfiles/Reconstructing-Arrhenius-for-web.pdf, "Date: April 2016"). Intro: "This also allows me to correct what appears to be a problem with his sixth and favoured theorem." §9: "However, there is a mistake in Arrhenius's proof. What can be proved, instead of VRA, is: (VRA*) ... For any welfare level z < 0 and any m ∈ N, there is some population P such that, for any n ∈ N, there exists N ∈ N with P + m z + N 3 ≻ P + n A. This is slightly weaker than VRA, because P is allowed to depend z and m. I think that VRA* is not much less 'repugnant' than VRA, but then again it is not easy to understand the difference between them." Theorem 6: "ED, GNEP, NE, and WNS jointly entail VRA*." He calls his argument "a corrected version of Arrhenius's Lemma 6.1" (the book's numbering; = Lemma 1.3 of 2011, Lemma 3 of 2009). Thomas does not state a reformulated WQA, does not say Arrhenius was informed, and does not claim VRA is underivable. Stafforini's bibliography lists the note as unpublished (verified).
- **Thomas, "Some Possibilities in Population Axiology", *Mind* 127(507), 2018** (verified from ORA accepted manuscript, https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:09135568-0538-49e4-901d-81bcde4db49b). Footnote 4: "I note that the proof of his favoured sixth theorem contains an error in the derivation of the 'Restricted Quality Addition Condition' (Arrhenius, 2011, Lemma 1.3). As far as I know, one has to slightly strengthen the premisses of the theorem, in a way unlikely to cause further controversy. This will not affect my discussion." The strings "Very Repugnant Addition", "VRA", "Weak Quality Addition", "Lemma 6.1" do not occur. I did not check the typeset *Mind* version (paywalled), but this is the accepted manuscript so the footnote is almost certainly in print (inferred).
- Thomas, "Separability and Population Ethics" (*Handbook* 2022) and "The Asymmetry, Uncertainty, and the Long Term": not accessed; nothing in search results suggests they discuss the point (inferred).

## 3. Others discussing the error

- **Thornley, "The impossibility of a satisfactory population prospect axiology (independently of Finite Fine-Grainedness)", *Philosophical Studies* 178 (2021), DOI 10.1007/s11098-021-01621-4** (verified, open-access PDF via d-nb.info). This is the one place the condition has been publicly *restated*. §3: "Weak Quality Addition: There is a number of very negative welfare lives such that, for any population X, there is a number of very positive welfare lives such that the addition of the very positive welfare lives to X is at least as good as the addition of the very negative welfare lives plus any number of barely positive welfare lives to X. Weak Quality Addition (exact formulation): There exists a ∈ W with a < 0 and m ∈ N such that, for any population X, there exists b, c ∈ W with 0 < b ≤ 3, c ≫ b, and n ∈ N, such that for any q ∈ N, X + m[[a]] + q[[b]] ≼ X + n[[c]]." Fn. 7: "This condition differs slightly from that of Arrhenius (2011). Arrhenius has the first two quantifiers the other way around, so that the condition begins 'For any population X, there is…' (2011, 9). As Thomas's manuscript notes, the Sixth Impossibility Theorem actually requires the slightly stronger condition stated here. In any case, the stronger version remains a compelling adequacy condition." Fn. 14: "Thomas's manuscript points out that the proof in Arrhenius (2011) contains a minor mistake. The theorem actually requires the slightly stronger version of Weak Quality Addition formulated above." Thornley nonetheless labels the result "Arrhenius's Sixth Impossibility Theorem ... (Arrhenius, 2011, 9; forthcoming)". Note his strengthened WQA is exactly ¬VRA*, while the original is ¬VRA.
- Thornley, "A dilemma for lexical and Archimedean views", *Economics & Philosophy* 38(3) 2022: no mention of WQA or the sixth theorem (inferred from abstract/page fetch).
- Carlson, "On Some Impossibility Theorems in Population Ethics", *Oxford Handbook* 2022, DOI 10.1093/oxfordhb/9780190907686.013.14: Thornley and Arrhenius both cite it as a Finite-Fine-Grainedness-based escape. **Could not access full text** (paywalled; ResearchGate rate-limited). ## 4. Published countermodels

- Only Finite-Fine-Grainedness-denying lexical views: Thomas 2018; Carlson 2022; Thornley 2021 §4 ("As Thomas (2018) and Carlson (forthcoming) note, Lexical Totalism is a counterexample to Arrhenius's Sixth Impossibility Theorem. It satisfies all five adequacy conditions") — verified, and his verification is explicitly of the *strengthened* WQA. Arrhenius 2011 (verified, pp. 6–7) assumes Discreteness and argues the theorem is insensitive to Discreteness vs Denseness, but the welfare levels are integer-indexed, so lexical views fall outside the stated framework. No search hit for a countermodel satisfying all five original conditions inside the discrete framework; "jointly satisfiable"/"countermodel"/"false" searches returned nothing relevant.

## 5. Current wording (verbatim, 2009 and 2011 identical)

Arrhenius 2011, p. 9 (verified): "The Weak Quality Addition Condition: For any population X, there is a perfectly equal population with very high positive welfare, and a very negative welfare level, and a number of lives at this level, such that the addition of the high welfare population to X is at least as good as the addition of any population consisting of the lives with negative welfare and any number of lives with very low positive welfare to X, other things being equal." Exact: "For any population X, there is a negative welfare level Wx, x < 0, two positive welfare ranges R(u, v) and R(1, y), u > y, and two population sizes n > 0, m > 0, such that if A⊂Wz, z ≥ u, N(A)=n, B⊂R(1, y), C⊂Wx, N(C)=m then A∪X is at least as good as B∪C∪X, other things being equal." Theorem: "There is no population axiology which satisfies the Egalitarian Dominance, the General Non-Extreme Priority, the Non-Elitism, the Weak Non-Sadism, and the Weak Quality Addition Condition." Lemma 1.3 proof (2011 pp. 18–19; 2009 pp. 32–33, verified): clause (2) fixes "R(w, t) and R(1, v), w > v, ... Wz a negative welfare level, and p and m two population sizes, which satisfy the Weak Quality Addition Condition for X"; then "It follows from (2), (4), (6), (11), and the Weak Quality Addition Condition that (12) A1∪A2∪X is at least as good as A1∪B2∪C∪X" — applying X's witnesses to background A1∪X, exactly as you describe. Suzuki confirms the July 2015 book manuscript (p. 86) retains the "For any population X, there is ..." order.
