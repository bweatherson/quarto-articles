# Screening report v2 (6k cut, revised draft)

**Paper:** A Counterexample to Arrhenius's Sixth Impossibility Theorem
**Word count:** author's figure c. 5,840 (Word-style, including title, abstract and footnotes). My counts: 5,560 by pandoc plain text; 5,710 prose words with every formula and citation removed; c. 6,090 counting each of the 341 inline formulas and each citation as one word. A Word count of the *rendered* PDF, in which "$n \cdot z + X$" is five words, would be materially higher (rough estimate 6,500–6,800). See competition item 1.
**Thesis:** Arrhenius's sixth impossibility theorem is false as stated: the capacity view (rank by $\min(0, H + T^- + c)$, then by total welfare) is a complete, transitive axiology on Arrhenius's integer levels satisfying ED, GNEP, NE, WNS and WQA in his exact formulations, accepting the Repugnant Conclusion and rejecting the Very Repugnant one. The Thomas/Thornley repair (WQA\*) makes it true, but all it adds is the stipulation that the quantity of uncompensable misery be background-independent; for any axiology satisfying Compensation, WQA in either form already entails Quality Addition, so the sixth theorem's advertised advantage over the fourth is nil for Archimedean views and is bought by stipulation against lexical ones.

**Verdict: PASS, FORMAL**

## Key strengths

- The countermodel checks. I re-verified Theorem 1, the WQA\* violation, Proposition 2 and the $c \geq 1$ claims against the definitions as stated; every formal claim in the text is correct (the one validity gap is in a footnote, below). The location of the failure at Lemma 1.3 step (12), with the explanation of why the enlarged background $A_1 \cup X$ defeats the witnesses chosen for $X$, ties the countermodel to the gap rather than leaving two facts side by side.
- Proposition 2 / Corollary 3 is the philosophical result and is independent of the countermodel: given Compensation and the four shared conditions, the weakening of QA to WQA buys nothing. That reclassifies what the sixth theorem shows, in one sentence a non-formal reader can assess.
- The "second quick answer" paragraph now does what its signpost promises: it states the capacity theorist's description of VRA\*, then the non-capacity critic's, concedes that "I do not think the capacity view answers that critic without presupposing itself," and gives the diagnosis (the critic's quarrel is with ED, GNEP, NE and WNS, not with WQA). That is the right amount of honesty for a paper whose countermodel was found by search.
- The nearest predecessor (Thomas 2018's sufficientarian toy model) is named, the two differences are stated, and the one that does the work (the cap at zero) is identified. I checked the toy-model quotation against Thomas's text; it is verbatim.

## Key weaknesses

- **The footnote converse (QA + WNS ⇒ WQA, WQA\*) is invalid against WNS as the paper states it.** Section 2 gives WNS with "a level $x < 0$ and a number $n$" (no $n > 0$; this matches Arrhenius 2011, p. 8–9, which I checked), while every other condition says $n > 0$. The footnote sets "$m = n'$" where $n'$ is the WNS number, but WQA requires $m > 0$. If WNS holds only with $n' = 0$ (then it is just Mere Addition for positive lives and says nothing about bad lives), the argument gives nothing about $B + m \cdot x + X$ with $m > 0$. The paper itself notes in Section 6 that "the theorems assume only the weaker form." The rest of the footnote is sound: $B = 1 \cdot 1 \subset W_1$ with $y = 1 > 0$ is a legitimate WNS instance, $C = B + X$ is a legitimate background, $B + 1 \cdot 1 \subset R(1,y)$ since $y \geq 3$, and the WQA\* step (witnesses $x', n'$ independent of $X$) follows. One clause fixes it: "given WNS with a positive number."
- **The Conclusion re-collapses the two glosses that Section 4 now keeps apart.** Section 4's summary is careful: "What ED, GNEP, NE and WNS jointly force is VRA\* ... On the capacity view those backgrounds are the ones that have redeemed their misery." But the Conclusion ends: "Whether that is repugnant is a question for people who have made up their minds about the Repugnant Conclusion itself; it is not a new result about misery." That is the capacity theorist's description, which Section 4 conceded does not answer the non-capacity critic "without presupposing itself." For that critic VRA\* is exactly a result about misery. The abstract and first paragraph also describe WQA\*'s content as excluding dependence on "how much excellence the world already contains," which is the capacity view's particular dependence; the neutral statement (Section 4) is dependence on the background at all.
- **The Section 4 defence against absurdity still glosses the family's most exposed verdict.** The rewritten passage is true as stated: $c \geq 1$ absorbs one unit, "what no member of the family can remove is uncompensable misery at the empty background once the misery exceeds $c$," and Lexical Totalism also denies Compensation. But "that is not peculiar to the capacity view" hides a difference: because $T^-$ is welfare-weighted, every member of the family makes $c+1$ lives at level $-1$ together with any number of lives at 3 worse than nothing. Lexical Totalism has no such verdict; its superior badness attaches to *very* negative lives. "Slight misery" in "the five conditions do not force the uncompensability of slight misery" is therefore ambiguous between degree (true) and quantity (false for every member of the family). The author could have seen this, and could have removed it: a variant in which only lives below a very negative threshold $V$ count toward $\Phi$ (weight $\ell - V$ for $\ell < V$, 0 on $[V, A)$, 1 at or above $A$) satisfies the Section 5 inequalities, and I brute-force checked all five conditions for it on populations of up to four lives over levels $-7$ to $6$ with $A = 4$, $V = -3$, $c \in \{0, 2\}$: no violations, and any number of lives at $-1$ is compensable at every background. Either adopt that variant or say why the welfare-weighted version is preferred.
- The headline question "Is that a structure an adequacy condition should rule out?" is still answered "I do not think the question has an obvious answer." The diagnosis that follows is a real answer to the adjacent question, and the Conclusion takes a stance, but the paper's own framing of its evaluative question remains declared open.

## Critical issues for revision

2. Conclusion, last two sentences of paragraph 1: replace "it is not a new result about misery" with the Section 4 formulation (the four conditions force VRA\*; on the capacity view that is RC for covered backgrounds; the non-capacity critic's quarrel is with the four conditions). Align the abstract's "depends on how much excellence the world already contains" with Section 4's "the same for every background."
3. Section 4, "first quick answer": change "slight misery" to "a small quantity of misery," drop or qualify "not peculiar to the capacity view," and either present the threshold variant or acknowledge that on every member of the defined family enough slightly bad lives are lexically uncompensable.
4. Section 2, range definition: add the page (Arrhenius 2011, 7 and n. 8, where he says the three-level restriction "allows us to simplify the exact statements"). Section 6 calls "$y \geq 3$" a "fidelity choice," which reads as the paper's interpretive decision; it is Arrhenius's definition, so say so in both places.
5. Small accuracy items: the Thomas 2016 quotation reads "allowed to depend z and m" in the source; the paper silently supplies "on" (mark it). "For such views both WQA and WQA\* collapse into Quality Addition" (Section 4) asserts equivalence before the converse is given, and the converse needs WNS, which "such views" (Compensation-satisfiers) need not satisfy. Repugnant Addition as stated ("for any $X$ and $n$ there is $N$") is stronger than "the denial of Quality Addition"; Corollary 3 needs only the denial.

Nothing load-bearing was lost in the cut from the previous draft; the gaps the previous report listed were filled with clauses rather than paragraphs and the result reads as complete.

**2. One-sentence contribution.** Arrhenius's sixth impossibility theorem, whose only noticed defect was described in print as fixable "in a way unlikely to cause further controversy," is false on its own integer welfare levels, and the repair that makes it true adds exactly one stipulation: that the quantity of misery which marginal lives can never make up for must not vary with the background. **Rating: surprising.** A specialist would, with Thomas and Thornley, have expected the strengthening to be a formality. The gloss (given Compensation and the four shared conditions, WQA buys nothing over QA) is interesting rather than surprising, but it changes how the sixth theorem should be described.

**3. Thesis in words first.** Now met. Paragraph 1 states the claim in words: "This paper shows that it is false, and that the repair which makes it true adds exactly one stipulation: that the quantity of misery which lives barely worth living can never make up for must not depend on how much excellence the population already contains." The abstract does the same. The title remains theorem-first ("A Counterexample to ..."); the previous report's suggestion to name the philosophical claim in the title was not taken. Section 4 presents the formal results as argument for the claim, as required.
