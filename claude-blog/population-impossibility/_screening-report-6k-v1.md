# Screening report v1 (6k cut)

**Paper:** A Counterexample to Arrhenius's Sixth Impossibility Theorem
**Word count:** c. 5,700 including title, abstract (235) and footnotes; 5,560 by pandoc plain-text count, 5,730 counting each display formula and citation as one word. Headroom under the 6,000 ceiling is c. 270–440 words depending on how the organisers count.
**Thesis:** Arrhenius's sixth impossibility theorem is false as stated: the "capacity view" (rank by min(0, excellent-lives + negative-welfare + c), then by total welfare) is a complete, transitive axiology on integer welfare levels that satisfies ED, GNEP, NE, WNS and WQA in Arrhenius's exact formulations while accepting the Repugnant Conclusion and rejecting the Very Repugnant Conclusion. The Thomas/Thornley repair (WQA\*) makes the theorem true, but what it adds is only a stipulation that the quantity of uncompensable misery be background-independent; for any axiology on which misery can be compensated by marginal lives, WQA in either form already collapses into Quality Addition, so the weakening that distinguishes the sixth theorem from the fourth has content only against lexical views, and against those it is true only by stipulation.

**Verdict: PASS, FORMAL**

## Key strengths

- The counterexample is checkable by hand and it checks (details below). Theorem 1 is a short, transparent argument; the mechanism paragraph ("WQA lets the quantity of misery depend on the background, and the capacity view uses exactly that freedom") is the right level of explanation, and the location of the failure in Lemma 1.3 step (12) ties the countermodel to the gap Thomas identified rather than leaving them as two separate facts.
- Proposition 2 / Corollary 3 is the paper's genuinely philosophical result and is independent of the countermodel: given Compensation, WQA entails QA, so the sixth theorem's advertised advantage over the fourth ("binds even those who accept the Repugnant Conclusion") is nil for every Archimedean view. That is stateable in one sentence, is not in Thomas or Thornley as far as the paper's own account goes, and changes how the sixth theorem should be described.
- The paper locates itself precisely against its nearest predecessor (Thomas 2018's sufficientarian toy model), names the two differences (welfare-weighted misery; cap at zero), and explains which one does the work. That is what the priority standard asks for.
- The scope remarks are the right ones: the countermodel lives on integer levels, so the Carlson/Thornley Finite-Fine-Grainedness escape is not what is going on; the view's lexicality is in the population ranking, not the welfare ordering.

## Key weaknesses

- **A false sentence in the central philosophical paragraph (§4, "first quick answer").** The paper writes: "the c = 0 view denies [Compensation] at every background, including the empty one, and so does Lexical Totalism … The five conditions do not force this, as the c ≥ 1 view shows; so far as I know it is the first axiology satisfying them that does not have it." But the c ≥ 1 view also denies Compensation at the empty background: for m|z| > c, J(m·z + K·3) = c − m|z| < 0 = J(∅) for every K, so m·z + K·3 is worse than nothing for all K. What c ≥ 1 removes is only the verdict about *one slightly bad* life; (c+1) units of misery plus a billion marginal lives is still worse than nothing, which is structurally Thornley's "(r, s) with r < 0" verdict with a different threshold. The paragraph conflates "a billion marginal lives with one slightly bad life is worse than nothing" with "denies Compensation at the empty background", and the claim to be "the first axiology satisfying them that does not have it" is unsupported on either reading (Lexical Totalism's verdict concerns *very* negative lives, so it may not have the one-unit consequence either). The paper's conclusion that the one-unit verdict "is not a commitment of the structure" survives, but the sentence contrasting the view with Lexical Totalism does not.
- **"Equivalent" is asserted, "entails" is proved.** Abstract: WQA "in either form collapses into Quality Addition"; Conclusion: "is equivalent to the Quality Addition Condition". Proposition 2 proves only WQA + Compensation ⇒ QA. The converse (QA ⇒ WQA) needs something like WNS to license "adding m very bad lives to X + B does not help", and that is never said. It is a one-sentence fix, but as written the conclusion overstates the theorem.
- **A capacity-view gloss is presented as a theorem-neutral statement of what the four conditions establish.** §4 end and the Conclusion say the four conditions "force the Repugnant Conclusion relative to any background that has redeemed its misery, and nothing stronger"; the abstract says they force "only a background-relative Very Repugnant Conclusion". The theorem-neutral content is VRA\*: for each (z, m) *some* background exists. "Any background that has redeemed its misery" is how the *capacity view* describes which backgrounds those are. The two glosses should be kept apart, and the abstract and body should agree on which one is being asserted.
- **The §5 inequalities use undefined symbols and one is reversed.** "GNEP with witness G requires w_{z−1} + G w_x ≥ w_z + G w_y, NE with witness n requires (n+1) w_{x−1} ≥ w_x + n w_y, WNS requires m w_x ≥ D w_Z": G, m, D, Z are never introduced, x and y silently change meaning between GNEP and NE, and the WNS inequality as written (m w_x ≥ D w_Z with x < 0 negative-weighted and Z > 0) is the wrong way round; the condition requires D w_Z ≥ m w_x. This reads like the residue of a longer passage that defined its variables.
- The paper raises its own evaluative question, "Is that a structure an adequacy condition should rule out?", and answers "I do not think the question has an obvious answer." The diagnosis that follows (the fight is over the four conditions, not over WQA) is a real answer to a nearby question, and the Conclusion's "Whether that is repugnant is a question for people who have made up their minds about the Repugnant Conclusion itself" is a stance. But a judge reading for "thesis in words" will notice that the paper's own headline question is declared open.

## Critical issues for revision

1. Rewrite the §4 sentence beginning "By Corollary 3, anyone who accepts the five conditions denies Compensation" so that it says exactly what the baseline removes: the view with c ≥ 1 still has uncompensable misery at the empty background once misery exceeds c; what it lacks is uncompensability of *slight* misery. Drop or qualify "the first axiology satisfying them that does not have it" unless it can be shown that Lexical Totalism has the one-unit verdict.
2. Either change "equivalent to" (Conclusion) and "collapses into" (abstract) to "entails", or add the one-line converse: given WNS with witnesses (x′, n′), choose x = x′, m = n′ in WQA and QA + WNS gives WQA.
3. In §5, define G, m, D, Z, fix the WNS inequality, and use variable names that do not collide with the GNEP and NE inequalities on the same line.
4. Source or justify "Ranges R(x, y) have at least three levels" (§2). It is load-bearing: the WQA and GNEP witnesses use y = 3, Compensation is stated for level 3, and Proposition 2's proof needs y ≥ 3 to place K·3 in R(1, y). If this is Arrhenius's stipulation, cite the page; if it is the paper's, say so and note that Compensation can be restated for level 1.
5. In the Introduction, "The theorem has been treated as sound. Thornley states it as a result" is contradicted three sentences later, where Thornley is reported as saying "the Sixth Impossibility Theorem actually requires the slightly stronger condition stated here". Thornley states the *repaired* theorem as a result. Recast the sentence (the Handbook and Arrhenius 2026 are the examples of treating the original as sound).

## Cut-specific: gaps

Places where the text reads as if something load-bearing was removed:

- §5 first paragraph (see weakness 4 above): the linear-inequality list arrives with undefined witnesses. One sentence introducing "with GNEP witness G lives at very high level x against G lives at very low level y, NE witness n, and WNS comparing D lives at positive level Z against m lives at negative level x" would repair it (c. 40 words).
- §4: "Thomas's fourth theorem, that ED, GNEP, NE and WNS entail Repugnant Addition, the denial of Quality Addition" is the first and only mention of Repugnant Addition, and Quality Addition's exact form appears for the first time inside the statement of Proposition 2. Corollary 3 depends on both. A reader who does not already know Thomas's reconstruction has to reverse-engineer what Repugnant Addition says (c. 30 words to state it).
- §2: "Ranges R(x, y) have at least three levels" is asserted without source and then relied on in three places (see revision item 4).
- §4, "second quick answer": "though I should be clear whose reason it is" promises a distinction (the capacity theorist's reason vs. a neutral reason) that the paragraph then draws only implicitly. The paragraph works, but the signpost points at something that is now a half-sentence long.
- §4: "Thornley's condition, which entails the denial of VRA\*" is stated for the exact form of WQA\* against a fixed-threshold VRA\*. Exact WQA\* lets the axiology choose u after X, and nothing guarantees u ≤ A, so the entailment is not immediate across settings. The Lean section says the exact-form unsatisfiability was proved directly (`T6star_exact`), which is what actually carries the claim; a clause saying "in Thomas's fixed-threshold setting" here would stop a referee pausing.
- §1 paragraph 3: "The strengthened condition does make the theorem true; Thomas proved it in his note" relies on an unpublished 2016 manuscript for the repaired theorem's proof. The paper's own Lean verification covers it, but the text should say in one clause that the proof is independently machine-checked here, so the reader is not sent to an unpublished note for the only proof.

None of these is a hole in the argument; all are places where a reader expects a sentence and finds a clause.

## Mathematics checked

All checked by hand against the definitions as the paper states them (integer levels; A ≥ 4; H = lives ≥ A; T⁻ = sum of negative welfare; I = H + T⁻; J = min(0, I + c); lexical on (J, T)).

- **Definition.** Lexicographic order on Z × Z: complete, transitive, anonymous, welfarist. Correct. Rejection of VRC: for m|z| > c, J(m·z + N·3) = c − m|z| < 0 = J(n·A). Correct for all N. Acceptance of RC: misery-free populations all have J = 0 so T decides. Correct.
- **ED.** T(A) = nx > T(B) needs n > 0 (the paper records this as a fidelity choice). H and T⁻ cases as given are exhaustive and correct; the case x ≥ A ∧ x < 0 is vacuous. So I(A) ≥ I(B), hence J(A) ≥ J(B), and T strict. Correct, and strict.
- **GNEP** (u = A, y = 3, n = 2). ΔH = +2 − [z+1 = A]; ΔT⁻ = −[z+1 ≤ 0]; the two indicator cases are exclusive, so ΔI ≥ 1. ΔT = 2x − T(B) − 1 ≥ 2A − 7 ≥ 1. Correct, and the witnesses (u, y, n) do not depend on z, so the proof also covers the stronger quantifier order in which n is chosen before z.
- **NE** (n = 1). ΔT = x − 2 − y ≥ 0 since y ≤ x − 2. Case x − 1 ≥ A: ΔH ∈ {0, 1}, ΔT⁻ ≥ 0. Case x < A: ΔH = 0; ΔT⁻ = |y| − 2 ≥ 0 at x = 0 and x − 2 − y ≥ 0 at x < 0 (the paper mentions only x = 0; x < 0 is the same computation). Case x = A: ΔH = −1; if y < 0, ΔT⁻ = |y| ≥ 1; if y ≥ 0, D ⊂ R(y, A) has no negatives, both J = 0, T decides. All correct. The claim that the view violates General Non-Elitism (unrestricted D) is also correct: take D with uncovered misery and y ≥ 0.
- **WNS** (x = −1, n = 1). ΔI = H(B) + 1 ≥ 1, ΔT = T(B) + 1 ≥ 1. Correct, including for B empty.
- **WQA** (x = −1, u = A, y = 3, n = 1, m = max(0, I(X)+c) + 1). I(B + m·(−1) + X) + c = I(X) + c − m: equals −1 if I(X)+c ≥ 0, and I(X)+c−1 otherwise; in both cases strictly below min(0, I(X)+c+1) = J(1·z + X). Correct, and strict. B's size is irrelevant, as claimed.
- **WQA\* violation.** X = m|x| lives at A gives I(X) = m|x|; both sides then have I ≥ 0 hence J = 0; T(B + m·x + X) − T(n·z + X) = T(B) − m|x| − nz, positive for B of nz + m|x| + 1 lives at level 1. Correct; the choice of z ≥ u is the adversary's (z = u) and the inequality is strict, so WQA\* fails.
- **Proposition 2.** Compensation applied to Y = X + B with the WQA witness level x and number m gives K with X + B + m·x + K·3 ⪰ X + B; B + K·3 ⊂ R(1, y) because y ≥ 3; WQA (same witnesses, since X is unchanged) gives n·z + X ⪰ B + K·3 + m·x + X; transitivity. Correct. The same proof works for WQA\* as claimed. Proof does not establish the converse (see weakness 2).
- **Corollary 3** follows from Prop 2 plus Thomas's T4 (ED, GNEP, NE, WNS ⇒ ¬QA) as the paper states that result; I cannot check T4 itself from the text, but the capacity view and total utilitarianism both satisfy the four and violate QA, which is consistent with it.
- **§4, c ≥ 1 claims.** "Every condition still holds": covered by Theorem 1's range c ≥ 0. "The empty population absorbs a unit of misery": J(10⁹·3 + 1·(−1)) = min(0, −1 + c) = 0 for c ≥ 1, T > 0, so better than ∅. Correct. "The Very Repugnant Conclusion still fails for misery exceeding the baseline": correct as shown above. The further sentence about Compensation at the empty background is false (weakness 1).
- **§4, graded version** (weight 0 at 0..3, rising linearly to 1 at A, 1 above A, ℓ for ℓ < 0; Φ = Σ weights; rank by min(0, Φ + c) then T). I checked all five conditions by hand for all A ≥ 4, not just A ≤ 10: ED by monotone weights; GNEP since any single step of weight is ≤ 1 and two excellent lives add 2; WNS trivially; WQA with m = ⌈max(0, Φ(X)+c)⌉ + 1; NE because 2w_{x−1} − w_x − w_y ≥ 0 whenever y < 0 (as −w_y ≥ 1 and w_{x−1} − (w_x − w_{x−1}) ≥ −1), and when y ≥ 0 both populations are misery-free and the cap makes T decide. So the paper's "checked numerically for A ≤ 10" is conservative; the claim is true. Two small wording points: the redemptive weight is constant above A, so "in proportion to how far above it lies" holds only up to A; and the Φ-lowering transfer for the graded potential is NE at x = 4 (2w_3 − w_4 = −1/(A−3)), not "at the very high level" as §5 says, so §5's "one transfer lowers every such Φ" is exactly right for the step potential and right in spirit (the transfer at the first level where the weight becomes positive) for the family.
- **§5 inequalities.** GNEP and NE inequalities are correct after reindexing; the WNS inequality is reversed as written (see weakness 4). "ED requires the weights to be non-decreasing" is sufficient, and is what is needed. The derivation w_3 ≤ 0 ⇒ (with w_ℓ ≥ 0 for ℓ ≥ 0 and monotonicity) w_0 = … = w_3 = 0 is correct.
- **Lemma 1.3, step (12).** Cannot be checked from the text; the description is consistent with Thomas's quoted remark and with the mechanism of the countermodel.

Net: every formal claim I could check is correct, with the WNS inequality in §5 the only error, and the only false sentence is the prose claim about Compensation at the empty background in §4.

## Competition-mode items

**One-sentence contribution.** Arrhenius's sixth impossibility theorem, which has stood since 2003/2011 and whose only noticed defect was described in print as fixable "in a way unlikely to cause further controversy", is false on its own integer welfare levels, and the repair that makes it true adds exactly one thing: the stipulation that the quantity of misery marginal lives can never make up for must not depend on how much excellence the population already contains.

**Rating: surprising.** A specialist would expect, with Thomas and Thornley, that the strengthening is a formality; a complete transitive countermodel on Arrhenius's own levels is not what the field expected. The philosophical gloss (Prop 2: the fourth-to-sixth weakening has no content against Archimedean views) is interesting rather than surprising, but it is a genuine reclassification of what the sixth theorem shows.

**Thesis in words first?** Partly. The abstract states the philosophical claim in words after the formal claim ("I show what its extra premise buys", followed by three sentences a non-formal reader can assess). The title ("A Counterexample to …") and the first paragraph of §1 are theorem-first: the first paragraph only sets up the theorem, and the thesis in words arrives in paragraph 3 ("So the sixth theorem does not refute the position it was built to refute"). Given the organisers' line that proving a theorem "is not itself philosophy", a judge skimming title and first paragraph will file this as a technical note. The fix is cheap: one sentence of thesis in paragraph 1, and a title that names the philosophical claim (e.g., "What Arrhenius's Sixth Impossibility Theorem Does Not Show" or "Misery, Capacity, and Arrhenius's Sixth Theorem"). The body does present the formal results as arguments for the philosophical claim (§4 is organised around "what the strengthening buys"), so the structural requirement is met below the fold.

**Source-representation risks (internal evidence only).**
- "Thornley states it as a result" (§1) under the heading that the theorem "has been treated as sound" misrepresents Thornley by the paper's own later account of him. Fix in revision item 5.
- The motivation for weakening QA to WQA is attributed to a quotation from Arrhenius, Budolfson and Spears (2021, 119). The weakening was made in 2003/2011; a 2021 co-authored remark about the Very Repugnant Conclusion being "intuitively more compelling" to avoid is evidence of Arrhenius's view but not of the 2003 motivation. Either cite the original motivation or phrase as "as Arrhenius and co-authors later put it".
- Heavy reliance on an unpublished manuscript (Thomas 2016) for the corrected theorem's proof, VRA/VRA\* notation, and the "not easy to understand the difference" quotation at p. 11. If this is not publicly available, the judges cannot verify any of it; the paper should say where it is available or lean on its own Lean proof.
- The Carlson quotation ("… given that the order of welfare levels assumed in the proof is fine-grained and A-discrete") is doing argumentative work (it is the concession the paper's result overturns); its wording and section should be verified against the published text, since "A-discrete" is Carlson's term and the paper's gloss of it ("Carlson concedes [integer-indexed welfare levels] to it") must match his definition.
- "Ranges R(x, y) have at least three levels" is presented as part of Arrhenius's framework with no page reference. If Arrhenius does not say this, the paper has added a stipulation to his framework and should own it.
- Arrhenius 2026 is cited for a quotation and a footnote claim (n. 10, that the exact versions are the weaker ones). A paper dated 7 October 2026 citing a 2026 source with section and footnote numbers is fine, but the quotation is the paper's evidence that Arrhenius still endorses the X-first quantifier order; its wording should be verified against the final published text, not a preprint.
- Five citations (2000a, 2000b, 2003, 2009, 2011) are given for "six" theorems. Probably correct (the dissertation contains more than one), but a judge may count.
