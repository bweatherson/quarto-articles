# Screening Report

**Paper:** "A Counterexample to Arrhenius's Sixth Impossibility Theorem"

**Word count:** ~7,450 (body, excluding front matter and bibliography; 30 bibliography entries)

**Thesis:** Arrhenius's sixth impossibility theorem is false as stated. The "capacity view", which ranks populations lexically by min(0, H + T⁻) (excellent lives minus uncovered misery) and then by total welfare, satisfies all five conditions in Arrhenius's exact formulations on integer welfare levels while accepting the Repugnant Conclusion and rejecting the Very Repugnant Conclusion. The Thomas/Thornley strengthening (WQA*) restores the theorem, and what it adds is exactly the exclusion of views on which the uncompensable quantity of misery depends on the background; all results are checked in Lean 4.

**Decision: EVALUATE**

## Key Strengths

- **The central result is correct, new, and checkable.** Non-Elitism holds in every case: for x−1 ≥ A both new lives are excellent; for x < A only T⁻ moves and the one delicate case x = 0 gives ΔT⁻ = |y| − 2 ≥ 0; for x = A with y < 0 the raised life gives ΔT⁻ = |y| ≥ 1; for x = A with y ≥ 0 the restriction D ⊂ R(y, x) forces T⁻ = 0 on both sides so J = 0 and T decides (ΔT = x − 2 − y ≥ 0). WQA holds with m = max(0, I(X)) + 1. The Lean predicates (`Conditions.lean` 25–67) match Arrhenius's exact text with no quiet weakening; `WeakQualityAdditionStar` differs from `WeakQualityAddition` only in the placement of `∃ x m`. The diagnosis of Arrhenius's error is accurate: step (12) on p. 19 does apply WQA to A₁ ∪ X with witnesses supplied for X.
- **Proposition 2 is right and does real work.** Combined with T4 (the four conditions entail Repugnant Addition), it yields that, given the other four conditions, WQA *entails the denial of Compensation*. That is a sharper statement of what the sixth theorem's weakening is about than anything in Arrhenius, Thomas or Thornley, and the paper should state it in that form.
- **The Lean description is faithful.** `T1`–`T5`, `T6star`, and `T6star_exact` are as described, including the uniform high level `xh` and the extra IAA merge step (`TheoremsExact.lean` 114–135); the two deviations (y ≥ 3; n > 0 in WNS) are recorded in the source header. The script reproduces the stated weights for the sixth theorem and infeasibility for the fifth.
- **The problem is of broad interest.** Whether the canonical statement of "no satisfactory population axiology" is true is a question ethicists outside population ethics have reason to care about, and the positive content (a complete, transitive axiology on fine-grained levels that accepts RC and rejects VRC under the weakest conditions) is a result about the problem, not about an exchange.

## Key Weaknesses

- **§5's explanation of why the method fails for Theorems 1–4 is wrong, and the script bakes the answer in.** The paper says refusing those conclusions "requires Φ(n·A) > Φ(N·3) for all N, which needs w_A < 0 where the cap needs w_A ≥ 0." It does not: w₃ < 0 with w_A = 1 satisfies the displayed inequality for every N. What refusal actually requires is J(N·3) < 0 for large N, i.e. w₃ < 0, which is what conflicts with the cap (w_ℓ ≥ 0 for ℓ ≥ 0). `refuse_RC_or_RA` encodes w_A ≤ −1 directly against the cap's w_A ≥ 0, so the "infeasible" verdicts for T1–T4 are an artefact of a mis-encoded refusal, not a finding about the conditions. The claim that the method "when it fails, [delivers] a diagnosis of which condition does the excluding" is therefore unsupported for four of the six cases. A careful author reading their own constraint list would have seen this.
- **§4's key dialectical move is asserted, and one factual claim is unsupported.** The defence of the capacity view's worst consequence (a billion lives at 3 plus one at −1 is worse than nothing) is: "It is the price of denying Compensation... Lexical Totalism... does exactly this, since on it a very bad life subtracts a higher good." Thomas 2018 defines total lexic utilitarianism with neutral (0,0), blissful (1,0) and drab (0,m) lives and says nothing about negative welfare; on the natural extension in which a very bad life is (0, −M), Compensation holds and the consequence does not follow. The general claim "the consequence is shared by every view that the sixth theorem was aimed at" needs Compensation to fail *at the empty background*, which failure elsewhere does not give; the hedge "marginal lives have no value of a kind misery lacks" is doing undefined work. The paper's own §3 parenthetical also sets up a dilemma §4 never faces: at A = 4, "excellence" begins one step above "barely worth living", so the "misery is redeemable by excellence" gloss is hollow; at larger A, a billion lives at A−1 plus one at −1 is worse than nothing.
- **Quantifier looseness in a paper about quantifiers.** "Thomas's corrected theorem... is that ED, GNEP, NE and WNS entail VRA*; equivalently, that ED, GNEP, NE, WNS and WQA* are jointly unsatisfiable" and "The denial of VRA* is Thornley's strengthened condition": exact WQA* (all z ≥ u, all B in the range) is strictly stronger than ¬VRA*, so these are not equivalent; Lean proves both separately, which is fine, but the prose should not say "equivalently." The WNS fidelity note is one-sided: n > 0 strengthens the countermodel claim but *weakens* `T6star_exact`; since n = 0 (Mere Addition) also drives the proof, the restriction should be dropped rather than recorded. ED's `0 < n` is a third, unrecorded (harmless, necessary) deviation.
- **Framing, fairness and overclaim.** The intro is "Arrhenius proved, Thomas and Thornley repaired, I refute." Thomas's 2018 note that "one has to slightly strengthen the premisses" already says the theorem, not just the proof, must change, so "Both treat the matter as a repair to a proof" and "survived... a decade of citation" undersell him. "Restated in Arrhenius's forthcoming book" is unverified here and load-bearing for the opening. "Its entire content is the exclusion of capacity views" overclaims: the WQA/WQA* gap is any background-dependence of m, not specifically dependence on excellence. The bibliography is adequate on verification and thin on critics of the adequacy conditions (Hájek and Rabinowicz, Gustafsson, Nebel; Carlson is cited but not engaged).
