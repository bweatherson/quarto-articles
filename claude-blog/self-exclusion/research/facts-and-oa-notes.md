## Part A

### A1. Long (2023), CJLJ 36: 407-35. VERIFIED (full text, open access)
Source: https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/44C5D3AB8CF9917F8ED40EFEF923CA71/S0841820922000303a.pdf/theorising-gambling-self-exclusion-agreements-the-inadequacy-of-procedural-autonomy.pdf (doi:10.1017/cjlj.2022.30). File: `long2023-cjlj-self-exclusion.txt`.

**Main claim.** Self-exclusion's special value over forced exclusion must be respect for autonomy, but procedural autonomy cannot supply it. Operators cannot tell which of the t1/t2 preferences is autonomous, so both must be treated as equally autonomous. Self-exclusion therefore rests on a substantive judgment that abstinence is better, and paternalism is the more promising home (p. 435).

**Quotations.**
- p. 426: “Procedural autonomy is distinguished by its indifference to the content of decisions.”
- p. 427: “In practice, however, the opacity of the self-excluder's thought processes makes it impossible for operators and their staff—as enlistees to enforce the Ulysses pact—to reliably make this judgement.”
- p. 427: “It must then be assumed, for practical purposes, that both the t 1 and t 2 decisions are equally autonomous.”
- p. 428 (on Davis's resolution preference): “There is nothing to stop the gambler from simply saying that they now have a new resolution preference in favour of t 2.”
- p. 429: “In the case of a self-excluded gambler, the assumption that long-term planning is necessarily represented by the addict's self-denial rather than, say, a lifelong commitment to libertinism is a substantive condition.”
- p. 434: “Rather, it must be based on an affirmative position—commonly acknowledged from the outset by all parties—that abstinence from gambling is preferable to indulgence.”
- p. 434: “self-exclusion may simply exist to allow problem gamblers to self-identify for exclusion, which they might be subjected to without their consent at a later stage anyway.”
- p. 434: “Rather, the self-excluder's consent would be irrelevant and they would be excluded because they are a problem gambler in the context of a gambling policy which seeks to funnel all problem gamblers toward exclusion.”
- p. 435: “The lack of a mechanism for excluding problem gamblers who do not self-exclude surely refutes the view that self-exclusion exists only to more easily identify some prospective excludees.”

**The informational reading.** Long never uses the word "informational". His version is the consequentialist reading on pp. 434-5: self-exclusion as a device for problem gamblers to "self-identify for exclusion". He rejects it twice: it disregards autonomy and is "probably disingenuous" (pp. 434-5), and it fails to explain why the UK has no forced-exclusion route (p. 435).

**Forced exclusion** is the foil throughout (pp. 407, 409, 414-15, 419, 427). New Zealand is his hybrid case: an involuntary order "seemingly differs little from forced exclusion procedures" and makes the "self-" "largely irrelevant" (pp. 408-9).

**Flag.** The premise at p. 435 predates the current SR 3.4.3, which requires operators to act on harm indicators, including ending the relationship (B2). That is not formal forced exclusion, but it weakens the premise.

### A4. Stephenson, Scholten, Kane & Owen (2026). VERIFIED (full text, open access)
"Ulysses looks back: a case report on the experience of using a mental health self-binding directive", *IJPM* 43: 333-42, doi:10.1017/ipm.2025.10147. Source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/6474D8825FB614B0219EC32EB5182310/S079096672510147Xa.pdf/div-class-title-ulysses-looks-back-a-case-report-on-the-experience-of-using-a-mental-health-self-binding-directive-div.pdf. Files: `stephenson-et-al-2026-ulysses-looks-back.txt` (pdftotext -layout; two columns interleave) and `...-READING-ORDER.txt` (same PDF, default mode; the quotations below match this one).

**Main claim.** A first case report: a service user with bipolar disorder valued her self-binding directive (SBD) and endorsed its use in retrospect, supporting SBDs as autonomy-enhancing. It concerns mental-health treatment, and the t2 self lacks capacity, unlike self-exclusion.
- p. 333: “Viewed legally, an SBD is a request for involuntary treatment that should inform but not fetter such decision makers.”
- p. 339: “Jessica's well self was able to assert autonomy over her unwell self to try and avoid harm to herself, her loved ones, and her relationships with them.”
- p. 339: the "'thank you' theory" holds that coercion can be justified given “a loss and then regain of capacity during an episode of illness plus the service user approves of the use of coercion in retrospect”.
- p. 340: “assuming that the self at one point in time should have primacy over the self at another point in time is unjustified”.
- p. 340: capacity assessment “may be considered as a clinical quality and implementation issue rather than something which is conceptually insurmountable”.

### A6. The judgments
**Calvert v William Hill Credit Ltd [2008] EWCA Civ 1427 (16 Dec 2008). VERIFIED.** Source: https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2008/1427/data.xml (BAILII was Anubis-blocked). File: `calvert-v-william-hill-2008-ewca-civ-1427.txt`. Paragraph numbers are the judgment's.
- Special disadvantage and unconscionability do not feature. The case turns on duty of care, scope of duty and causation; equity appears only for fiduciary duty ([52]-[55]).
- [2]: “One approach was to offer problem gamblers who had accounts and dealt with the bookmaker on the telephone a form of self-exclusion agreement.”
- [6]: the broad duty to protect problem gamblers was rejected below and not appealed; the narrow duty stood: “The judge found that William Hill had assumed this responsibility and that they were accordingly in breach of duty.”
- [47]: “William Hill did not assume responsibility to prevent Mr Calvert from gambling. They assumed a responsibility not to allow him to place telephone bets with them.”
- [48]: the claim fails because “the scope of William Hill's duty of care did not extend to prevent him from gambling”. The appeal was dismissed ([73]).
- [70]: “The assumption of responsibility by William Hill for excluding him from further telephone betting with them arose precisely at the moment and because he sought William Hill's assistance as a problem gambler.”
- [71]-[72], obiter: 30% contributory negligence; on 10 Oct 2006 “he was expressly offered to opportunity to self-exclude and he declined to take it”.

### B3. LCCP self-exclusion code. VERIFIED, with one gap
Sources: https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/licensees-and-businesses/lccp/condition/3-5-3-remote-sr-code and the sibling slugs 3-5-1-non-remote-and-trading-rooms-sr-code, 3-5-2-non-remote-ordinary-code, 3-5-4-remote-ordinary-code, 3-5-5-remote-multi-operator-sr-code. File as in B2.

The binding SR code is 3.5.3 (remote; 3.5.1 non-remote). The period and cooling-off rules sit in the ordinary codes 3.5.4 (remote) and 3.5.2 (non-remote). Remote operators must join the national scheme (3.5.5): “Licensees must participate in the national multi-operator self-exclusion scheme.”
- 3.5.3 (SR): “Licensees must close any customer accounts of an individual who has entered a self- exclusion agreement and return any funds held in the customer account.”
- Status of ordinary codes: “any departure from ordinary code provisions by an operator may be taken into account by the Commission on a licence review, but cannot lead to imposition of a financial penalty.”
- 3.5.4: “the minimum self-exclusion period offered is of a duration of not less than 6 nor more than 12 months”; an option of “up to at least five years”; no cooling-off before entering.
- 3.5.4, end of period: “at the end of the period chosen by the customer, self-exclusion remains in place, for a minimum of 7 years, unless the customer takes positive action to gamble again”. On request, “the customer is given one day to cool off before being allowed to access gambling facilities”.
- Refund and forfeiture: the codes require return of funds held. I found no forfeiture of stakes or winnings in 3.4.x or 3.5.x (searched each retrieved page for winnings, forfeit and stake); wider Commission guidance not searched exhaustively: UNVERIFIED. For repayment see 888 in B5.
