# Reading note: Franklin-Hall, "Binding the Self: The Ethics of Ulysses Contracts", *Ethics* 134(1) (Oct 2023): 57–88 [READ in full]

Source: PDF supplied by BW (texts/franklin-hall2023-binding-the-self.pdf; extracted text in fh2023-clean.txt). doi:10.1086/725806.

## Thesis
The paper defends the **Authentic Authorization Model** (pp. 58, 71):
- **Authority Principle.** At t1, A may authorise B to take "legitimate and reasonably proportionate measures" binding A at t2 against "backsliding on, or straying from" a configuration of values. This holds only if those values, and the endorsement of the contract, are still attributable to A at t2.
- **Attribution Principle.** The values are still attributable unless A's mind has stably changed in a way for which A is "answerable".

The model combines two kinds of respect, which Franklin-Hall calls "sovereignty respect" and "authenticity respect" (p. 70).

## Structure
- **Definitions (§II, pp. 59–63).**
  - A Ulysses contract is *cooperative* precommitment: the deputy is authorised "to act in a way that interferes with or limits the principal's freedom ... that would otherwise be impermissible or inappropriate" (p. 61).
  - The deputy "must aim to assist Ulysses in self-management" (p. 61).
  - The normative powers involved are authorising, waiving the power to rescind, and securing the deputy's obligation (p. 62).
- **Four simple models rejected (§III, pp. 63–71).**
  - Sovereignty (Feinberg, Schelling).
  - Competence (Buchanan & Brock).
  - Diachronic Autonomy (Davis 2008, *KIEJ* 18).
  - Authenticity (Savulescu; Savulescu & Dickenson 1998).
- **The evidentiary reading is named and rejected (p. 70).**
  - "As both Savulescu/Dickenson and Davis admit, the moral significance of the Ulysses contract for them is purely evidentiary."
  - Franklin-Hall's objection: "This seems wrong. Intuitively, an important part of what makes it permissible for me to help Ulysses remain true to his values is that he authorized me to do it" (pp. 70–71).
  - **This is the main foil for T3.**
- **Limits (§IV.B, pp. 75–77).**
  - Constraints must be proportionate.
  - Some ends one can consent to but cannot bind oneself to: sex, and "arguably" refusing life-saving treatment (p. 76).
  - Note 53 cites Walker 2012 ("Ulysses Contracts in Medicine", *Law & Phil* 31: 77–98).
- **Change of mind (§V, pp. 77–81).**
  - Rational reconsideration is neither necessary nor sufficient. Spellecy 2004 *KIEJ* 13: 372–92 is the target (p. 77).
  - What counts is a stable replacement of evaluative dispositions, judged by credentials (pp. 79–80).
  - **Waiting periods (p. 80).** Contracts "may, at the principal's discretion, build in a waiting period to see whether an apparent change of view sticks." Franklin-Hall's model thus *also predicts* the exit delay.
  - **No-release contracts as second best** where the deputy cannot discriminate (p. 81), compared to a no-swimming ban.
- **Answerability, for dementia and permanent impairment (§VI, pp. 82–87).**

## What it does NOT discuss
- gambling, self-exclusion or casinos;
- refusal-to-supply arrangements as such;
- testimony;
- exploitation;
- duties a third party has independently of the authorisation;
- remedies (refund or forfeiture);
- childbirth analgesia.

The only third-party considerations are the deputy's own judgement of harm and proportionality (pp. 76–77) and the interests of others, which are set aside (p. 62).

## Consequences for T3
1. **T3 must answer Franklin-Hall's objection to evidentiary readings (p. 70).**
   - Reply: the objection presupposes that the deputy *interferes* with the principal's freedom, which needs authorisation (his own definition, p. 61).
   - Self-exclusion asks only that a seller *refuse to supply*. A seller needs no authorisation to decline a sale. So consent is not needed where Franklin-Hall says it is, and the evidentiary or testimonial reading is not vulnerable to his objection.
   - On his own definition, self-exclusion may not even be a Ulysses contract: the operator does not "aim to assist" in self-management; it discharges its own duty. **Check this carefully. It could be the paper's central structural point.**
2. **The exit-asymmetry prediction is not unique to T3.** Franklin-Hall predicts waiting periods (p. 80), and Spellecy predicts reconsideration interviews. T3's distinctive predictions must be:
   - the *remedy* (refund of the operator's gains, not forfeiture);
   - the *divergence* between gambling and labour analgesia. Franklin-Hall might explain the epidural by his limits on binding oneself in bodily or medical matters (p. 76). T3 must show its explanation is principled, not a list.
3. **Do not misdescribe Franklin-Hall.** He rejects the Sovereignty Model; he allows binding a competent later self against backsliding; and he accepts no-release contracts as second best.
