# Idea memo v1: Notice, not self-binding: what makes gambling self-exclusion work

## 1. Question

You ask a casino to refuse you. Months later you are competent and insistent, and you ask to play. The casino must refuse. Why may, and must, it do so? Two familiar answers have trouble here.
- **Ulysses answer.** Your earlier self has authority over your later self (Feinberg; in qualified form, Franklin-Hall 2023). It must explain how a competent will can be overridden by an earlier one.
- **Paternalist answer.** The casino may protect you for your own good, and prior consent is irrelevant (Husak 2009; Long 2023; in weak form, Spellecy 2003). It must explain why paternalism is *mandatory* for a commercial operator that otherwise has no standing to protect you (Husak's fifth criterion, pp. 125–126).

## 2. Thesis

Gambling self-exclusion is not self-binding. It is **notice**.
- The registration tells the operator something about the registrant: that her gambling is not under her control.
- Dealing with her afterwards would be taking advantage of a vulnerability the operator knows about, on terms where its gain is her loss. That is knowing exploitation.
- The operator's refusal discharges *its own* duty not to exploit. It does not carry out her earlier will, and it does not protect her for her own good.
- So the earlier self acquires no authority over the later self, and nothing in the practice requires that it does.

This also explains what the rival views cannot:
- the duty arises without any registration;
- the remedy should be gain-based;
- formally similar precommitments (labour analgesia, firearm do-not-sell lists) work differently.

## 4. Argument and tests

### 4.0 The basic structure
1. A seller is at liberty to decline a sale. So the operator's *permission* to refuse needs no authorisation from the registrant. Only its *duty* to refuse needs explaining.
2. Exploitation consists in taking advantage of another's vulnerability in a transaction. One standard version is unconscionable dealing: taking advantage of a special disadvantage one knows of.
   - Wagering is a transaction whose expected yield to the operator *is* the gambler's expected loss.
   - For a gambler whose play is out of her control, that loss flows from the very vulnerability.
3. The registration is the registrant's sincere first-person report of that vulnerability, made when she is best placed to make it. It gives the operator knowledge.
4. A later request to play, made in the state the report predicts, does not defeat the knowledge, because it is what the report predicted. Evidence from outside the predicted state can defeat it: elapsed time, independent report, behaviour.
5. So the operator must refuse. This holds because dealing with her would now be knowing exploitation, not because her earlier self commands it.

### 4.1 Test 1: notice without registration
- On the authority, consent and undertaking views, an operator with no registration and no undertaking has no duty.
- On the thesis, knowledge from *any* source triggers the duty.
- **Fact (VERIFIED).** UK LCCP SR 3.4.3 requires remote operators to "monitor customer activity to identify harm" and to "take appropriate action in a timely manner", including "ending the business relationship where necessary". No registration is needed.
- **Enforcement.** Platinum Gaming was fined £10m (Oct 2025) partly for "not interacting with a customer during a 23-day period in which they staked £73,000". In Touch Games was fined £6.1m (Jan 2023).
- This is Husak's Smith–Jones parity, made into a duty. It also refutes Long's p. 435 premise.

### 4.2 Test 2: the remedy
- On the thesis the wrong is the operator's gain. So the fitting remedy is gain-based: disgorgement to the person exploited. It is owed regardless of whether she would have lost the money elsewhere.
  - On the undertaking view, the remedy is loss-based tort damages, which fail on causation. *Calvert* failed on scope of duty; the trial judge's causation finding is that he would have ruined himself anyway (check the first-instance judgment).
  - On the paternalist view, there is no remedy theory.
  - On the authority view, the gambler is the party in breach of her own commitment.
- **Facts.**
  - The UK 888 settlement (2017) included "repayment of the £3.5million of deposits made by the self-excluded customers" (VERIFIED). That is gain-based and goes to the gamblers.
  - New Jersey (VERIFIED, N.J.S.A. 5:12-71.2–71.3): the self-excluded person "may not collect any winnings or recover any losses". Winnings are forfeited to the State. Even a licensee's ill-gotten gain is forfeited to the State, not returned to the gambler.
- **The thesis's verdict.** New Jersey has the direction of the remedy backwards. It penalises the person the duty protects and diverts the disgorged gain away from her. The rule makes sense only on a model where *she* broke *her* binding. This is a concrete institutional critique.

### 4.3 Test 3: the divergence, a minimal pair
**Labour analgesia.** A woman's birth plan says "no epidural, even if I ask". In labour she asks.
- Practice: NICE NG235 says she is "free to ... change [her] mind at any time, including during labour". ACOG says "maternal request is a sufficient medical indication".
- This has the same structure as self-exclusion: a precommitment against a predicted request made in a predicted state. Christensen-Szalanski 1984 found preference reversal in labour, to be checked. Yet the later request governs.
- The thesis explains why. The anaesthetist's fee is payment for a benefit to her, not a transfer that flows from her vulnerability, so there is no exploitation and no duty to refuse. With no authority in the earlier self, the later request governs.
- The authority view must invoke an ad hoc limit (Franklin-Hall p. 76, "arguably" one cannot bind oneself in some bodily matters). The paternalist view must judge analgesia good and gambling bad. Both testers flagged that judgement as a smuggled premise.

**Firearm do-not-sell lists** ("Donna's Law": WA, VA, UT, DE, CO).
- The dealer's gain is not the buyer's loss. So the thesis predicts these are *not* notice-based. If they bind, they bind as genuine Ulysses or waiver arrangements.
- **Fact.** They are framed as waivers of rights: Vars's form reads "I may be waiving some of my rights" (VERIFIED via priority-v2). They carry fixed revocation delays (WA 7+7 days; VA 21; UT 3 business days; DE 30; CO 30; VERIFIED).
- **Contrast.** Gambling exclusion persists by default beyond the chosen period. LCCP 3.5.4: "self-exclusion remains in place, for a minimum of 7 years, unless the customer takes positive action". GAMSTOP: "up to a further seven years" (VERIFIED). That fits knowledge persisting, not authority lapsing.
- **Caution.** The default persistence is also explicable as a libertarian-paternalist default (Husak p. 112). The memo does not rest weight on it.

### 4.4 Splitting the institution
- Real schemes also involve removal, trespass and confiscation. These are *interference* and do need authorisation.
- The thesis concedes this and makes a classification claim. The core of self-exclusion, refusal to deal, is notice. The enforcement add-ons are a separate, Ulysses-type layer, and they are the contested part in law: trespass and forfeiture.

## 5. Main objections to answer
1. **The paternalism smuggle.** Doesn't "impairment" require judging gambling bad?
   - Reply: the operator needs only the registrant's own report that her play is not under her control. The exploitation structure ("gain = loss") is non-evaluative.
   - Check: is this a standard switch? The memo must use one standard throughout: what the operator may or must do *given what it knows*. It must never use what is good for her.
2. **Kakavas.** The capacity to self-exclude told against special disadvantage.
   - Reply: the capacity to give notice is not the absence of disadvantage. What matters is whether notice was given and stood. Kakavas was readmitted after a 2005 psychologist's report (UNVERIFIED; check the judgment). That is a defeater from outside the predicted state, which the thesis allows.
3. **"Casinos gain from every gambler."** Exploitation requires the transfer to flow *from* the known vulnerability. Recreational losses are payment for entertainment.
4. **The statutory duty.** Isn't the duty created by the regulation, so paternalism one step removed?
   - Reply: the regulation codifies a pre-legal duty. Evidence: *Calvert* found an assumption of responsibility arising from knowledge, and unconscionable dealing pre-dates gambling regulation.
5. **Which theory of exploitation?** The thesis needs one on which standard terms can still be exploitative because of the counterparty's known impairment: Amadio and Kakavas in law; Sample 2003; Wood 1995. Wertheimer's fairness-of-terms account may *not* deliver this. That is a real risk.

## 6. Contribution and risks
- **Contribution.** A non-paternalist, non-authority account of the commonest real self-binding institution. It yields:
  - a classification: notice versus Ulysses;
  - a remedy critique: New Jersey;
  - a minimal pair: gambling versus analgesia.
- **Generalist hook.** "Can you bind your future self?" Answer: in the case everyone cites, you can't and don't need to; you can only tell others about her.
- **Risks.**
  - (i) It may read as Husak plus *Kakavas* applied. The defence is §4.2 and §4.3.
  - (ii) Exploitation theory may not support "standard terms + known impairment = exploitation".
  - (iii) The analgesia explanation may itself be evaluative ("benefit").
  - (iv) The predictability testers predicted the headline (third parties do the work) but not the mechanism.
