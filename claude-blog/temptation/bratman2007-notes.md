# Reading note: Bratman, "Temptation Revisited" (2007) [READ in full]

**Citation.** Bratman, M. E. (2007). "Temptation Revisited." In *Structures of Agency: Essays*, ch. 12, pp. 256–282. Oxford University Press. doi:10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195187717.003.0012.

**Source.** PDF supplied by BW (texts/bratman2007-temptation-revisited.pdf; extracted text in bratman2007-clean.txt). Page numbers are OUP's in-text "p. NNN" markers; each marks where that page *begins*.

## What the essay is about

The essay asks when willpower against temptation is rational. It does not try to say what temptation *is*. The question it asks is whether it can be "instrumentally rational in the thick sense" to follow through with a prior one-glass policy against a present ranking that favours a second glass (pp. 258–261).

It answers with two strategies:
1. **Agential authority.** A *singular*, just-this-once valuing has only an "attenuated claim" to say where the agent stands. A general action policy has the role of organising thought and action over time (pp. 272–274).
2. **Intention stability.** There is a pragmatically grounded default in favour of a prior policy. That default is normally overridden by a strictly contrary present ranking, unless anticipated regret undermines the ranking's authority (pp. 275–279).

## Claims relevant to T7 (temptation = an excluded option going live against a standing decision; desire neither necessary nor sufficient)

1. **Temptation, in the cases Bratman targets, is more than a felt pull.**
   - "not merely a temporary, felt motivational pull" (p. 257).
   - It "seems to involve a kind of evaluation" (p. 257).
   - Treating it as mere felt inclination would be "a fairly rigid form of philosophical legislation" (p. 258).
   - He restricts this to "some such cases" (p. 258). He does not claim it holds of temptation in general.
2. **The tempting attitude is a singular valuing.**
   - It is "a singular commitment to give relatively more justifying weight on this occasion to a second glass" (p. 272).
   - Such commitments are "not merely felt inclinations; but they also are not general policies" (p. 272).
   - Valuing involves wanting: he quotes Watson, "to value is also to want" (p. 269).
   - So Bratman's tempted agent *wants* the second glass. He does not drop desire.
3. **The structure of the conflict.** It is "between an action-focused general policy and a reasoning-focused singular intention" (p. 273). This is the nearest he comes to T7's "standing decision". Bratman's "policy" plays the role of T7's exclusion, but temptation is still individuated by the singular *valuing*, not by the option's status relative to the policy.
4. **Pre-emption of deliberation (note 39).**
   - When you stick with the policy, it "displaces or preempts" deliberation. "You do not engage in the kind of deliberation that is the concern of that intention."
   - This is close to T7's idea that a standing exclusion keeps an option out of live deliberation. Bratman does not use it to define temptation; he uses it to describe what resisting looks like.
   - **The paper should cite this note as the nearest structural ancestor of "exclusion".**
5. **Non-reconsideration and reconsideration.**
   - "Processes of nonreconsideration play a central role in our temporally extended agency" (p. 275).
   - Note 45 reports Holton: willpower is "refusing to reconsider one's resolutions" (Holton 2003, p. 49).
   - Bratman adds that willpower is sometimes exercised *after* reconsideration has begun.
   - This bears on T7's over-generation problem: new reasons can also reopen options.

## Verdict for priority

Bratman 2007 does **not** anticipate T7:
- It gives no account of what temptation is.
- It does not individuate temptation by an excluded option going live.
- It does not claim desire is unnecessary (his tempting attitude is a valuing, which involves wanting) or insufficient.
- It uses no desire-free or pull-free cases.

Tester X's suspicion that it "individuates by admissibility" is **not borne out**.

## Uses for the paper

- **Background framework for "exclusion" without an explicit decision.** Policies and non-reconsideration (pp. 263–264, 275) together with pre-emption of deliberation (note 39) address the priority search's "never decided" problem (item 4).
- **A foil, of a sort.** Bratman's view is that the interesting temptations involve evaluation, not mere pull. T7 says a pull without desire or evaluation can still be temptation (the balcony case). The paper must say whether the balcony pull is Bratman's "mere felt pull", and why that still counts as temptation.
- **Avoid misattribution.** Do not say Bratman defines temptation as a judgement shift; that is Holton. Bratman's term is a singular, just-this-once *valuing*.
