# Reading note: Holton, *Willing, Wanting, Waiting* (OUP 2009), chs. 4–6

**Read status.**
- [READ in full] ch. 5, "Temptation", pp. 97–111.
- [PART] ch. 4, "Weakness of Will", pp. 70–96, and ch. 6, "Strength of Will", pp. 112–136. In these I read the passages on resolutions, reconsideration and temptation.

**Source.** PDFs supplied by BW; text in texts/holton2009-*-clean.txt. Page numbers come from OUP's "p. NNN" markers.

## Claims that bear on T7

**1. Resolutions defeat *contrary inclinations*, not only desires.** Ch. 4, p. 77:
- Resolutions overcome "the desires or beliefs that the agent fears they will form".
- The defeated states also include "fear, panic, lethargy—that are neither beliefs nor desires".
- Hence "let us talk of resolutions being contrary inclination defeating".
- Weakness of will is the unreasonable revision of a resolution "in response to the pressure of those very inclinations" (p. 78).

**2. Resolutions can be implicit. Temptation extends beyond desire.** Ch. 6, p. 135:
- "Not all resolutions will be explicit resolutions. Some will be more like general policies not to be moved in certain ways ... some could even be innate."
- "Not all temptations will be what we would normally think of as temptations. Fear can knock one from a resolution; so can disgust. Again the process is much as before."

So Holton already generalises the structure of temptation, a pressure against a standing commitment resisted by non-reconsideration, to inclinations that are not desires. He also lets the commitment be implicit. That covers T7's "desire is not necessary" (in a generalising remark) and the "never decided" problem (the balcony agent has an implicit policy). He marks such cases as not temptations "as we would normally think of" them. Here T7 parts company: T7 says they *are* temptations.

**3. Holton's notion of desire is an *urge*.** Ch. 5, p. 102:
- What makes the children yield "is desire, in one sense of that rather broad term".
- He rejects Scanlon's "directed-attention" sense (considerations presented "in a favorable light") as insufficient: "What is missing ... is the idea that desire pulls me to a course of action: that I have an urge".
- "Desire in the sense we are after is a state that preoccupies an agent's attention with an urge to perform a certain action."

**Consequence for T7's double dissociation. This is the decisive problem.**
- Take desire to be Holton's urge. Then the balcony "call of the void" (a pull or urge to jump) *has* desire. The vegetarian who finds bacon appealing but feels no pull *lacks* it.
- So temptation and desire then line up exactly, and the double dissociation separates temptation only from Scanlon-style "favourable light" desire. Holton has already argued that this is not the relevant notion.
- T7's headline, "desire is neither necessary nor sufficient", is therefore either verbal (it depends on which "desire") or false on the strongest rival notion.
- To survive, T7 would need a case of temptation with **no urge and no favourable light**, or a case of an **urge against a commitment with no temptation**. Neither scout case is that.

**4. Judgement shift.** Ch. 5:
- Temptation "frequently works not simply by overcoming one's better judgement, but by corrupting one's judgement", and this is an empirical claim, "not ... an essential claim" (p. 97).
- If an option is "not attractive enough to be a real temptation", there is no shift (p. 99, Karniol & Miller).
- Addiction decouples judgement from wanting (pp. 98, 104–109).

**5. Reconsideration, rehearsal, and the never-tempted.** Ch. 6:
- "To fully reconsider a resolution is to open oneself to the possibility of revising it ... that is to withdraw one's current commitment to it" (p. 121).
- "When temptation is great, its force will quickly turn a reconsideration into a revision" (p. 122). Temptation and reconsideration are distinct here.
- "Rehearsal" is awareness of the resolution without suspending it (p. 123).
- The strong-willed person "who, as we might say, is never really tempted by the alternatives" (p. 122). This is near T7's vegetarian, and near McDowell's silencing.

**6. Willpower is refusing to reconsider** (p. 121). The same mechanism resists ordinary temptation, addictive temptation, fear and disgust (p. 135).

## Verdict for priority

Holton 2009 **substantially anticipates** T7:
- "Desire is not necessary" is anticipated in its generalised form. Resolutions defeat fear, panic, lethargy and disgust as well as desire (pp. 77, 135), and commitments can be implicit (p. 135).
- What is new in T7 is only the insistence that those cases are temptation proper, and the "liveness" individuation.
- Holton's urge notion of desire (p. 102) absorbs the balcony case, so the double dissociation does not establish "neither necessary nor sufficient" against him.
- "Not sufficient" (the vegetarian) is close to Holton's "never really tempted" (p. 122) and to McDowell-style silencing.

**What would be left:** the claim that *liveness*, not inclination, individuates temptation. Arguing for it would need new decisive cases: an urge against a commitment without temptation, or temptation without any urge. That rests on contested intuitions about uncommon cases.
