# Priority check: T9a (grief's object is the loss as fresh) vs T9b (sorrow for the dead expires at the counterfactual death date)

## 1. Verdicts

**T9a: PARTLY ANTICIPATED, and the new part is thin.**
- **Already in print:**
  - The mechanism, grief as a response to a loss "not yet absorbed": Ratcliffe, Richardson & Millar (JAPA 2023) and Na'aman (2021).
  - The phrase "reasons for grief expire": Marušić (2018, p. 16).
  - The changing-object move: Nussbaum (2001, p. 82, as cited by Moller); Cholbi (2022).
  - The surprise/novelty analogy, which is raised and rejected in print by Moller (2025, p. 503).
- **New:**
  - The Chrysippean "fresh opinion" as a resource in the analytic fittingness debate. None of the papers I read cites Graver, Sorabji or the Stoic definition.
  - The claim that freshness is a tensed feature of the *loss*, not of the subject's processing.
  - The Ann/Beth late-news pair: not found, but Marušić 2018/2022 were unreadable and his Clive Wearing case is a close relative.
- **Open check:** Nussbaum, *Upheavals of Thought* (2001), pp. 79–85, which Moller cites (p. 82). From memory (unverified), it explains grief's abatement by a change of *tense* in the eudaimonistic judgement, possibly citing Chrysippus. If so, T9a's "tensed object" is ANTICIPATED.

**T9b: CLEAR on its central inference (provisional).**
- **Not found anywhere checked:** the claim that sorrow on the dead person's behalf stops being fitting at their counterfactual death date, so that subsequentist timing fixes when one component of grief expires. Checked: Atkins 2024 (abstract and full reference list, with no timing literature), Ratcliffe et al., Moller 2025, Wilkinson 2023, Timmerman 2016 and his *TPM* piece, Luper's NDPR review of Bradley (no link to grief or pity), and the Howard, Kim & Kim and Mason abstracts.
- **Already in print:**
  - The negative framing, that grief's object is not wholly an unchanging past. Ratcliffe et al. make it a "loss of life possibilities", and Kim & Kim attack the unchangeable-past premise. That framing is not the novelty.
  - The two components of grief: Ratcliffe et al. and Atkins.
- **Decisive unread items:** Bradley 2009, ch. 3 (replies to objections to subsequentism); Marušić 2022 (index: "sorry for", "deprivation", "pity"); Cholbi 2022, ch. 2 (whether grief is for the deceased's sake); Fan 2026 (online 17 Oct 2026).

## 2. Closest prior work

**For T9a**
- **Ratcliffe, Richardson & Millar, "On the Appropriateness of Grief to Its Object", *JAPA* 9(2) (2023): 318–334 (online 2022).** [READ]
  - Grief is "a gradual process of 'sinking in', where comprehension, emotional response, and adaptation are inextricable" (§1 "Sinking In").
  - Hence "it is not surprising or puzzling that grief changes over time" (§2, end).
  - This is T9a's "not yet absorbed", stated as a process view.
- **Moller, "The Rationality of Emotions Across Time", *Dialogue* 64 (2025): 493–510.** [READ, §§4–5]
  - He glosses the dynamic view as fittingness "depending on whether or not someone has absorbed their loss" (p. 502).
  - On surprise and amusement: "clearly these are poor analogies for persistent change" (p. 503).
  - He calls the changing-object view (Nussbaum 2001, p. 82) "solipsistic" (p. 502).
- **Na'aman, "The Rationality of Emotional Change: Toward a Process View", *Noûs* 55(2) (2021): 245–269.** [VIA Moller]
  - "some emotions [...] are rationally self-consuming" (p. 251).
- **Marušić, "Do Reasons Expire? An Essay on Grief", *Phil Imprint* 18(25) (2018): 1–21.** [ABSTRACT; VIA Moller, VIA Ratcliffe et al.]
  - "reasons for grief expire" (p. 16). His overall verdict is "double vision": the puzzle has no solution. The primary object is "someone's being dead".
- **Howard, "Forever Fitting Feelings", *PPR* 107(1) (2023): 80–98.** [ABSTRACT]
  - "our fitting feelings remain fitting forever, until the day that we die."

**For T9b**
- **Atkins, "Empathy for the Dead", *Phil Imprint* 24 (2024).** [ABSTRACT + reference list]
  - "profound grief stems largely from our empathy for the dead."
  - Her frame is Epicurean imagining oneself in the dead person's place, plus Adam Smith. Nothing on timing.
- **Timmerman, "Your Death Might Be the Worst Thing Ever to Happen to You (But Maybe You Shouldn't Care)", *CJP* 46(1) (2016): 18–37.** [ABSTRACT]
  - Lament for one's own death is partly indexed to "reasonable expectations about when she will die."
  - He also argues that lament and badness come apart, against the "Nothing Bad, Nothing to Lament" assumption.
  - Nearest neighbour: a death-date-indexed attitude, but first-personal and about magnitude, not expiry.
- **Bradley, "When Is Death Bad for the One Who Dies?", *Noûs* 38 (2004); *Well-Being and Death* (OUP 2009).** [MEM; Luper review PART]
  - Luper's rendering: "bad deaths are bad for their victims posthumously." Nothing on grief, as reported.
- **Kim & Kim, "Is Grief Fitting Forever?", *Analysis* 86(1) (2026; online Nov 2025): 51–59.** [ABSTRACT]
  - "the hardliners have failed to show that once fitting, grief is fitting forever." Purely negative.
- **Mason, "Emotional Fittingness over Time", *Ethics* 136(3) (2026): 569–594.** [ABSTRACT, partial]
  - Fittingness changes as "reasons for being motivated to fulfill the goals of emotions change."
  - T9b must say whether the lapse of the dead person's interests is a Mason-style change in grief's goal.

## 3. Which is the better paper

**T9b**, on condition that the decisive reads in §1 come back clean.

**Why not T9a.**
- Its distinctive content has nearly collapsed into the dynamic/process view (Na'aman; Ratcliffe et al.). Moller's p. 502 gloss ("absorbed their loss") is T9a's own gloss.
- Its key analogy (surprise) is already rebutted in print.
- Its own cases favour the subject-side reading. Ann/Beth varies time since *learning*, which is the process variable; Clive Wearing is about absorption failing; foreseen losses (praemeditatio) are pre-absorption.
- What remains is Stoic provenance plus a relabelled object: "commentary" and "predictable" risks (tester Y predicted it at 35%).

**Why T9b.**
- It was the only grief thesis tester X did not predict.
- It links two literatures not yet linked, on present evidence: the timing of death's badness and emotional fittingness over time.
- It pays off for both: an emotion-based argument for subsequentism, and a reason why lifelong grief for a child fits while lifelong acute grief for a 95-year-old seems odd.

**The twins pair must go.** Tester Y is right. "He'd have died within a year anyway" consoles from day one, so the pair shows *magnitude* of deprivation, which eternalism also explains, not a *date*. A pair that isolates the date needs two things:

1. **A magnitude-matched synchronic pair: the Ada/Bea pair.**
   - Ada and Bea, both 70 and equally dear, die in one accident. Ada would have had one more good year; Bea three years at a third of that quality. Total deprivation is equal.
   - Two years on, eternalism says sorrow on their behalf fits equally. T9b says present-tensed sorrow ("what she is missing") fits Bea only; for Ada only past-tensed regret fits.
2. **A living-subject parity case.**
   - A friend is wrongly imprisoned for five years. Sorrow for what she is missing fits during the sentence. On release it gives way to "thank goodness that's over", not to permanent sorrow.
   - This shows on-behalf sorrow is present-tensed; the dead case follows by parity. It is the direct answer to the eternalist.
3. **Supporting data:** the anniversary and milestone phenomenology ("she'd have been 50 today", "she's missing the wedding"). Sorrow on another's behalf attaches to dates in *their* counterfactual timeline.

**Indeterminate death dates.** Use expectation-weighted subsequentism: sorrow on behalf fades as the counterfactual survival curve falls. That *predicts* the child versus 95-year-old asymmetry.

## 4. Are the testers' objections fatal?

**T9a**
- **"A Stoic version of Na'aman's process view":** close to fatal for distinctiveness (agreeing with tester X). The mechanism and its gloss are in print, and freshness belongs to the loss only if indexed to the time of *learning*, which is a fact about the subject.
- **Moller's "you wrote 'recent' into the object":** not fatal to truth, but in print (2025, p. 503). The paper would start by answering a published rebuttal of its central analogy.

**T9b**
- **"Bradley or Atkins already make the inference":**
  - Atkins: no. Her thesis and reference list show no engagement with timing.
  - Bradley: not in the review or any search, but his text is unread. **Not fatal on present evidence**; this is the decisive remaining check.
- **The eternalist reply ("only the consolation's aptness changes"):** serious, and answering it is the paper's main work. The answer is the parity argument from sympathy for the living (§3).
  - Timmerman 2016 warns that attitudes need not track badness. So frame the fitting-maker as "the person is now missing goods", not "death is now bad for them".
  - **Not fatal.**
- **"It explains only one part of grief":** not fatal if stated as "one component of grief has a dated expiry, indexed to the dead person", not as a theory of diminishing grief. The two-component structure is in print (Ratcliffe et al.; Atkins), so it is not ad hoc.
- **"The twins pair shows no expiry":** fatal to the pair, not to the thesis. Replace it as in §3.
