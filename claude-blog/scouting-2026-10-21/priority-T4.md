# Priority check: T4, the vigilante state (21 Oct 2026)

Tags: [READ] read in full; [PART] read in part or grepped for key passages; [ABSTRACT] abstract or catalogue page only; [MEM] from memory, not checked.

## 1. Verdict: PARTLY ANTICIPATED

**What is already in print**
- **The verdict.** Compensating people whose convictions are quashed, without requiring proof of innocence, is argued by Hoyle & Tilt, Roach (2024), the five Nealon dissenters, Greenwood (2024), and probably Quirk & Requa (2012).
- **The core of the ground.** Lady Hale in *Adams* [2011] UKSC 18 at [116] says that without proof of guilt the state is not "entitled to punish", and that compensation follows "irrespective of whether he is in fact innocent". This is an entitlement ground, not an innocence ground. It is the single closest source.
- **The credit lever, in part.** *Pearce* itself describes credit on reconviction as returning the years, and notes the asymmetry with acquittal. Roach (2024) notes the same inequity for pretrial detention.
- **The anti-Wellman question.** Whether the guilty are wronged by punishment without proper procedure is already disputed: Wellman (2014) says no; Enoch (2018) and N.P. Adams (2019) reply.

**What I did not find anywhere**
- (a) Compensation is owed even to the *known* guilty, unless a fresh conviction claims the time. Hale ties entitlement to whether guilt can be proved; T4 ties title to a valid conviction.
- (b) The credit-on-reconviction rule used, in the quashed-conviction setting, as the state's concession that the time needs a title.
- (c) The minimal pair: a guilty man who can be retried versus one who cannot.
- (d) Wrongful-conviction compensation or credit practice used as a counterexample to Wellman's forfeiture theory, or to his denial of procedural rights.

## 2. Closest prior works

1. **Lady Hale, *R (Adams) v SSJ* [2011] UKSC 18 at [116]** [PART: as quoted in Hoyle & Tilt, AAM p. 20, and Roach p. 738].
   - "Only then is the state entitled to punish him"; he is not guilty "irrespective of whether he is in fact innocent". Compensation is owed if it is "conclusively shown" the state was not entitled.
   - She sided with compensating categories 1 and 2 [MEM: the 5–4 line-up].
   - Gap: her entitlement depends on whether guilt can be proved, not on a valid conviction. She does not reach unretried category 3 cases, category 4 (abuse of process), or the known guilty.

2. **Hoyle & Tilt, "Not innocent enough: State compensation for miscarriages of justice in England and Wales"** [PART: ORA author's accepted manuscript; published venue not verified].
   - They say s.133 "denies protection to those who have been let down by the State" (p. 1).
   - But they accept that "simply being exonerated cannot give rise to an automatic right to compensation" (p. 9). They quote Hale approvingly (p. 20). Not T4.

3. **Kent Roach, "International and Comparative Law on Compensating Miscarriages of Justice: From Proven Innocence to Wrongful Detention", *Colum. J. Transnat'l L.* 62(3) (2024)** [PART: PDF grepped, key sections read].
   - He argues for compensating wrongful detention as well as proven innocence, framed as "tragic choices" about scarce resources.
   - Credit asymmetry: "those found guilty receive some remedy for unwarranted pre-trial detention" (p. 780; cf. p. 775).
   - His ground is "the benefit of reasonable doubts about their guilt" (p. 809), i.e. possible innocence, not lack of title. He calls Nealon "problematic" (p. 809).

4. ***North Carolina v Pearce*, 395 U.S. 711, 718–19 (1969)** [PART: passage verified].
   - Credit on reconviction is constitutionally required. If the defendant is reconvicted, "those years can and must be returned". If he is acquitted, "there is no way the years ... can be returned".
   - So credit is framed as returning the time, not as mitigation. This helps T4, but "credit is compensation-in-kind" is then a reading of *Pearce*, not a new observation.
   - Criminal Appeal Act 1968 Sch 2 remains [MEM, unverified].

5. **Wellman, "Procedural Rights", *Legal Theory* 20 (2014) 286** [ABSTRACT, via Enoch's abstract]. **Enoch, "In Defense of Procedural Rights (or Anyway, Procedural Duties)", *Legal Theory* 24 (2018) 40–49** [ABSTRACT]. **N.P. Adams, "Grounding Procedural Rights", *Legal Theory* 25 (2019) 3–25** [ABSTRACT].
   - Wellman: with qualifications, "there are no procedural rights"; a guilty person convicted by lottery is not wronged.
   - Enoch defends procedural rights, or at least duties. Adams grounds them in a right against unreasonable risk of punishment. (No verbatim quotes obtained.)
   - This is the debate the anti-Wellman move enters. As far as the abstracts show, none discusses compensation or wrongful-conviction practice.

6. **Dolinko, NDPR review of Wellman, *Rights Forfeiture and Punishment* (2017)** [READ: the review]. The theory permits vigilante punishment and is "neutral ... between anarchism and statism". Vigilantism is ruled out only by separate legitimacy principles.

7. **Greenwood, Strasbourg Observers, 20 Aug 2024** [READ]. The majority made Article 6(2) "a primarily semantic exercise". Entirely presumption-of-innocence framing; no title argument.

8. **Ferzan, "Why Credit Time Served?" (draft, NYU colloquium)** [PART: grepped].
The "conversion" of non-punitive confinement "into credit for punishment is curious" (p. 1). The draft covers pretrial detention only; a search for vacated/quashed/exoneration/*Pearce* found nothing.

## 3. What a paper must add or answer to be new

1. **Separate T4 from Hale explicitly.** The test case: a guilty defendant whose conviction is quashed for abuse of process or fabricated evidence and who cannot be retried. Hale's provability test arguably denies him compensation (it is not "conclusively shown" the state could not have proved guilt). T4 compensates him. Without this, a referee will say "this is Hale [116]".
2. **Own the *Pearce* reading.** Cite *Pearce*'s "returned" language as the state conceding the point. The new step is to apply it to the non-retried quashed case, and to argue that "mitigation, not title" (Tester Y's alternative) cannot explain why credit must be *full*.
3. **Enter the Wellman–Enoch–Adams debate directly.** Show that compensation practice is evidence that the guilty can be wronged by unlicensed hardship. Then say what Wellman's best reply is: procedural duties without rights, or that the wrong is to the public, not to the prisoner.
4. **Bite the "murderer gets a million" bullet in the main text.** Also address Roach's scarce-resources framing.

## 4. Are the testers' objections fatal?

**"The detention was lawful when imposed."**
- Fatal to the word "vigilante" as a description of the original detention; not fatal to the thesis.
- In English law a quashed conviction does not retroactively make the custody false imprisonment [MEM]. So the state was not a vigilante at the time.
- Recast the analogy as remedial and retroactive: once the conviction is gone, the state has no better title than the vigilante to *keep the time on its books*.
- Pair this with a Thomson-style infringement (permissible act, compensation still owed). Possibly add *R v Governor of Brockhill Prison, ex p Evans (No 2)* [2001] 2 AC 19, where detention in good faith but without legal authority was still compensable [MEM, verify].
- As currently framed, the objection is serious.

**"The verdict is predictable; is the ground new?"**
- The verdict is predictable, and it is in print.
- The ground is half-anticipated by Hale [116]. What remains new is the conviction-based title (versus provability), the known-guilty extension, the credit argument applied to quashed convictions, and the Wellman counterexample.
- This is enough for a paper only if item 3.1 is done up front. Otherwise the originality score will suffer.
