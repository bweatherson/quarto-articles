# Pretest: candidate theses (DO NOT OPEN until Phase 1 is saved)

Each thesis answers the question with the same number in pretest-questions.md. The full scout write-ups are in the
scout-*.md files; you may read them in Phase 2.

- **T1 (Q1)** A proof differs from overwhelming probabilistic evidence because a proof can mislead only if
  something malfunctions; lottery evidence lacks that property, which is why we cannot know lottery outcomes.
  Certainty, reliability, safety and transferability do not mark the difference. (Scout A1)
- **T2 (Q2)** Censoring dissent leaves lay belief in a consensus justified but can stop it being knowledge: a
  consensus maintained by a filter keyed to the consensus itself is a fake-barn environment, whatever the
  quality of the objections removed. (Scout A2)
- **T3 (Q3)** Self-binding arrangements do not bind the self at all. A self-exclusion is testimony about oneself
  addressed to a third party, and its whole force is the force of that party's independent duty not to exploit a
  vulnerability it now knows about. This predicts:
  - the exit asymmetry;
  - refund rather than forfeiture;
  - the divergence in the case of an advance-refused epidural.

  (Scout B1)
- **T4 (Q4)** Factual guilt gives the state no title to hardship imposed without a valid conviction. Once the
  conviction is quashed, the state stands to the prisoner as a vigilante does and owes compensation for the
  whole term regardless of guilt, unless a fresh conviction reaches back and claims the time. The credit-on-
  reconviction rule already concedes this. This is a counterexample to Wellman's forfeiture theory. (Scout B2)
- **T5 (Q5)** To stipulate that an object is the standard of a unit is to give it defeasible calibration
  authority, not to fix what the unit-word refers to. So "S is one metre long" is never known a priori, not even
  by those who made the stipulation; the kilogram's demotion shows this. (Scout C1)
- **T6 (Q6)** An organised group survives a split as whichever faction its own pre-split procedures authorise to
  continue it, whatever that faction's size, doctrine or founder. When the split is a dispute about those
  procedures, neither faction is the group. (Scout C2)
- **T7 (Q7)** To be tempted is for an option you have excluded to become live again against your standing
  decision. Desire is one way of making it live, and is neither necessary nor sufficient. The double dissociation
  is:
  - the "call of the void" on a balcony: a pull without desire;
  - a vegetarian smelling bacon: an appeal without pull.

  (Scout D1)
- **T8 (Q8)** Over-persistence is characteristically a deliberative failure, not a motivational one. A plan
  filters what registers as bearing on it, so the exit never becomes a candidate. It is cured by triggers that
  do not depend on the agent noticing, not by willpower. (Scout D2)
- **T9a (Q9)** The reasons for grief expire because grief's object is the loss *as fresh*: a change in one's world
  not yet absorbed. Freshness is a tensed feature of the loss itself, so grief fades for the same reason relief
  and surprise fade (after Chrysippus's "fresh opinion"). (Scout E1)
- **T9b (Q9)** Grief's other-regarding part, sorrow for what the dead person is missing, is fitting only until the
  date on which they would otherwise have died. So the shared premise that grief's object lies wholly in an
  unchanging past is false. (Scout F1)
- **T10 (Q10)** Judgements of a person's character fall under justice, not the epistemology of belief. On
  inconclusive evidence one is required to believe well of the person while remaining free to take precautions,
  because the cost of error falls on the person judged. The norm is a directional presumption, not moral
  encroachment and not suspension (after Aquinas, ST II-II q.60). (Scout E2)
- **T11 (Q11)** "Avoid risk for others" is false for *standing* proxies. Respect for another's risk attitude
  attaches to the whole relationship, and for every reasonable attitude this requires near-risk-neutral choice
  in each small, independent decision. (Scout F2)
