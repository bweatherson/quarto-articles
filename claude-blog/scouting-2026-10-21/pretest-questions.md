# Pretest: questions only (no theses)

Eleven philosophical questions follow. For each, a competent philosopher who knows the current literature is
asked: what papers would people naturally write in answer, and what theses would those papers defend?

**Q1. Proof and probability.**
- Why don't we count overwhelming probabilistic evidence as proof, and why can't we know that a lottery ticket lost?
- A case from mathematical practice: in the search for large Mersenne primes, a "probable prime" test result (error probability astronomically small) is officially "not final". A prime is certified by deterministic Lucas–Lehmer runs, even though individual runs on volunteer hardware go wrong roughly 1–2% of the time and are double-checked.
- What explains the difference?

**Q2. Censored consensus.** Does suppressing even absurd objections to an expert consensus damage the public's epistemic standing? If so, how, given that what is suppressed is bad?

**Q3. Self-binding.**
- Can you bind your future self?
- Gambling self-exclusion schemes (e.g. the UK's GAMSTOP, with over half a million registrants) legally require operators to refuse a registrant, even when the later self is competent and insistent.
- What, if anything, makes the earlier request authoritative over the later one?

**Q4. Quashed convictions.**
- When a conviction is quashed as unsafe, does the prisoner's possible guilt give the state any claim to the years it took?
- UK law (Criminal Justice Act 1988 s.133(1ZA), as amended in 2014) refuses compensation unless the person shows beyond reasonable doubt that they did not commit the offence.
- The European Court of Human Rights' Grand Chamber upheld such refusals in 2024 (Nealon and Hallam v UK).

**Q5. Stipulated standards.**
- Can you come to know a contingent fact about the world just by laying down a definition? Kripke's standard metre is the textbook "yes".
- In 2018–19 the international prototype kilogram was demoted. Its mass is now officially "1 kg within a relative standard uncertainty of 10⁻⁸", to be determined experimentally.

**Q6. Schisms.** When a church or a party splits, which faction is the original group, and can the smaller faction be it?

**Q7. Temptation.** What makes something a temptation? Is it just wanting something more than you think you should?

**Q8. Over-persistence.** Why do capable people go on past the point where they should stop: the climber past the turnaround time, the pilot pressing on with the approach? Is that a failure of will?

**Q9. Diminishing grief.** Grief fades, and that seems all right. But the loss does not fade, and the death never stops having happened. Is grief fitting forever? If not, what makes diminishing grief fitting rather than a lapse?

**Q10. Judging character.** When the evidence about someone's character is inconclusive, what should you believe about them? Can what you believe, or decline to believe, wrong them?

**Q11. Risk for others.** When you take risks for someone whose attitude to risk you don't know, should you play safe? Does it matter whether you choose for them once or repeatedly, as a guardian, trustee or doctor managing a chronic illness does?
