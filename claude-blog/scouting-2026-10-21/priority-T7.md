21 Oct 2026. About 32 search and fetch calls. PhilPapers search and record pages mostly failed. Several OUP and CUP items are paywalled.

## 1. Verdict: PARTLY ANTICIPATED

**Already in print**
- *A "live option" definition.* Hill (2025) defines temptation broadly as considering a not-best act "as a live option".
- *A desire-free sense.* Hill also keeps an older, "external" sense of temptation with no desire condition.
- *Theology.* Analytic theology on Christ's temptations has long debated whether temptation needs desire.
- *Holton.* Holton (2009; 2017) treats temptation as more than desire. It works through judgement shift and pressure to reconsider resolutions.
- *Silencing.* "Attracted but not tempted" is near McDowell-style silencing.

**Not found anywhere**
- No source individuates temptation by an option's status relative to a standing decision or exclusion.
- No source argues, in philosophy of action, that desire is neither necessary nor sufficient.
- No source uses the balcony/vegetarian double dissociation.
- No source treats the call of the void as temptation. The psychology of the high place phenomenon has no philosophical uptake that I found.
- No source predicts that liveness-removers work where desire-reduction does not.

So the core thesis looks new. The frame and the desire-free possibility are not.

## 2. Closest prior works

**Hill, Jonathan (2025). "Resisting Desire: Models of Christ's Temptations." *AGATHEOS: European Journal for Philosophy of Religion* 2(1): 4–25. [PART]. This is the closest.**
- **General definition.** Temptation occurs when a person "considers, as a live option, the prospect of performing an action" believed not best (p. 5).
- **Temptation^e (external).** The clause is "factors external to p are causing p to focus in particular on t" (p. 5). There is no desire clause.
- **Temptation^i (internal).** This sense requires that p "feels a desire to perform t", plus motivational conflict (p. 6).
- **Footnote 10 (p. 7).** Lyons 1994, Crisp 2007b, Murray & Rea 2008, McKinley 2009 and Pawl 2019 make desire essential.
- **What it lacks.** There is no standing decision, exclusion or reopening. There is no Bratman, Holton, Mele or Schwenkler. There is no claim that desire is insufficient.
- **Risk.** A referee may say T7's desire-free temptation is just Hill's external sense. T7 must show the balcony case is internal and is not mere focusing.

**Holton, Richard (2017). "We Don't Torture: Moral Resolutions, Temptation, and the Doctrine of Double Effect." *Journal of the British Academy* 5: 309–329. [PART]**
- **Judgement shift.** Under temptation's lure, "our view of what is best will often shift" (p. 310).
- **Desire.** Proximity-driven desire is "only part of what is going on" (p. 312).
- **Reconsideration.** Resolutions should not be opened "up to reconsideration when the temptation is upon us" (p. 312).
- **Relation to T7.** This is near T7's reopening and "I don't" themes, but desire is never declared unnecessary.
- **Background [MEMORY].** *Willing, Wanting, Waiting* (2009), chs. 5–6, has the same view.

**Schwenkler, John (2024). "How Temptation Works." *JESP* 27(3): 443–468. [PART; the scout READ it]**
- **Desire condition.** Succumbing is violating a decision "out of the desire to do what she knows to be contrary to it" (p. 446).
- **Temptation to violation.** There is temptation that does not involve "any inclination to change one's mind" (p. 445). He calls it "temptation to violation" (p. 448).
- **Relation to T7.** He is the named opponent on desire. His violation-without-revision cases may also count against reading T7 as reopening.

**Hughes, P. M. (1999). "Temptation and the Manipulation of Desire." *Journal of Value Inquiry* 33: 371–379. [PART]**
- Hughes reports J. P. Day ("Temptation", *APQ* 30, 1993): "temptation requires that the subject desire" what he thinks wrong (p. 372).
- Hughes agrees (pp. 373, 378). This is a classic statement of the desire condition.

**Desire-centred, abstracts only**

- Liberman (2016), "Reconsidering Resolutions", *JESP* [ABSTRACT]. Resolutions need a second-order desire.

## 3. What a paper must add or answer to be new

1. **Engage Hill and the theology literature.** Then claim three things:
   - one unified notion, individuated by exclusion against a standing decision, not by "believed not best";
   - desire is insufficient, which no one argues;
   - desire-free cases can be internal and agential.
2. **Give a non-question-begging mark of temptation.** The candidate is a pull against a standing exclusion that is:
   - resisted by acting, not reasoning;
   - intensified by asking "should I?";
   - relieved by removing liveness.
3. **Reconcile with Schwenkler's violation without revision.** On the reconciliation, liveness is not reopening the decision.
4. **Give a notion of exclusion that needs no explicit decision.** The balcony agent never decided not to jump. Use Bratman's background framework, or narrow the thesis.
5. **Block over-generation.** New reasons also reopen options. Temptation is reopening not mediated by what the agent takes as reasons. Holton's split between reasonable reconsideration and judgement shift is the template.
6. **Give a prediction that separates T7 from desire and alief accounts.** For example, liveness-removers help on balcony-type pulls where desire-reduction does not.
7. **Say what the vegetarian adds beyond silencing.** Cite McDowell, and Seidman 2005, "Two Sides of Silencing" [MEMORY].

## 4. Are the testers' objections fatal?

None is fatal. Two are serious.
- **"Merely verbal" (X, Y).** Serious. "Urge", "alief" and "intrusive thought" are ready labels, and Stocker/Velleman's "desiring the bad" is available to the opponent. It is answerable only through item 6.
- **Over-generation (Y).** Fixable (item 5). As Y says, "the fix is the paper".
- **"Never decided" (X).** Fixable (item 4). It costs some of the slogan.
- **Bratman 2007 already individuates by admissibility (X).** Unsupported by anything I could check, and my memory is that the essay concerns preference reversal and no-regret. It must still be read.
- **Schwenkler and Holton as neighbours (Y).** Confirmed as neighbours, not pre-emptions. Both keep desire.
- **New risk: Hill 2025.** It is not raised by the testers. It must be cited and distinguished.
