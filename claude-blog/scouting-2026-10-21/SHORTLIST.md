## 1. Grief runs on two clocks (scout F1; pretest T9b)
**Thesis.** One part of grief is sorrow for what the dead person is missing. That part is fitting only until the date on which they would otherwise have died. So the premise shared by both sides of the diminishing-grief debate, that grief's object lies wholly in an unchanging past, is false.
- **Pretest.** This is the only thesis of twelve that a tester did not predict (X). Tester Y ranked the rival version, T9a, higher.
- **Priority.** CLEAR, provisionally; nothing found makes the expiry inference. Closest is Timmerman (CJP 2016) on timing.
- **Risks.**
  - The case pair must be rebuilt: the scout's twins pair shows the size of the loss, not a date.
  - The eternalist reply: only the aptness of the consolation changes.
  - The paper must be scoped to one component of grief.
- **Decisive unread texts:**
  - Bradley 2009 ch. 3 (*Well-Being and Death*);
  - Marušić 2022;
  - Cholbi 2022 chs. 1–2;
  - Atkins 2024 (Phil Imprint; open access but bot-blocked);
  - Fan 2026 (online 17 Oct 2026).

## 2. Self-exclusion is self-testimony (scout B1; T3)
**Thesis.**
- Self-binding arrangements do not bind the self.
- A gambling self-exclusion is testimony about oneself, addressed to a third party.
- Its force is that party's duty not to exploit a vulnerability it now knows about.
- This predicts three things: the exit asymmetry, refund rather than forfeiture, and the advance-refused epidural case.

**Pretest.** Both testers kept it. The headline "binding works through others" was predicted; the mechanism was not.

**Priority.** PARTLY ANTICIPATED.
- Long (CJLJ 2023) states the informational reading in one paragraph and rejects it (pp. 434–5). The paper must answer him.
- Elster has the through-others headline.

**Risks.** The credibility judgement may smuggle paternalism back in, and the duty is statutory.

**Decisive unread texts:** Franklin-Hall (*Ethics* 2023) and Walker (*Law & Phil* 2012).

## 3. Tempted without wanting (scout D1; T7)
**Thesis.** To be tempted is for an option you have excluded to become live again against your standing decision. Desire is neither necessary nor sufficient. The cases are a double dissociation:
- the "call of the void" on a balcony (a pull without desire);
- a vegetarian smelling bacon (an appeal without pull).

**Pretest.** Both testers kept it, and both said it was only partly predicted.

**Priority.** PARTLY ANTICIPATED. Hill (AGATHEOS 2025, on Christ's temptations) has temptation as considering a not-best act "as a live option", but keeps desire. Holton and Schwenkler keep desire.

**Risks.**
- "Merely verbal" (alief, urge).
- Over-generation: new reasons also reopen options.
- The balcony agent never decided not to jump.

**Decisive unread texts:** Bratman 2007 "Temptation Revisited" and Holton 2009 chs. 5–6.

## Reserve: the vigilante state (scout B2; T4)
**Thesis.** A valid conviction, not guilt, gives the state title to the years. A quashed conviction leaves compensation owed even to the known guilty.

**Priority.** The verdict is in print: Lady Hale in *Adams* [2011] at [116], Hoyle & Tilt, and Roach 2024. What is new is the ground and its use against Wellman.
