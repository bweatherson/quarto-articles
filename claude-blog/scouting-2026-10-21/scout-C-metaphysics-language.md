## 1. Demoted standards: stipulation gives no a priori knowledge

**Question.** Can you come to know a contingent fact about the world just by laying down a definition? Kripke's standard metre is the textbook "yes".

**Candidate thesis.** To stipulate that an object is the standard of a unit is to give it defeasible calibration authority, not to fix what the unit-word refers to. So "S is one metre long" is never known a priori, not even by the people who made the stipulation. The 2019 demotion of the kilogram prototype shows this.

**Core argument and case.**
- *The real case.* From 1889 (sanctioned 1889, worded 1901) the kilogram was "equal to the mass of the international prototype" (IPK). By definition, the IPK's mass had an uncertainty of exactly zero.
- In 2018 the same body (CGPM Resolution 1, in force 2019) declared that the IPK's mass "is equal to 1 kg within a relative standard uncertainty" of 1.0×10⁻⁸, and that in future it will be "determined experimentally" [READ, bipm.org].
- The value fixed for *h* was chosen to keep the unit the same. No calibration certificate was reissued.
- *Minimal pair.* Take the same sentence, "Le Grand K had a mass of exactly 1 kg in 1889". It uses the same unit and is about the same object at the same date. In 2010 it was certain by definition; today it is an open empirical question. Verifications suggest that over a century the IPK drifted about 50 µg against its copies. A priori knowledge is not lost by finding out that a word had always been used deferentially. So it was never a priori knowledge.
- *The pattern repeats wherever a public standard is stipulated:*
  - The metre: the community kept the 1799 bar even though it was about 0.2 mm off the meridian definition. The bar's successor was itself demoted in 1960.
  - The second: no longer 1/86 400 of a mean solar day.
  - The prime meridian: GNSS zero lies about 102 m east of Airy's transit circle, so that Universal Time stays continuous (Malys et al. 2015, *J. Geodesy*) [READ, abstract].
  - Anno Domini: Christ was born "c. 4 BC".
  - The Pleistocene: its base was moved in 2009.
- In every case the practice later overrules the stipulated anchor, without anyone treating this as a change of meaning.
- *Diagnosis.* Reference is carried by the practice of traceability. The stipulation gives one object authority within that practice, and the authority can be revoked.
- *Kripke's idealised private stipulator faces a dilemma.*
  - If her word is a *unit* (used later to measure other things), her own later practice can demote S, as the CGPM did.
  - If her word is only a label for "S's length at t0", what she knows is metalinguistic (Donnellan).

**Obvious answer, and why this thesis differs.**
- A fresh reader predicts one of three answers: Kripke's (contingent a priori); Donnellan and Evans's (a priori, but only a superficial or metalinguistic truth); or Wittgenstein's (the standard is neither one metre nor not).
- This thesis rejects even Donnellan's guarantee, because the stipulation does not fix reference.
- Against Wittgenstein, it gives the standard an ordinary empirical length, which metrologists now assert of the IPK.
- The demotion evidence is new to this debate.

**Closest prior work.**
- Kripke 1980, *N&N* pp. 54–7 [PART: from memory, not re-read].
- Donnellan 1977; Evans 1979 [PART]. The stipulator knows only that the sentence expresses a truth, and that truth is "superficially" contingent.
- Ruffino 2022 (book) and the *Crítica* 57(170) 2025 symposium: Salmon, Demartini, Villanueva, Boccardi [ABSTRACT, all four]. Ruffino reads baptisms as illocutionary acts creating institutional facts; his critics deny that this yields de re knowledge. None of the abstracts uses an actual redefinition.
- Pollock 2004, *Phil. Investigations* [READ, full-text summary]; Mácha 2024 [ABSTRACT]. Both defend Wittgenstein, and Mácha says Kripke's metre presupposes absolute lengths. Neither uses metrological history.
- Salmon 1988, "How to Measure the Standard Metre", *PAS* [ABSTRACT only; paywalled]. It raises an epistemic paradox about measuring the standard. This is the closest in spirit and must be read in full.

**Generalist appeal.** "Can you learn about the world by defining a word?", with public cases everyone recognises: Christ born BC, Greenwich not at 0°, Le Grand K not exactly a kilogram.

**Biggest risk.** A Kripkean says the 2018 resolution *re-fixed* reference, by at most 10⁻⁸, so the knowledge held before 2019 stands. The paper lives or dies on its argument for continuity. *Unchecked:* philosophy of measurement (Tal, Chang, Riordan, and de Courtenay, Darrigol and Schlaudt eds. 2019). It may already say that unit definitions are revisable conventions, though perhaps not against Kripke.

**Confidence:** medium.

## 2. Can the minority be the church? Schisms and the identity of groups

**Question.** When a church or a party splits, which faction is the original group, and can the smaller faction be it?

**Candidate thesis.** An organised group survives a split as whichever faction its own pre-split procedures authorise to continue it, whatever that faction's size, doctrine or founder. When the split is a dispute about those procedures themselves, neither faction is the group.

**Core argument and cases.**
- *Free Church of Scotland (1900–05).* The General Assembly majority used the church's own procedures to unite with the United Presbyterians. A tiny minority refused. The House of Lords (1904) gave the minority everything on grounds of doctrine; Parliament then reallocated the property (1905 Act). On this view the Lords answered a trust-law question, and the majority was the Free Church.
- *Shiv Sena (2023) and NCP (2024).* India's Election Commission gave each party's name and symbol to the legislative majority, against the founder-led organisation. The Supreme Court (May 2023) held that legislative majority alone is the wrong test. The procedural view sides with the Court.
- *United Methodists, ¶2553 (2019–23).* About 7,600 congregations left under a rule the church itself made. Nobody doubts the UMC continues, even where the departing congregations were a regional majority.
- *Minimal pair.* In the Free Church the majority acted *through* the procedures, and it continues. In the UMC case the congregations left *under* the procedures, and they do not continue, even where they were a local majority. Size cuts opposite ways, and procedure decides both.
- *Western Schism.* In 1409 there were three popes. The Roman line counts as legitimate because Gregory XII *convoked* Constance before resigning. That is why Roncalli could take the name John XXIII in 1958.
- *Payoff.* Groups are individuated by structures of authority, not by memberships or creeds. Schisms *over* authority (1054) are exactly where there is no fact of the matter.

**Obvious answer.** "The majority", or "there is no fact: it is convention, or duplication destroys identity" (Papineau). This thesis rejects the majority answer. It also says the question is usually determinate, and it predicts where it is not.

**Closest prior work.**
- Ritchie 2013 and 2020: organised groups as realised structures [PART].
- List & Pettit 2011, *Group Agency* [PART]. They may already individuate group agents by their constitution; must check.
- Epstein 2015, *The Ant Trap*: frame principles [PART].
- Papineau 2017, *Knowing the Score*: team splits, and duplication means neither is the original [READ, summary].
- Figgis 1913, on the Free Church case [PART].

**Generalist appeal.** "Which is the real church, or party?" is a recurring political and religious fight, and it tests what groups are.

**Biggest risk.** It may read as Ritchie's structuralism or List and Pettit applied to cases (EXPECTED). A hostile reader will also say the courts were answering legal-purpose questions, not metaphysical ones.

**Confidence:** low–medium.

## 3. One sword, two copies: what virtual objects are

**Question.** Are the objects in shared online worlds real, and if so, what are they?

**Candidate thesis.** A persisting virtual item is individuated by its provenance in the world's authoritative record, not by any data structure or functional profile. So Chalmers's virtual digitalism misidentifies what virtual objects are.

**Core argument and case.**
- *Dupe glitches.* An exploit creates a bit-for-bit copy of a rare sword. The two are intrinsically and functionally identical in the game. The operator's logs settle which one is *the* sword, and the copy is deleted as counterfeit.
- *Minimal pair.* Two swords with the same data have different status (original versus dupe). One sword keeps its identity across different data, surviving server migration, re-encoding and restore from backup, after all its data structures have been replaced.
- *Law.* The Dutch Supreme Court (2012, RuneScape) treated a virtual amulet as a "good" that can be stolen. What changed hands was an ownership entry, not data.
- *Conclusion.* Virtual items are record-objects, like bank balances: real, but not data structures.

**Obvious answer.** Digitalism (they are data structures), fictionalism, or "the question is verbal". This thesis says provenance does the individuating, and neither data nor causal role can.

**Closest prior work.**
- Chalmers 2017, "The Virtual and the Real" [READ, summary]. He mentions redundancy across servers and "higher-level" objects in passing. He does not discuss duplicates or migration.
- Chalmers 2022, *Reality+* [PART].
- McDonnell & Wildman 2019, fictionalism [PART].
- Brey 2014: a virtual X is an X if and only if X is an institutional kind [ABSTRACT, via Chalmers's footnote].
- Murphy 2026, *AJP*, "perceptibilism" [ABSTRACT].

**Generalist appeal.** Whether virtual worlds are real has been a live public question since *Reality+*.

**Biggest risk.** Brey, and Fairfield's legal account of "virtual property" (2005), may already contain the thesis. Chalmers can absorb provenance as a "social layer" on top of data, which would make the dispute verbal.

**Confidence:** low.

## Ranking

1. **Demoted standards.** This is the only one I would bet on. It is a classic question. The CGPM's own 2018 wording supplies the minimal pair. Its debate is live (the 2025 symposium) but has not used real practice, and the thesis is not one of the three answers a reader would predict. The one decisive check left is the philosophy-of-measurement literature.
2. **Schisms.** Rich, current cases and a determinate answer where most expect none. There is a real danger that it is structuralism applied to cases.
3. **Virtual objects.** I believe the thesis, but I doubt it is far enough from Brey.

**Checked and dropped.**
- Counterfactual skepticism via unspecific antecedents: Schwarz 2024 has it, and there is already a paper running counterfactual skepticism into future skepticism.
- Sports-team identity: Varzi & Slater 2007; Papineau 2017.
- Retroactive institutional facts (vacated titles, transferred franchise histories): these deflate to present-tense status.
- The standard metre via quantity absolutism: Mácha 2024.
- Deadnaming and renamed slur place-names: the "renaming creates the offence" structure is already in the deadnaming literature.
- The Rule Against Perpetuities and the open future: an analogy, not an argument.
