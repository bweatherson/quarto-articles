# Pretest: candidate theses (DO NOT OPEN until Phase 1 is saved)
- **T1 (Q1, scout G1).** A question put within an institutional role (employer, insurer, licensing body) has its domain fixed by the institution's rules, not by the asker's intentions. So a statutorily licensed denial is a true answer to a restricted question, not a permitted lie. Key evidence: the UK exception for the wider question applies only if the asker announces it at the time of asking. Rival: the US courts' "legal fiction" reading.
- **T2 (Q2, G2).** A report of an accusation, offered as bearing on whether the accusation is true, has to meet the epistemic standard of that question. It is proper only if the reporter has reason to think that someone in the chain vouches for the accusation on grounds other than its circulation. So "I'm only telling you what people are saying" is no defence.
- **T3 (Q3, H1).** Jury eligibility and liability should follow ordinary residence under the state's justice system, not citizenship. Jury service is a compulsory duty of justice owed to defendants, not an exercise of popular self-rule.
- **T4 (Q4, I1).** For side effects produced mainly by the patient's expecting them, the doctrine of material risk is fully met by silence. The risk counts as material only relative to the disclosure policy, and under silence it is not material. So the "nocebo dilemma" is not a conflict between autonomy and non-maleficence, and it needs no authorisation from the patient.
- **T5 (Q5, I2).** Velleman's argument that a right to die burdens patients actually favours prescription models over clinician-administered models. Once the means are in the patient's private keeping, the option returns to the standing of the ever-available option of suicide, which leaves life as the default. A clinician model keeps the option institutional, with a date.
- **T6 (Q6, J1).** An eponym honours its bearer in the act of naming, not in later uses. So keeping a tainted name honours no one, and renaming cannot withdraw an honour. Only features located in each use can justify changing an established name: an offensive word, or a harmful reminder.
- **T7 (Q7, K-A).** A performer's duty of truth in a story presented as true attaches to whatever the intended belief-dependent emotion is about.
  - Invention is innocent where the emotion aimed at is belief-independent (amusement at absurdity), or is directed at a kind of thing (an evident or disclosed composite).
  - Invention wrongs the audience where it invites pity, admiration or resentment toward a particular person or event that did not occur.
- **T8 (Q8, L1).** Asking someone's permission gives them a veto over what you asked to do, even when you needed no permission. The veto comes from the asking itself, an act of deferral that hands them a share in the decision. It does not come from an authority they already had (Darwall, Caponetto 2023) or from an implied promise (Scanlon).
- **T9 (Q9, L2).** A donor may revoke a gift when the donee gravely wrongs her. So the benefactor's correlate of a debt of gratitude is neither a claim to thanks nor an "imperfect right" to remonstrate (Manela). It is a power to undo the benefit, held only against wrongs to the benefactor and extinguished by forgiveness.
