# Scouting brief, round 3: a topic from inside philosophy (21 Oct 2026)

## The job
Find **two** candidate topics, each with a candidate thesis, for a philosophy paper of at most 6,000 words. It is for the AI Philosophy Competition (deadline 31 Oct 2026; blind judging). The judging criteria are:
- clarity of thesis and reasoning;
- argumentation quality;
- significance;
- originality;
- engagement with the literature;
- accuracy in representing sources.

The paper will be screened against a strict desk-review standard. Any one of the following makes it NOT READY:
- no positive thesis statable in one substantive sentence;
- mere commentary on a small literature;
- an unanswered well-known objection;
- a thesis of the form "X can be Y" or "X is compatible with Y";
- weak generalist appeal. Would a philosopher outside the subfield find the *problem itself* interesting?

Two candidates you believe in are worth more than three you don't.

## Why previous rounds failed (binding lessons)
Five candidate theses have died. All but one were **anticipated by a canonical or standard work in their own area**, which the scout's search of *recent* papers missed:
- Pomatto 2021 on testable forecasts.
- Holton, *Willing, Wanting, Waiting* (2009), on temptation and resolutions. Fear and disgust count as contrary inclinations, and "desire" means an urge.
- Husak 2009, "Paternalism and Consent", on gambling self-exclusion. Prior consent is irrelevant; it serves as evidence.
- Shiffrin 2000 (*PPA*), on refusing to assist as non-paternalist.

So, for each candidate, you must:
1. **Name the canonical treatments of the question:** the books, classic papers, SEP entry and standard textbooks a specialist would expect to see cited. Read the relevant parts if they are accessible, and say what each already says about your thesis. A thesis that a standard work already states, or that a standard work's framework already delivers, is dead.
2. **Do a predictability check on yourself.** State the answer a competent specialist would give on first seeing the question, and say why your thesis is not that answer.
3. **Avoid the shapes that keep failing:**
   - "a known theorem or formula plus an application";
   - "the norms are silent on X";
   - importing a result from economics, statistics, computer science or linguistics;
   - a reframing that the rival views can absorb ("it's really notice, not consent");
   - a thesis whose argument rests mainly on contested intuitions about rare or exotic cases. Prefer arguments from structure, from widely shared judgements, or from real practices.
4. **Prefer theses where the argument does real work.** A paper judged INTERESTING in an earlier series was an intervention in a live debate, with a vivid minimal pair of ordinary cases that the existing views get wrong. Another success started from real institutional practice that an established theory handles badly.

## How to search (budget-conscious)
- **Budget:** at most about **25 search or fetch calls** in total. Spend them on checking canonical works, not on browsing.
- **Sources:** use WebSearch and WebFetch. Do not use mirrors, pirate sites or Sci-Hub. If something important is paywalled, say so; a colleague can obtain PDFs. If an API asks for an email, use research@example.org.
- **Read status:** mark everything [READ], [PART], [ABSTRACT] or [MEM] (memory, unchecked).

## Output (save to the file named in your prompt; under about 1,500 words)
For each of your two candidates, give:
- **Question.** One or two sentences, recognisable to a generalist as a real philosophical question.
- **Candidate thesis.** One substantive sentence.
- **Core argument or case.** 5–10 lines, including the minimal pair or real practice.
- **Canonical treatments.** 3–6 items, each with its read status and what it already says about the thesis.
- **Recent closest work.** 1–3 items.
- **Obvious answer.** The answer a specialist would give first, and why the thesis differs.
- **Biggest risk.**
- **Confidence.** Low, medium or high that a 6,000-word paper could pass a hostile desk screen.

Then rank the two candidates.
