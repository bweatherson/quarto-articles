# Pretest: questions only (no theses)

For each question: what papers would a competent philosopher who knows the current literature naturally write, and what theses would they defend?

**Q1. Lawful denials.** Many legal systems let people deny spent or expunged convictions. In Connecticut, a person whose record is erased is "deemed to have never been arrested" and "may so swear under oath". In the UK, the Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974 lets a rehabilitated person treat a question about previous convictions as not covering spent ones. There is one exception, made by statutory order: the asker may ask about spent convictions only if they state, at the time of asking, that spent convictions must be disclosed. When such a person answers "No" to "Have you ever been convicted?", have they told a lie the law permits, or have they told the truth? Can an institution, rather than the person asking, fix what a question asks?

**Q2. "People are saying."** When you pass on an accusation marked as hearsay ("I've heard…", "allegedly", "people are saying"), what are you answerable for? Is it the wording, the fact that people are saying it, or the accusation itself?

**Q3. Juries and residence.** Resident non-citizens are tried, taxed and subpoenaed. In the US, men aged 18–25 must register for the draft. Yet resident non-citizens are excluded from juries almost everywhere. Who should be eligible, and liable, to judge a defendant: "the people", or those who live under the same law?

**Q4. Nocebo warnings.** Must a doctor warn of a side effect when the warning itself would mostly cause it? Can the duty to inform be met by silence when the information is self-fulfilling?

**Q5. Models of assisted dying.** Does it matter whether assisted dying works by a prescription the patient keeps (Oregon) or by a clinician at an appointed time (Canada)? Can having an option harm you, and does it matter who sees that you have it?

**Q6. Tainted eponyms.** A species, disease or law is named after someone later judged a wrongdoer. Does keeping the name keep honouring him? Do names honour, or only refer? Can an honour be withdrawn? In 2024 botanists retroactively changed "caffra" species names (the epithet is a slur), but for eponyms they screened only names to be given in future.

**Q7. Invented "true" stories.** A performer says "this happened to me" and has invented it. When is the audience wronged? Cases include:
- Mike Daisey's Foxconn monologue, retracted in 2012;
- James Frey's *A Million Little Pieces*;
- Hasan Minhaj's stand-up stories, defended as "emotional truth".

There is wide agreement that some invented anecdotes are fine and some are a betrayal. What separates them?

**Q8. Asking permission.** If you ask someone's permission to do what you were entitled to do anyway, and they say no, may you go ahead? If not, where did their veto come from?

**Q9. Revoking gifts.** If someone you gave a gift to later gravely wrongs you, may you take it back? Does a benefactor keep any power over a benefit once it is given? (German civil law, BGB §§530–534, allows revocation for "gross ingratitude".)
