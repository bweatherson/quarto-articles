# PHASE 1: what a competent referee would predict

## Q1. Proof and probability
(a) Likely theses:
1. **Normic or explanation-based (~30%).** A certificate's output is explained by the fact, so error would need special explanation. A statistical result is not so explained. Proof requires this, not high probability.
2. **Proof is a social or transferability norm (~30%).** Proof is a practice-relative standard (collective goals, checkability, transfer of warrant), not a degree of evidence. So the lottery analogy is shallow.
3. **Not different in kind (~20%).** Both are fallible. "Proof" labels role and finality, and the lottery case is special because of sensitivity or safety failure.
4. **Pragmatic or stakes (~15%).** Record claims and downstream reliance raise the threshold.

(b) Memory: Smith (normic support); Enoch/Spectre/Fisher (sensitivity); Thomson, Nesson, Cohen (Blue Bus, gatecrasher); Gardiner; Moss (probabilistic knowledge); Hawthorne (*Knowledge and Lotteries*); Nelkin; Pritchard (safety); Littlejohn; Blome-Tillmann; Staffel; Ross; Di Bello; Hedden/Colyvan. On mathematics: Fallis (probabilistic proof, 1997 and 2011), Easwaran (transferability), Tymoczko (four-colour), Rabin/Miller.

(c) The lottery and legal-proof side is **well-trodden**. Probabilistic proof in mathematics is **live**. The GIMPS hardware-error contrast is **fresh**.

## Q2. Censored consensus
(a)
1. **Higher-order evidential (~40%).** Suppression turns consensus into a filtered signal. Agreement stops indicating reliability, so the public's warrant for deferring is eroded.
2. **Millian (~30%).** The public holds the true view as dead dogma, without understanding the grounds or being able to defend it.
3. **No real damage, or net benefit (~15%).** Epistemic paternalism: if the experts are reliable and the objections absurd, only trust or legitimacy suffers.
4. **Agency or autonomy (~10%).** A right to evidence, or damage to trust and legitimacy rather than to knowledge.

(b) Memory: Mill (*On Liberty*, ch. 2); Goldman (*Knowledge in a Social World*; "Experts: which ones should you trust?"); Ahlstrom-Vij (*Epistemic Paternalism*); Nguyen (echo chambers); Lackey; Sunstein; Anderson (lay assessment of expertise); Kitcher; Longino; Kripke/Harman dogmatism paradox; Howard (dangerous speech).

(c) **Well-trodden** on Mill, **live** on the social epistemology of deplatforming. The "absurd objections" framing is fairly **fresh**.

## Q3. Self-binding
(a)
1. **Autonomy plus compromised later self (~40%).** The earlier self's authority comes from her considered values. The later insistence is addictive, hot, or alien (Frankfurt-style).
2. **Promise or commitment-like (~25%).** Authority comes from voluntary assumption, reliance by third parties, and procedural safeguards (cooling-off, revocability).
3. **Really third-party paternalism (~25%).** The request is only evidence of, or consent to, paternalism. It is not authoritative.
4. **Nothing (~10%).** The competent later self wins, and irrevocable schemes are rights violations.

(b) Memory: Elster (*Ulysses and the Sirens*); Bratman; Holton (*Willing, Wanting, Waiting*); Ainslie; Schelling; Kavka (toxin puzzle); Dworkin (*Life's Dominion*, advance directives); Dresser; Parfit; Callard; Paul (transformative experience); Conly; Quong; the Ulysses-contract literature in psychiatric ethics.

(c) **Well-trodden** in principle. The gambling-exclusion institution is **live**.

## Q4. Quashed convictions
(a)
1. **Presumption of innocence survives quashing (~45%).** Demanding proof of innocence violates it. The wrong lies in process and unsafety, not in factual innocence, so compensation is owed.
2. **Defence of the UK position (~25%).** Compensation is for the innocent. The possibly guilty have no claim to years that may have been deserved.
3. **Set-off (~15%).** Rights violation plus desert gives partial or reduced claims.
4. **Epistemic (~15%).** Proving innocence is impossible, so the test is a standards mismatch.

(b) Memory: Nealon and Hallam v UK (GC 2024); Allen v UK; Adams [2011] UKSC 18; Sekanina and Minelli; Tadros/Tierney; Duff; Stumer; Laudan; Lippke; Dennis; Ashworth.

(c) Presumption of innocence is **well-trodden**. Compensation and the afterlife of the presumption is **live**, and the 2024 Grand Chamber case makes it recent.

## Q5. Stipulated standards
(a)
1. **Kripke's case never worked (~40%).** Reference-fixing by description gives only conditional or trivial knowledge. The kilogram demotion shows the artefact was always a fallible physical object.
2. **Provisional, defeasible entitlement (~30%).** Epistemology of measurement: standards are coordination devices that can be replaced (Chang, Tal).
3. **Defend the contingent a priori (~20%).** Superficial versus deep contingency (Evans, Hawthorne). Redefinition changes meaning and does not undo the earlier knowledge.
4. **Meaning change (~10%).** After 2019 "kilogram" simply means something new.

(b) Memory: Kripke; Wittgenstein PI §50; Donnellan; Evans; Kaplan; Salmon (the standard metre); Soames; Hawthorne; Weatherson; Williamson; Chang (*Inventing Temperature*); Tal; Mari.

(c) The contingent a priori is **well-trodden**. The kilogram hook is **fresh**, but measurement epistemology is **live**.

## Q6. Schisms
(a)
1. **Constitutive norms or charter (~35%).** The faction keeping the founding doctrine or rules is the original, even if smaller.
2. **No fact, or two successors (~30%).** Fission. "Original" is interest-relative or conventional (property, name).
3. **Institutional continuity (~25%).** The faction retaining structure, legal recognition or the majority. Closest-continuer.
4. **Self-recognition (~10%).** Internal or member recognition.

(b) Memory: Ritchie (social structures and groups); Uzquiano; Effingham; List/Pettit; Ludwig; Epstein; Nozick (closest continuer); Parfit (fission); church-property cases (Watson v Jones, Jones v Wolf, Overtoun).

(c) **Live to thin.** Group persistence has a small literature, and schism is rarely the case studied.

## Q7. Temptation
(a)
1. **Pull against resolution or judgment (~40%).** Conflict analysis, refined: temptation is a desire that threatens one's resolve or commitment, not merely a desire stronger than judgment.
2. **Evaluative appearance (~25%).** Temptation is an apparent good not endorsed (Tenenbaum-style).
3. **Situational or relational (~20%).** The temptation is the offer or opportunity, and its weight exceeds its authority.
4. **Not defined by conflict (~15%).** One can be tempted to do what one judges best.

(b) Memory: Holton (resolutions); Mele; Bratman; Tenenbaum; Velleman; Foot, Aristotle (continence versus temperance); Kavka; Augustine.

(c) **Thin and live.** Weakness of will is well-trodden, temptation itself much less.

## Q8. Over-persistence
(a)
1. **Cognitive, not conative (~40%).** Motivated reasoning, plan-continuation bias, sunk cost. It is a failure of judgment.
2. **Over-resolution (~30%).** The mirror image of weakness: inflexibility, a failure of reconsideration.
3. **Failure of precommitment (~15%).** The turnaround time is a Ulysses contract that the compromised later self breaks. Links to Q3.
4. **Social or structural (~15%).** Group pressure, identity, guides.

(b) Memory: Bratman (reconsideration); Holton; Kelly (sunk costs, *Noûs* 2004); Arkes and Blumer; Staw; Roberto (Everest 1996); aviation "plan continuation bias"; Duckworth (grit); Kahneman and Tversky.

(c) **Well-trodden** in psychology and economics, **thin** in philosophy. The "failure of will?" framing is **fresh-ish**.

## Q9. Diminishing grief
(a)
1. **Grief tracks reorganisation of identity or life (~35%).** What is lost changes as one's life readjusts, so fittingness is time-indexed.
2. **Fitting in kind forever, not in intensity (~25%).** Dispositional versus occurrent. Intensity is not owed continuously.
3. **Deprivation account (~20%).** The loss is ongoing but its felt significance changes with adaptation.
4. **Wrong notion (~20%).** Fading is permissible or healthy, not fitting. Wrong-kind-of-reasons.

(b) Memory: Cholbi (*Grief: A Philosophical Guide*); Moller ("Love and Death"); Naar; D'Arms and Jacobson; Prior ("Thank goodness"); Parfit (bias toward the future); Freud; Klass et al. (continuing bonds); DSM-5-TR prolonged grief disorder.

(c) **Live** and getting crowded. The fittingness-versus-fading tension is a known pressure point.

## Q10. Judging character
(a)
1. **Moral encroachment (~40%).** The moral cost of unfavourable belief raises the threshold. Withhold, and doxastic wronging is possible.
2. **Against encroachment (~25%).** Beliefs do not wrong. Only actions and treatments do. Evidentialist purism.
3. **Relationship-based or trust (~20%).** The wrong lies in failing to extend trust or benefit of the doubt (friendship partiality).
4. **Credence versus belief (~15%).** Keep credence, avoid outright belief, treat accordingly.

(b) Memory: Basu and Schroeder ("Doxastic wronging"); Basu; Moss; Gardiner; Fritz; Bolinger; Gendler; Stroud; Keller; Marušić; Hawley; Buchak; Begby; Schroeder.

(c) **Well-trodden and crowded** (moral encroachment is saturated). A fresh angle would need a specific hook.

## Q11. Risk for others
(a)
1. **Play safe for others, moderately (~35%).** The imposition of risk without consent adds an agent-relative cost. Precaution when attitudes are unknown.
2. **Repeated choice is risk-neutral (~30%).** Long-run diversification and the law of large numbers support expected-value maximisation for trustees and doctors. One-off choices differ.
3. **Mix over attitudes (~20%).** Uncertainty over the person's risk attitude is itself handled by averaging. This does not obviously favour caution.
4. **Defer to what a reasonable person would want (~15%).** Irreversibility and harm asymmetry.

(b) Memory: Buchak (*Risk and Rationality*; "Taking risks behind the veil of ignorance"); Pettigrew (*Choosing for Changing Selves*); Thoma; Hare ("Take the sugar"); Samuelson (fallacy of large numbers); Buchanan and Brock (*Deciding for Others*); Oberdiek; Lazar; prudent-investor rule.

(c) **Live**, with the repeated-versus-one-off cut **fresher**.

## Ranking: top four

1. **T3 (self-testimony).** Main risk: crediting the self-report imports the same substantive judgment as paternalism, so it collapses into a notational variant. Long 2023 or Franklin-Hall 2023 may also cover the ground.
2. **T7 (temptation as liveness).** Main risk: over-generation (new reasons also reopen options) and a "merely verbal dissociation" charge. Schwenkler 2024 and Holton are close neighbours.
3. **T4 (vigilante state).** Main risk: the vigilante analogy fails because the detention was lawful when imposed. The conclusion is what the dissenters already say, so the paper lives on the title ground and the Wellman counterexample.
4. **T9a (fresh grief).** Main risk: a Stoic dress on a known process view (Na'aman), plus Moller's "tensed object" objection. If a grief paper is wanted, T9b is the more surprising but its pair fails as built, so fix the pair first. Write only one of the two.

Next in line: T10, then T2. Both are crowded and have serious soundness smells.
