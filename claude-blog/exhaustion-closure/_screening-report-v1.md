# Screening report v1: "Exhaustion and Closure" (fresh-context screener, 2026-10-12)

**Word count:** body ~3,960; abstract ~215.
**Contribution (screener's one sentence):** The support an empty search gives to completeness is capped by the shared-blind-spot probability, search data cannot estimate it, and closure results only relocate the problem to their premises. **Rating: EXPECTED** (closure-relocation point at most INTERESTING).
**Verdict: NOT READY**

## Deal-breakers (summarised from the report)
- Crit. 1/6: the model is the beta-factor / common-cause-failure formula, which the paper itself disclaims; the "asymmetry" is essentially Stanford's point (which the paper adopts as premise) and a standard fact of capture-recapture; the paper calls its model "a refinement" of DHS. Closure-relocation is a few paragraphs and near-truism ("any theorem has premises"); section 4 itself says a definition cannot be a hidden empirical assumption.
- Crit. 7: sources flagged by the paper's own bib as unread / abstract-only / from memory (Stanford, Ruhmkorff, Jellema, Menon, Oriti, DHS skimmed, Bunge-Fitzpatrick and Mao-Lindsay upper-bound claim unchecked, Lovelock/Coleman-Mandula from memory).
- Crit. 7 likely inaccuracies: Jellema 2024 is about reasonable doubt in criminal trials (misattributed from abstract); Loe/Jensen first name wrong (Chuan Wen Loe); "Lovelock silent about Gauss-Bonnet in higher dimensions" is misleading (Lovelock's 1971 paper generalises to higher dimensions, Lovelock tensors include Gauss-Bonnet); Moseley integer ordering gave no upper bound; "a thirty-fourth element in the gap" unintelligible.
- Crit. 4: the survey/closure contrast is undefended: real closures (Moseley, differential diagnosis, physics theorems) are outputs of searches, so the ceiling applies to them. DHS's variable D already represents shared community resources ("cleverness of the scientists, or the available computational, experimental, and mathematical resources") and DHS note that FA also confirms high D.
- Crit. 4/6: missing antecedents: Dawid's reply to Menon (forthcoming BJPS), van Fraassen "bad lot", Wenmackers & Romeijn open-minded Bayesianism. 13 of 29 refs are from other fields; philosophical literature thin.
- Crit. 5 (minor): the flat-likelihood "asymmetry" is a simple analytic fact, old in capture-recapture.

## Strengths
Arithmetic of the ceiling and diversity tables correct (recomputed). Nearest predecessors located. Usable reading of what "looking harder" can and cannot do.

## Is the core idea salvageable? (screener)
Partly: only the closure-relocation thesis. A version clearing the bar would drop the ceiling model as headline, make closure-relocation the thesis, argue it from worked primary-source cases (Lovelock, Coleman-Mandula, Bell, Moseley read in full), show at least one case where the missing alternative lay in an unstated premise, give a survey/closure distinction that survives "closures are outputs of surveys", and engage van Fraassen, Dawid's reply, open-minded Bayesians; every flagged citation verified. Could plausibly reach PASS, not STRONG.

## Decision (orchestrator)
Switch, do not revise. Reasons: (1) the headline is a known formula plus Stanford's diagnosis; (2) the only candidate for novelty is a thin, near-truistic thesis that needs a large reading programme to make non-trivial, with a likely ceiling at PASS; (3) cheaper to generate and test other ideas on antecedents first. Lesson recorded: pick an idea whose central claim is itself surprising, not one whose novelty is an application.
