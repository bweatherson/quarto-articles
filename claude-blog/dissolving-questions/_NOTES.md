## Thesis as now written

Narrowed from v1. The paper no longer claims a general theory of success or that the strong norms are "idle or self-defeating". The claims are:
1. Underdetermination: whether an inquirer who learns her question is defective may rationally stop is not fixed by the question, its defect, the evidence or the world. Shown by a same-world pair (Ines the manager, Tomas the auditor, same question, same defect, same camera evidence, different purposes) and by a version A/B pair.
2. What decides it: whether the trouble that made the question worth pursuing is relieved by what the inquiry found (problem = trouble + standard of relief; success conditions (i) relief, (ii) due to the finding, (iii) epistemically good end state).
3. Consequence: norms and success conditions stated over the asked question alone are not accounts of when inquiry may end.

## What changed in response to the screening report

- Framing: now opens with the problem (when may an inquirer who has found a defect rightly stop?), with Dewey/Peirce/Collingwood/Laudan as ancestors; the 2023-26 papers are described as the occasion, and are used as the best statements of the question-first picture.
- Bigger-question objection: no longer conceded in a sentence and left. The "answer space exists only after inquiry" reply is withdrawn (it fails on Hamblin/partition views). I accept that a bigger question is always available, accept the regress (by my definition "What happened to the money?" is defective in version A), show that the catch-all repair makes soundness free and gives A and B the same answer, and state what remains: the success-fixing question is fixed by the trouble, is not a function of the asked question and its defect, and the norms as stated are not about it.
- Agenda-clearing rival: now a section. I concede that suitably qualified it is my view in other words; the thesis is that it needs a source for the items that belong after discovery and a discharge rule for items that leave without being answered, and that both mention purpose.
- Attitudes vs activities; evaluative/objective reading; Friedman's continuing norm: all answered in the section "What the norms can and cannot say". The claim "idle" is replaced by "silent on stopping". The norm that governs continuing is accepted and shown not to be idle but unable to distinguish the cases.
- Verbal vs substantive: a section states what the opponent must accept (purpose-individuated unit), why a success notion should track stopping (Friedman 2024: ending is a separate step), and reduces the residue to the aim of inquiry, argued in both the personal and structural readings.
- Sources: read in full or characterised only as far as read (see below). Reading-record `note` fields removed from the .bib. Hubacher Haerle already makes the discovery point against KNI, and Falbo the "mistaken and confused" point; the paper says so and no longer claims the discovery argument as its own.
- Case base: Mercury, from Le Verrier (1859) and Einstein (1915), as a documented pair of stages (B-like 1859, A-like 1915). The ether case was dropped (it did not need the apparatus, and Einstein's "superfluous" could not be handled without more reading of the German original). The paper states that the Mercury case does not support the claim that defects are found by pursuing the question they infect (Einstein did not find it that way), and that it is a community-level case.
- Soundness as degree: a short paragraph ("Degrees") using Le Verrier's refusal "to decide in an absolute manner".

## Dead ends and changes of mind

- The v1 claim that defects are typically found by pursuing the question is no longer used as a premise. The Mercury case is a counterexample to its universality (the defect, if any, was found by a theory built for other reasons), and Hubacher Haerle already makes the weaker point.
- The ether case (v1) is dropped.
- Friedman 2017 p. 318 "dropped or abandoned" concerns frustrated inquiry, not defective questions; not used.
- D. Friedman's paper says nothing about defective questions; v1's speculation that her "broad construal" might absorb my claim is removed.
- The web search budget was exhausted before an extended antecedent search could be run this round (two attempts refused). The antecedent work is therefore by reading (Peirce, Dewey, Collingwood, Laudan, and the nearest recent papers) and by the v1 searches. A reader may know closer antecedents on the erotetic logic of question dissolution, on pseudo-problems (Carnap, Wittgenstein), or on problem-solving views of inquiry (Nickles), which I have not read.

## Closest prior work, and what the paper adds

Closest: Khalifa and Millson (false presuppositions as engines of good inquiry; corrective answers carry value; their concern is value, not stopping); Hubacher Haerle (Success Norm; admits his model of improvement excludes concept change; "switch to Q*"); Falbo (permission); Palmira (evaluative norm); Rosa (pro tanto, aim-based norms); Peirce (doubt as the motive and end of inquiry, the nearest ancestor of "trouble"); Dewey and Laudan (problems). The paper adds: the same-world pair (underdetermination of stopping); the diagnosis that the bigger-question and agenda replies each concede a purpose-fixed unit; the discharge rule for agenda items; success conditions (ii)-(iii) that relief-of-doubt accounts lack; the Mercury pair as a documented case. It does not claim that retiring questions is new, that inquiry into defective questions can be reasonable, or that inquiry is organised by trouble.

## Weak points I know of

- "Trouble" and "standard of relief" are informal; the goalpost constraints are three sentences.
- The Mercury case is community-level and covers only two documents; the 56 years between them (including searches for an intra-Mercurial planet) are not described.
- The agenda-clearing section concedes that the rival, suitably qualified, is the view; a screener may judge that nothing then separates the thesis from "residual open questions" beyond the source and discharge points.
- The central contribution is now narrow (underdetermination of stopping by the question), and a reader may find it obvious once stated; the argument for it rests on the constructed Ines/Tomas pair.
- The treatment of the aim of inquiry is short and leans on Friedman 2024, whose own view is quietist.
- Bibliography short (19).
