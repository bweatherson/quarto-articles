# Screening report v9: "Fairness Has Memory" (6k version)

**Paper:** Fairness Has Memory (`fairness-has-memory-6k.qmd`, typeset `_test-build-6k.pdf`)
**Word count:** 5,936–6,011 by two counting conventions (abstract and body, bibliography excluded; the author's 5,967 is consistent). At the 6,000 limit: every addition below is paid for by a named cut.
**Thesis:** Broome's own theory of fairness, which requires a divisible good to be divided in proportion to claims and treats a lottery as a second-best surrogate for division, implies that a sequence of individually fair lotteries for a recurring indivisible good is an unfair allocation and that later chances must depend on each claimant's history of unsatisfied claims. What memory tracks is shortfall (in units, or in waiting where later units are worse), and how hard it may press is governed by weighing the division it buys against the chance owed to those who may leave before the division reaches them: cheap where past winners stay in the pool and pay for it, dear where they leave, so that points systems and queues are dominated in recurrence while in bottlenecks with attrition the queue is forbidden and the forgetting lottery is (the author judges) fairer.

**Verdict: PASS, FORMAL**

## Answers to the four questions put to this screening

**(1) Same terms and strength across abstract, §1, §5.2, §5.3, §7, §8?** Nearly, with three sentences that still claim more than §5.3 supports. §5.3 is careful: "between forgetting and a weight the theory fixes the frontier, not the point; I judge forgetting the fairer … and say so", and the visa paragraph keeps the hedge ("by that judgement"). §1 matches ("the structure in which it is all but forbidden"; the third claim is stated with "weighed as Broome weighs"). §7 does not restate the bottleneck result. The three that overshoot: (a) abstract, last sentence: "where winners leave the pool and some will leave unserved, the queue is forbidden and the lottery that forgets is the fairer rule", an unhedged verdict; (b) abstract: "how heavily it may press … where winners leave the pool and some will leave unserved, it is very little", stated as what the theory fixes, where §5.3 says the theory leaves the exchange rate "to the case"; (c) §8: "every weight on waiting is paid by those who will never be served, and the same weighing tells against memory". "Same" is wrong in kind: the recurrence weighing was settled by dominance and a leak "too small to weigh"; the bottleneck weighing is a judgement at a rate the theory does not fix.

**(2) Is "overridden rather than met in part" a principled line between queue and mild weight?** Yes, on the paper's premises, because it is Broome's own line, and the paper should anchor it where Broome draws it. Broome 1991a: "weaker claims require some satisfaction. Weaker claims must not simply be overridden by stronger ones" (95); "if the good goes to the people with the strongest claims, the others will not have been fairly treated; their claims will have been overridden" (98); "it is implausible it should require some people to have no chance at all when their claims fall only a little below equality" (99). A queue gives whoever will leave early no chance in any period she is present; any positive weight gives her some. The phrase the paper actually quotes, "never on the cards" (98), comes from the talented candidate whose claim was weighed against a non-claim reason (talent); the queue case is a claim overridden by other claims, which is the p. 95/98–99 case, so the citation should be 98–99 and the lexical "some satisfaction" requirement named. Consistency with κ = 10: the newcomer gets 52% of an equal chance at κ = 10 in recurrence and 61% under the mild weight in the bottleneck; both are positive, both lie on a frontier the theory does not resolve, and the paper forbids neither. That is consistent. The cost of resting on a lexical zero is the familiar knife-edge: a queue with a one-in-a-thousand wildcard draw is "met in part". The paper's honest "I judge forgetting the fairer" covers that case too, but the *theory's* prohibition is of the exact zero only, and one sentence should say so.

**(3) Worth the formalism; is the third thesis too weak?** The contribution is nameable and generalist: fairness is not separable across occasions, derived from premises the standard theory already holds (cake raffled slice by slice), plus a structural explanation of when memory is cheap and when dear (whether past winners remain to be taxed). The second part is the paper's real formal result and it is correct: the leak to a newcomer in recurrence is second-order (0.023% at κ = ½, 0.71% at κ = 1, both reproduced independently) because it arises only from clipping, whereas in a bottleneck any weight costs the newcomer at first order (my check: weight 0.01 per period costs her 7% of her chance to cut the spread of waits by 6%; weight 0.1 costs 39% for 24%). The softened bottleneck verdict does not hollow the third thesis, because the thesis's content is the frontier and its steepness, which are derived; the point on it was always going to be a judgement. What the abstract must stop doing is reporting the judgement as a result.

**(4) Anything else decisive?** One finding the author could have seen. The dominance of the rules in use is established for Table 1's regime (one unit per stay) and then stated generally: "To remember by points or by queue is forbidden: each such rule is beaten on both counts by one that remembers less harshly." I re-ran the churn model at N = 100, q = 0.1 (units per stay 0.1, the regime the paper itself invokes: "where a hundred claimants compete for each unit and stay ten years"). Points rules remain dominated (Arizona by κ = 1; Nevada by κ = 30; Grand Canyon by κ = ½). The queue is not: its dispersion of realized shortfall (0.260) is below every member of the shortfall family, which bottoms out near 0.264 as κ → ∞ (κ = 100, 1,000, 10,000 checked). At N = 30 and at N = 10, q = 0.03 the queue is dominated again. So where units per stay are small the queue joins the frontier, and the paper's reason for forbidding it there must be the §5.3 reason (zero chance to whoever leaves early), not dominance. The paper's own "shades into" sentence anticipates this; the summary sentence does not. Not decisive, because the verdict survives by the other route, but the general sentence is false as written.

## Key strengths

- **The divisibility argument.** Non-separability and memory follow from the Division Principle plus a stated Fungibility premise, with the raffled cake as reductio; the extinction thesis is disarmed by distinguishing re-run, interrupted allocation and recurrence. Nothing here needs more than Broome's text, which is checked (pp. 95, 97–99; 1984, 47; 1984b, 628–629).
- **The surplus mechanism.** The recurrence/bottleneck asymmetry is explained, not just observed: a newcomer enters level with the pool, so her first-period chance is exactly 1/N and her expected standing has no constant term; the only leak is clipping. I derived the stationary variance $(N-1)/(2\kappa N)$ and the exit variance $(N-1)/(N(Nq+2\kappa(1-q)))$ independently; both are exact absent clipping and match the paper.
- **Every number checks.** The recorded `simulations-6k.out` is reproduced bit-for-bit from `simulations-6k.py` (seed 2026); a second seed reproduces the bottleneck figures (0/0.62, 1.000/8.69, 0.615/6.59; unserved fraction 0.082–0.083 under all three, as conservation requires). All nine rows of Table 1, the prose ratios ("a quarter", "a twelfth", "a sixth more", "15 per cent"), 3.5/2.1/1.4/0, "one in seventy", and the κ = 1 reduction "over a third" are correct.
- **Honesty about judgement.** §5.3 says what the theory fixes (queue forbidden; frontier) and what the author adds ("I judge"), and the visa paragraph inherits the hedge.

## Key weaknesses

- Abstract and §8 report the bottleneck judgement as a result (quoted under (1) above).
- Dominance is generalised beyond the regime where it holds; at low units per stay the queue is on the frontier (under (4)).
- An internal tension in the one judgement the paper makes. §3 says of the visa applicant whose sponsor will not wait, "Then the claim is largely to timeliness." §5.3 then judges forgetting fairer "since what the unserved lose is the good and what the served gain is its timing", and applies that judgement first to visas. By the paper's own account, visas are where timing is nearly the whole claim, so the stated reason has least force exactly where it is first used. There is a reply available: in a bottleneck with attrition, those who leave unserved are those for whom the unit's timing mattered most, so the surrogate they are owed is the chance now, and equal waiting among the served benefits those who could afford to wait. The paper does not give it.
- Two small accuracy points. (i) "Broome himself, Vong reports (p. 481), suggested adjusting later lotteries' probabilities so that every claimant to the unit has an appropriate chance." As Vong (thesis 93–94) and Feldblyum Le Blevennec report it, Broome's adjustment runs later lotteries between previous winners and new arrivals, excluding the past loser ("A, a previous loser, cannot be saved by a subsequent lottery"), with probabilities set so each has an equal overall chance of benefiting. It is history-dependence in the winner's favour; the paper's "though" presents it as leaning the other way. (ii) "whatever weight the two are given" (dominance on the two named measures) is true, but strict cell-by-cell dominance fails once: at tenure 10 the queue (1.01) is closer to proportionality than κ = 10 (0.97). Harmless, but the sentence should name the two measures.

## Critical issues for revision

1. **Match the abstract and §8 to §5.3.** Abstract: replace "the queue is forbidden and the lottery that forgets is the fairer rule" with "the queue is forbidden, and I argue that the lottery that forgets is fairer than any weight on waiting"; replace "it is very little" with "the theory fixes only the frontier, and I argue for its forgetting end". §8: replace "and the same weighing tells against memory" with "and I have argued the weighing tells against memory". Net +14 words. **Cut:** compress the Lackner paragraph in §5.1 to one sentence ("Lackner's [-@Lackner2020, 2106] Perpetual Quota weights each voter by $\max(0,\, \text{quota} - \text{satisfaction})$, which is shortfall in my setting; but his rules are deterministic, so here it is rotation in its tie-breaking order, an arbitrary fixed order among equal claims, which the theory of claims forbids."), saving about 40 words.

2. **Restrict the dominance claim to the regime shown, and route the queue through §5.3 elsewhere.** §5.2's "To remember by points or by queue is forbidden: each such rule is beaten on both counts by one that remembers less harshly" should read "…is forbidden: in @tbl-churn's setting each such rule is beaten on both counts by one that remembers less harshly, and where units per stay are few, so that the queue joins the frontier, it is excluded by the argument of @sec-bottleneck instead." Also soften "The rules in use are not on that exchange at all: each is dominated" to "In @tbl-churn's setting the rules in use are not on that exchange at all". Net +30 words. **Cut:** the second half of the "Equality, not fairness" objection in §7 can lose "That is comparative, concerns the good in question, and is addressed to the allocator, the marks of a fairness complaint in Broome's sense;" (26 words) with "a priority view cannot explain why it lapses when she stops claiming" carrying the point; and drop "Call the procedure that does so *rotation*: draw a random order once and cycle through it" down to "Call this *rotation*: a random order drawn once and cycled" (−6).

3. **Resolve the timeliness tension.** After "I judge forgetting the fairer, since what the unserved lose is the good and what the served gain is its timing," add: "and where Fungibility fails hardest, as for the visa applicant, those who leave unserved are the very claimants for whom timing was the claim, so the chance now is what they are owed." (+36 words.) **Cut:** §6's "Goods are individuated by claims, not names: a Nevada tag and an Arizona tag are different goods, since a claim to hunt in one state is not satisfied by a tag for the other, but two draws run by one agency for the same tag are one good, so an allocator cannot shed its obligation by splitting a practice" can be shortened to "Goods are individuated by claims: a Nevada tag and an Arizona tag are different goods, two draws by one agency for the same tag one good, so an allocator cannot shed its obligation by splitting a practice" (−25); and the §3 footnote's second sentence on "repeated" fair division (−12).

4. **Anchor the queue prohibition in Broome's lexical requirement and admit the knife-edge.** Change "so that her claim is, in Broome's phrase, 'never on the cards' (98), overridden rather than met in part" to "so that her claim is overridden rather than given the 'some satisfaction' Broome requires of every claim (95, 98–99): 'never on the cards' (98). The theory forbids the exact zero; how much above it fairness requires is the weighing that follows." (+22.) **Cut:** in §5.2 "Both are departures from proportional satisfaction, and where neither can be reduced without increasing the other they must be weighed" → "Where neither departure can be reduced without increasing the other they must be weighed" (−9); and "the two being weighed as Broome weighs one imperfect fairness against another" appears in both abstract and §1; the §1 occurrence can go to "the two weighed in Broome's own manner" (−8).

5. **Fix the Vong/Broome sentence.** "though Broome himself, Vong reports (p. 481), suggested adjusting later lotteries' probabilities so that every claimant to the unit has an appropriate chance" → "and Broome himself, Vong reports (p. 481), suggested adjusting later lotteries' probabilities in the winner's favour so that every claimant has an appropriate chance of benefiting". Word-neutral. Also cite the Utilitas wording of "cannot be lost due to the results of a procedurally fair lottery" from the article itself; the thesis (p. 90) reads "cannot be lost due to results of a lottery", so the quoted form has not been confirmed locally.

**Formal claims derived**

- Expected standing: $E[d_{t+1}\mid d_t]=(1-\kappa/N)\,d_t$ exactly (newcomer at pool mean; no constant term), hence first-period chance exactly $1/N$ and every tenure class proportional absent clipping. Confirmed.
- Chance hits zero at $d=-1/\kappa$ ("$1/\kappa$ units ahead"). Confirmed.
- Fixed-pool variance $(N-1)/(2\kappa N)$ (derived from $V=(1-\kappa/N)^2V+(N-1)/N^2-(\kappa^2/N^2)V$). Confirmed; κ = 1, N = 10 gives sd 0.671.
- Exit variance with geometric tenure $(N-1)/(N(Nq+2\kappa(1-q)))$; κ = 0 gives $(N-1)/(N^2 q)=0.9$, sd 0.949 ✓; "under a tenth" at N = 100, q = 0.1 (7.9%) ✓; "over a third" at κ = 1 (closed form 40%, simulation 37%) ✓. Units per stay $1/(Nq)$ ✓. Small drift of the pool mean through exits is omitted; the paper says "about".
- Conservation: chances sum to one each period; same fraction unserved under all bottleneck rules $=0.09/1.09=0.0826$ ✓.
- $N(1-1/N)^T=3.487$ ✓; $(0.9)^{40}=1/67.7$ ✓; $E[W]=9$ ✓; queue wait 9 ✓.
