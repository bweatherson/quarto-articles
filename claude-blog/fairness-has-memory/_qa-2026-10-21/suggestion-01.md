**The surrogate constraint is your premise, not Broome's, and it is what settles your third thesis, so you should say so and argue for it in your own name.**

I admire the cake. But the abstract says the argument "uses only premises the standard theory already accepts." That roughly holds for non-separability and memory, though Fungibility (§3) is a further premise. It does not hold for the third thesis, which carries your most quotable verdicts: κ ≈ 1, and a visa lottery that forgets.

The slip happens in §2. From Broome's "meets the requirement of fairness to some extent," you infer that the surrogate is "not merely permitted but owed." Broome's words describe a matter of degree. In "Fairness," unfairness comes in degrees, and fairness can be outweighed (from memory; check). Nothing there ranks the surrogate as a floor above other departures from proportional satisfaction. But §5 needs that: "a floor, not a consideration to be weighed against the dispersion." For Broome, a newcomer's thin chance and a stayer's realized shortfall both fall short of one standard. Ranking one lexically above the other adds a new principle.

Your own vocabulary then works against you. From §4 onward, you say, claims carry history: the persistent loser "has a stronger claim to the next unit." Yet the newcomer objection opens: "a newcomer has a claim exactly as strong as anyone else's." That is true only in the history-blind vocabulary you have set aside. In yours, her claim to this unit is weaker. So Broome's proportionality, extended to weighted lotteries as the literature extends it (Vong 2020; from memory; check), permits her smaller chance. On the measure your cake suggests (outstanding claim = this period's share plus shortfall), it requires weights proportional to 1 + N·shortfall. That is κ = N, the κ = 10 row you condemn. Applied to claims as you construe them, the standard theory never even raises the newcomer objection.

What to do:
1. In §2, mark "owed" as your extension, not Broome's.
2. In §5, restate the objection in your own terms. Each claimant has a base claim, renewed each period, and some also have a corrective claim. May the corrective claim be met out of a newcomer's base claim? Then present your argument that the surrogate is owed while dispersion is only tolerated as a priority of base claims over corrective ones. It is good; it must stand as your premise.
3. Concede that a Broomean who weighs rather than ranks will set κ higher, and lean towards the queue in bottlenecks.
4. Limit the "only premises" sentence, in the abstract and §1, to the first two theses plus Fungibility.

For room, cut "Losing on purpose" to two sentences (about 100 words).
