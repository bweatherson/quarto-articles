**State the surrogate constraint so it does not depend on claimants leaving independently of their history; the verdicts on Nevada, Colorado and New Mexico rest on an assumption real applicants do not meet.**

*Where:* "Newcomers and the surrogate constraint" and @tbl-churn: "leave with a fixed probability each period, independently of their history", and "proportional at every tenure, 1.00 by construction".

*The problem.* Independent exit makes tenure an unforeseeable, luck-neutral fact about a claimant, so "expected share over whatever tenure she turns out to have" is a sensible floor. Real applicants quit after losing runs, and a points system announces the price of staying, so people sort themselves by horizon. The bighorn tags in your opening are, as I recall, once-in-a-lifetime in both states (from memory; check). Winners leave: on your typology a bottleneck, where your verdict favours New Mexico.

I reran your code with exit hazard 0.03 + 0.03 × (current losing streak), mean tenure about eight, nothing else changed:

- *Forgetting fails your floor.* Its share is 0.51 of proportional for one-period claimants and 0.59 for two to five periods, but 1.44 and 1.67 for 16–40 and 41+. Tenure now measures luck. "By construction" is false.
- *κ = 1 does better on every count.* Across the bins: 0.68, 0.75, 1.02, 1.11, 1.03. Dispersion falls from 0.84 to 0.54; claimants leaving never having won fall from 56% to 44%. The newcomer's chance is 1.16.
- *The rules in use look worse.* Newcomers get 0.33 (Arizona), 0.12 (Nevada), 0 (Colorado).

So the ranking survives; your route to it does not. Under an announced queue the allocator does know who will leave: short-horizon people never enter. The complaint against Colorado becomes exclusion by design, not unforeseen exit, which is different.
