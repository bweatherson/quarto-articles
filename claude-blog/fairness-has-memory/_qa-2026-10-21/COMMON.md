# Instructions common to every Q&A participant

You have just heard a talk and you have the floor for ONE contribution.

## Materials
- The paper: /mnt/user-data/outputs/fairness-qa/paper/fairness-has-memory-6k.qmd. This is Quarto markdown; citation keys such as @Broome1990 refer to entries in /mnt/user-data/outputs/fairness-qa/paper/fairness-has-memory.bib.
- The simulation behind the paper's table: paper/simulations-6k.py, with its output in paper/simulations-6k.out. Run the code (python3) if your character would want to.

## Context
- The author is preparing this version for a philosophy competition: papers of at most 6,000 words, judged blind by professional philosophers.
- The paper is already at about 5,900 words. If your suggestion adds material, say roughly what could be cut to make room.

## Your task
Give the single suggestion that would most improve the paper by your lights. It could be:
- an objection the paper must answer;
- a mistake;
- a missing argument;
- a case that breaks a claim;
- a better framing;
- a source that changes things.

Make it concrete:
- where in the paper (section or quoted phrase);
- what is wrong or missing, and why it matters;
- what the author should do about it.

Stay in character. That is the point of the exercise: the ten participants have deliberately different backgrounds, commitments and temperaments.

Rules:
- Work from the paper, the code and your own knowledge. Do not browse the web. If you cite literature from memory, mark it "(from memory; check)" and do not invent page numbers.
- Write at most 450 words. Start with a one-line **bold** headline that states the suggestion.
- Write your contribution to the output file named in your brief, and nothing else.
- Do not use any messaging, email, compose, widget, chart or artifact tools.
- Then reply with your headline only.
