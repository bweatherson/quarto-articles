**Make "expected units per stay" the variable the thesis turns on: under your own surrogate constraint, memory buys almost nothing where claimants per unit far exceed tenure, which is where your lead example sits.**

*Where.* Table 1, §4.2–4.3, and the use of hunting tags as the paradigm of recurrence ("a tag a year"; "tags, permits, chores").

*What is wrong.* Table 1 fixes N = 10 and mean tenure 10, so a claimant expects about one unit per stay. That regime favours memory most, and is never varied. Section 4.3 forbids memory in bottlenecks because nobody holds the surplus that must pay for it. That reason applies by degree: where claimants per unit far exceed tenure, hardly anyone holds surplus. I reran your code (30–40 runs; rough). With 100 claimants per unit and mean tenure 10, κ = 1 keeps the newcomer at 1.00 but cuts dispersion only from 0.31 to 0.29. κ = 2 already breaches the floor (0.95), and 90 per cent leave with nothing under every rule. At 30 per unit the cut is a fifth. At five expected units per stay it is two-thirds.

*Why it matters.* Bighorn draws run at odds far worse than one in ten, and in several states the ram tag is once-in-a-lifetime (Arizona, Colorado, New Mexico; from memory; check). Then winners leave and losers exit unforeseeably. By §4.3 the opening case is a bottleneck with attrition, and New Mexico is right. The visa lotteries are already conceded, so a referee will ask what actual allocation the headline condemns, and will read §4.3 as the paper retracting its abstract.

*What to do.*
1. State the third claim as one result. Within the floor, memory may press only on surplus, so what it can do scales with expected units per stay. As that number grows the rule approaches rotation; as it falls toward one, with winners leaving, it approaches forgetting. Section 4.3 becomes the limiting case, not a reversal.
2. Tabulate κ = 1 against forgetting at tenure/N of 0.1, 1 and 5.
3. Take κ ≈ 1 off the simulation. Without truncation the linear rule keeps every tenure class's expected shortfall at zero (up to normalization), so the floor binds only through truncation. And κ = 1 is the largest weight at which one surplus unit never truncates, because the weight after a single win is r/N.
4. Concede the sheep to New Mexico and pair them with a high-odds recurrent draw or a rota. That beats the current bait-and-switch.
