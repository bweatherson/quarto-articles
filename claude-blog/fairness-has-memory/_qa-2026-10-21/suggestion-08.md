**Fix the transition: switching to memory cuts above-average past winners to a quarter of their share, and the paper's "losses count" sentence never confronts it.**

**Where.** The *Transition* paragraph of "How far does memory reach?", read against the New Mexico passage in "Claims, chances, and division."

**What is wrong.** The paragraph counts losses, but shortfall is r/N − w ("What memory tracks"), so a switch counts wins as well, and wins do most of the damage. I ran the paper's κ = 1 rule in its own fixed-pool setting (ten claimants, 4,000 runs) after 300 periods of forgetting. At the switch 45 per cent of claimants have zero weight. Over the next thirty periods, those with above-median wins average 0.74 units against 3.0 proportional. Even a ramp in κ spread over the whole history leaves them at 2.4. The paper's own rule that a floor may not be traded for dispersion either forbids this switch or must say why the floor is not engaged.

**Expectations.** A hunter who won in 2020 has lived under a published rule giving every applicant the same chance each year, and plans her 2027 application on that footing. Cutting her expected units by three-quarters is the kind of change legitimate-expectations doctrine polices (*Coughlan*, [2001] QB 213; from memory; check). Such change owes notice, a chance to adjust, and a justification that outweighs reliance. The paper provides none of these, since it treats the announcement as description. The distinction it needs is available: an announcement does not fix what fair treatment requires, but it does fix what the agency has led people to plan around.

**Wrong.** The same gap undermines "the lottery did no wrong." If the forgetting sequence was unfair, as "The divisibility argument" holds, its agency did something wrong in allocating it. If not, the paper must separate an unfair outcome from a wrongful act. Public law draws roughly that line: lawful conduct under a published rule grounds no liability, but it does govern how the rule may change.

**What to do.** Replace the sentence with an explicit choice. Either the whole record counts, wins included, the switch is ramped, and the cost to those who planned otherwise is stated and defended; or the ledger starts level and the forgetting-era shortfall is corrected prospectively, with reasons why that is owed. Rerun the simulation with a switch column. Publish the formula and the ledger's start date, so that same-year applicants can see they face one rule and check their own chance. To make room, cut "Equality, not fairness" to two sentences; its priority point survives in one.
