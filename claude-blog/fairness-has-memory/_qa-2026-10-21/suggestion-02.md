**Replace the one-sentence dismissal of the sanitizing critics in §1 with an argument sorting which of your claims survive without the "chances as surrogate satisfaction" picture. Memory does; the weight cap and the bottleneck verdict do not.**

*Where:* §1, "reach the same verdict: an impartial procedure is fair whatever last year's did"; §5.2, the surrogate constraint; the abstract's last clause.

**1. The dismissal is unargued, and partly false.** On the Stone/Wasserman view a lottery is fair because it sanitizes the decision of reasons that ought not to bear. It is the tool for ties, where legitimate reasons have run out. The question is whether Maggie's 2026 loss is a legitimate reason in 2027, and the view has no stock answer. Grant Fungibility and the Division Principle, and outstanding shortfall is a relevant reason. A fresh lottery discards it, and it is no tie. A sanitizer can accept Non-separability and Memory, and the cake reductio restates in their terms, with no surrogate-satisfaction story, which they think confused. Say so; it makes your first two claims sturdier.

**2. The cap does not survive.** §5.2 says the short-stayer is "owed" a chance as "the surrogate". The sanitizers reject that picture. Why is her lower expected share *unfair* rather than merely unequal? Your text gives no other ground. "A newcomer has a claim exactly as strong as anyone else's" sits badly with §3 ("one thesis in two vocabularies") and §4 (Maggie's claim "has more force than a past winner's"). If claims carry history, the incumbent's claim is stronger and "unequal chances to equal claims" dissolves. What remains is a floor on expected share over unknown tenure, an ex ante measure your last objection disowns.

**3. The bottleneck verdict is the casualty.** For a sanitizer a queue is clean: time waited is a reason nobody can bias or game, and one who leaves unserved has had bad luck, not a wrong. "Fairness requires the lottery that forgets" becomes at most "permits". The visa verdict rests on the surrogate picture alone.

- (a) Ground the floor without surrogates: arrival date as such is morally arbitrary, so a rule treating claimants by when they arrived, not by what remains outstanding, imports a bad reason. I doubt it delivers the cap, since memory also keys on arrival. Or
- (b) Say the cap and the bottleneck result are conditional on Broome's surrogate view, in the abstract too.
