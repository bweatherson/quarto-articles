## 2. Summary

**One cluster dominates: the surrogate constraint.** Eight of the ten contributions (01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 09) converge on it, from different directions:
- **Grounding (01, 02).** The floor is your premise, not Broome's. The paper's own history-carrying vocabulary dissolves the newcomer objection that motivates it. On Broome's proportionality applied to history-carrying claims, the weight is κ = N, which is the κ = 10 row you condemn.
- **What it is (03, 05, 07).**
  - It is an ex ante principle indexed to entry, so the §7 dilemma ("when is the ex ante?") omits your own answer.
  - By conservation it cannot be a floor: it must hold with equality at every tenure.
  - κ = 1 transfers about half a per cent from long-stayers to short-stayers, so "dominated at no cost" and "within the floor" are not literally true. I reproduced this.
  - 03 supplies the repair: a *centred* version of the κ-rule that satisfies the constraint exactly, absent clipping, with closed-form dispersion. I checked it analytically and by simulation. Its cost is that the safe weight is nearer κ ≈ 0.5 (dispersion down a quarter) than κ = 1 (down two-fifths), unless you defend a tolerance.
- **When it holds (04, 06).**
  - As formulated it depends on exit being independent of history.
  - With discouraged exit (04), forgetting itself fails it: conditioning on realised tenure selects losers.
  - With a hazard rising with waiting (06), the stated premise of the bottleneck verdict ("uncorrelated with waiting") fails, and by the paper's own test transplant lists move to the queue side.
- **How much it buys (09).** What memory can do within the floor scales with expected units per stay. It is large in the table's regime and tiny where claimants per unit far exceed tenure.

**A factual problem with the opening example.** Two participants (04, 09) independently recalled, from memory, that bighorn ram tags are once-in-a-lifetime. I verified this for **New Mexico**. The exceptions are certain youth, private-land and population-management licences, and auction and raffle licences.

### 03. Computational social-choice researcher (Opus): "Prove the surrogate constraint rather than simulate it: it is an equality, not a floor, and the proof convicts κ = 1 and repairs the rule."

**Gist.**
- **Proposition.** With history-independent exit and one unit per period, renewal-reward gives expected units at exit = E[L]/N under every rule. So "A(l) ≥ l/N for every tenure l" forces equality at every tenure. This independently confirms 07.
- **Corollary.** κ = 1's newcomer premium (1.006) is paid for by long-stayers, who get about 0.997 from tenure 20 on. The cause is normalisation: dividing by N + κS, where S is the pool's total shortfall, gives a Jensen premium. By the paper's own standard for κ = 2 ("a small but real breach"), κ = 1 also breaches.
- **Lemma (the repair).** Use the *centred* rule p_i = 1/N + (κ/N)(sh_i − mean sh). It is identical to the paper's rule in a fixed pool. Given any exit history, every expected shortfall stays zero, so every tenure class gets exactly 1/N unless a chance clips at zero.
- **Closed-form dispersion.**
  - Fixed pool: variance (N − 1)/(2κN).
  - At exit under churn: approximately (N − 1)/(N(Nq + 2κ(1 − q))). This gives sd .95 at κ = 0 and .57 at κ = 1.
  - The paper's "two-fifths" is 1 − 2.8^(−1/2).
- **What limits κ.** Memory's weight is limited by chances not going negative. Clipping is negligible up to κ ≈ 0.5, where dispersion is .70, a quarter below forgetting rather than two-fifths. At κ = 1, clipping costs newcomers about 0.7%. So either defend an ε-floor or solve "least dispersion subject to exact age-wise proportionality".
- **Novelty.** The κ-interpolation looks new; claim it. Its κ → ∞ limit is the largest-deficit rule.
- **Footnote 3 misdescribes the field.**
  - With identical units and equal claims, envy-freeness up to one unit over the sequence *is* rotation, so "envy, not claims" marks no difference.
  - Aleksandrov et al.'s food-bank mechanisms LIKE and BALANCED LIKE (IJCAI 2015) are your forgetting and lexical rules.
  - Benade et al. (EC 2018) give the random rule's √T envy.
  - Lackner's perpetual voting is your fixed-pool model, and its Phragmén rule becomes rotation.
  - All from the participant's memory.
- Says the three results could replace the table and its paragraphs, saving about 350 words.

**My check.**
- The conservation proposition is correct, and it is the same identity as 07's.
- I derived the fixed-pool variance myself: (N − 1)/(2κN) is exact absent clipping.
- The churn formula reproduces .949 at κ = 0 and gives .567 at κ = 1. The arithmetic 1 − 2.8^(−1/2) = .402 is right.
- I simulated the centred rule (`checks/centred.py`; 400,000 periods), giving expected share by tenure (bins 1 | 2–5 | 6–15 | 16–30 | 31–45):

  | Centred rule | Expected share by tenure bin | sd at exit (simulated vs formula) | Periods with clipping |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | κ = 0.5 | 1.000, 1.000, 1.000, 1.001, 0.998 | .698 vs .688 | 3% |
  | κ = 1 | 0.992, 0.995, 0.999, 1.002, 0.999 | .587 vs .567 | 35% |

  So the claims check out, including the roughly 0.7–0.8% clipping cost to short-stayers at κ = 1. Note that this cost runs in the *opposite* direction from the uncentred rule's premium.
- **On the literature.**
  - Your own 13 October priority check already found Lackner 2020 and Lackner & Maly 2023 (with Perpetual Quota, a max(0, quota − satisfaction) weight) to be the principal technical antecedents. It also found that footnote 3's characterisation of Aleksandrov & Walsh is unverified. Neither finding has been applied to the text yet. This participant independently reaches the same conclusion.
  - Aleksandrov, Aziz, Gaspers & Walsh 2015 (food bank) is new to your notes. Your source check mentions BALANCED LIKE only via the A&W survey.
  - Gölz, Peters & Procaccia (EC 2022, on randomized apportionment) is also new. Check both before citing.

**My recommendation: ACCEPT. This is the most useful technical contribution in the round.**
- The centred rule plus the conservation identity gives you three things:
  - an exact surrogate constraint that holds as an equality, by proposition rather than simulation;
  - a closed-form dispersion that replaces most of the table;
  - a principled account of what limits κ: clipping, not a balance read off a table.
- It also dissolves 07's "no cost" problem, because under the centred rule there is no transfer except through clipping.
- **Cost.** κ ≈ 0.5 (a quarter less dispersion), rather than κ = 1 (two-fifths), is the defensible "safe" region unless you defend a tolerance. Headline numbers change.
- Rewrite footnote 3 now, using your own priority-check findings plus this participant's EF1 point.
