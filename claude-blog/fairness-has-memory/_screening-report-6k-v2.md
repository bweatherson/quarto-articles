# Screening report v2: "Fairness Has Memory" (6k cut)

**Paper:** Fairness Has Memory
**Word count:** 5,904 by my count (title, abstract, body, footnotes, table caption and cells; bibliography excluded); author's figure 5,910. Under the 6,000 ceiling with c. 90 words of headroom.
**Thesis:** Broome's theory of fairness, which requires a divisible good to be divided in proportion to claims and treats a lottery as a second-best surrogate for division, entails that when indivisible units of one good are allocated repeatedly among overlapping claimants, later-round chances must rise with each claimant's shortfall from her proportional share, because the sequence is divisible even though each unit is not. Memory tracks shortfall in units (recurrence) or in waiting (bottlenecks), and may press only as hard as a "surrogate constraint" allows: no claimant's expected share over her actual tenure may fall below proportionality. Forgetting rules (New Mexico, the visa lotteries) and lexical rules (preference-point queues, squared bonus points) are unfair in opposite directions; a moderately shortfall-weighted lottery is what fairness requires.

**Verdict: PASS, FORMAL**

The central argument (Sections 3–4) is in words, from the opponent's premises, and is sound. The third claim rests on a simulated model whose rule is mis-described in the prose (New issue 2) and whose extension to bottlenecks is, as written, inconsistent with the paper's own constraint (New issue 1). Those are faults a referee with formal competence will find in an hour, and in competition mode there is no revise-and-resubmit, so they must be fixed before submission. They do not reach the paper's core contribution, which is why this is not NOT READY.

## Deal-breakers

None against the Screening Criteria. The faults below are concrete and the author could have seen them, but the first two claims and the recurrence half of the third survive all of them intact.

## Key strengths

- **The main argument is from the opponent's premises and is correct.** "The units are indivisible and the sequence is not" uses nothing beyond Broome's Division Principle and surrogate account; the slice-by-slice cake raffle is the right reductio; and the closing observation that Broome's own illustration of division (conscripting everyone briefly) "is a rotation" is a genuine find.
- **The non-separability result is now stated with the right scope.** The new paragraph in Section 3 ("Non-separability with respect to history-blind claims and memory in claims are one thesis in two vocabularies; what is not available is history-blind claims *and* separable fairness") forestalls the obvious "then just say claims carry history" reply by absorbing it. This is cleaner than v1.
- **The extinction objection is met.** The three-way split (re-run, interrupted allocation, recurrence), the concession that Broome is right about the first two, and the diagnosis that his objection needs a stronger thesis ("that losing a fair lottery for one unit extinguishes any bearing the loss might have on later units") are correct, and the treatment of Vong is careful.
- **The two-sided recurrence result is the contribution.** That Arizona, Nevada and Colorado breach the constraint from the other side, "by transferring expected share from claimants whose tenure turns out short to those whose tenure turns out long," is something a specialist would not have predicted from the headline, and the shortfall-versus-points-for-losses distinction ("they count a loss among two hundred applicants as a loss among two") is sharp and consequential.

## Key weaknesses

- **The paper uses two measures of claim strength and needs both at once for its third claim.** For the floor, the newcomer's claim is history-blind: she is owed "a chance, each period, proportional to [her] claim," i.e. 1/N. For the pressure, claims carry history: "the newcomer's present claim and the incumbent's accumulated one are claims of unequal strength." Section 3 says these are "one thesis in two vocabularies," which is true of non-separability but not of the weight claim, because they give different numbers. If claims are history-laden, "proportional to her claims" over a tenure is whatever a chance-proportional-to-strength rule gives by construction, and the surrogate constraint is empty (κ = 10 satisfies it). If claims are history-blind, the constraint bites, but then the sentence that is supposed to ground the floor, "weaker claims must not simply be overridden by stronger ones," has nothing to apply to, since the newcomer's claim is not weaker. The paper should drop the "unequal strength" framing: claims are equal and history-blind; what memory tracks is cumulative *satisfaction*; and the floor is owed because the surrogate is owed to anyone for whom division may not complete.
- **Broome's maxim does not yield a floor.** Even granting unequal claims, "weaker claims must not simply be overridden" is, in Broome 1990, the reason for *proportional* rather than lexical satisfaction: the weaker claim gets proportionally less, not a guaranteed equal share. So the maxim condemns Colorado (newcomer's chance 0.00) and nothing else in the table. The sentence "the maxim forbids that outright, and the Division Principle operates within what it leaves" is still, as in v1, the lexical priority asserted rather than derived; it is now asserted with a citation that does not support it.
- **The bottleneck recommendation is infeasible under the paper's own constraint.** See New issue 1. In a bottleneck no one holds a surplus, so no one can pay for an incumbent's higher chance except the newcomer, whose tenure-1 expected treatment therefore falls below what the forgetting rule gives her. The "waiting-weighted lottery" of Section 5.3 does not exist as described, and the visa verdict rests on it.
- **The simulated rule is not the rule the prose describes.** See New issue 2. The prose says surpluses are ignored ("surpluses are clipped and shortfalls are not"); the numbers can only have come from a rule in which surpluses reduce weight below a newcomer's until the weight hits zero. Under the rule as described, the newcomer could never reach 1/N and the κ = 1 result would be false.

## Critical issues for revision

1. **Rewrite Section 5.3's second paragraph and the visa paragraph** so that they follow from the constraint. Two honest options: (a) concede that where attrition is history-independent and nobody is in surplus, the surrogate constraint selects the forgetting rule, so that in bottlenecks memory is *forbidden* with unforeseeable attrition and *required* (as a queue) without it, and Hersch and Rowe's verdict on visas is right though not for their reason; or (b) give a bottleneck-specific benchmark below the forgetting rule's for short stays, with an argument for why a claimant who leaves after one period is owed less than an equal chance. Option (a) is coherent, sharper, and arguably more interesting; option (b) reopens the weighing question the paper says is closed.
2. **State the κ-rule as a formula and describe it correctly.** Write "chance proportional to max(0, 1 + κ·shortfall)" and delete "The floor at zero is the extinction thesis built in: past winners keep what they won. It makes the rule asymmetric, since surpluses are clipped and shortfalls are not." Replace with one sentence noting that a past winner's weight falls below a newcomer's until she is 1/κ units ahead, and that this is what lets the newcomer's floor be met.
3. **Fix the claim-strength vocabulary in 5.2** (first weakness). Replace "the newcomer's present claim and the incumbent's accumulated one are claims of unequal strength, and Broome's maxim for such cases is not a weighing rule" with the satisfaction framing, and either find a Broomean ground for the floor's priority or present it openly as the natural extension of the surrogate account to populations of unknown tenure.
4. **Reconcile the table's first two columns.** With history-independent exit, "Newcomer's first-period chance" and "Expected share, tenure 1" estimate the same quantity; say so, or drop one. The forgetting row's 0.98 at both tenures should be 1.00 by construction with a pool held at exactly ten; either the pool is not held at ten or this is noise, and the caption should say which.

## New issues

1. **The bottleneck rule contradicts the surrogate constraint (critical).** Section 5.3: "the fair rule in a bottleneck with unforeseeable attrition is a waiting-weighted lottery, in which chances rise with time waited at a rate that keeps every claimant's expected treatment proportional over whatever stay she turns out to have." In a pool of N with one unit per period and weights increasing in time waited, the newcomer has the smallest weight, so her chance is strictly below 1/N whenever weights are not all equal. With history-independent exit, the expected treatment of claimants whose stay turns out to be one period *is* the newcomer's first-period chance. So every waiting-weighted lottery gives the tenure-1 class less than the forgetting rule gives it, and less than "a chance, each period, proportional to their claim," which 5.2 says is owed outright. In recurrence the floor can be met because surplus-holders pay (their weight falls below a newcomer's); in a bottleneck "nobody accumulates more than one unit," so there are no surplus-holders and nobody pays. Under the constraint as the paper defines it, the only rule that satisfies it in a bottleneck with attrition is the forgetting rule. The paper's visa verdict ("The fair rule is a lottery weighted by years of unsuccessful registration") and its reply to Hersch and Rowe therefore do not follow from the paper's premises; what follows is Hersch and Rowe's verdict. This was visible from 5.2 plus the first sentence of 5.3.
2. **The prose describes one rule and the table reports another.** Section 5.1: "each claimant's chance is proportional to 1 + κ · shortfall, negative values set to zero. … It makes the rule asymmetric, since surpluses are clipped and shortfalls are not." Read as written (negative *shortfalls* set to zero), a past winner's weight is 1, the same as a newcomer's, and the newcomer's chance is 1/(N + κ·Σ positive shortfalls) < 1/N whenever anyone is behind, so κ = 1 could not give 1.01 and no κ > 0 could meet the floor. The fixed-pool numbers settle which rule was run: with N = T = 10 and κ = 10, "0" left with nothing is exact only if the *weight* 1 + κ·s is clipped at zero (then every past winner has weight 0 until period 10 and the rule is deterministic rotation); under the described shortfall-clipped rule the expectation is about 1.3, and at κ = 1 about 2.9 rather than 2.1. (I checked both by simulation.) So the simulation clips the weight, which means past winners *are* pushed below a newcomer's chance; "surpluses are clipped in only about one claimant-period in thirty" is then a statement about weights hitting zero, not about surpluses being ignored. The gloss "The floor at zero is the extinction thesis built in" is empty under the correct reading, since a chance cannot be negative anyway.
3. **The forgetting row should be exactly proportional.** With "the pool is kept at ten by replacement" and one unit per period, every claimant has chance exactly 0.1 each period under the forgetting rule, so expected share is 1.00 at every tenure. The table shows 0.98 at both tenures. Within the stated .04 error, but a systematic reading at two tenures suggests the pool is not always ten (exit before the draw, or replacement after it). The text says "as it must be" against a number that is not what it must be.
4. **"As κ grows the rule approaches giving the unit to whoever has the largest shortfall, which with equal claims is exclusion of past winners."** Largest shortfall is fewest units per period present, not "past winner"; after everyone has won once, it excludes those who have won most. Minor, but it invites conflation with Colorado's rule, which the table treats as a different thing.
5. **"a priority view could not explain why it dies with her interest in the good."** Fine as a mark of fairness, but the paper has just said (Section 6, *Memory is bound to the claim*) that someone who "gave up hunting is owed nothing" because "there is no tag she has a claim to." A critic will ask why losing interest extinguishes a *claim* (as opposed to a preference) on an account where claims arise from the allocator's undertaking to eligible applicants; if she remains eligible, the undertaking still stands. One clause ("she asserts no claim on later units" is already available from the *Absence* case) would close it.

## Competition-mode items

**One-sentence contribution.** Broome's own Division Principle, applied to a sequence of indivisible units allocated among overlapping claimants, requires that later-round chances rise with each claimant's shortfall from her proportional share, but only up to the point at which no claimant's expected share over her actual tenure falls below proportionality, so that both forgetting rules and the preference-point rules institutions actually use are unfair, in opposite directions.

**Rating: interesting.** The headline ("fairness has memory"; rotation beats a repeated raffle) is close to *expected*; Hersch and Rowe have it in two remarks and most specialists would grant it on reflection. What lifts it is that it is derived from the standard theory's own premises, the extinction reply, and the two-sided bound that puts Nevada and Colorado on the wrong side. The two-sided bound approaches *surprising* in recurrence. The bottleneck extension, as written, would have been the most surprising part and is the part that fails; the correct consequence (memory required in recurrence, forbidden in bottlenecks with unforeseeable attrition, a queue only without it) would be at least as interesting and is available to the author at no cost in premises.
