# Screening report v3: "Fairness Has Memory" (6k cut)

**Paper:** Fairness Has Memory
**Word count:** 5,904 by my count (title, abstract, body, footnotes, table caption and cells; markup, anchors and citation keys stripped; bibliography excluded). 5,992 by a raw token count that includes anchors and math. Author's figure c. 5,920. Under the 6,000 ceiling with c. 80–95 words of headroom.
**Thesis:** Broome's own Division Principle, applied to a sequence of indivisible units of one good allocated among overlapping claimants, requires that a claimant's later-round chances rise with her shortfall from her proportional share, because the sequence is divisible even though the unit is not. Memory tracks shortfall in units (recurrence) or in waiting (bottlenecks), and may press only as hard as a "surrogate constraint" allows: no claimant's expected share over her actual tenure may fall below proportionality. In recurrence this condemns both forgetting rules (New Mexico) and lexical rules (Nevada, Colorado); in bottlenecks with unforeseeable attrition it selects the forgetting lottery, so the visa lotteries are fair as lotteries and wrong only in weighting by wage.

**Verdict: PASS, FORMAL**

The core (Sections 3–4, and the recurrence half of Section 5) is sound and derived from the opponent's premises, and the recurrence verdict against the rules in use survives even the paper's weakest step. The bottleneck argument is now valid as stated. What is not ready is the surrounding text: the floor's priority over division is still asserted, now with a disclaimer that contradicts the use made of it, and four sentences in Sections 1, 3, 6 and 7 still state the verdict the revision reversed. Those are author-visible and fixable without new premises; in competition mode they must be fixed, since there is no second round. The text screened here is not the text to submit.

## Key strengths

- **The sequence-divisibility argument is correct and uses only Broome's premises.** "The units are indivisible and the sequence is not"; the slice-by-slice cake raffle; and the dilemma in Section 3 ("what is not available is history-blind claims *and* separable fairness") together leave the opponent nowhere to stand except denying Fungibility, which the paper confronts.
- **The recurrence result does not depend on the floor being lexical.** "Memory is required: the forgetting rule is dominated at no cost in anyone's expected share" is true in the table (κ = 1: 1.01 / 1.00 / 1.01 / 0.59 against 1.00 / 0.99 / 0.97 / 0.95). And the moderate shortfall rules match the rules in use on dispersion (κ = 2 at 0.49 against Colorado's 0.47; κ = 1 at 0.59 against Nevada's 0.58) while giving the newcomer 0.97–1.01 instead of 0.00–0.08. So Nevada and Colorado are condemned on any weighing of the two desiderata, not only on the lexical one. That makes the two-sided recurrence verdict robust, which is more than the paper claims for it.
- **The bottleneck argument (5.3, second paragraph) is valid as stated.** With a constant pool of N, one unit per period and exits independent of history, chances sum to one each period, so if every tenure class's per-period chance is at least 1/N every class's is exactly 1/N; and under the cumulative reading of the constraint (expected treatment over a stay), the same follows by Abel summation because the pool's waiting-time distribution is decreasing under history-independent exit. "In recurrence the newcomer's floor could be met because past winners hold a surplus and can be pushed below her; in a bottleneck nobody holds a surplus, since winners leave" is the right diagnosis of the asymmetry.
- **The table and the prose now agree.** Every cell matches the accompanying simulation output to the stated precision; every percentage in the prose ("a quarter," "a twelfth," "17 per cent more," "97 per cent," "little more than half," "one claimant-period in twenty") is correct; the fixed-pool figures (3.5, 2.1, 1.3, 0) and "about one in seventy waits forty periods or more" reproduce under the stated formula.

## Key weaknesses

- **The floor's priority is asserted and simultaneously disclaimed.** Section 5.2: "It is a floor rather than a consideration to be weighed against the dispersion that memory reduces, and not because one principle outranks the other. The Division Principle and the surrogate account govern different claimants … A fair rule does right by both, and since the allocator cannot tell which claimant is which, the only way to do right by the second group is to give nobody less than the surrogate; within that, the first group is owed as much division as can be had." When the two requirements conflict a rule cannot "do right by both," and the symmetric sentence ("the only way to do right by the first group is to divide; within that, the second is owed as much chance as can be had") is equally available and yields Colorado in recurrence and a queue in bottlenecks. The paper's conclusion is that one requirement does outrank the other. In recurrence this costs only the choice of κ ≈ 1 over κ = 2 (a 3 per cent breach against a further 17 per cent fall in dispersion). In the bottleneck the priority is the entire verdict.
- **The knife-edge the priority produces is not addressed.** Where everyone will be served, "fairness requires a queue"; where "some will leave unserved and the allocator cannot tell who, fairness requires the lottery that forgets." So one possible unforeseeable exit among a thousand lifelong claimants converts the required rule from queue to lottery and makes the queue unfair. The paper owns the same discontinuity in recurrence ("Only where the pool is fixed is it zero, and there the rule is rotation") but never says why a floor owed to a possibly empty class should outweigh dispersion borne by everyone else. The same paragraph endorses John and Millum's claim "that a queue is often fairer than a lottery," yet on the paper's own account (Section 5: "the two run together in practice") real bottlenecks, with housing lists and transplant lists the paradigms, all have unforeseeable attrition, so a queue is almost never fair. The paper does not notice that it has withdrawn the endorsement it just gave.
- **Four sentences still state the pre-reversal view.** Section 1: "*Memory*: when a good is allocated repeatedly among overlapping claimants, fairness requires that each claimant's chances in later rounds depend on her history of unsatisfied claims." The H-1B lottery is a good allocated repeatedly among overlapping claimants, and Section 5.3 says its chances must *not* depend on history. Section 3: "Fairness requires that the *waiting* be divided in proportion to claims (@sec-bottleneck)," and "the argument changes currency rather than lapsing"; in the attrition case Section 5.3 says the waiting must be raffled, which is the argument lapsing. Section 6: "two lotteries run by one government for the same visa are one good, so an allocator cannot shed its obligation by splitting a practice in two": the visa lottery has, by 5.3, no memory obligation to shed. Section 7: "as the visa lottery could through the fee it already charges per registration": memory attached to visa registration is what 5.3 forbids.
- **Small internal mismatches.** "dispersion falls by two-fifths" (5.2) and "Such a rule roughly halves the dispersion" (same section) describe the same κ = 1 figure (0.95 → 0.59, a 38 per cent fall). "Under the forgetting rule expected share is proportional to tenure at every tenure, as it must be" sits beside 0.99 and 0.97; within the stated .02 error, but the sentence should say the value is 1.00 by construction and the cells are simulated. Abstract, Section 1 and Conclusion say "proportional"; the body's constraint is "fall short of proportionality," and the paper's reading of the table (tenure-30 shares of 1.17–1.26 are symptoms, the breach is at the newcomer) needs the floor reading.

## Critical issues for revision

1. **Argue the priority or withdraw the disclaimer.** The Broomean route is available and short: a claimant given less than her proportional chance has her claim partly overridden, which Broome's account treats as a failure in kind; a claimant given her chance but not division has her claim "met to some extent," a failure in degree; a rule may not buy the second kind of improvement with the first kind of harm. If that is the argument, state it, delete "and not because one principle outranks the other," and add one sentence facing the knife-edge (the floor is owed per claim, so the size of the possibly-short-staying class is irrelevant to whether it is owed). If the author will not defend a lexical floor, the bottleneck verdict reverts to a mild waiting-weighted lottery and the v2 objection returns; the paper cannot have the disclaimer and the verdict.
2. **Fix the four leftover sentences.** Section 1: "when units of a good are allocated repeatedly among claimants who remain in the pool after receiving one." Section 3: "Fairness requires that the *waiting* be divided in proportion to claims, where the surrogate constraint of @sec-proportion allows it (@sec-bottleneck)," and "changes currency rather than lapsing" → "changes currency." Section 6: replace the visa with a recurring good ("two draws run by one agency for the same tag are one good"). Section 7: replace "and as the visa lottery could through the fee it already charges per registration" with a recurrence example or cut the clause.
3. **Say what the bottleneck verdict means for the cases the reader has in mind.** One sentence after "fairness requires the lottery that forgets" on housing and transplant lists, and a correction of the John and Millum endorsement to the paper's actual position (a queue only where exit is foreseeable or absent).
4. **Minor.** Reconcile "two-fifths" and "roughly halves"; add "1.00 by construction" for the forgetting row; "not below proportional" in abstract, Section 1 and Conclusion; "reaches zero once her surplus exceeds 1/κ" → "reaches 1/κ"; "giving up" in the list of exits is a history-dependent exit, which the stated independence assumption excludes; either drop it or note that the result needs attrition uncorrelated with waiting.

## Competition-mode items

**One-sentence contribution.** Broome's Division Principle, applied to a sequence of indivisible units allocated among overlapping claimants, requires later-round chances to rise with each claimant's shortfall from her proportional share, but only up to the point at which no claimant's expected share over her actual stay falls below proportionality; this condemns both the forgetting rules and the preference-point rules institutions use where winners stay in the pool, and selects the forgetting lottery where winners leave and some will leave unserved, because there is then no surplus-holder to pay for memory.

**Rating: interesting.** The headline ("fairness has memory"; rotation beats a repeated raffle) is close to *expected* and Hersch and Rowe have it in two remarks. What lifts it is the derivation from the standard theory's own premises, the extinction reply, and the two-sided recurrence result, which is robust (second strength) and which no specialist would have predicted from the headline. The bottleneck reversal reads as *surprising* but is not yet earned: it follows from a lexical floor the paper asserts and disclaims in the same sentence, and it carries a knife-edge the paper does not face. If Critical issue 1 is done well the whole would sit at the upper end of *interesting*; it does not reach *surprising* because the mechanism (chances must sum to one; only surplus-holders can pay) is one a specialist sees at once once the constraint is stated.
