# Screening report v5: "Fairness Has Memory" (6k version)

**Paper:** Fairness Has Memory (`fairness-has-memory-6k.qmd`, typeset `_test-build-6k.pdf`)

**Word count:** at the 6,000 limit with no slack. Source count (citation keys as single tokens): 5,780 for everything from §1 to the end of §8 including the table, captions and footnotes, plus a 220-word abstract = 6,000. Typeset PDF text before the bibliography, excluding page numbers and section numerals: about 6,050 (title and date account for six of those). Any addition must be paid for by a cut.

**Thesis:** A sequence of individually fair lotteries can be an unfair allocation, because over a sequence an indivisible good becomes divisible and Broome's own Division Principle then requires division (rotation) rather than a fresh raffle each period. Where claimants stay in the pool after being served, fairness therefore requires that chances depend on each claimant's shortfall from her share, pressed only as hard as leaves no claimant's expected share over her stay below proportionality (the "surrogate constraint"); where winners leave and some leave unserved, the same constraint forbids memory and requires the forgetting lottery.

**Verdict: NOT READY**

---

## Deal-breakers

**Criterion 4 / the central test (the paper's own test undercuts its thesis).** The paper's third claim, which it calls the derivation of "how hard memory may press", is stated as a lexical floor and then applied as a tolerance, and the paper's own table shows the floor is breached by every rule it recommends.

§5.2 states the constraint and its status in the strongest terms: "It is a floor, not a consideration to be weighed against the dispersion that memory reduces ... A rule may not buy an improvement of the second kind with a harm of the first." It then establishes, correctly (I verified both; see Appendix B), that (i) by conservation the floor "can only be met exactly", so any shortfall for one tenure class is a breach, and (ii) the shortfall rule meets it "so long as no chance is clipped at zero". But clipping occurs with positive probability for every κ > 0 (the paper says so: "runs of wins do"), and the paper's non-negativity of chances ("never taxed, which is the extinction thesis built in") guarantees that when it occurs the deficit "falls on everyone, the newcomer included". So (i) + (ii) + non-negativity entail that for every κ > 0 the newcomer class stands strictly below proportionality. Table 1 shows exactly this: 0.999, 0.995, 0.945, 0.533 against the forgetting rule's 1.000 (SE 0.001). The paper then writes: "Clipping costs the newcomer nothing at κ = 1/2, half of one per cent at κ = 1, five per cent at κ = 2 and nearly half her chance at κ = 10, against reductions in dispersion of a quarter, two-fifths, a half and three-fifths." That sentence weighs a harm of the first kind against an improvement of the second kind, which the floor paragraph forbids two paragraphs earlier. It then concludes "Memory is required: the forgetting rule is dominated, and up to κ = 1 the price in anyone's expected share is clipping's half of one per cent." On the paper's own ordering the forgetting rule is not dominated; it is the only rule in the table that meets the lexically prior criterion. Read strictly, the paper's constraint delivers κ = 0 under churn, i.e. no memory in every real case in the opening (all of which have churn), and the Memory thesis survives only in a fixed pool. Read tolerantly, the "bound in weight" that the abstract and §8 advertise as derived is a threshold set by eye (half a per cent acceptable, five not), and the two-sided result of §5.3, which relies on the strict reading ("the only rule under which no tenure class's expected treatment falls below the equal chance is the forgetting rule"), becomes a difference of degree rather than of kind. The paper cannot have it both ways, and the sentence that shows the problem is in the paper.

A second, independent breach of the same constraint, also visible from the paper's own rule, is set out under Weaknesses: the floor is stated per claimant and "indexed to her entry", but verified only on average over entry states.

## Key strengths

- The divisibility argument is a genuine, nameable contribution: it turns Broome's Division Principle (quoted in a passage that already divides across time) against the standard verdict on repeated lotteries, using only premises the standard theory accepts, and the reply to the extinction objection (re-run / interrupted allocation / recurrence) is clean and, so far as I can check against the thesis text, fair to Vong.
- The formal core is essentially right. Claims (i) and (ii) in §5.2 are correct (my derivation: with D the others' summed shortfall, the pair (E[s_i], E[D]) evolves by a linear homogeneous map from (0, 0), so E[s_i | tenure] = 0 absent clipping); the fixed-pool variance (N−1)/(2κN) is exact absent clipping, not merely "close"; the churn variance formula follows from an AR(1) observed at a geometric exit time and matches the simulation to within clipping effects. Every number in Table 1 and the prose matches the recorded output, and the simulation reproduces exactly from the seed.
- The two-sided structure (memory required in recurrence, forbidden in bottlenecks with unforeseeable attrition, because only recurrence has surplus-holders to pay for it) is the kind of result a generalist would find worth the price of the formalism, and it corrects the opening's own intuition about the visa lotteries.
- The problem is broad (fairness across repeated allocations) and the engagement with computational social choice is honest about the deterministic rules being nearest.

## Key weaknesses

- **The floor is verified for tenure classes, but stated for claimants.** The constraint reads "no claimant's expected share, over whatever tenure she turns out to have, fall short", and §7 says it "is indexed to her entry". At entry the pool state is public (points are public in every hunting draw). Under the paper's rule a newcomer's first-period chance is 1/N − (κ/N)·s̄, where s̄ is the pool mean shortfall, which is nonzero whenever departed claimants took shortfall with them. In my simulation of the paper's own setting at κ = 1, the newcomer's first-period chance has standard deviation 12 per cent of 1/N; one newcomer in ten is handed 83 per cent or less of a proportional chance, knowably, in order to pay a debt created by winners who have left. Table 1's 0.995 is the average over entry states. On the paper's own words ("a rule that gives her less partly overrides her claim") that is an override, and the forgetting rule is the only rule in the table that avoids it in every state. The §7 reply "the ex ante view must say when the ex ante is" turns on the paper: its own constraint says "on entry" and is then evaluated as if it said "before entry". A one-line change to the rule (enter each newcomer at the pool-mean standing, so her distance from the mean is zero in every state; then E[Δ(s_i − s̄) | state, she stays] = −((κ+q)/N)(s_i − s̄) and the floor holds state-wise absent clipping) would fix this; in my test it shrinks the newcomer spread from 0.12 to 0.014. The paper does not have it.
- **The priority statement understates the nearest predecessor.** §1 says "The theory of fair allocation has not connected this disagreement to the theory of claims" and calls the nearest antecedents "two remarks by Hersch and Rowe", quoting only the clause about chores. The passage (Hersch and Rowe 2025, 3) actually states the paper's premise and conclusion: "many goods can be divided temporally as well as spatially, leading to the possibility of dividing spatially indivisible goods over time through a rotation (Elster 1989) ... the recurring chore, which extends temporally, is not [indivisible]. We can rotate who empties the trash, and who completed the chore last time matters to who has a claim to avoid it this time." That is the divisibility argument in miniature, connected to claims, with Elster 1989 cited for rotation as temporal division. Elster 1989 is in the .bib and nowhere in the text. What the paper adds (the derivation inside Broome's theory against the extinction thesis, the constraint, the bottleneck reversal) is real, but a reader who knows that page will think the paper has not read it.
- **The variance formula is mistyped.** "(N−1)/N(Nq + 2κ(1−q))" parses, by ordinary precedence and in the PDF, as ((N−1)/N)·(Nq + 2κ(1−q)) = 2.52 at the table's parameters; the intended value is 0.32. Same ambiguity in "(N−1)/2κN". And "the expected number of units handed out during a stay, 1/Nq" is wrong as written (1/q units are handed out during an expected stay; 1/(Nq) is the number a claimant can expect to receive).
- **Steps asserted rather than argued in §5.2.** "which is zero on entry and stays zero" is the whole proof of claim (ii), and the non-obvious step (why the pool mean's expectation is zero at the moment she enters, and why her distance from it stays zero in expectation although the mean moves when others exit) is not given. It is true, but a formal reader has to reconstruct a two-variable linear recursion to see it.

## Revision items (each with the cut that pays for it)

1. **Make the floor and the κ-bound consistent.** Two honest options. (a) Keep the floor lexical and change the rule so it never clips, with a proof that claim (ii) survives; note that my test of the obvious variant (lower κ in any period in which a chance would go negative, instead of clipping and renormalising) brings the newcomer's first-period chance back to 1.000 ± 0.001 at κ = 1 but still shows 0.9965 at κ = 2, so this is not free. (b) Keep the rule and restate the floor as forbidding *first-order* transfers of expected share (which is what a bottleneck weighting, points-for-losses and a queue do) while tolerating the *second-order* residue of the non-negativity of chances, on the ground that non-negativity is the indivisibility of the unit reappearing and its residue is of the kind the standard theory already tolerates; then the principled bound is the largest κ at which the residue stays second-order, which the paper already has in hand: "A single win never clips a chance while κ ≤ N/(N−1)". Under (b) the sentences "It is a floor, not a consideration to be weighed ..." through "A rule may not buy an improvement of the second kind with a harm of the first" must be rewritten (they currently forbid what the next page does), "the forgetting rule is dominated" must go, and §5.3 must say that the bottleneck verdict is that no weighting there is second-order, not that the floor is lexical. *Cut to pay:* the paragraph beginning "It is a floor" (c. 190 words) can be halved, since the override/partial-satisfaction distinction is all it needs; the second reply in the ex ante objection ("Second, my view is ex ante exactly where ...", c. 60 words) becomes redundant once the floor is restated and can go.

2. **Make the floor hold state-wise, or say openly that it does not.** Either adopt the pool-mean entry standing for newcomers, re-run Table 1, and add one sentence to §5.2 ("A newcomer enters at the pool's mean standing, so her chance in her first period is 1/N in every state, and her expected distance from the mean then shrinks each period by the factor 1 − (κ+q)/N"); or state that the constraint is an expectation over entry states and defend that against the §7 "when is the ex ante" question. *Cut to pay:* the "Transition" sub-paragraph of §6 (c. 90 words, "A boundary question follows ... would count only the losses"), which is not used anywhere else.

3. **Typeset the closed forms as fractions** ($\frac{N-1}{N(Nq+2\kappa(1-q))}$, $\frac{N-1}{2\kappa N}$) and change "the expected number of units handed out during a stay, 1/Nq" to "the number of units a claimant can expect to receive during a stay, 1/Nq". No net words.

4. **Locate the paper honestly against Hersch and Rowe 2025, 3 and Elster 1989.** Replace "The nearest to my thesis are two remarks by Hersch and Rowe" with a sentence that quotes the "dividing spatially indivisible goods over time through a rotation (Elster 1989)" clause and says what the paper adds: the derivation from the Division Principle against the extinction thesis, the surrogate constraint, and the bottleneck reversal. Soften "The theory of fair allocation has not connected this disagreement to the theory of claims" to a claim about Broome's theory and its critics. Cite Elster 1989 in the text or remove it from the .bib. *Cut to pay:* the Frege clause ("and was in Frege's proposal for an election law [...]", c. 15 words) and "The wider literature takes the single lottery as its unit [six citations]" can be compressed into the preceding sentence.

5. **Give claim (ii) its missing line.** After "so long as no chance is clipped at zero", add: "exit independent of history makes the pool's expected shortfall zero when she enters, and thereafter her expected shortfall and the others' evolve by a linear map with no constant term, so both stay at zero." *Cut to pay:* "and the memoryless lottery enters only as a benchmark that 'converges to proportional outcomes' but is 'not a suitable alternative' over short sequences (2109)" can lose its second quotation.

6. **Small accuracy items.** (a) The Vong 2015 quotation "cannot be lost due to the results of a procedurally fair lottery" (pp. 479–480) could only be checked against the thesis, which reads "cannot be lost due to results of a lottery"; verify against the Utilitas text. (b) "[E]ach person can be given ..." — the source has a capital E; drop the brackets. (c) Table caption: the runs discard the first 200 periods and "standard errors below .003" should be "at most .003" (κ = 10 is ±0.003). (d) The paper's "shrinks by a factor of 1 − 1/N each period" is for the fixed pool; under churn the factor for her distance from the mean is 1 − (κ+q)/N.

## Appendix B: formal claims and numbers checked

- **Rule.** p_i = 1/N + (κ/N)(s_i − s̄), floored at zero, renormalised (code: `np.maximum(0, ...)` then `wt/wt.sum()`); unclipped weights sum to 1. Matches the prose.
- **"Distance from the mean shrinks by 1 − κ/N each period."** E[Δs_i | state] = 1/N − p_i = −(κ/N)(s_i − s̄). Correct in the fixed pool (s̄ ≡ 0). Under churn, conditional on her staying, her distance from the mean contracts by 1 − (κ+q)/N.
- **"Reaches zero once she is 1/κ units ahead"; "a single win never clips while κ ≤ N/(N−1)."** Both correct (p = 0 at s_i − s̄ = −1/κ; a win from the mean moves her to −(N−1)/N).
- **Claim (i), conservation.** Σ p_i = 1 each period, so the sum over tenure classes of expected units equals the sum of proportional entitlements; a floor on every class can only be met with equality. Correct.
- **Claim (ii).** Let D = Σ_{j≠i} s_j. Absent clipping, E[s_i′ | state, i stays] = s_i − (κ/N)((1−1/N)s_i − D/N) and E[D′ | state, i stays] = (1−q)(D + (κ/N)((1−1/N)s_i − D/N)): a linear homogeneous map. E[Σ_pool s] = 0 at stationarity (it is multiplied by 1−q each period under history-independent exit), so E[D] = 0 when she enters, hence E[s_i | tenure r] = 0 for all r. Correct. **State-wise it fails:** conditional on the entry state, E[s_i(1)] = (κ/N)((N−1)/N)s̄₀ ≠ 0. With clipping, E[Σ_pool s] = 0 still holds, so the newcomer class's expected chance is 1/N minus the expected renormalisation loss, strictly below 1/N for every κ > 0.
- **Fixed-pool variance.** V′ = (1−κ/N)²V + E[p(1−p)] with E[p²] = 1/N² + (κ/N)²V gives V = (N−1)/(2κN) exactly absent clipping. Paper's "close to" is conservative. κ = 10, N = 10 is random-order rotation re-drawn each round (every post-win chance clips); its stationary within-round variance ≈ 0.165 (sd 0.41), matching the table's 0.40.
- **Churn variance.** AR(1) with innovation variance (N−1)/N² and contraction 1 − κ/N observed at a geometric exit time gives Var = σ²/(1 − (1−q)(1−κ/N)²) ≈ (N−1)/(N(Nq + 2κ(1−q))) to first order. Values 0.949/0.688/0.567/0.442 (κ = 0, ½, 1, 2) vs simulation 0.950/0.697/0.586/0.494; the gap grows with clipping (16 per cent of chances clipped at κ = 2). The reduction ratio is Nq/(Nq + 2κ(1−q)), so it depends on κ and on 1/(Nq) only to first order in q. N = 100, q = 0.1, κ = 1: sd reduction 8 per cent ("under a tenth", correct). N = 10: 40 per cent ("two-fifths", correct).
- **Table 1 vs `simulations-6k.out`** (and my rerun with seed 2026, identical to the recorded output line for line): all 32 cells match after rounding (S1/L10 0.995 → 1.00 is a rounding choice). First-column SEs ≤ 0.003 (κ = 10 exactly 0.003); other shown columns ≤ 0.018. Newcomer column equals the tenure-1 expected share in expectation (exit independent of outcome); the unshown L1 values are within 1.3 SE of the newcomer values.
- **Prose numbers.** Clipping cost 0.1/0.5/5.5/46.7 per cent ("nothing", "half of one per cent", "five", "nearly half": fine); dispersion reductions 27/38/48/58 per cent ("a quarter, two-fifths, a half, three-fifths": fine); Arizona newcomer 0.263 ("a quarter"), tenure 30 1.149 ("15 per cent more"); Nevada 0.082 ("a twelfth"); queue 0.000 and 1.261; "some by more than the moderate shortfall rules" (0.69 vs 0.70, 0.58 vs 0.59, 0.47 vs 0.49): all correct.
- **§3 and §5.1.** N(1−1/N)^T = 3.487 ("three and a half"); fixed-pool nothing counts 3.488/2.126/1.353/0.000 ("3.5, 2.1, 1.4, 0"): correct.
- **§5.3.** 0.9^40 = 0.0148 = 1/67.7 ("about one in seventy"); expected wait N−1 = 9 under the forgetting rule with a pool kept full; queue wait 9 for every arrival: correct.
- **Benade envy.** Difference in units between two claimants under the forgetting rule has per-period variance 2/N, so sd √(2T/N): "growing as √(T/N)" correct; rotation holds it below one unit.
- **Perpetual Quota = rotation.** Verified by hand (see Appendix A).
