## Key formulations (verbatim)
- Independent Closure: "If an agent S may believe that q given question Q and that r given question R, where Q and R
  are independent, then S may believe that q ∩ r given question QR."
- Inquisitive Closure (IC without independence) -- immediately incompatible with Guessing (horse A: 'A' to which-horse,
  'not A' to whether-A; conjunctive question = which-horse; contradiction).
- Closure Under Parthood (whole-to-part): "If an agent S may believe that r given a question R, and question Q is a
  part of R where r ⊆ q for some q ∈ Q, then S may believe that q given question Q." Fn 32: two versions,
  whole-to-part and part-to-whole; cites Hoek 2019 and Teague 2024.
- Tournament: races {A,B,C} and {D..H}; 'A' and 'D or E'; conjunction '(A,D) or (A,E)' unfiltered on QR since (B,D)
  more probable than (A,E).
- Asher/Bob/Carl: Bob asks Asher race 1 -> 'Alexander'; race 2 -> 'Bella or Beth'; Carl asks Bob what Asher thinks
  about today's races; "It seems perfectly fine for Bob to reply: 'He thinks that Alexander will win the first race,
  and either Bella or Beth will win the second.'"

## s4 Conjunctive Filtering: one natural reading of condition X = "When questions Q, R, and QR are all salient to S"
  -- "the principle becomes a constraint on belief revision ... she might want to -- perhaps ought to -- revise her
  earlier composite beliefs". Fang's objections: (1) "Why should the rational conditions on some belief depend on
  one's belief about some other independent question? Intuitively, what I think about marbles in one urn need not
  change merely because you now present me with another urn." (2) challenge from naturalness (452 entrants).

## s6.2 THE KEY PASSAGE for our project: the "question-sequence discourse"
- Guessing theorist's best attempt: distinguish a "probability-comparison discourse" (salient question is QR;
  '(A,D) or (A,E)' sounds bad and is unfiltered) from a "question-sequence discourse" ("where the two original
  questions are asked first, and then the conjunctive question. There, what the agent in effect answers isn't the
  new, conjunctive question (iii). Instead, they are still answering the two separate questions, (i) and (ii), and
  submit their answer by simply repeating their previous answers.")
- Fang's reply: "I'm not convinced that when the agent is in the question-sequence discourse, their conjunctive
  belief report is not answering the conjunctive question, but only the two composite questions. Claiming that would
  come close to denying any inter-question principle at all".
  Plus: even in probability-comparison discourse the conjunctive report can sound fine: "'I think it's most likely
  that A will compete with D; second most likely that B will compete with D. But I think A will win. So it's gonna
  be A competing with D or E.' This sounds perfectly fine to me."
- FN 36 (anticipates a conditional/sequential response!): "One may think this is yet a different kind of discourse,
  for the agent already thinks that A will win and is presumably now answering the conditional question:
  'Conditional on A will win, who will compete in the final race?' Similarly, one might take this to be the
  response to the current puzzle. But this sort of treatment would also rule out the felicity of certain answers to
  the conjunctive question -- that are not reporting the conjunctive belief -- which we've been staying silent on.
  For instance, it seems to also rule out the felicity of reports like: 'It's true that I think A will win the first
  race. But B competing with D is also quite likely overall. So, as to the final race, I think it's gonna be A vs. D
  or B vs. D.'"
  ==> Our paper must answer fn 36. Answer sketch: the sequential view is a view about which question a guess answers
  given the line of questioning; the 'A vs D or B vs D' report explicitly steps out of the line ("overall") and
  answers QR afresh -- the view predicts both are available, depending on whether the speaker builds on her earlier
  guess or re-opens it. Fang's objection assumes the conditional reading would be mandatory.
- Fn 24/25: Filtering Failure extends to some dependent cases "where the answer pairs never form a contradiction";
