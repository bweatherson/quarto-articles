- BGA (Holguín): "S is rationally permitted to believeQ p iff e&gC,Q entails p." gC,Q = most probable cell (or union of
  tied cells).
- Bond numbers in Pearson: London 40, Munich 30, Berlin 30. (7) "# I believe/think Bond is in Germany and in London."
- Six-sided die: Alice wins 1-4, Bob 5-6. (12) "I think Alice will win." (13) "I think that if the die lands greater
  than 3, Bob will win." (14) "I think Alice will win; nevertheless, I think that if the die lands greater than 3, Bob
  will win." -- felicitous; no QUD makes both BGA-permissible.
- s3.1: rejected reply: "posit bridge principles allowing what one can permissibly thinkQ1 to impact what one can
  permissibly thinkQ2. However, doing so will undermine what made positing question-sensitivity initially attractive.
  ... we require a substantive independence between what one can permissibly think with respect to different
  questions in order to avoid predicting that the belief expressed in (7) can ever be rational."
- fn 11 (referee): mid-context shifts (Dorr 2014; Goodman & Lederman 2021) -- risk of over-generation.
- fn 13: "Hoek suggests that if one can form beliefs about a conjunctive question, those beliefs should cohere with
  one's beliefs about the separate conjunct questions."
- CBGA (s3.2): "S is rationally permitted to believeQ p → q iff e&gCP,Q entails q" where gCp,Q is the cell of Q that
  C(·|p) assigns highest probability. Fails Modus Tollens and Proof by Cases (ten-sided die; "CBGA predicts one can
  believe Bob will win!").
- fn 15 (referee): isolated vs collectively permissible beliefs; "a promising strategy for further research".
- s4 puzzle: Conditional Weakness vs Closure.

Relevance: CBGA is the closest formal antecedent to using conditional credence inside a guessing norm. Our line view
conditions on the speaker's own earlier GUESSES in a line, not on the antecedent of a conditional. Explicit
conditional questions ("if the die lands >3, who wins?") must NOT inherit earlier guesses (else the line view predicts
'Alice' after 'Alice wins', contra (14)); treat an explicit supposition as opening a subordinate line on P(.|A).
