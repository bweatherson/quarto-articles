## 1. Verdicts

### I1 Standing (closure as a condition on use): PARTLY

**What is already there.**

1. **The slogan.**
   - **Foley 2009**, "Beliefs, degrees of belief, and the Lockean thesis" (Huber & Schmidt-Petri, eds., p. 41). Rejecting the conjunction rule for belief costs deductive reasoning nothing, he says. The conjunction rule that reasoning needs governs context-relative attitudes such as assuming, positing and presuming. Within a context those attitudes are conjunctive. They are not belief.
   - From memory: Cohen 1992 (acceptance as premising, closed under deduction, unlike belief); Bratman 1992 (context-relative acceptance for practical reasoning).
   - From memory and the PIMS 2004 slides: Kyburg's corpus has only "limited adjunction". It is closed under single-premise consequence, so a set can be used jointly only via an accepted conjunction.
   - The single-premise versus multi-premise closure debate about risk aggregation has the same structure (Hawthorne 2004; Lasonen-Aarnio 2008; memory).
   - So "closure is a condition on use, not on possession" is **not new as a slogan**.
2. **Weak speech acts.** Incurvati & Schlöder 2019, p. 763, rule *Weak Inference*: weak assertion is preserved under entailment from one weakly asserted premise, with strong side premises. Weak assertions do not agglomerate. Joint use of two weak assertions therefore needs a weak assertion of their conjunction. This is I1's form without questions.
3. **The question-relative version, closest prior work.** Lin & Kelly, preprint §§13–14 (published as Synthese 186, 2012: 531–575):
   - They define the conjunction of two questions as their maximally coarse common refinement, which is exactly R's "join" (p. 43).
   - They name the benefit that question-invariance would bring: pooling accepted conclusions across distinct questions (p. 40).
   - They prove that pooling ("conjunction-preservation", equivalent to refinement-monotonicity, Prop. 14) is incompatible with consistency, closure and non-sceptical acceptance (Thm 6, Thm 7).
   - Their moral (p. 42): an accepted proposition is a proxy for the credal state, *not* information to condition on.
   - R's standing is the natural exception clause to their impossibility result (pool or condition only when the conjunction is itself acceptable at the join). They do not state it.
4. **Fang 2026 §4, Conjunctive Filtering.**
   - Under condition X, holding q (to Q) and r (to R) is permitted only if qr is filtered at QR.
   - Her natural reading of X is "Q, R, QR all salient", which makes it a constraint on **revision**.
   - Her objections are (i) other-urn independence and (ii) naturalness (4 vs 452 entrants).
   - **R is Conjunctive Filtering moved from holding to building.** That move does escape objection (i). Objection (ii) carries over in modified form (§4 below).
5. **D&M, "Good guesses"** (PhilArchive preprint p. 34, concluding section).
   - Their proposed function of guessing is reasoning inside the guess: "You reason within your guess". You suppose the guess and plan or form finer opinions within it.
   - Their examples are **"So"-marked follow-ups** to the guess "Yale or Harvard": we can visit him at weekends; he will probably need a car.
   - So "guesses are for building on" is D&M's. They give no condition on building from several guesses.
6. **Pearson 2025, fn 15** (preprint p. 9). A referee proposes distinguishing *isolated* from *collective* permissibility, with collective permissibility given by Hoek-style constraints. Pearson calls it "a promising strategy for further research". This is standing on the possession side. Pearson's dissertation abstract (2024) states the dilemma on the use side: weak-belief theorists must either give up their method or deny that weak beliefs in conditionals can be used in reasoning.
7. **Related but weaker.**
   - Hoek 2025 §VI: posing the conjunctive question adjoins earlier views (full belief; always).
   - Jovićević 2025 §§4.2, 4.4: conjunction closure holds within a QUD, fails across QUDs; no condition on use.
   - Mackenzie 1987, p. 299: a guessed statement goes into "balk" and may not be used in the guesser's own defence. An *absolute* restriction on use.
8. **Leitgeb** (memory; not checked). Belief is partition-relative; in the lottery, "ticket i loses" is stable relative to the coarse partition but lost under the fine one; P-stability is what makes conditioning on belief safe. A close parallel, and "standing" will be heard as an echo of "stability".

**Not found:** the principle itself (joint conditioning iff the conjunction is permissible at the join; failed members stay reportable).

**Hostile summary.** I1 = Fang §4 + Foley/Kyburg's use–possession split + D&M p. 34 + an exception clause to Lin & Kelly. A referee will see that quickly.

### I2 Contraction to the full meet of maximal standing subsets: ANTICIPATED as a mechanism

- **Inference from maximal consistent subsets:** Rescher & Manor 1970 (Theory and Decision 1).
- **"Free" formulas and cardinality-maximal subsets:** Benferhat, Dubois & Prade 1993/1997. Free formulas are those in no minimal inconsistent subset, which is the intersection of all maximal consistent subsets. Cardinality-maximal subsets correspond to R's "maximum-cardinality" option. (The IJCAI 2021 survey's definitions agree.)
- **Full-meet contraction:** Alchourrón & Makinson 1982; Hansson on contraction of belief bases.
- **Choosing what to drop:** Levi 1991 (memory). **In dialogue:** Mackenzie 1987, p. 292 (BQD's "resolution demands" force adjustment of an immediate inconsistency).
- **New:** replacing "consistent" with "standing". But consistency is closed under taking subsets and standing is not (§4, items 1–2), so I2 is not well defined as stated.

### I3 Follow-ups as requests to continue; conformity as inheritance: PARTLY

**Anticipations.**
1. **Ninan 2026** (Analytic Philosophy 67(2), p. 3 of 10). The follow-up question about Gavriil is odd unless it is heard as a request for B to continue guessing. He says nothing about what continuing consists in.
2. **D&M fn 6** (preprint p. 6). The stickiness datum is **itself "So"-marked**: after "Yale" to *where?*, "So, do you think he'll go to Yale?" gets "yes". D&M explain it by the earlier QUD staying salient. That is already a non-normative, contextual explanation of conformity. R must show what inheritance predicts that QUD-persistence does not; D1 ("So where?" after "no" to Yale) and the raffle are the candidates.
3. **D&M p. 34**: "So"-continuations as reasoning within the guess (I1, item 5).
4. **Fang fn 36** reads the follow-up as a conditional question ("conditional on A winning, who will be in the final?"). She rejects that reading because it would rule out fresh answers that step outside it. R's optional building answers that objection, provided "optional" is made principled.
5. **Mechanism precedents** (memory):
   - Roberts 1989 on modal subordination (continuing in a non-CG hypothetical context) and its extension to attitude contexts;
   - Isaacs & Rawlins 2008 on conditional questions (a question asked in a temporary context);
   - Horowitz's suppositional guesses (guessing from conditional credence).

**What is new.** Two things:
- the unification: D&M fn 6, G&H fn 24, Holguín §9.2 and Teague's WTP/PTW are all inheritance;
- the contrast between "so"-marked and unmarked sister questions.

**Caveat.** Teague's principles are about *solitary* inquiry (DAC: wondering *where*, then wondering *whether Yale*). There are no requests there, so R needs a self-directed analogue of "request to continue" before it can claim to dissolve Teague's puzzle.

### I4 The raffle as an argument for Ninan: PARTLY

**What is old.** The inference "jointly inconsistent elicited answers cannot all be added to the CG" is the lottery/preface argument in dialogue form (Kyburg 1961; Makinson 1965). Two published treatments are close to it:
- Incurvati & Schlöder 2019, pp. 754–755: a weak assertion of p does not propose p; it only blocks the addition of not-p.
- M&D, "Assertion is weak" §3.1.1 (the 98-RSVP preface variant). They take "Ninety-eight" as a fine weak assertion. Under R that set stands: "all 98 come" is the top cell of the join. That is worth a line.

**What is not found in print.** The raffle line itself (60 polar "no"s, then CG ⊨ #60). Ninan's own arguments are the possibility principle and the question principle, not this.

**Overreach.** The raffle refutes only CG accounts that add the guessed *content*. It does not touch Ninan's own proposal (the CG gets "speaker believes φ"), Farkas–Bruce discourse commitments, derived-context accounts (CG plus guesses, which is R itself), or defect-and-repair accounts. So "refutes common-ground explanations of conformity" overreaches; "refutes content-adding explanations" is defensible.

### I5 Against cost-based retraction: NOT FOUND (and low stakes)

- **Searched:** Levi 1991; Genin & Kelly 2019; Bratman on non-reconsideration (memory); Holguín's toggling draft §3, whose law (toggle once fine, twice borderline, thrice unacceptable) is count-based, not loss-based, and concerns one question.
- **No published account to refute.** None conforms when the loss is below a retraction cost; the target is this project's own B′. So I5 is a footnote, not a payoff.
- **It refutes only constant costs.** A cost graded by locatability (Hunter & Konieczny's 1/|M|) escapes it: stickiness and the raffle differ in the size of the minimal conflicting set (1 vs 21+).

## 2. Overall verdict: PARTLY ANTICIPATED (not DEAD)

No one states I1 together with I3. That is the claim that "So"-marked follow-ups request continuation, and that continuation is licensed exactly from sets whose conjunction is permissible at the join. But each half has close neighbours, and the paper must be written as building on them.

**Must cite, with the precise relation:**
- Fang §4, §6.2, fn 36: R is Conjunctive Filtering relocated, plus a version of the reply Fang rejects.
- D&M fn 6 (rival QUD-stickiness explanation) and p. 34 (reasoning within guesses); Ninan p. 3.
- Lin & Kelly §§13–14: join, pooling, impossibility, no conditioning on acceptances.
- The use–possession split: Foley 2009 p. 41; Cohen 1992; Bratman 1992; Kyburg's limited adjunction; Incurvati & Schlöder's *Weak Inference*.
- Pearson fn 15 and his 2024 dilemma about use; Hoek 2025 §VI; Jovićević 2025 §4.
- Mackenzie 1987, pp. 292 and 299.
- For I2: Rescher–Manor, Benferhat et al., AGM full meet.
- Leitgeb (partition-relativity, stability, the name clash); Roberts and Isaacs & Rawlins (the mechanism of continuing).

**What remains new:**
1. The join-relative condition on **use**, not on holding, with failed members kept reportable.
2. Deriving the "So"/unmarked asymmetry, and the raffle switch point, from that condition.
3. The re-description of the conformity data as inheritance.
4. The raffle line as a dialogue argument against content-adding CG views.

**Ceiling as it stands:** "interesting, not surprising".

## 4. Objections and counterexamples

**1. Standing is not closed under taking subsets, and the full meet can fail to stand. This is internal to R.**
- Credences: Cat .22, Dog .08, Eel .07, Fox .26, Gnu .13, Hen .24.
- Guesses, each filtered on its polar question:
  - g0: "Hen, Gnu or Dog?" No (.55)
  - g1: "Hen?" No (.76)
  - g2: "Eel, Hen, Dog or Cat?" Yes (.61)
  - g3: "Eel or Fox?" No (.67)
- Both {g0,g1,g2} and {g1,g2,g3} stand. Their conjunctions are Cat∨Eel (.29) and Cat∨Dog (.30), each the top cell of its join. These are the only maximal standing subsets.
- Their full meet is {g1,g2}, which does **not** stand. In the join of those two questions, its conjunction Cat∨Dog∨Eel (.37) is beaten by Fox∨Gnu (.39).
- So clause 3 instructs building on a set that clause 2 forbids building on.
- Also, {g0,g1,g2} stands while its subset {g0,g2} does not.
- Script: /tmp/claude-0/-home-claude/5e4308f6-fe95-539b-ad49-78c9865c2ca8/scratchpad/canonR/scripts/verify.py. **Fix:** fall back over subsets all of whose subsets stand.

**2. The maximum-cardinality selection ties exactly at the switch point.**
- At k = 21, {¬#1…¬#21} stands, and so does each {¬A} ∪ (20 of the 21 tickets). All have 21 members.
- Building on {¬A, ¬#1…¬#20} gives "one of #21–#60", not "A".
- So the claim that the maximum-cardinality subset "gives the same results in every case" fails at k = 21.
- **Separately, the numbers.** standing.py uses Latif at Y .30, H .28, S .22, N .20. D&M and Teague use .38/.30/.20/.12. On those numbers {¬Y,¬H} does not stand: neither (.32) is below Yale (.38). The D5 list of maximal subsets changes, though "Yale" survives. Use the published numbers.

**3. Fang's question-sequence datum against I1.**
- On standing.py's tournament (A .45, B .35, C .20; D .40, E .22, …), take the guesses "A" and "D or E", then "So who'll be in the final?". The natural answer is "A against D or E"; Fang says the sequence version sounds fine.
- But {A, D∨E} does not stand, since (A,E) at .099 is below (B,D) at .14. R predicts a fall-back to fresh "A vs D".
- R must classify the natural answer as a *report*. Separating report from answer is exactly the reply Fang §6.2 considers and rejects ("would come close to denying any inter-question principle").
- Without an independent test that separates reporting from building in "So"-questions, I1 is protected by reclassification.
- The naturalness challenge returns in this form, with A1 .40, A2 .30, A3 .20, A4 .10 and B1 .10, B2 .07, plus 450 others at about .0018. The most natural pair, "A1" and "B1 or B2", cannot be built on, because (A2,B1) at .030 is above (A1,B2) at .028.

**4. Teague and solitary inquiry.** R's inheritance runs through requests, but Teague's DAC/WTP data involve one inquirer wondering where, then whether. Without self-addressed continuation, R does not touch Teague's puzzle.

**5. A sharp switch point.** Standing flips exactly between k = 20 and k = 21, on Filtering and on the J-value version alike (the conjunction is a single cell). Intuitions about "So who?" after 20 versus 21 "no"s will be graded, so the prediction is a liability unless tested.
