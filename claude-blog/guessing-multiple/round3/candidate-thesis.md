# Candidate thesis (DO NOT OPEN before Phase 1 is saved)

**Priced commitment.**
- A guess commits the guesser in later turns. Later answers must cohere with it whenever coherence is cheap.
- When coherence would cost more answer-value than retraction costs, the guesser retracts the guess, and the retraction is faultless.
- The trigger is comparative:
  - conform iff the loss in expected answer-value from building on the earlier guesses is less than the cost of retracting;
  - the cost of retracting is the hearer's uptake and the coordination built on the guess.
- So guesses agglomerate only "at need": when a follow-up question builds on them, as with anaphoric "so"-questions. Sister polar questions do not bank one another (D3). A follow-up that would require an absurd composite triggers retraction (D4, D5).

**What this distinguishes.**
- Guessing differs from asserting, whose retraction is not faultless.
- Guessing differs from reporting credence, which creates no commitment.
- So "assertion is weak" is true of the attitude but not of the speech act.

**Corollaries.**
- Fang's and Teague's puzzles about closure across questions get a single account. Combining guesses is required only when a later question builds on them, and then only if the price is right.
- Path dependence is the expected corollary, not a selling point (Gold & List 2004).

**The decision-theoretic shape.** The comparison follows Bratman-style reasonable reconsideration: stick with a commitment unless reopening it is worth its cost.
