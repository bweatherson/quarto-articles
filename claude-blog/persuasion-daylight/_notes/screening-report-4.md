# Screening Report: "Persuasion in Daylight"

**Paper title:** Persuasion in Daylight
**Word count:** ~10,500 main text + ~700 appendix (85 bibliography items)
**Thesis summary:** The philosophy-of-science models of "propaganda by true results" assume audiences that update on what they are shown but not on the fact of being shown it ("cursed" audiences). Removing the curse splits the phenomenon: *selection* of existing results is neutralised by unravelling, but *design* of which experiments exist is not, and in the two-option case a sender's optimal experiment leaves a fully rational receiver with exactly her no-evidence expected utility. The paper argues that the wrong in design is mutually advantageous exploitation (Wertheimer), not deception or manipulation, and that transparency reforms therefore relocate rather than solve the problem; the remedies are receiver-side design authority and a public standard of proof fixed above the receiver's own threshold.

**Decision:** DESK REJECT

This is the closest call in the batch and could plausibly have been EVALUATE: the formal core (Bayesian persuasion, decision rules, evidence games) is squarely in the editor-in-chief's areas, and the selection/design split is a genuinely useful framing for the industry-propaganda debate. It is a DESK REJECT because the philosophical section that carries the paper's own contribution (§7, exploitation) has gaps the author raises and then waves through, and the paper's own concessions leave the defended thesis considerably weaker than the one the abstract states.

## Deal-Breakers

**Criterion 4 (unanswered objection the paper itself raises).** §7.2 raises the obvious rival: "A simpler rival account would say that the sponsor is under a duty to produce informative evidence and has breached it." It is dismissed in two sentences: "I do not think the two are rivals. The exploitation account is what explains why there is such a duty." No argument is given, and the paper's own construction points the other way. The fairness baseline the exploitation claim needs is, by the paper's admission, supplied entirely by "the purpose the institution assigns to the evidence" (§7.1). Once the institution's purpose fixes the cooperative experiment, "the sponsor produced evidence that falls short of what the institution's purpose requires, and profited from the shortfall" is the whole of the wrong, and the exploitation vocabulary adds a label, not an explanation. Every downstream prediction the paper credits to the exploitation account (that the wrong is serious where receivers lack power; that remedies are design authority and standards) is equally a prediction of the duty account plus the observation that duties get enforced where enforcers exist. Since the exploitation classification is what the paper says distinguishes it from the economics ("The economics is not new"), this gap is decisive.

**Criterion 5 (length relative to achievement).** Eleven thousand words, of which §§2–5 and §8.2 (roughly 4,500 words) present results the paper explicitly disclaims as its own: unravelling (Grossman/Milgrom), Bayesian persuasion (Kamenica–Gentzkow), the receiver-welfare corollary ("familiar in the persuasion literature"), the commitment result ("The formal point is not mine"; Henry–Ottaviani), and the commitment-is-idle-against-disclosure contrast (Hart–Kremer–Perry). The "diagnosis" contribution (that the WOB audiences are cursed) is something WOB flagged themselves, as the paper quotes. What remains as the paper's own is a classificatory thesis that occupies about two thousand words and has the problem above.

## Key Strengths

- **The selection/design split under full rationality is crisp and correctly grounded.** The observation that transparency reforms, by making the count known and the design credibly committed, *supply* the precondition for the design channel (§5, drawing on Felgenhauer–Loerke 2017 and Fréchette et al. 2022) is a genuinely useful synthesis that the philosophy-of-science literature on industry funding has not stated.
- **Proposition 1 is correctly stated and proved**, and the paper is right that the "whatever her payoffs" form of the zero-surplus result has not been used in the philosophical literature. All the worked numbers check (selection: 0.83/0.47 cursed, 0.30/0.89 sophisticated; design: 0.60/0.70; commitment curve as stated).
- **The pairing of the commitment result with Hart–Kremer–Perry** (§8.2, "scepticism for selection and commitment for design") is a neat way of showing that the two channels call for different receiver-side responses, and it is the paper's best original move.
- The engagement across two literatures is broad and, where I could check it, accurate.

## Key Weaknesses

1. **The exploitation category does not fit the paper's own headline case.** Wertheimer's mutually advantageous exploitation requires that both parties gain and that the exploited party consent. In the paper's clean case the receiver gains *exactly nothing* (that is the point of Proposition 1), so the transaction is non-harmful, not mutually advantageous; the paper's sentence "She consents, in the sense that she relies on the sponsor's evidence voluntarily and would rather have it than not" (§7) is contradicted by its own result, under which she is indifferent in expectation. The paper alternates between leaning on "left with nothing" for rhetorical force and on "mutually advantageous" for the category.
2. **Consent and power conditions attach to different receivers.** The consent/no-worse-off framing fits the regulator; but the paper concedes (§7.3) that regulators now have power over the terms, so on its own account they are at most mildly exploited, and that the seriously exploited parties are "the public, ... most patients, ... most jurors," who never consented to rely on anything and who, in the motivating cases (CAST), were harmed rather than left even. The framework's conditions are satisfied by a receiver the paper says is not seriously wronged, and fail for the receivers it says are. The agent/principal paragraph (§7) asserts the transfer without addressing this.
3. **"Cannot or does not use it."** §7.3 says the receiver "is exploited only to the degree that she cannot or does not use" her power over the terms. The "or does not" clause makes a receiver who could have committed to a rule and did not still count as exploited, which contradicts the "cannot reasonably refuse" condition (Valdman) the paper adopted a page earlier, and it is precisely the position of the act-by-act regulator in the running example. The paper needs to choose, and does not.
4. **The defended thesis is narrower than the stated one.** The abstract says "reason is engaged rather than bypassed ... I argue that the wrong is exploitation." The body concedes that on Gorin's and Klenk's accounts "design is manipulation" and "I accept that." What is actually defended is that the *part* of the wrong that varies with the terms is exploitation. That is a reasonable claim but a modest one, and the argument for it (§6.2, the two-regulator comparison) is loose: the 0.95 regulator is also left short (0.984, not 1), so "the first has a complaint the second lacks" is a difference of degree presented as a difference in kind.
5. **A false statement at the argumentative hinge.** §5: "rejecting at any belief below t* is worth exactly what rejecting at her prior is worth, since the value of rejecting is linear in her belief." Rejecting at belief 0 is worth more than rejecting at 0.3. The correct claim, which the appendix makes, is that the *expectation* over posteriors of the rejection payoff equals the rejection payoff at the prior. The prose in the paragraph the paper says "matters for the ethics" gets it wrong.

## Critical Issues for Revision

(The spec omits this section for desk rejects; included at the caller's request.)

1. Give a real argument that the exploitation account is not redundant with the institutional-duty account, or adopt the duty account and drop the Wertheimer apparatus. If exploitation is kept, show what it predicts that the duty account does not.
2. Decide which receiver is exploited and make the framework fit that receiver: either drop the consent/mutual-advantage conditions (and find an exploitation account that does without them) or drop the claim that the public and patients are the seriously wronged parties.
3. Resolve the "cannot or does not use it" ambiguity and say explicitly whether the act-by-act regulator who could publish a rule is exploited or imprudent.
4. Bring the abstract's thesis into line with the body's concessions on manipulation.
5. Cut §§2–5 by roughly half. The economics can be stated in two thousand words with a pointer to the surveys; the paper's own content should carry the length.
6. Fix the §5 linearity sentence to match the appendix.

## Classification of residual objections

- **Weakness 1 (zero gain vs. mutual advantage):** (A). Visible to any reader who puts Proposition 1 beside the §7 sentence "would rather have it than not." Author could have seen it.
- **Weakness 2 (consent/power mismatch across receivers):** (A). The paper itself states both halves (§7 agent/principal paragraph; §7.3 "until regulators acquired the powers they now have") and does not connect them.
- **Weakness 3 ("cannot or does not use it"):** (A). An internal inconsistency in the paper's own statement of its account.
- **Weakness 4 (abstract stronger than body):** (A). The body's "I accept that" is the author's own concession.
- **Weakness 5 (false linearity sentence):** (A). The appendix has the correct argument, so the author knew it.
- **Deal-breaker on Criterion 4 (rival duty account):** (A). The paper raises the rival and dismisses it in two sentences. This is the spec's "raises the problem and defers it" case.
- **Deal-breaker on Criterion 5 (length):** (A).

Objections I would classify as (B), which are *not* what the decision rests on:
- Whether Eyster–Rabin's "cursed equilibrium" is the right description of the WOB policymaker, who is not a strategic player forecasting an action distribution; a game theorist might prefer "naive conditioning" (Grünwald–Halpern) and drop the E–R label.
- Whether the trial-count evidence structure satisfies Hart–Kremer–Perry's conditions (truth-leaning refinement, the evidence-structure assumptions), which the paper asserts "the natural cases satisfy" without checking.
- Whether the informed-sender results (Hedlund; Koessler–Skreta) support the general claim that "the sender's gain from design survives" private information, as opposed to surviving in some equilibria.
- Whether Vrousalis's domination-based account can be applied to a relation in which the putatively dominated party (the regulator) has legal power over the dominator; the paper's redefinition of the relevant power dimension is a stretch an exploitation theorist would press.

If the (A) problems were fixed, the (B) problems are what review would be for, and the paper would then be an EVALUATE.

## (a) Claims that are false or unsupported

1. §5: "rejecting at any belief below t* is worth exactly what rejecting at her prior is worth, since the value of rejecting is linear in her belief." False as stated; see Weakness 5.
2. §7: "She consents, in the sense that she relies on the sponsor's evidence voluntarily and would rather have it than not." Under Proposition 1 she is exactly indifferent in expectation; "would rather" is unsupported in the headline case.
3. §7: "The structure is what Wertheimer calls mutually advantageous exploitation." Requires mutual gain; the headline case has zero gain to the receiver.
4. §6.2: "The first has a complaint the second lacks." The second regulator is left at 0.984 rather than 1; the sponsor still takes something. Difference of degree.
5. §7.3: "exploited only to the degree that she cannot or does not use it." Inconsistent with the "cannot reasonably refuse" condition adopted from Valdman.
6. §2: The inference from Lundh et al. (industry-funded studies favourable without worse risk-of-bias scores) to "a residual mechanism that those measures do not capture, and design is one such mechanism" is suggestive, not evidence for design specifically; the Cochrane authors' own candidate explanations include comparator choice and publication bias, and the paper does not distinguish these.
7. §1: "In the language of Eyster–Rabin, she is cursed." Loose: cursed equilibrium is defined for strategic players who correctly forecast the action distribution; the WOB policymaker has no model of the propagandist at all. (Expert-level; listed for completeness.)
8. §5: "in the case as I have set it up, he knows nothing the regulator does not" is fine, but the sentence "That is why the sophisticated column for design equals the cursed one" is true by construction (the table's design column is computed once and copied), so the table does not *show* what the paragraph says it shows.
