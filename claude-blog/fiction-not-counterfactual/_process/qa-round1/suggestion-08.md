**Your main contribution rests on four self-reported ✓/✗ judgements that are not minimal pairs and allow a reading B&H share; rebuild them as a joint-credence design, and cut or repair the King of France example.**

Acceptability contrasts like these usually replicate (Sprouse and Almeida; from memory, check). My worry is what the acceptable sentences are about.

**1. (i)–(iv) in §5.**
- **Two things vary at once.** (i) and (ii) differ in attitude ("sure" versus "about 50%") and in polarity ("didn't change" versus "changed"). Cross them. Add controls where "changed" is plainly about content: a later story that settles the hair count, and a retcon.
- **The "status" reading.** "Changed whether p" can mean changed the answer, or changed p's status as settled or open. On the second, B&H also predict (i) ✓ and (ii) ✗, since the principal truths did not move. Only (iii) is meant to block this, and it fails. No number is a principal truth, so (iii) is vacuously true on the standard analysis too. (iv) inherits the ambiguity.
- **The 50% is yours, not B&H's.** The text concedes it assumes independent selections. So elicit the joint credence instead of testing a sentence. Present original A and extension B, and collect sliders for P(h_A), P(h_B) and P(h_A ∧ h_B). Independent selection predicts agreement at about .5. Defaults and the state view predict about 1.
- **This exposes a gap.** The draft never says what the state view predicts for a conjunction of claims evaluated in two different shifted states. It needs a joint state or cross-story coindexing of worlds. Without that, "the state view predicts (iii)" is an assertion.
- **Stimuli.** "The Gloria Scott" is obscure, and hair parity invites a "who cares?" refusal of any number. Use B&H's JAG coin, with a later episode that adds backstory and leaves the outcome open. Run it within subjects, with fillers and counterbalanced order.

**2. The King of France (§3).**
- **Not a minimal pair.** It changes the description to a pronoun, "is" to "would", and adds a supposition sentence.
- **It doesn't discriminate.** Modal subordination and local satisfaction (Roberts; Heim; from memory, check) let a supposition rescue a presupposition. An undefinedness theorist predicts the improvement too, so it cannot "tell against" that view.
- **Repair.** Vary form and modal separately. Add a third-person probe: "Ann, who knows France has no king, wonders whether the King of France is happy." Otherwise cut it.

**3. Framing.** Say the growth data are proposed, not collected. Soften "Our judgements side with the second" accordingly.

**Room.** Cut the Higginbotham paragraph (about 110 words; its example is conceded to fail) and the Suspense objection (about 45).
