## The convergent problem with §5 (01, 03, 08, 10)

**10, the close reader (decisive).**
- Data (i)–(iv) use explicit "in f" or work-level talk ("changed", "disagree", "the complete canon"). §3 itself treats that kind of talk as Lewis-style quantification over P-Fic.
- B&H's footnote 51 (p. 182) offers a variably strict Reality Principle as the theory of *principal* truth. That theory validates CV, so principal truth grows monotonically for B&H too.
- On that reading B&H predict (i)–(iv) as well. Only the bare "residue" reshuffles, and the data never reach it.
- Also: on the versions option nothing *changes* (p. 194), so (i)–(ii) bite only on time-indexing.

**01, the selection semanticist.**
- What does the damage is independence of the selections, not uniqueness.
- A lexicographic default ordering, decoupled from counterfactuals (so dropping the PRP), keeps FEM and gets (i)–(iv) right.
- The paper cannot call the PRP idle in §4 and treat losing it as fatal in §5.
- The draft's sentences "uniqueness does the damage" and "applies to any well-order" are false as stated.

**03, the imaging epistemologist.**
- Absorption needs two explicit bridges: a chance–selection link per antecedent, and the condition that the selections S leaves open are the image's support.
- It covers only single-fiction contents. B&H's joint distribution over (s(A,w), s(B,w)) is unconstrained.
- So "no attitude datum separates them" is false as stated, and §3 gives (i)/(iii) no value.
- Suggested fix: a coupled or refining shift; imaging must behave like revision.
- One sub-point is wrong. 03 says "sure of □_f p iff P-Fic ⊆ p" fails because non-principal truths (a spleen) are knowable. But B&H *define* the principal truths as "the fictional claims that can be known to be true in f—at least in principle" (p. 156), so anything knowable is principal and the step holds by their own definition. Note this in the paper.

**08, the experimental semanticist.**
- (i)/(ii) vary attitude and polarity at once.
- "Changed whether p" has a status reading on which B&H also predict the verdicts.
- (iii) is vacuously true on the standard analysis.
- The 50% is the author's assumption, not B&H's.
- The state view's predictions for cross-story claims are asserted, not derived.
- Present the data as proposed, not collected.

**Diagnosis.** The data must concern the truth of *bare* claims, B&H's residue, rather than what works settle. They must not presuppose independence. And the state view's prediction must be derived. The opponent's best escapes are defaults without the PRP (01), total narrative (draft), relativism about assessment (McGonigal-style), and anchoring intentions (04).

## Revised §5 (plan)

1. **Concede up front** (10). Principal truth grows monotonically on every view (B&H n. 51), so work-level comparisons cannot discriminate. The question is the residue.
2. **The PRP reshuffles the residue.** CV under CEM gives s(B,@) ≠ s(A,@). Under the PRP, similarity to @ carries no information about s(A,@)'s open answers, so the selections are near-independent (03: uniform indifference over admissible selection functions). Correct the "uniqueness does the damage" and "any well-order" sentences (01).
3. **Bare data.** Ann asserts in 1890 that Holmes had an odd number of hairs when he met Watson. Carl utters the same sentence in 2026.
   - **Versions:** Ann's and Carl's claims can differ in truth value. Datum: "They made the same claim about Holmes, so either both are right or neither is" ✓.
   - **Time-indexing:** Ann's claim can go from right to wrong through stories that say nothing about Holmes's hair. Datum: "Nothing published since 1890 could have made Ann's claim wrong" ✓; "It's quite possible Ann was right in 1890 and is wrong now" ✗.
   - These are claims about the truth of bare assertions, so the principal-truth reading is not available. They mirror McGonigal's cross-temporal "That's true" datum (2013: 173). Present them as proposed judgements (08).
4. **The state view's prediction, derived.** The shift conditions a "reality prior" π_w on P_f(w). Then B ⊆ A gives refinement (conditioning), which answers 03's imaging-as-revision point. "Right" means support. Ann's and Carl's claims are both unsupported: neither is right, and nothing changes.
5. **Escapes, as a dilemma.**
   - (a) **Defaults decoupled from the PRP** (01) save the data but drop the PRP. Then nothing but the attitude data and Scopelessness supports a single world, and §3–§4 show those do not require one. There is also a ranking problem under determinism (01's own point). Causal defaults within the PRP (grain dilemma) go in a footnote.
   - (b) **Path-dependence** also drops the PRP at @. One sentence plus a footnote (import-export; Darwiche–Pearl C1).
   - (c) **Total narrative:** eternalism about authorship, plus B&H's own Jesse text. Recategorised per 04: if *A Study in Scarlet* is a *part*, it has no world of its own; Ann's claim is then about the total, which needs eternalism.
   - (d) **Assessment relativism:** pushes the reshuffling into comparisons between contexts of assessment. In McGonigal's framework such shifts are driven by episodes that bear on the claim; here they would be driven by irrelevant ones.
   - (e) **Anchoring intention** (04): the sequel's principal truths include "this is the world of *A Study in Scarlet*". That intention is almost surely unsatisfiable, because s(A,@) already settles the sequel's subject matter at random. One sentence.
   - **Lesson:** the single world either mispredicts growth, if it is located by the PRP, or is located by nothing the data require.
