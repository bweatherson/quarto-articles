**Split the JAG case by how the production stood: only the never-decided ending needs B&H's hidden fact.**

**Where.** §2 "The data" (the stipulation about the writers); §6 "One of you is right"; the 2019 illustration at the end of §5.

**What is wrong.** The draft treats JAG as one kind of case and takes the stipulation that the writers "never thought about the events of JAG again" as the normal state of a finished episode. In the rooms I worked in it was the rarest of three states, and viewers talk about each differently.

1. *Decided but not shown.* The room had an answer and kept it. B&H's footnote 26 quotes Stein: "The show decided to leave us in suspense." A preview headlined "NCIS: LA Will Finally Reveal..." only makes sense if the answer existed before it aired. Fans say "somebody knows, and it isn't us." B&H's principal truths include what the author "intended or believed" to be true, so intention fixes the answer and no selection is needed. Dallas's "Who shot J.R.?" was held this way until it aired (from memory; check).

2. *Deliberately open.* The creators say they left it open on purpose. This is the interview in B&H's reviewer dialogue (165–66), which B&H answer by calling "fact of the matter" semitechnical and by rejecting the premise. Fans read such a statement as ending the canon hunt and move to headcanon. The reviewer reads it as a gap ("There isn't a fact of the matter"). If that is a principal truth, B&H's own RESTRICTED FEM (192) stops forcing a fact. The draft's "open, not gappy" line in "Deliberate openness" is where this must be argued.

3. *Never decided.* The stipulated case. The honest verdict is "neither of you is right; there is nothing to look up." Only here must B&H's selection supply a fact nobody decided.

**What to do.**
- Replace the "One of you is right" bullets with the three-way split, giving each verdict and what each view must say. Delete "I rest nothing on this": the split is the argument.
- In §2, add one sentence after the stipulation: the case that needs a hidden fact is the rarest kind in practice.
- Cut the 2019 illustration to two sentences. NCIS: LA is a separate series by a separate production, so its reveal shows what that production decided, not how JAG's state stood. For a same-series case of an open question being answered, use Dallas, with the same caveat.
- Room: the split adds about 110 words; the §5 cut frees about 80. Trim the Suspense paragraph to one sentence for the rest.
