**Withdraw the concession to Stalnaker's counterfactuals (§4) and resist B&H's counterfactual parallel at source: indeterminate counterfactuals are false, not hidden-true.**

**Where.** §4, l. 141 ("I can even grant Stalnaker's semantics for counterfactuals") and l. 140 ("credences in Stalnaker conditionals").

**What is wrong.** The parallel is B&H's main support for the PRP (pp. 182–183). The PRP needs uniqueness, and uniqueness yields CEM (p. 180). Granting Stalnaker's counterfactuals grants what B&H need. The paper then concedes the premise its own growth argument blames. Section 5 locates the damage in uniqueness, so deny it where B&H get it.

Three points tell against CEM for counterfactuals; B&H engage none.

1. **"Might" pair.** "If Flippy had been flipped, it might have landed heads, and it might have landed tails." To my ear both are true. On Stalnaker's semantics "might-p" just is "would-p" (C-Scopelessness), and CEM makes one of "would-heads" and "would-tails" true, so the pair cannot both hold. (From memory; check: Lewis 1973 on "might" counterfactuals.)

2. **Footnote 54.** B&H grant it is "determinately the case that these possibilities are equally similar." On Lewis's semantics every equally close world counts. "Would have landed heads" is then false, "might have landed tails" true. "Selects one at random, so to speak" (p. 184) describes plurality from inside a selection semantics.

3. **Wide-scope negation.** B&H test only the narrow "wouldn't" (33b). "It's not the case that Flippy would have landed heads" is, to my ear, true and knowable on Lewis's semantics. C-Scopelessness makes it equivalent to "would have landed tails," which B&H say cannot be known. No one has tested this. If the judgement holds, the parallel fails where the PRP needs it.

**What to do.**
- Replace l. 141 with: "Indeterminate counterfactuals are false and their 'might' counterfactuals true. Attitudes are evaluated against the plurality of closest antecedent-worlds, the state structure §3 gives fiction." Section 3 lets Fic_f stay plural (l. 87).
- Add the dilemma: CEM and uniqueness, or plurality and no single world. Either way the single-world thesis (p. 176) fails.
- Answer the counterfactual Higginbotham pair (B&H 40–41, p. 181), which B&H present as the main evidence for CEM. The fiction version at l. 111 does not do this.
- Pay for this, roughly 90 words, by compressing "Precursors" (ll. 118–122), which repeats §1, and cutting the parenthesis at l. 111.
- Revise the conclusion. "Lewis's verdict stands, though not for Lewis's reason" misreads §5. Plurality survives growth, since CV applies whenever the extension is compatible with some closest A-world (l. 155). Uniqueness does not. Lewis's verdict stands for Lewis's reason.

**Cost.** Lewis gives up Scopelessness for counterfactual negation, so the paper must say why §3's bilateral negation is not suspect too.
