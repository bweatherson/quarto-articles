**Say what the "story shift" is. Re-readers' hope shows it is an imaginative parameter, so your hope clause and your "ordinary verbs, not imaginative ones" boast both need revising.**

**Where:** the Introduction bullet ("*ordinary* attitude verbs, not sui generis imaginative ones"); §3's hope entry and the Blumberg–Strohminger paragraph; §6 "Suspense".

**The problem.** Everything in §3 runs through D_S⊕f, but the paper never says what makes a subject's state shift. As defined, it is S's ordinary information imaged onto what f settles. Your hope clause (D_S⊕f neither supports nor rejects φ) then says that someone who knows how *Romeo and Juliet* ends cannot hope Romeo gets the letter. §6 says this "troubles both views equally". It does not, in the way that matters. B&H can shrug: hope tracks ignorance of principal truths, and re-readers are not ignorant. Your view is offered as a mechanism, so it owes an account of this datum.

The datum is also the best evidence about what the shifted state is. It is not fixed by what the work settles plus what S knows. It is fixed by where S is imaginatively standing in the work. A re-reader can re-occupy a first-time reader's epistemic position at chapter three. That is an imaginative act, and it is what Walton's and Currie's accounts, and Gerrig's "anomalous suspense", point to (from memory; check).

So "ordinary verbs, not sui generis imaginative ones" is oversold. The verbs may be ordinary, but the state they take is made by imagining the story. An imagination theorist will say "shift" papers over exactly this, and that Williams and Woodward's "i-credence" already said so.

**What to do.**
1. Rewrite the Introduction bullet: attitude verbs keep ordinary entries, applied to the state produced by imagining f. Stay neutral on whether the attitudes themselves are sui generis. Absorption (§4) holds either way, and if they are imaginative, B&H's table is even weaker evidence for a hidden world.
2. In §3, let the shift take a reading position: P_f restricted to what S is imagining as revealed, defaulting to everything S knows. That is one clause in the hope entry. Re-reader hope is then predicted rather than anomalous, and "wish" gets the same treatment.
3. Move "Suspense" into §3 as a short paragraph. Soften "no sui generis attitude is needed". Blumberg and Strohminger's (11a) shows that hope is constrained by belief about the story, but imaginative accounts predict that too, since what we imagine is constrained by what we believe (Walton's Reality Principle).

**Net words.** About +100. Cut the "Higginbotham pairs" paragraph (~110 words), which is peripheral and concedes that its example needs repair. Cut the Precursors bullets (~35) and the Khoo sentence under "Explicit operators" (~35).
