**Make growth the paper: lead with the Gloria Scott datum and cut §3–§4 to what it needs.**

**Where.** §1 (lines 17–28), §3 (68–122), §4 (124–143), §5 (145–196).

**What is wrong.** Line 28 calls growth "the main contribution," but page one does not show it. §1 announces "three claims" (18–20), then "what I add" in four bullets (25–28). The first bullet is a semantics, though line 24 calls the view "not new in spirit." Growth, the one thing that makes this more than a reply to B&H, is not previewed. Its datum first appears in §5, after about 2,800 words.

§3 is hard for a non-specialist and not finished. "Img" is never defined. The measure on s⊕f is described ("spread by in-story chance and, where there is none, by typicality") but never given, and "typicality" is never defined. A union of sets carries no measure, yet the table at line 99 depends on one. The verb entries (92–97) are a list a table could carry.

§5's diagnosis, that uniqueness rather than selection does the damage, sits in one dense paragraph (line 155) of CV, CEM and Lewis 1973. A generalist cannot find the paper's most interesting claim there.

Datum (iii) presupposes a determinate count: "whatever number of hairs Holmes had ... in A Study in Scarlet." That presupposition is what is in dispute, so a referee may hear (iii) as B&H's own claim.

**What to do.**
1. Rewrite §1 around growth: the thesis (line 22), the Gloria Scott test in one paragraph, and one sentence each on the state view and on B&H's prediction.
2. Restate (iii) without the presupposition: "Did *The Gloria Scott* settle any question about Holmes's hairs that *A Study in Scarlet* left open?" The state view says no. On B&H's view the answer is about even odds.
3. Cut §3 to the shift and its refinement property. Move the bilateral definitions and verb entries into one table. Give the measure, or say what the argument needs of it, and define "typicality" or drop it. Delete the Higginbotham paragraph; its last sentence ("The example can be repaired") does not belong in a submission.
4. Put the CV/CEM point in plain prose at the head of §5, with the formal statement in a footnote.
5. Bring §3–§4 to about 800 words combined, from about 1,550. That frees the room for step 1.
