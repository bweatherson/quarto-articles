**The growth argument refutes the PRP, not the single world. Make fiction's tie-breaking lexicographic and FEM survives with (i)–(iv) right. The only cost is the PRP, which your own §4 calls idle.**

Your weakest premise is one you flag in §5 and then drop: "the half assumes the two selections are independent draws." Selection semantics doesn't require independence; it comes from treating tie-breaking as a lottery. B&H concede (184) that similarity can't choose between odd and even hairs. So on their view tie-breaking settles every open answer, and nothing in it says the tie-breaks for nested antecedents are unrelated.

Two sentences are therefore false:
- "What does the damage is … uniqueness." Break a Stalnaker ordering's ties lexicographically, over ranked natural questions with fixed, unknown default answers. You still get one world per antecedent, and CEM and CV hold. If B ⊆ A and a listed question is independent of B given the higher-ranked defaults, s(B, @) keeps s(A, @)'s answer to it. So uniqueness survives and the hairs stay put. What does the damage is independence.
- "The argument above applies to any well-order." Lexicographic defaults are such an order; your next bullet shows they fix the hairs.

That leaves one objection to defaults, the moth. The PRP makes counterfactuals share the ordering, which freezes chancy outcomes. So stop the sharing:
- Counterfactuals keep their own selection.
- The fiction operator picks the P-Fic world the default ordering ranks first.
- FEM, Scopelessness, uniqueness and unknowability survive, and so does your §4 absorption, given the chance–selection link you already grant B&H.
- In-fiction counterfactuals ("had Bud tossed harder") use the ordinary selection, so nothing chancy freezes.

The cost is the slogan that fictional truth *is* counterfactual truth. But you can't call the PRP idle in §4 ("Against the state view it draws") and then treat losing it as fatal in §5. B&H themselves "lack the space to argue for" it (182).
