**Separate revision, part, sequel and canon before running the growth argument: it bites hardest on sequels, and Repair 3 escapes only because "version" and "earlier stage" are run together.**

§5 treats "version", "stage", "later story" and "complete canon" as interchangeable. The serial-art literature (McGonigal, Caplan, Walters, Lee; from memory; check) separates four relations, and B&H's replies differ across them:

- *Revision*: a superseded version of a work the author took to be complete (Gilligan, B&H §11.3).
- *Part*: an instalment of one planned work (Dickens in numbers). The whole has the world; the instalment has none of its own.
- *Sequel or prequel*: a separate work in a shared universe. "The Gloria Scott" is this.
- *Canon*: a designation by author, estate or studio, which includes some works and excludes tie-ins and pastiches. "The complete canon" is a designated fusion of works, not a work.

**1. Repair 3's first cost is miscategorised.** You say the 1887 stories "are such a version" as B&H's "first version" (193–194). They are not; B&H mean a superseded state of the work. Trace the options:
- If *A Study in Scarlet* is a part, it has no world of its own, (iv) is false by category, and only eternalism remains.
- If it is a version, you get time-indexing.
- If it is a separate work, B&H's individuation of works by worlds makes its world independent of the canon's. Then (iii) and (iv) concern Holmes's identity across works, and B&H owe an account.

Their best account is an anchoring intention: the sequel's principal truths include "this is the world of *A Study in Scarlet*". Add it as Repair 4 and refute it briefly: the sequel's own content (Holmes's college years) is among what s(A,@) already settles, at random, so the intention is almost surely unsatisfiable. Make the sequel the paradigm case.

**2. "Consistent extension" needs defining.** B ⊆ A requires more than logical consistency. Principal truths include defeasible implicatures, and consistent sequels routinely withdraw them (a narrator revealed as unreliable; "from a certain point of view"). Define *pure extension* as one that leaves A's principal content intact, and scope claim 3 to it. The real Holmes canon is also inconsistent (Watson's wound), so its P-Fic is empty. "Let us suppose" does real work; say so, or use a planned series. Check, too, your claim that the literature treats pure addition as unproblematic.

**3. The JAG paragraph shifts its index.** "The franchise's state" quietly makes the NCIS: LA episode part of the fiction at issue. Say that "JAG leaves it open" and "the franchise settles it" are true at different indices.
