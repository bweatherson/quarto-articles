**Absorption needs two bridge principles and covers only single-fiction contents. §5's data fall outside it, and there your §3 semantics gets (i) and (iii) wrong.**

*Where:* §4; §5 ("The state view predicts the judgements"); §7 ("no attitude datum separates").

**1. What §4 can prove.** For a fixed s, Lewis's 1976 identity is definitional: C(□_f p) is C imaged by s. Reaching the *general* image needs a bridge principle, CSL: given each cell of base facts, S's credence about where s(P_f(w), w) lands equals the imaging weights. CSL is a Skyrms-style norm, not B&H's indifference. It also fails once credence is conditioned on where the selection lands. Put all credence on one world and the Stalnaker image is 0 or 1; the general image is not (cf. Williams 2012, from memory; check).

The qualitative step needs a second bridge, and as written it is wrong. "Sure of □_f p iff P-Fic ⊆ p" is false for B&H. Their selection is arbitrary only "modulo large differences in similarity" (184), so some non-principal truths (Holmes has a spleen) can be known. The bridge you need: the selections S leaves open are exactly the image's support.

Given both bridges, Absorption is a theorem. Under CEM it extends to compounds and conditional credences within f. Prove it in a paragraph.

**2. What it cannot cover.** CSL fixes credences one antecedent at a time. A general image decomposes into Stalnaker selections in many ways, so the joint distribution of (s(A,w), s(B,w)) stays open. Every §5 datum is an attitude toward that joint. So "no attitude datum separates" is false. Say instead: attitudes toward one fiction cannot discriminate; attitudes across fictions can.

**3. The gap.** For B&H, indifference taken as uniform over admissible selection functions yields near-independence. That can replace your hedge.

For you, §3 has no joint: each f:φ gets its own shift. Your anti-FEM clause supports a disjunction only if some disjunct is supported. Neither (f_A:odd ∧ f_B:odd) nor (f_A:even ∧ f_B:even) is supported, so "the two agree" is not supported. Hence (i) and (iii) come out ✗, and (ii) and (iv) get no credence. "Refines the state" relates two states. It gives (iii) no semantic value.

*Fix:* add a coupled shift that pairs each world of s⊕B with itself in s⊕A, and evaluate claims across fictions pointwise. This works only if each A-image gives B positive weight and the B-image is the A-image conditioned on B. Imaging must act like revision, not update (Katsuno and Mendelzon 1991, from memory; check). Say why your images are wide enough; otherwise your "divergence is cheap" objection to Repair 2 applies to you.
