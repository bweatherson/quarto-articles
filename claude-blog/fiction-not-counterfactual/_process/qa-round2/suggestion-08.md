**Growth tells against the PRP, not against single worlds, so the paper needs an explicit parsimony ledger to remove the single world.**

**Where.** §4 (Absorption), §5 ("The lesson"), §6 ("Classical conservatism"), Conclusion.

**Problem.** Growth tells against the PRP's random selection. But the defaults repair (§5(a)) keeps a single world that gets (1)–(4) right by construction, so "supported by nothing in the data" overstates. The defensible claim is comparative: the data tie. The paper never states its criterion for theory choice, never mentions simplicity, and dismisses classical logic in one clause that assumes the cost it is trying to show. Judgements (1)–(4) are the author's own and need testing.

**Ledger.** Both views need π_w. The chance–selection link, which B&H's indifference claim (175) requires, makes their selection's distribution π_w, so the §4 credences coincide. The single world adds one thing, a determinate selection, whose only work beyond the attitudes is external bivalence. The fragment already covers what B&H cite FEM for. The unimpeachable inference follows from Scopelessness (§3), and "exactly one of (9a) or (9b) is true" is internally true. The Higginbotham equivalence (B&H 181) needs no FEM, since (42a) and (42b) are both internal with the same content. B&H reach it via (43b), which has external negation. The paper should argue that (42b) lacks that reading.

**Costs to count.** The story-state view has trivalent support and rejection and two truth notions, not obviously simpler than a selection function. Refinement (§3, item 3) holds for support, which §5 uses, but its credal claim is false, because C⊕f′ keeps candidate weights fixed. Take C(w₁) = C(w₂) = ½, π_{w₁} uniform on {x, y, z}, π_{w₂} concentrated on x, and P′ = {x, y}. Then C⊕f′ = (¾, ¼, 0), but C⊕f conditioned on P′ = (⅘, ⅕, 0). A Bayesian update needs candidates reweighted, one more posit. And granting Stalnaker's counterfactual semantics, with CEM there and homogeneity for fiction, gives up the truth-conditional unification the PRP offers.

**What to do.**
1. Restate the conclusion comparatively: no datum in §§4–5 favours the single world over the story state.
2. Add a short table of posits and virtues for each view, with the criterion stated: empirical adequacy, then posit count, then bivalence.
3. Quote B&H's trade-off (188): they "would sooner accept" facts "whose grounds will in many cases be almost entirely arbitrary." Then argue, using the Higginbotham point, that bivalence is not worth that cost.
4. Correct Refinement to a support claim, or add the reweighting posit.
5. Either extend homogeneity to counterfactuals or state the exchange rate for the concession.

**Room.** The additions are about 150 words; the draft is about 5,000. To stay near 5,000, cut the "Re-readers" paragraph in §6.
