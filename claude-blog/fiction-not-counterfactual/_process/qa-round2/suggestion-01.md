*Where.* Repair (a) and note [^causal]. You say a correlating order "cannot be the counterfactual selection function" because it would hold a chancy outcome fixed under "had Bud tossed harder" and "had a moth flown past". Ordinary judgement separates these. "Had Bud tossed harder, it would still have landed heads" is bad. "Had a moth been in the room, it would still have landed heads" is fine; it belongs to the Morgenbesser family you cite. Your grain argument needs the coin to depend on the moth. It doesn't.

*The escape.* B&H make selection arbitrary only "modulo large differences in similarity" (184). So order worlds by similarity to @, and break near-ties by agreement with one fixed assignment to the exogenous variables of the apt causal model, the same assignment for every antecedent. A well-order yields a Stalnaker selection function, with CEM, success and centering, and this one is the counterfactual selection function. Force is a parent of the outcome, so "tossed harder" redraws the coin. The moth is not a variable, so the outcome stays put. Nothing in "The Gloria Scott" is a parent of Holmes's hair count, so s(B, @) keeps the parity of s(A, @), and (1)–(4) come out right. The PRP is untouched. "At random, so to speak" becomes one draw of the assignment, not a fresh draw per antecedent; your §4 grants that nothing fixes the joint.

*Costs.* (i) The hidden facts become relative to the apt model, hence to context. But so are counterfactuals, and the PRP never promised sturdier facts than counterfactuals have. (ii) I bite one bullet. An addition that narrates a parent of an open question without settling it can redraw the answer: a later story in which Holmes's hair was cropped the morning he met Watson may redraw the parity.
