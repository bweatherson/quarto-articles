**Move the growth argument (§5) to directly after §2, and present §3–§4 as the rival account that answers B&H's attitude data.**

The paper's new claim is growth, but a reader reaches it only after about 2,600 words, over half the draft. Of those, §3 and §4 supply about 1,300 words of formal apparatus, which answers a narrower question: whether B&H's attitude data favour the PRP. By the time "The residue under the PRP" arrives, a referee who is not a semanticist has lost the thread. The argument reads better as a sequence: the PRP gets some of (1)–(4) wrong on either option; its support is the attitude profile and Scopelessness; a story-state account predicts that profile without a single world; the repairs fail.

Concretely:

- Move "Monotonic growth," "The residue under the PRP" and "The data" to follow §2, with one sentence naming the rival. They use only the PRP, Stalnaker's success condition and B&H's options at 193–194.
- Then §3 and §4, now framed as the answer to the attitude argument.
- Then "What the story state predicts" and "Repairs," which cite Refinement and §3's claim that Scopelessness needs no single world.
- Reorder the introduction's claims so that growth comes first.
- Move the Absorption derivations to a note, keeping the result in one sentence. This helps readability only: notes count toward the 6,000 words, so nothing is freed.

Two fixes come with the move:

- Set (1)–(4) out as a table, with columns for the judgement, the versions option, time-indexing and the story state. At present the same ✓ and ✗ mark the author's judgements and B&H's predictions a sentence apart, so the argument cannot be seen on one screen. The 1/2 and 1/4 also need the two selections to be independent. §5 mentions this only in passing, and the introduction's "about even odds" does not mention it.
- Refinement's credal half is false as written. The mixture of per-world conditionals equals the conditional of the mixture only after the starting worlds are reweighted by how likely each makes the addition. With equal weights on two worlds that give {a, b} probabilities .5 and .8, the mixture gives a .625 where conditioning the mixture gives .654. The support half, which §5's verdicts need, is correct. The "Bayesian update" gloss should go, or gain the reweighting step.
