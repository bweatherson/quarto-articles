# Idea memo v1: a reply to Blumberg & Holguín, "Fictional Reality" (Phil Review 134(2), 2025)

Working title: "Open Questions in Fiction" (or "Fiction Without Hidden Facts").

Process status:
- **Pretest:** the core idea was PREDICTED. My prompt leaked the theme. The tester's own top blind family was "attitudes relative to the story's information", so the core is the obvious reply.
- **Canon check:** PARTLY ANTICIPATED. The core is in Williams & Woodward 2021, "The Cognitive Role of Fictionality" (PPR 102: 423–38): "i-credence" matches the conditional probability of p given the fiction, and there are "no fictional facts of the matter". They even use a fair-vs-biased die ending. B&H cite W&W three times, but only for examples, and never engage its central proposal.
- **Existing replies:** Oppy & Raivars 2026 deny or deflate the data. Khoo 2026 holds that informed agents have no non-trivial credence. Khoo's §4 on fiction is unread (paywalled; requested from BW).

## The target in one paragraph

B&H's argument runs in four steps:
1. **The data.** Attitudes toward fictionally indeterminate claims pattern as follows: know ✗, sure ✗, think ✓ (biased coin), doubt ✓, credence ✓, wonder ✓, wish ✗, hope ✓.
2. **Against the standard analysis.** That analysis (fictional truth = principal truth) predicts "I know whether Bud's coin landed heads", via Attitude Equivalence. So it gets the data wrong, and so does an undefinedness view.
3. **Scopelessness.** The inference "I don't know it landed heads; I don't know it failed to land heads; so I don't know whether it landed heads" requires Scopelessness. That yields a unique world of the fiction and FEM: every question about a fiction has an answer, fixed by arbitrary Stalnakerian selection via the Principal Reality Principle.
4. **The parallel.** Counterfactuals show the same profile, and vagueness shows a different one.

## Thesis (four parts)

**(1) The data are the profile of attitudes held relative to a story's information state, not uncertainty about hidden facts.**
- Make W&W's evidentialism semantic. Ordinary attitude verbs with bare internal complements are evaluated against the subject's state, shifted by the story. Technically, an information-state parameter is revised by the story's principal truths: support semantics, with credences given by general imaging, which spreads mass by in-story chance or typicality.
- Know and sure mean support in the shifted state. Credence is the shifted measure. Think means high shifted credence or best guess. Wonder means the question is open in the shifted state. Wish needs ¬p supported. Hope needs p open.
- This one parameter delivers B&H's whole table and makes inference (28) valid (negation is evaluated in the shifted state). It gives the vagueness contrast (no shift, and semantic indecision supplies no measure). It also gives the counterfactual column, if counterfactual attitudes are likewise suppositional (Bennett/Edgington/Khoo 2022), though the paper can stay neutral on that.
- It escapes B&H's §7 objections: no attitude-specific projection rules are needed, and there are no abominable conjunctions, because internal "true" is disquotational.
- New datum: B&H's King-of-France control flips under explicit supposition. "Suppose France still had a king. I wonder whether he'd be happy" is ✓. What matters is whether a supposition is in force, not the kind of gap.

**(2) Absorption: B&H's view and the story-relative view are equivalent on every attitude datum.**
- By Lewis 1976, credence in a Stalnaker conditional P-Fic > p equals imaged credence on P-Fic. B&H's credences over selections must be chance-guided to fit (13)–(16), and Schulz and Bacon, cited in their fn. 59, supply that. So the two views coincide.
- The attitude data therefore cannot support FEM. B&H's abductive argument fails because it compares FEM only with the standard analysis-plus-Attitude-Equivalence and with undefinedness. It omits the rival already in their own bibliography (W&W).

**(3) What discriminates the views: how open questions get closed (the revelation dilemma).**
- **The case.** B&H's own flagship case was closed by later canon. In NCIS: Los Angeles (season 10 finale, May 2019), "Mac won the toss, and Harm passed on a JAG job in London to join Mac in San Diego" (CinemaBlend, 21 May 2019, via a page summary; verify against the episode). On JAG's terms that is tails. B&H's fn. 27 cites a 2019 piece headlined "NCIS: LA Will Finally Reveal Who Won the Coin Flip on the JAG Finale" as evidence that no one knows. Practice treated the 2019 episode as a *revelation*.
- **On B&H's view.** If the franchise is one fiction (time-indexed or "final version"), then before 2019 fans' attitudes were uncertainty about future canon. That needs no hidden facts, because future canon is an ordinary future contingent about authors. If 2005-JAG and the franchise are distinct fictions, then 2005-JAG has a hidden outcome that the 2019 episode did not reveal. B&H must give credence .5 that NCIS: LA's writers, purporting to say what happened after JAG's finale, got it wrong (or retconned it). Either way the hidden fact is idle wherever anything could bear on it, and present only where nothing could.
- **On the story-relative view.** A legitimate continuation adds support, and an open question gets answered. Continuations never err about open questions (a continuation-authority principle). Retcons are reserved for overturning what was supported (Lee, forthcoming, on retcon).

**(4) Reconciling the dispute over the data.**
- B&H's ✓ judgements track the bare internal reading.
- Khoo's denial of informed credence and Oppy & Raivars's denial of rational wonder track the explicit external reading: "it is true in / according to JAG that…". Khoo's fn. 29 uses "In the fiction of F, p".
- Neither side's ears are wrong. B&H mistake an internal profile for evidence about the external relation of truth-in-fiction.

## Further material
B&H's examples, re-examined:
- Sam Gamgee's later life is largely settled by LOTR Appendix B: Mayor for seven terms, thirteen children.
- Whether Deckard is a replicant is a disputed principal truth (Scott vs Ford), not known indeterminacy.
- *The Breakfast Club* has an essay, not a test, so (42)'s "the test" fails its presupposition.
- (15)–(16) use epistemic-modal "It's .5 likely", which has an in-story-chance reading.

Mixed quantification ((42), Higginbotham): read internally and de re about the characters; any residue goes to homogeneity (Križ).

## The general lesson (to make it more than a reply)
Felicity of graded, inquisitive and bouletic attitudes toward p is not evidence that p is factual when a background information state (a story, a supposition) carries the uncertainty. What does diagnose factuality is how questions get closed: settlement, revelation, error. This bears on CEM-from-credence and future-contingent arguments.

## Risks and open items
- **Precursor.** W&W is the precursor and must be front and centre. The paper's own contributions are the semantics, the absorption argument, the revelation dilemma, and the reconciliation.
- **Khoo §4** might pre-empt (1) or (4).
- **Supposition vs imagination.** Affective attitudes like hope and wish suggest imagination (W&W, Kind, Arcangeli). The account should be framed as an information-state shift that is neutral between the two.
- **Anomalous suspense.** Re-readers still hope Romeo gets the letter.
- **Blumberg & Strohminger 2025** (*Analysis*, "Imaginative hopes and other desires") argues against imaginative-hope principles. It is unread.
- **Judgements.** The revelation data are practice and judgement, not experiment.
