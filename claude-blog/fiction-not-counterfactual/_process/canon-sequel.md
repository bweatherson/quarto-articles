# Canon check: the sequel argument against Blumberg & Holguín (B&H)

## 1. Verdict: NOT FOUND

No one has argued that a unique-world, CEM-style theory of fiction makes consistent sequels reshuffle the original's open details. The formal core is textbook, so present it as an application, not a discovery.

## 2. Soundness check

**Step 2 is correct.**
- The "only if" direction needs only success: s(B,@) ∈ B.
- The "if" direction needs Stalnaker's condition (4). B&H never state it; fn. 46, p. 180, lists only success and centering. The argument does not need it.
- The cleanest logical form: CV (rational monotonicity) protects A's consequences against a consistent addition B whenever ¬(A > ¬B). Under CEM, ¬(A > ¬B) ≡ A > B. CV therefore fires only when the sequel's content was already true at the selected world.
- On Lewis's variably strict semantics, CV fires whenever B is compatible with *some* closest A-world, which is the normal case. So C-Uniqueness, not selection as such, does the damage.
- "~never" holds on B&H's gloss: "the selection function selects one at random, so to speak" (p. 184).

**Step 3 is right about per-antecedent arbitrary selection, but overstates the counterfactual point.**
- The chaotic coin shows that strengthening by a *causally relevant* conjunct (a harder toss) can flip the outcome.
- A sequel's new content is mostly causally downstream of, or irrelevant to, the 1881 breakfast. For that kind of strengthening, counterfactual judgement favours preservation:
  - Morgenbesser cases;
  - Schaffer 2004, *Analysis* 64;
  - Kment 2006, *Mind* 115;
  - interventionist semantics (Briggs 2012, *Phil Studies* 160).
- So "non-separability is correct for counterfactuals" does not follow.

**Step 4 needs qualifying.**
- **The .5 figure assumes the selections from A and from B are independent draws.** B&H's semantics is silent on joint credence over selections.
- **"Change" arises only for a growing fiction:** the canon as a serial, TV series (B&H's JAG and Breaking Bad), or a Caplan/Lee view of changing work content. For distinct works (*A Study in Scarlet*, "Scandal") the cost is disagreement between versions.
- **B&H already accept such disagreement for revisions:** versions' truths "(whether principal or otherwise) can come quite dramatically apart" (p. 194). The argument must show why disagreement is worse when the later fiction *contains* the earlier. Caplan's contradiction problem supplies this (§3).

**Step 5: the repairs.**
- **(a) A fixed tie-breaking well-order is not a repair.**
  - Stalnaker's condition (4) amounts to selection by a well-order, so step 2 holds for every well-order: min(B) ≠ min(A) whenever min(A) ∉ B.
  - A well-order helps only if it is lexicographic over privileged macro-questions with default answers.
  - That version has exactly the claimed cost: the chaotic coin's outcome becomes rigid under every strengthening that leaves it logically open (harder, left-handed, at 12:01).
- **(b) A missed, cheaper repair: interventionist selection with one fixed default assignment to the exogenous variables.**
  - Here arbitrariness is drawn once per exogenous variable, not once per antecedent. Selection is separable at the exogenous level but sensitive at the outcome level.
  - Hence:
    - the coin flips under relevant strengthening;
    - Morgenbesser cases are preserved;
    - the eggs survive "Scandal";
    - there is no path-dependence;
    - PRP-at-@ is kept, relative to the default.
  - It validates CEM for interventional antecedents, as deterministic structural models with a complete exogenous assignment do.
  - Its costs are real but different:
    1. It needs a privileged causal factorisation of logical space, with exogenous variables for merely possible individuals that have no actual values: in effect, a default world for possibilia.
    2. PRP's antecedent is not an intervention. Backtracking story content needs an abduction step, and non-interventional antecedents in such models are unsettled.
    3. Content causally *upstream* of an open detail still reshuffles it: prequels and backstory, such as "The Gloria Scott" (Holmes's college years). At fine grain, anything in the detail's past light cone.

    Coarse grain protects the breakfast but also makes the coin insensitive to a moth near the toss. PRP shares the selection function, so the grain must be shared.
  - **The argument should be rebuilt around prequels and this grain dilemma.** As stated, its horn (5a) is not established against (b).
- **(c) Path-dependence: the claimed costs are confirmed, and there are more.**
  - s(B, s(A,@)) is the nested conditional A > (B′ > p). Its divergence from (A∧B′) > p is the failure of import-export for Stalnaker conditionals.
  - It violates Darwiche & Pearl's (1997) C1: if β ⊨ α, then B(E∗α∗β) = B(E∗β). Booth & Meyer 2006, *JAIR* 26, gloss C1 thus: the first input "is made redundant by the second".
  - Release format would matter (weekly versus binge).
  - Under Lewis's 1979 similarity (divergence cheap, convergence costly), even path-dependent selection reshuffles everything downstream of prequel content.
- **(d) Final-version realism (missed horn).** B&H's own default under option 1 is that ordinary talk "is directed at the final version" (p. 193). This is McGonigal's "extreme realism". There is no change, but the cost moves:
  - into disagreement between part and whole;
  - into dependence on future episodes for serials still running.

**Step 6 is not new.** Refinable story-states are in Lewis 1978 (variably strict, so CV fires), Stalnaker's 1980 supervaluation, Khoo §4, W&W's evidence view and Badura & Berto's AGM revision (K\*7 and K\*8 give K\*(A∧B) = (K\*A)+B when B is consistent with K\*A). The contribution is the discriminating datum, not the alternative.

## 3. Closest prior work, and what the argument adds

**Caplan 2014, p. 70.** This is the structural ancestor.
> "Together, (A1)–(A3) entail that Hope is a part of an extended fiction that doesn't contradict itself but that it nonetheless contradicts. That seems, if not contradictory, at least weird."

Caplan's problem arises from an explicit retcon (Obi-Wan's "betrayed and murdered"). FEM makes it ubiquitous: with probability ≈1, an original contradicts a consistent canon containing it on about half the questions both leave open. Caplan's solution (a work's content changes over time) is B&H's time-indexing horn, which brings change to open details.

**McGonigal 2013.** His "extreme realism" posits "an unknown but nevertheless determinate matter of fact about what happens within the fictional world, before later episodes are created or released" (p. 168). His cross-temporal datum is (g), "That's true—Luke is Darth's son" (p. 173). He concerns matters later episodes settle; the sequel argument concerns matters *no* episode settles.

**Lee 2024, p. 669.**
> "In retconning, a fact in a specific fictional world established in an earlier work is changed by a later work."

On B&H's time-indexed horn, every consistent sequel is a silent retcon of about half the original's open details. Lee defends content change with preserved identity (B&H's option 2), but discusses only overt retcons of determinate truths.

**Others.**
- **Cameron 2012** (quoted by Caplan, p. 70): *A New Hope* "leaves open what [the Darth Vader character in Hope] has for breakfast according to the fiction". Breakfast is the paradigm open detail, but within a gap theory.
- **Khoo 2026 §4:** "Let i be a choice function that outputs a unique world from any set of worlds" (p. 1209). No coherence across sets, but he supervaluates over i, so no sequel problem.
- **O&R** speak of "extensions of JAG" informally, with no reshuffling argument.
- **B&H §11.3** treats only non-monotonic revision.

**What the argument adds:**
1. It applies the collapse of CV under CEM to the growth of fiction.
2. It supplies a datum that attitude reports cannot provide, given the imaging equivalence: details open in both works stay stable under consistent extension. This separates unique-world views from story-state views.
3. It offers a trilemma. Once repaired to face (b) and (d), it becomes a dilemma between prequel reshuffling and either a privileged causal factorisation or path-dependence.
