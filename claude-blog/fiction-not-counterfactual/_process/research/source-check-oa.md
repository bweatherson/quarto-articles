## 1. Schaffer (2004), "Counterfactuals, causal independence and conceptual circularity", *Analysis* 64(4): 299–309

**File:** `schaffer2004-counterfactuals.pdf` (+ `.txt`). Source: https://www.jonathanschaffer.org/counterfactuals.pdf (the author's website). It is the published Blackwell offprint with journal pagination. Page 299 is shared with the end of Morton's paper.

**Claim:** discusses Morgenbesser's coin and defends holding fixed the facts that are causally independent of the antecedent. **CONFIRMED (with a caveat on wording).**

1. p. 303: "(Morgenbesser's coin) At indeterministic w11, while the coin is in mid-air, Lucky bets heads. The coin lands tails, so Lucky loses. The following counterfactual seems intuitively true at w11: 'If Lucky had bet tails, he would have won.'"
2. p. 305: "only match among those facts causally independent of the antecedent should count towards similarity."
3. pp. 306–307: "the outcome of the flip is causally independent of Lucky's bet, while the outcome of the bet is not. Hence w12 now comes out closer to w11 than w13 does."

**Caveats:**
- Schaffer does not talk about "holding fixed". His mechanism is that match only in causally independent regions counts towards similarity. He builds this into Lewis's weights as (2c) and (4c) on p. 305, and the result is that the flip outcome is preserved.
- He presents the proposal as an illustration rather than a full defence. See the closing paragraph of §3 on p. 307.
- In fn. 15 on p. 307 he grants that, strictly, there will be subtle causal influences of the bet on the coin.

## 4. von Fintel (1997), "Bare plurals, bare conditionals, and *only*", *Journal of Semantics* 14: 1–56

**File:** `vonfintel1997-bare.pdf` (+ `.txt`). Source: http://mit.edu/fintel/fintel-1997-bare.pdf (the author's MIT web space). It is a scan of the published version, and its PDF page numbers equal the journal page numbers. The OCR is imperfect, so I checked the quotations below against page images.

**Claim:** proposes a homogeneity (excluded-middle) presupposition for bare conditionals, explaining why the negation of a conditional behaves like the conditional with negated consequent. **CONFIRMED (with a caveat).**

1. p. 33 (§7.2.2): "GEN is lexically specified to trigger a Homogeneity Presupposition, which means that generic bare plural sentences and bare conditional sentences will obey the Excluded Middle"
2. p. 31: "If we now deny the truth of a conditional *if p, q*, what we are saying is that the selected *p*-case is not a *q*-case. This amounts to asserting that the selected *p*-case is a *non-q*-case."
3. p. 35: "I don't know whether it is crucial that we assume that Homogeneity is a presupposition."

**Notes:**
- Quote 2 is von Fintel's statement of how *Stalnaker's* semantics validates the Excluded Middle. His own route is the homogeneity presupposition on GEN, given as (75)–(76) on p. 33, which covers generics and bare conditionals together.
- A counterfactual example appears as (77d) on p. 34.
- Quote 3 shows he hedges on whether homogeneity is a presupposition or a pragmatic inference.
- The paper's main topic is *only*. The homogeneity proposal is in §7.

## 5. MacFarlane (2003), "Future contingents and relative truth", *Philosophical Quarterly* 53: 321–336

**File:** `macfarlane2003-futcon-offprint.pdf` (+ `.txt`). Source: https://johnmacfarlane.net/futcon-offprint.pdf (the author's site, labelled "[published version]"). PDF page n is journal page 320+n. I confirmed the numbering on rendered images of pp. 325 and 331.

The earlier file `macfarlane2003.pdf` in the same folder is a 404 HTML page, not the paper.

**(a) Claim:** truth is relative to a context of assessment, and a sentence is true as assessed from a context iff it is true on all histories through that context. **CONFIRMED (with a precision note).**

1. p. 322: "We must relativize the truth of utterances to a context of assessment, and we must relativize the truth of sentences to both a context of utterance and a context of assessment."
2. p. 331, his gloss on the displayed "Double time reference postsemantics": "We evaluate φ with respect to the moment of utterance and all of the histories passing through both it and the moment of assessment"
3. p. 332: "Jake's utterance at m0 is true as assessed from m1, false as assessed from m2, and neither true nor false as assessed from m0."

**Precision note:** the sentence is evaluated at the moment of *utterance*, on every history that passes through that moment *and* (if it is later) through the moment of assessment. So "all histories through that context" is a simplification.

**(b) Claim:** criticises the "thin red line" and attributes the term or the criticism to Belnap and Green (1994). **CONFIRMED.**

1. p. 325: "Out of all the possible futures at the moment of utterance, one is marked out as 'the actual future', as if with a 'thin red line'"
2. p. 325: "My view is that the eating precludes the having. Like Belnap and Green, I hold that positing a thin red line amounts to giving up objective indeterminism."
3. p. 325, fn. 5: "The metaphor is due to Nuel Belnap and Mitchell Green, 'Indeterminism and the Thin Red Line', in J. Tomberlin (ed.), *Philosophical Perspectives* 8: *Logic and Language* (Atascadero: Ridgeview, 1994), pp. 365–88"

**Note:** fn. 10 on p. 330 says his own formal "thin red line postsemantics" is "not touched by Belnap and Green's semantic arguments". His objection (pp. 325–326) is his own, though it agrees with theirs.

## 6. Badura & Berto (2019), "Truth in fiction, impossible worlds, and belief revision", *AJP* 97(1): 178–193

**File:** `badura-berto2018-standrews-VoR.pdf` (+ `.txt`). Source: St Andrews Research Repository, handle 10023/15951, file `Badura_2018_AJP_Truthinfiction_CC.pdf`.

- **What the file is:** not just the accepted manuscript. It is Taylor & Francis's open-access version of record (CC BY-NC-ND), "Published online: 27 Feb 2018".
- **Page numbers:** this online-first version is paginated 1–16, and the page numbers below refer to it. It probably maps to issue pp. 178–193 (p. n = p. 177+n), but I have not checked that against the issue.
- **Earlier file:** `badura-berto2019.pdf` in the same folder is an HTML error page from an earlier attempt.

**Claim:** models truth in fiction using (non-standard) belief revision with impossible worlds. **CONFIRMED.**

1. p. 1 (abstract): "we expand Lewis's possible worlds apparatus by adding non-normal or impossible worlds. Second, we model truth in fiction as (make-believed) belief revision via ideas from dynamic epistemic logic."
2. p. 6 (§4): "Our account is based on Lewis's Analysis 2, expanded via the addition of (a) impossible worlds and (b) an apparatus modelling our understanding of fiction as a kind of belief revision."
3. p. 11: "A is true in fiction f iff every f-world, considered by group G most plausible with respect to the common beliefs of G after revising with the explicit content of the fiction, makes A true"

**Note:** the phrase "non-standard belief revision" does not occur in the paper. The paper's own terms are "soft upgrades" of a plausibility ordering over possible and impossible worlds (p. 7). "Truth in Fiction via Non-Standard Belief Revision" is the title of Badura's 2016 ILLC MoL thesis.

## 7. Stockton (1882), "The Lady, or the Tiger?"

**File:** `stockton1882-lady-or-tiger-pg396.txt`. Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/396.txt.utf-8 (Project Gutenberg eBook #396). The Gutenberg text has no page numbers.

**Claim:** the story ends by leaving it to the reader which came out of the door. **CONFIRMED.** The final sentences, verbatim:

1. "The question of her decision is one not to be lightly considered, and it is not for me to presume to set myself up as the one person able to answer it."
2. "And so I leave it with all of you: Which came out of the opened door,--the lady, or the tiger?"
3. Earlier, in the setup: "Now, the point of the story is this: Did the tiger come out of that door, or did the lady?"
