**Make the story-state verdict on (B) checkable in plain English, and motivate the *Stages* clause independently.**

**Is the contribution clear?** Yes, through §3. The strengthening test is the paper's real idea, and a generalist can follow why (B) and (D) come apart. As a reply to Blumberg and Holguín it is strong. What would make it more than a reply is the second claim: that the story-state view passes the test and so separates the rivals. That claim is made in §4, which runs to about 1,275 of the draft's 4,588 words, and it is where a non-specialist loses the argument.

**Where.** §4, from "The fragment" to "The test," above all the *Stages* clause and the bullet on (B).

**What is wrong.** The ✗ on (B) is derived in one sentence: "By *Stages*, it is evaluated at the worlds of s⊕B, where the coin lands one way, once. So 'the coin would have landed the other way' is rejected at every such world." To check this, the reader must work out that "the other way" is fixed to the landing at each world, so the consequent fails everywhere by construction. The draft never says so, and never says how "the other way" is read in the fragment. In (A) the same phrase is assessed after a counterfactual shift to another world; in (B) it is assessed at one world. Nor is *Stages* motivated except by the verdict it yields: it is stated as a rule with no case it answers to. *Neutrality* is defined and never used, and the four consequences come without examples.

**Why it matters.** The judges are blind professional philosophers who will skim formal blocks. If they cannot see why the story state gives (B) ✗, they will read §4 as a promissory note. The paper then reduces to a refutation of the PRP, with the defaults view of §5 left standing, and the closing claim that Lewis's answer stands outruns the argument.

**What to do.**
1. Replace the fragment with prose. For (A) to (D), say which worlds are in play and what each consequent says about them, and state the treatment of "the other way" in one sentence.
2. Motivate *Stages* from Blumberg and Holguín's own version data (193–194), which §3 already discusses. A comparison of two versions judged this way, independently of the (B) verdict, is what that verdict needs.
3. Move the definitions, *Neutrality*, and the proof in footnote 152 to an appendix or cut them. Keep the prose for *Absorption*, since claim 2 depends on it. This frees roughly 350 words, which covers steps 1 and 2 and keeps the paper inside 6,000.
