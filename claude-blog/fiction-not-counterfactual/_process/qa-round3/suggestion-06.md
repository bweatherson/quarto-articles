**Test the "makers decided it" reading in §3, because the special's planted "trick" makes it the reading viewers will take.**

**Where.** §3, "The case" and its table; the sentence "So the rejection of (B) is not a retreat to a 'what the story says' reading"; and the §6 objection "Is (B) about authors, not coins?"

**What is wrong.** §3 sets chance against what the story settles. TV-literate viewers have a third reading: the makers decided the outcome, and the audience awaits it. Three features make it the default.

- A reunion special comes after a show ends, to answer what the finale left open. One set before the finale that withholds the landing is a tease, and viewers read a withheld payoff as an answer the makers hold.
- "A trick he had learned as a boy" implies a designed outcome. A trick is something one does to control a coin, so the special reads as the writers' intention about the landing, which the stipulation the paper inherits from B&H (161) excludes.
- The coin decides which character must resign, and the cut comes before they act on it. Viewers assume the characters know the result.

**Why it matters.** Under the decided reading, (B) is ✗ because someone chose the answer. The story state gives the same verdict for another reason, so (B) cannot count for it. (D) separates the readings: on chance it is ✓; on decision, "even odds" is the wrong frame. §3 asserts (D) ✓, and fn. 148 calls the judgements untested. §5 concedes that the defaults view, close to the decided reading, ties with the story state; §3 should say so. B&H's fn. 26 quotes a headline promising the sequel would "reveal" who won the flip. "Reveal" presupposes an answer the makers hold, so the practice supports the decided reading.

**What to do.**
1. Cut "a trick he had learned as a boy". Give the special a non-agentive cause, such as a crosswind, and state, as B&H do, that the makers had no intention about the landing.
2. Add two items to fn. 148: "Had the makers already decided how the coin landed?" and "Did anyone in the story know?" Report (B) and (D) by answer. If they hold only for those who say no, say so.
3. Replace the §6 objection (about 130 words) with a paragraph on the decided reading and its prediction for (D).
4. Cite the real case in fn. 1. Its recaps treat the 2019 sequel as the answer, which is the decided reading in practice. It cannot run the test, since it settles the coin, but it is better evidence of practice than the hypothetical.
