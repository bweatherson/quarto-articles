**(B) comes out false because of *Stages*, not because of story states. As written, *Stages* is applied inconsistently, overgenerates, and works just as well for the PRP.**

*Where.* §4, *Stages* and the derivations of (B) and (D); §3, third bullet.

*The problem.* *Stages* does not interpret the counterfactual about the telling. It drops the antecedent and reads every claim about stage X off the worlds of the more informative stage Y: at v ∈ s⊕Y, T_X φ is just |φ|(v). Its undefinedness proviso never applies, because Refinement gives s⊕Y ⊆ s⊕X.

1. *Inconsistency.* (B) is evaluated in s⊕B, as *Stages* requires. (D), which has "the same antecedent and the same frame", is evaluated "in the story without the special", in s⊕A. The two agree only because the hard toss leaves the chance of heads near .5.

2. *Overgeneration.* By *Stages*, "If the writers had left the special out, Bud would still have tossed the coin unusually hard" is supported, with credence 1. But s⊕A does not support it, and the paper's lesson from (C) is that the bare consequent "follows the counterfactual story". *Stages* lets it follow the counterfactual story when that story is larger, as in (C), and the actual story when it is smaller, as in (B).

3. *Portability.* The PRP can adopt the same rule: evaluate a two-stage compound at the world of the more informative stage, which is s(B,@) for (B) and (D) and s(B′,@) for (C). The result matches the story-state column. §3's third bullet excludes only "the actual story's world", which fails (C). If the rule clashes with what the PRP makes true in A, *Stages* clashes equally with *Bare claims* on s⊕A. So the table's contrast comes from treating telling-counterfactuals differently in the two columns, not from states versus worlds.

*The underlying gap.* Suppose the counterfactual shifts the story to A, as (C) and (D) require. Then "the other way" in (B) compares candidates across stages, and the fragment needs a relation R from s⊕A to s⊕B. If R pairs everything with everything, (B) comes out at about .5, the PRP's verdict. If R pairs each world with itself, the result is *Stages*. Any R that makes (B) false while keeping A's ordinary-toss candidates holds the outcome fixed while its cause varies. That is the defaults ordering, which §5 says "cannot be the counterfactual one".

*What to do.* Make the clause compositional: the counterfactual shifts the story parameter, and cross-stage anaphora goes through an explicit R. Then either show that R is not the defaults ordering under another name, or weaken claim 1. Add the sentence in point 2 to note 2's design. To make room, cut Neutrality, which is defined and never used.
