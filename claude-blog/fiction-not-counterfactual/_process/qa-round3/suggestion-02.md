**Model the PRP's antecedent as abduction, not intervention: the case for (B) ✓ survives, but it opens a backtracking escape that §5 wrongly closes.**

*Where.* §3: s(B, @) "differs from s(A, @) by an intervention on a cause of the coin's landing", and "the same assignment for every antecedent (cf. Briggs 2012)".

*What's wrong.* For Pearl, Halpern and Briggs, an antecedent intervenes on variables of the evaluation world's model, and that world's exogenous context stays fixed. That fits (A), evaluated at a story world. It cannot fit the PRP, which is evaluated at @. @'s model has no variables for Bud or his coin, so there is no context of theirs to hold fixed. A story's principal content describes a world (183). It does not manipulate this one. In causal-model terms it is Pearl's abduction step: pick an exogenous context whose solution satisfies the story. So s(A, @) and s(B, @) are two abductions, not a world and an intervention on it. As written, the argument passes from a constraint on action, (A), to a conclusion about abduction, (B).

*The repair.* Seeing and doing differ over a variable's causes, not its effects. Hold the coin's noise fixed. The special reaches the coin only through the force, so conditioning on a hard toss lands the coin where intervening would. So any abduction that ranks contexts by exogenous values redraws the coin, including yours and von Kügelgen, Mohamed and Beckers's (2023; from memory; check) backtracking semantics. A random ranking of worlds, the usual construction behind a chance–selection link (van Fraassen 1976; from memory; check), also redraws it. It redraws on irrelevant additions too, so [^gloria] undersells you. Swap this in for your second bullet.

*What it exposes.* The only abduction that keeps the coin ranks contexts by endogenous facts, holding effects fixed while causes vary. §5 says this "cannot be the counterfactual one". But that is just what backtracking counterfactuals do (Lewis 1979; Khoo 2017; from memory; check). The right objection is that backtracking holds *actual* effects fixed, and @ has no coin. s(A, @) does. Path-dependent backtracking selection, s_bt(B, s(A, @)), keeps the outcome and gives (A) ✓, (B) ✗, (C) ✓, (D) ✓. B&H's "cosmoscope" image (193) invites it. §5 says path-dependent selection fails the test, but this version passes.
