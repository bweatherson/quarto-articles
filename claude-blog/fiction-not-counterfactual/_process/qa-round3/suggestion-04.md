**Where:** §3, "(B) and (D) have the same antecedent… (D) shows that speakers give that consequent the reading B&H's credence data require"

**1. (D) is non-diagnostic.** Its odds are even whether the bare consequent is evaluated at the counterfactual story's world (the PRP's reading) or at the actual extended story's world (anchoring). It reads naturally as "special or no special, it's a toss-up". So (D) shows only that "odds" licenses a non-settledness reading, not that the story parameter moves with the antecedent.

**2. "The other way" is a contrastive anaphor**, like "still". It needs a reference outcome and, as in modal subordination, takes the one at the world under evaluation. In (A) it is bound world by world inside the shifted state. In (B) it forces anchoring, and ✗ is then automatic. Your Stages clause delivers ✗ this way. (B)'s badness counts against the PRP only if a cross-version reading is available and rejected, and nothing shows it is available.

**3. The anchoring escape is closed too quickly.** The third bullet under "What the PRP predicts" refutes only unconditional anchoring. A defender can reuse Stages with PRP worlds: evaluate the bare consequent at the world of the stronger of the actual and counterfactual versions, s(B,@) for (B) and (D), s(Z,@) for (C), where Z is the version in which the special shows heads. That gets the whole table with one world per fiction. Ordinary talk has both: "If Lucas had never made the sequels, Vader wouldn't have been Luke's father" (shifted) against "…would still have been" (anchored).
