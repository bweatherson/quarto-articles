# Candidate thesis T1 (DO NOT OPEN before Phase 1 of any blind test is saved)

**Working title:** "Supposing the Story" or "Uncertainty Without Fictional Facts".

## Thesis
Blumberg & Holguín's attitude pattern is the signature of attitudes held under a supposition, not evidence of hidden fictional facts. The pattern:
- know ✗, sure ✗;
- think ✓ (biased coin), doubt ✓;
- credence ✓;
- wonder/curious ✓;
- wish ✗, hope ✓.

**Proposal.**
- Internal fictional discourse is evaluated relative to a **story context**: the subject's information state, counterfactually revised by the story's principal truths (S's credence imaged on P-Fic_f).
- Attitude reports with internal (bare) complements report the subject's **story-relative attitudes**:
  - know/sure = settled by the story context;
  - credence = story-relative probability, given by in-story chances and plausibility (a Principal Principle under supposition);
  - think = sufficiently high story-relative credence, or a best guess;
  - wonder = question open in the story context;
  - wish = sure ¬p in the story context;
  - hope = unsure in the story context.
- Fictional truth itself stays principal truth (the standard analysis): bare internal assertion requires support by the story context.
- The compositional mechanism: the internal reading shifts the information-state parameter that attitude verbs quantify over (Yalcin-style informational semantics, as with "Suppose A. Then I think/wonder ...").

## Predictions shared with B&H
- the whole attitude table;
- inference (28) ("I don't know it landed heads; I don't know it failed to land heads; so I don't know whether it landed heads"), which is valid relative to the story context;
- the Breakfast Club "Higginbotham" pair (read internally, de re about the characters);
- the counterfactual column, via suppositional (Edgington-style) counterfactual attitudes;
- the vagueness column: there is no supposition, and semantic indecision supplies no probabilities.

## Central argument
- The same pattern appears under pure suppositions and thought experiments ("Imagine a fair die rolled in a sealed room, never inspected: I wonder whether it landed six; it's 1/6 likely; I hope it did; I know whether it did ✗"). There, nothing is "true in" the supposition beyond what is supposed.
- So the pattern cannot be evidence that truth-in-X is complete.
- If B&H reply that CEM supplies facts for suppositions too, then the case for fictional reality is the case for CEM under supposition, and fiction contributes no independent evidence.

## Divergences from B&H (discriminating data)
- **P1.** Explicit external operator: "I'm 50% confident that it's true in JAG that Bud's coin landed heads" ✗ on T1, ✓ on B&H if "true in" expresses their □f. (B&H §2 leave explicit operators open.)
- **P2.** Disputes: "A: Heads. B: Tails. C: Neither of you is right; the show doesn't say." T1 says ✓. On B&H, C speaks falsely.
- **P3.** Authorial openness: in Stockton's "The Lady, or the Tiger?" the narrator leaves the answer to the reader. T1: no fact. B&H: an arbitrary hidden fact.
- **P4.** Credence tracks in-story chance automatically under supposition. B&H need a further link between credence about an arbitrary selection and in-story chance.
- **P5.** Reconciliation: B&H's critics (Khoo 2026: informed agents have no non-trivial credence; Oppy & Raivars 2026: wonder is irrational when resolution is impossible) are right about the external reading. B&H are right about the bare internal reading. The dispute over the data is a dispute between two readings.

## Known risks
- Predictability. Pretence, i-attitudes and "extensions" readings already exist (Walton; Currie & Ravenscroft; Doggett & Egan; Oppy & Raivars). Novelty must lie in accepting B&H's data in full, a precise semantics, the argument from pure suppositions, and the reconciliation.
- Edgington-style views have known problems with quantified conditionals (the Higginbotham data). These may carry over.
- It is a reply paper, which bears on screening criteria 1–3.
