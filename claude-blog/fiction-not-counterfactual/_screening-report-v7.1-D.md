# Screening report: paper-v7.1 (screen D)

- **Paper title:** Fiction Without Hidden Facts
- **Word count:** about 6,000 for the body; about 6,250 with the abstract and front matter. Bibliography: 29 entries, three of them TV-press pieces.
- **Thesis summary:** Against Blumberg and Holguín (B&H), the paper first gives a semantics on which bare fictional claims, and attitudes toward them, are evaluated against the information a story provides, weighted by a Reality Principle for credence. This semantics has a homogeneous logic. It validates B&H's Scopelessness and embedding patterns and predicts their attitude table without a single world, and under the bridge principles their credence model needs, it agrees with their view on every attitude toward a single fiction. The paper then argues that fiction and counterfactuals come apart under strengthening: adding a detail that bears on an open chancy outcome (a gust) to a later telling does not change that outcome, while strengthening a counterfactual antecedent with the same detail can. So their Principal Reality Principle (PRP) fails, the single world is unsupported, and Lewis's verdict that the worlds of a fiction are plural is the better-supported view.
- **Verdict:** PASS

The first half is the positive reason to send this out. It finds the exact step in B&H's case that fails: footnote 39 infers a unique world from Scopelessness by treating the fiction operator as a Boolean universal quantifier. The story-state semantics validates their inference (28), Scopelessness and the Breakfast Club equivalence, and matches their view on single-fiction attitudes. That makes their conditional (188) false: a correct reply to the centre of their case.

Experts should press on the second half. The case against the PRP rests entirely on the author's own rejection of (B), and the paper's own commitments point the other way. Without the special there is no gust, and by (A) the coin then has even odds of landing the other way. The story-state view rejects (B) only through a stipulated maximal-agreement coupling. That coupling makes "had the special shown a breeze, the coin would not have landed differently" certain, and it mirrors the "defaults" rescue the paper dismisses. So the claim that the test "refutes" the PRP should be conditional on (B). Experts should also check the homogeneity move against the literature on homogeneity versus conditional excluded middle (Klinedinst, Križ, Schlenker), which the paper barely engages.
