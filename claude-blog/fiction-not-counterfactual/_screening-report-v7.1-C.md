# Screening report: "Fiction Without Hidden Facts" (paper-v7.1.qmd), screener C

- **Paper title:** Fiction Without Hidden Facts
- **Word count:** about 6,000 (body and notes), plus a 210-word abstract. The bibliography has 29 entries, 26 of them scholarly.
- **Thesis summary:** Blumberg and Holguín's (B&H's) case for a single, fully detailed "world of the fiction" fails at both of its stages. First, a homogeneous "story-state" semantics with chance-weighted credences validates B&H's embedding data (Scopelessness, inference (28), the Breakfast Club equivalence). Given two bridge principles, it also predicts all their attitude judgements about a single fiction, and it does so without a single world. Second, fictional truth is not Stalnakerian counterfactual truth (the PRP): adding a detail that bears on a chancy outcome to a story does not redraw an open outcome, whereas strengthening a counterfactual antecedent does. The paper concludes that single-world views survive only with fiction-specific rules, and that Lewis's plural worlds are the better-supported view.
- **Verdict:** PASS, FORMAL

*Scope of checks.* I checked the quotations against their sources: B&H against the published text, Lewis 1978 and 1976 against page images, and Williams and Woodward 2021, Woodward 2012, Khoo 2022 and 2026, Schaffer 2004, von Fintel 1997, Križ 2019, McGonigal 2013 and MacFarlane 2003 against the supplied texts. All are verbatim and correctly pinned, and the paper describes B&H's arguments in terms they would accept. I could not check Oppy and Raivars 2026, Blumberg and Strohminger 2025 or the 2019 TV recaps, because none of them is among the supplied texts. I re-derived the absorption identity and the PRP's prediction for (B); both are correct under the paper's stated assumptions.

## Key strengths

- **It attacks the step that carries B&H's argument, and the contribution can be named in one sentence.** B&H get from Scopelessness to a single world in note 39: if a claim's negation must mean "not every world in Fic is a p-world", Scopelessness forces Fic to have one member. The story-state fragment validates Scopelessness, the "unimpeachable" inference and the Higginbotham-style equivalence while the candidate worlds stay plural. The paper's line "Scopelessness needs a single point of evaluation, not a single world" is a real result against the core of B&H's case. B&H set the "no fact, but opinions" option aside at p. 167; the paper does more than revive it. It also answers B&H's §7.2 objections to views on which open claims have no truth value: no projection rule is needed, because attitudes read the story-shifted state, and with a transparent internal truth predicate the trivalence conjunctions (25a–b) come out as contradictions.
- **Absorption turns B&H's inference to the best explanation into a stand-off.** B&H admit they lack "a direct argument" for the credal structure their account needs (175). The paper states two bridge principles that make B&H's suggestion precise. It then shows, correctly, that under these principles every attitude in B&H's table toward a single fiction gets the same prediction on both views. The derivation is a two-line identity, a version of Lewis's imaging result. So neither the attitude data nor the fiction–counterfactual parallel that B&H use to motivate the PRP can favour a single world.
- **The strengthening test is new and well controlled.** Under the PRP, changing an event inside the story (A) and changing the telling (B) are the same comparison: gust versus no gust under one selection function. The judgements the paper reports treat the two differently. Items (C) and (D) block the obvious deflection, that (B) is rejected only on a "what the story says" reading. §5's survey of possible rescues (anchoring, defaults, path-dependence, supervaluation) shows exactly what a single-world theorist must add to pass the test. This is a substantive point against B&H's identification of fictional truth with counterfactual truth, not a peripheral one.

## Key weaknesses

- **Stage two rests on one fragile judgement.** The rejection of (B) is the author's own verdict on a doubly embedded, contrived sentence. The paper itself grants that a realist "revelation" reading gives the same verdict. "Would have landed differently" plausibly presupposes that there is a way the coin did land, so tacit single-world thinking may explain the datum better than the story state does. The experiment described in the footnote has not been run.
- **The coupling is the story state's own rule for fiction alone, and it removes the asymmetry the paper claims.** The paper introduces a coupling that maximises agreement between the two tellings in order to deliver (B). The coupling depends on which question is being asked. By the paper's own concession about the breeze, it holds outcomes fixed while the told causes vary, which is exactly what §5 holds against the Defaults rescue ("not the counterfactual one"). The paper then admits that both views predict the same data. Its closing verdict for Lewis is therefore a parsimony judgement: no unknowable facts on one side, against gaps, a coupling and two truth notions on the other. The abstract says the posited facts are "located by nothing our judgements track" and that Lewis's view is "better-supported"; that goes further than the parity allows. What the paper shows is that B&H's arguments give no support to a single world, and that the PRP as stated fails.
- **The rebuttal of a Defaults ordering for counterfactuals is incomplete.** Judgement 1 is elicited only for a coin that was actually tossed. The PRP's selections concern merely possible coins, and the paper's own causal tie-breaking also stipulates exogenous values for merely possible objects. A defender of the PRP can therefore adopt an outcome-fixing ordering for merely possible objects, satisfy judgements 1 and 2 for actual tosses, and call the result the counterfactual ordering. To close this off, the paper needs a judgement about a counterfactual whose setup was never realised (Flippy flipped in a gust versus in calm air) that such an ordering gets wrong.
- **The attitude clause is stated but not defended.** The paper treats the story as a context parameter that shifts the whole attitude complement, and this is what defeats B&H §7.2. But the paper does not engage:
  - B&H note 34, their reason for refusing to let the fiction operator take wide scope over negation built into words like "whether";
  - complements that mix in-story and external material under an attitude verb. By B&H's note 49, "The Breakfast Club" has no referent inside the story, so "I'm sure every character in The Breakfast Club failed" needs an unshifted domain inside a shifted attitude;
  - complements that compare two fictions.

  Contextualist treatments of fiction (Predelli 2008 and Antonsen 2019, both listed in B&H n. 12) face these same compositional questions, and the paper cites neither.
- **The contrast with Khoo is stated wrongly.** Inside the shift, support is supervaluation over the candidate worlds: f:(h∨¬h) is supported while neither disjunct is. A Khoo-style supervaluation over selections also validates Scopelessness point by point and the Higginbotham equivalence. So "a homogeneous logic rather than a supervaluational one" is not what answers the embedding argument. The real differences are elsewhere:
  - the transparent truth predicate (Khoo 2026's "true" quantifies over choice functions);
  - credences weighted by chance rather than left undefined;
  - reading the candidates as factual alternatives rather than precisifications;
  - the coupling.

## Critical issues for revision

1. Strengthen the datum for (B). Add the control case of a counterfactual whose setup was never realised, and show that any outcome-fixing ordering for merely possible objects gets it wrong. Address what "differently" presupposes. Since stage two now depends on (B), report the elicitation described in the footnote, or at least a pilot.
2. Give the coupling a rationale that does more than draw an analogy with imaging. Show that it is coherent across questions: either supply one joint coupling, or give a principled reason why letting the coupling depend on the question is harmless. Then either withdraw the claim that the Defaults rescue is "a rule for fiction that counterfactuals reject" while the story state is not, or argue that a coupling without a world costs less than a default world. Bring the abstract and conclusion into line with the parity that §5 concedes.
3. Defend the context-parameter semantics for attitudes against B&H n. 34, against attitudes with mixed complements and against complements that compare two fictions, and locate it relative to the contextualist literature.
4. State the relation to Khoo 2022/2026 precisely, along the lines of the last weakness above.
