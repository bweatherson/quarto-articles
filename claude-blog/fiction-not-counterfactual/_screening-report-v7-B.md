# Screening report: paper-v7 (screener B)

- **Paper title:** Fiction Without Hidden Facts
- **Word count:** about 5,700 (body and notes)
- **Thesis summary:** Blumberg and Holguín's case for a single, complete world of each fiction fails at both of its stages. A "story-state" semantics evaluates bare fictional claims against the information a work provides, with homogeneous negation and credence weighted by in-story chances. It validates their Scopelessness inference and the Higginbotham-style equivalence and predicts their whole attitude table without a single world. Given two bridge principles, it agrees with their view on every attitude toward a single fiction. Their Principal Reality Principle gets a "gust" variant of the JAG case wrong: adding a detail that bears on the chances to a story does not redraw an open outcome, but strengthening a counterfactual antecedent in the same way would.
- **Verdict:** PASS

The paper is not an obvious fail. It pins down the step at which B&H's case for a single world goes through: their n. 39 reads the negation in ¬□f p as Boolean negation of a universal quantifier. It then gives a semantics on which the inference they call unimpeachable stays valid while that step fails. That is a correct reply to the centre of their case. Together with the absorption result, it carries the main conclusion even if the strengthening test is set aside. Quotations checked against B&H, Lewis, Williams–Woodward, Schaffer, von Fintel, MacFarlane, Moss and Khoo 2026 are accurate.

For the referee:

1. Homogeneity and no-fact treatments of the parallel data for conditionals already exist: von Fintel's homogeneity presupposition, and the no-fact line of Khoo 2022, which the paper names but never compares itself to. The paper must say what its semantics adds beyond carrying these over to fiction.
2. The test rests on a single judgment by the author, (B). The paper itself concedes that (B) has competing readings. And the story-state view gets (B) right only through a coupling rule introduced for this case.
3. The replies to "anchoring" and "defaults" rest on judgments about a coin that was actually tossed. The matched case, strengthening a counterfactual about a toss that never happened, is never judged.
