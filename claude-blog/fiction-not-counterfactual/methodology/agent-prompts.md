## Canon check on the sequel argument (claude-opus-5-5; started 2026-10-23T12:52Z)

```
You are an expert in the metaphysics and semantics of fiction, with particular knowledge of serial fiction, fictional change and retcon, and Stalnakerian counterfactuals. Task: a focused CANON CHECK of one argument. Be hostile to novelty, but do not inflate loose resemblances.

## Context
Blumberg & Holguín, "Fictional Reality" (Phil Review 134(2), 2025). Published text at /mnt/user-data/outputs/fiction-paper/research/texts/bh2025-published.txt; read §§9–11, especially §11.3 on fictional change. They hold:
- for each fiction f there is a unique world Fic_f (FEM);
- the Principal Reality Principle (PRP): □_f p iff (P-Fic_f > p), with a Stalnakerian selection function evaluated at the actual world;
- selection among similar worlds is "arbitrary".

## The argument to check (the "sequel argument")
1. **Nesting.** Let A = P-Fic of an original work (e.g. A Study in Scarlet) and B ⊂ A = P-Fic of a consistent extension (the Holmes canon, or a sequel).
2. **Stalnaker's constraint.** By Stalnaker's 1968 constraint, s(B,@) = s(A,@) iff s(A,@) ∈ B. For a rich sequel this is ~never true.
3. **No coherence.** Similarity-based selection is not "separable", so s(B,@) need not agree with s(A,@) on questions both works leave open (e.g. what Holmes ate the morning he met Watson). For counterfactuals, such non-separability is correct: strengthening the antecedent of a chaotic coin counterfactual can flip its outcome.
4. **Reshuffling.** So B&H predict that consistent sequels reshuffle the original's open details. On their §11.3 options, either different versions disagree, or the original's open facts change over time. By their own credence method, it should be about .5 likely that "A Scandal in Bohemia" changed whether Holmes ate eggs that morning, whereas we are sure it did not.
5. **The repairs.**
   - Separable "default-world" selection: imposing it on the shared selection function misdescribes counterfactuals.
   - Path-dependent selection s(B, s(A,@)): this abandons PRP-at-@ and makes truth depend on the order of publication.
6. **The alternative.** A "story-state" view, on which a sequel refines an information state, predicts no change.

## Check against the literature
Use open access only (PhilArchive, authors' pages, OA publisher pages). No Sci-Hub, LibGen or paywall-bypassing mirrors. Any Crossref query should use mailto=research@example.org. Check:
- **Serial fiction:**
  - McGonigal 2013, "Truth, relativism, and serial fiction" (BJA 53);
  - Caplan 2014, "Serial fiction, continued" (BJA 54);
  - Walters 2015 (BJA 55; "dynamic"/serial fiction);
  - Lee 2024, "Retroactive continuity and fictional facts" (BJA 64(4): 669–86);
  - anything on "retcon" or "fictional change".
- **Unique-world and complete-fiction views:** Woods 2018, *Truth in Fiction: Rethinking its Logic*; Woodward 2011a, "Fictionalism and incompleteness" (Noûs), on CEM-based completion of fictionalism; any discussion of how completions behave under extension.
- **Counterfactuals:**
  - antecedent strengthening and the sensitivity of selection under CEM (Stalnaker 1968; Lewis 1973; Schulz 2014/2017; Hájek; Hawthorne 2005);
  - any "nested" or "separable" selection-function constraint, such as "coherence" of selection functions across nested antecedents.
- **Replies to B&H:** Oppy & Raivars 2026 (notes at /mnt/user-data/outputs/fiction-paper/research/replies-notes.md); Khoo 2026 (text at research/texts/khoo2026.txt, §4).
- **Any other** discussion of whether a complete-world view of fiction makes sequels or continuations change what was true in the original.

## Output
Write /mnt/user-data/outputs/fiction-paper/canon-sequel.md (under 1,500 words) containing:
1. **Verdict:** ANTICIPATED (who and where, with a verbatim quote), PARTLY, or NOT FOUND (what you searched).

3. **The closest prior work, and what the argument adds to it.**
4. **Paywalled items needed**, if any.

Then reply with a summary of no more than 150 words.
```
