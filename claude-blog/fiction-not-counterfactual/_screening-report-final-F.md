# Screening report: Fiction Without Hidden Facts (paper-v7.3-final.qmd)

- **Paper title:** Fiction Without Hidden Facts
- **Word count:** about 5,750 words of main text (abstract about 180 words; 26 references, all cited, no orphans)
- **Thesis summary:** The paper replies to Blumberg and Holguín (B&H), "Fictional Reality", *Philosophical Review* 2025. It makes two claims.
  - B&H's embedding argument for a single world of a fiction fails. On a "story-state" semantics, the chance-weighted information a work provides either supports or rejects each bare fictional claim, homogeneously. This semantics validates Scopelessness and fits B&H's attitude data with no single world. Once B&H's credence assumptions are made precise, the two views agree on every attitude toward a single fiction.
  - Their Principal Reality Principle (PRP) fails a "strengthening test". Adding a cause of an open chancy outcome to a counterfactual's antecedent redraws the outcome. Adding the same cause to a story does not.
- **Verdict:** PASS, FORMAL

The brief tells me to judge the reply as a reply, so I have not counted the reply format against it. I have asked whether it is a good reply. The FORMAL label has two grounds:
- The first stage's answer is a formal semantics with a probabilistic core: a shift operator over priors, clauses for support and rejection, and the absorption equivalence.
- The second stage's key prediction is derived from selection-function semantics.

Judging whether the fragment works needs someone with formal competence.

## Key strengths

- **It targets the step that carries B&H's case.**
  - B&H say their case is "primarily driven by judgments about embedding patterns" (183 n. 53).
  - Their step from Scopelessness to a single world (175 n. 39) assumes that the negation in ¬□p is ordinary Boolean negation of a universal quantifier over Fic_f. B&H never consider homogeneity.
  - In the paper's fragment, negation outside the story swaps support and rejection, and quantifiers outside the story bind into it pointwise. On that fragment, B&H's "unimpeachable" inference (28) and the *Breakfast Club* equivalence are both valid, and fictional excluded middle (FEM) fails.
  - This is a correct rebuttal of the centre of B&H's case, not of its edges.
- **Absorption turns B&H's own inference to the best explanation against them.**
  - B&H explain the credence data by an appeal to "doxastic indifference" (175), for which they admit they have no direct argument.
  - The paper makes this precise with two principles: a link between in-story chance and where the selection lands, and Openness. It then shows in a few lines that B&H's view and the story state agree on every attitude in B&H's table (the argument is a version of Lewis's 1976 imaging result).
  - So the attitude data cannot decide between a single world and none. I checked the derivation and it goes through.
- **The strengthening test is new and carefully built.**
  - Sentences (C) and (D) block the obvious retreat to a "what the story says" reading.
  - The paper deals with the reading on which the special reveals a fixed past, and with both of B&H's options for fictional change.
  - It lays out the single-world views that survive (anchoring, defaults, path-dependence) and supervaluation honestly, rather than hiding them.
- **Accuracy is high.**
  - I checked every quotation below and found each verbatim and correctly paged: B&H throughout; Lewis 1978 p. 42 and Lewis 1976 p. 311 (from page images); Williams and Woodward 2021; Woodward 2012; Khoo 2022 pp. 1000 and 1013–14; Khoo 2026 p. 1191 and §4; Schaffer p. 305; Moss's Bridge principle; McGonigal pp. 167–8; von Fintel p. 33; Križ.
  - The paper characterises B&H fairly, and it places Khoo precisely as its nearest relative.
  - I could not check Oppy and Raivars 2026, Blumberg and Strohminger 2025, or the CinemaBlend recap. The argument does not depend on any of them.

## Key weaknesses

- **The second stage rests on one delicate judgement, and part of the reasoning behind it misfires.**
  - The paper rejects (B), and that rejection is the author's own judgement. (B) is a complex construction: a credence report about a counterfactual on the telling, with a bare fictional consequent and the word "differently". Testing it empirically is left to a footnote.
  - The paper's judgement 1 concerns a coin that was actually tossed. But the PRP selects from the actual world, where no JAG toss occurred, so the relevant comparison is with a merely possible coin. The paper never gives that minimal pair, for example: "had Flippy been flipped in a gust, it would have landed differently from how it would have landed had it simply been flipped".
  - The case against Defaults misfires. The objection that "actuality supplies no values for the variables of merely possible coins" hits the paper's own tie-breaking just as hard, since that rule also fixes values of exogenous variables for possible coins.
  - The sound version of the point is that holding an outcome fixed while changing its cause is backtracking. The paper makes this point only in passing.
- **The payoff is smaller than the abstract claims.**
  - The story state gets (B) right only through a "coupling": a rule, set separately for each question, that pairs the two tellings so they agree as much as possible. The paper itself says this does for tellings what Defaults does for worlds.
  - It also concedes that the surviving single-world views match every prediction. So the test refutes the PRP, not the single world.
  - The conclusion that Lewis's verdict is "better-supported" therefore rests on a weighing the paper does not carry out: gaps, a coupling and two notions of truth on one side; classical logic and an unknowable default world on the other.
  - The conclusion says that in a story a detail "cannot" change the answer, "because the story never gave an answer to change". But the paper's own footnote gives probability .4 to "landed differently" when a detail shifts the chances.
  - The sentence "It has a cost, which I accept" never says what the cost is.
- **The reply to B&H's §7.2 argument against views with truth-value gaps is only implicit.**
  - The paper compares bare fictional claims to plural sentences like "Mr. Benfleet published the books". That comparison invites an objection: when a listener knows a plural sentence falls in the gap, it embeds badly under credence and *wonder* ("#It's .5 likely that he published the books"), much as B&H's King of France cases do.
  - The paper's real answer lies in how the semantics is built: the story is a parameter of the context, and attitude verbs read the subject's state shifted by the story. The paper never sets this answer against B&H's argument that undefinedness carries over into attitude reports.
  - It also does not extend the attitude clause to complements that mix fictional and factual material, or to attitudes ascribed to other people.
- **The literature is thin for the formal claims.**
  - The attitude clause is information-state semantics in the style of Yalcin and Veltman.
  - The "story state" has a near relative in accounts that give a story its own common ground (Maier, Semeijn).
  - Homogeneity as an alternative to conditional excluded middle for Higginbotham sentences is a live debate (Williams 2010, Cariani and Goldstein, Mandelkern, Križ).
  - None of this is engaged; on homogeneity the paper cites only von Fintel 1997.

## Critical issues for revision

1. Secure (B). Add the minimal pair with Flippy. Restate the case against Defaults in causal-model terms, as backtracking, and drop the "actuality supplies no values" argument. Run the experiment in the footnote if possible.
2. Make the coupling explicit.
   - Say what its cost is.
   - Explain how using one coupling per question avoids incoherent joint probabilities.
   - Bring the slogan in the conclusion and abstract into line with the footnote on details that shift the chances.
   - Either argue for the weighing of theoretical costs, or weaken "better-supported" to "unsupported by B&H's arguments".
3. Answer B&H's §7.2 argument directly. Explain why gaps in the story state do not behave under attitudes the way presupposition failures or plural gaps do. Extend the attitude clause to mixed complements.
4. Place the fragment relative to information-state semantics, accounts that give a story its own common ground, and the debate between homogeneity and conditional excluded middle. Say exactly what is new.
