## Per-source notes

**Fang, "Guessing and Its Limits" (target).** Q: Is the standard guessing theory (you may believe p given Q iff p is a union of the k most probable cells of Q) compatible with closure across independent questions? A: No. Independent Closure (may believe q given Q, r given R, Q and R independent, so may believe q&r given QR) fails; the tournament case shows "(A,D) or (A,E)" is not filtered in because (B,D) is more probable than (A,E). Assumptions: questions are partitions with a probability measure; permission is Weakness+Question-Sensitivity+Optionality+Filtering; the product question QR is a legitimate question to guess on. Open: whether to give up Filtering, Optionality, or the naturalness of product questions (s4 naturalness challenge: 4-horse vs 452-entrant); s7 sets out the belief-camp vs credence-camp split and leaves the choice of reaction to the reader.

**Holguin 2022 (Phil Imprint).** Q: What is belief? A: Believing p is treating p as true in one's best guess to the question under discussion (Best Guess Account), with Cogent Guess and Doxastic Choice constraints; this yields weak, question-sensitive belief and defuses lottery/preface worries. Assumptions: a single operative question at a time; cells ranked by probability; guesses are chosen, and picking among equally good guesses is a free choice. Open: how belief behaves across changes of question, and the relation between guesses to different questions (the territory Fang attacks). Tension: with Fang (closure across independent questions), and with HRS/Rothschild's treatment of closure as a basic datum about 'believe'.

**Dorst & Mandelkern 2022 (PPR).** Q: What is it to guess well? A: Guessing is governed by Filtering (if you include a cell you include all more probable cells) and Fit, derived from maximising expected value of an answer trading probability against informativity with a judgment-parameter J; Improbable Guessing falls out. They also treat the conjunction fallacy as a guess phenomenon. Assumptions: partition-based questions, a single weighting J, expected-value maximisation for answers. Open: multi-question guessing (my recollection, footnotes not captured). Tension: Fang uses their Filtering to produce her result; Skipper and Linnemann & Azhar change the value function.

**Goodman & Holguin 2023 (PPR).** Q: What is the structure of "thinking", "guessing" and "deciding" attitudes? A: They offer surety norms and Thinking/Guessing/Deciding principles; being sure is the least demanding attitude subject to a knowledge norm; other attitudes (thinking, guessing) relate to questions. Assumptions: question-sensitivity of attitudes is the right organising idea. Open: how attitudes interrelate across questions. Tension: with Beddor on what the weak attitude is for.

**Linnemann & Azhar 2024 (PPR).** Q: When is a guess permissible if answers trade off accuracy and informativity without fixed weights? A: Permissible guesses are the Pareto front (weak dominance); Fermi problems and variance addition (sigma_f^2 = sum c_i^2 sigma_i^2 + covariances) show how errors compound in multi-part estimates. Assumptions: the two goods can be ordered but not weighed. Open: how composite questions are handled; this is the neighbouring-literature principle I'd use for candidate 1. Tension: with D&M's single J (they reject the weighting assumption).

**Skipper 2023 (Phil Studies).** Q: What fixes the weight on informativity? A: Log-specificity S = log(|Q|/c_p) as the informativity measure, with Neutrality (J = e) and an IIA principle for guessing. Assumptions: specificity is measured by number of cells. Open: footnotes not captured. Tension: its multiplicativity makes certain product questions behave nicely, which would help Fang's opponent; I do not know if either author notices.

**Teague 2024 (Analysis).** Q: How do guesses to a question relate to guesses to its parts (Divide and Conquer)? A: Principles Whole-to-part, Part-to-whole and WAG (weak assumption of guessing?) constrain it; I read the paper but cannot reliably reproduce each principle's exact formulation. Cites Hoek on harmonic parts. Tension: with Fang's Independent Closure, since part-to-whole is closure-like.

**Pearson 2024 (Analysis).** Q: Do Conditional Weakness and Closure conflict? A: Die cases show they pull apart; footnote 15 raises that permissibility may not be additive. Assumptions: same partition framework. Open: collective vs isolated permissibility (fn. 15). Tension: with Teague; both lean on Hoek.

**Beddor 2024 (Phil Quarterly).** Q: What is it for belief to be unsettled? A: Weak belief leaves the question unsettled; settling is a distinct role. Assumptions: settled/unsettled is a functional distinction not a probability one. Open: whether weak belief has a distinctive functional role. Tension: with Holguin (belief as guess) over what weak belief is for.

**HRS 2016 and Rothschild 2020.** Belief is weak (thinking it likely suffices); evidence: entitlement equality, neg-raising, 'believes but not sure', 'who does Tom think won?', prediction cases (9). Rothschild defends (A) 'believe'='think', (B) weak reading exists (definitive), (C) no strong reading (tentative), rebuts Kriz-style homogeneity, and admits Lockeanism faces closure ("a basic datum of natural language inference", his (36)) and the (38-a)/(38-b) contrast; he proposes 'Hintikkian mysterianism' (belief-worlds are a high-probability subset, unspecified how fixed) and says "I am, however, not at all certain how philosophers should think about belief". Key hook: his (36) and the mysterian view are exactly where question-sensitivity could do work, and he does not mention question-relativity.

**Clarke (Episteme 2022)**: not read.

## Candidate paper questions

### 1. Why do independent guesses widen when conjoined? (error propagation)
(i) If you'd guess rain today and rain tomorrow separately, why isn't guessing both the same guess? (ii) Thesis: Conjunctive guessing is estimation under propagated uncertainty, so Independent Closure fails for the same reason a Fermi estimate is not the product of the best single-factor guesses. (iii) Argument: Linnemann & Azhar's variance addition. Minimal pair: a one-step estimate vs the same estimate as a three-factor Fermi chain; the guess should widen in the second though each factor is individually fine. (iv) Objection: Fang already shows the failure; the proposal explains it rather than denying it. Not fatal but weakens novelty. (v) Priority: Fang establishes the failure; L&A supply the variance machinery; Teague/Pearson cover related ground. This is "known formula to new case". I'd rate it EXPECTED. Drop unless a surprising prediction emerges.

### 2. What is weak belief for? (settling vs selecting a plan region)
(i) Why do we form beliefs that we know are probably partly wrong? (ii) Thesis: Weak belief is the selection of a region within which one plans, not a guess to a question. (iii) Minimal pair: the same credences, one agent answering an exam question, one packing for a trip; guessing predicts the same attitude, the plan-region view predicts different ones when stakes make the region large. (iv) Objection: Goodman & Holguin's Deciding principle and Beddor's settling role already cover this ground; Holguin identifies belief and guess so my thesis may just be Holguin's A-plus-B. Probably not fatal but possibly deflationary. (v) Priority: Beddor and G&H are close; medium-high risk.

### 3. The closure datum is a question-shift datum (strongest generalist fit)
(i) We say belief is closed under conjunction. Is that a fact about belief or about which question we have in mind? (ii) Thesis: The intuitive closure of 'believe' (Rothschild's (36)) holds only when the conjunction is a top cell of the salient question; where it isn't, speakers accept 'S believes p, S believes q, S doesn't believe p and q'. (iii) Argument: use Fang's tournament structure translated into ordinary reports to build a minimal pair of belief reports (natural wording needed, I have not tested any sentence pair); a survey/linguistic test is possible. (iv) Objection: speakers may simply shift the question silently and accept closure by accommodation, making the datum untestable; also Fang section 4 (naturalness of the product question) may already pre-empt part of this. Not fatal; it is the main risk. (v) Priority: Rothschild states closure as a datum but doesn't consider question-sensitivity; HRS silent. I did not find the specific claim in the papers read, but I haven't read Clarke or Hoek and cannot rule it out. This fits your winning pattern: widely held assumption in one literature (closure as basic datum for 'believe') challenged with a principle from a neighbouring one (question-sensitive guessing).

### 4. Permissibility is collective, not distributive
(i) If each of several guesses is fine, is the set fine? (ii) Thesis: Permission to guess is a property of a guess profile, so Optionality should be read as permission over combinations. (iii) Argument: Pearson fn. 15 and the die cases; minimal pair with two dice where each guess alone is permitted but the pair is not. (iv) Objection: this just relabels Optionality and Filtering, and Hoek (unread) may already treat web-of-questions coherence. (v) Priority: Pearson flags it; Teague close; high risk. Likely REPLY-TO-EXCHANGE, the type that failed.

### 5. (Drop) Log-specificity multiplicativity
Technical result: Skipper's log-specificity is additive over independent questions, so J = e makes product questions behave. Dropped: it is a formula applied to a new case and probably already implicit in Skipper.

## My ranking
Pursue 3 (with a real experiment or at least a carefully tested sentence pair) if you want a generalist paper; 2 second; 1 and 4 are EXPECTED; drop 5. Before committing, read Hoek and Clarke, and check Fang's footnotes and section 4 on whether product questions are already addressed.
