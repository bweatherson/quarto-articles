## Per-source entries

**Yalcin, "Belief as Question-Sensitive".** Question: what is the structure of belief states such that belief is not closed across the board? Answer: belief is relative to a question (a partition); one agent can have several question-relative states, and closure holds relative to a single state or resolution, not across them. Formal core: an agent believes p relative to Q iff her state, at Q's resolution, entails p. Closure under single-premise and multi-premise consequence holds within a state; across fragments it fails. Yalcin keeps a closure under "visible consequence" (V): you are closed only under consequences visible at the grain of the question, which is how he handles encoding cost. Conjunction: A and B are jointly believed only if both are believed relative to a state that has both subject matters in view; there is no principle producing the belief relative to QR from beliefs relative to Q and R. Takes for granted: that questions are partitions, that the agent has a stable fragmentation, and that belief is the attitude to be modified (not guessing). Leaves open: how states at different questions relate. Hoek (OUP, fn 9) says of this: "Yalcin posits no such constraints", i.e. no cross-question constraints.

**Hoek, "Questions in Action" (J Phil 2022).** Question: how do questions figure in practical rationality? Answer: agents act relative to the question they are asking; choice problems are questions, and an agent's rational preferences/views are relative to them. Core: "quizpositions" (attitudes to a question); views on a big question determine views on its parts (Closure under Parthood). Dutch Q-books show that incoherence across overlapping questions is exploitable. Conjunction/multiple questions: handled via overlap relations between questions, not by a general product rule. Takes for granted: that the question relevant to action is available to the agent. Leaves open: how to combine disjoint questions; an agent with views on Q and R separately is not thereby committed on QR.

**Hoek, "Minimal Rationality and the Web of Questions" (OUP 2025).** Question: what is the minimal rationality of non-ideal agents? Answer: coherence is local to a web of questions linked by parthood. Core principles: Harmonic Parts (a view on a question fixes the views on its parts), Closure under Parthood, Partial Adjunction (restricted to nested questions), Overlapping Views. Deduction is a "tautological update", i.e. effortful asking of a new question; closure is therefore only relative to a question that has actually been asked. Multiple questions: disjoint questions are unconstrained; QR views take effort. So closure failure across independent questions is a feature of the framework: nothing forces agglomeration. It takes for granted that the web is built by the agent's inquiry and that questions are partitions. Open: whether any attitude (like guessing) obeys Harmonic Parts. Note I read to p. 39 of 44.

**Schaffer, "Knowing the Answer".** Question: what is knowledge-wh and how does it generalize? Answer: K^Q_s p, a knowledge relation indexed to a question fixed by the QUD; knowledge-that is knowledge of the answer to a contextually given question. Core: one attitude, one question per ascription; the question is supplied by context. Multiple questions: not systematically treated; the contrastivist background gives each ascription a single contrast. Takes for granted that context supplies exactly one question. Open: how a single agent's K^Q for different Q combine; nothing about closure across questions.

**Drucker, "The Attitudes We Can Have".** Question: what limits the attitudes available to finite agents? Answer: belief is a choice among answers to a question (hairetic theory), so having an attitude requires having a question in mind; the answer chosen must satisfy Clarity. Core: belief formation as choice, relative to one question. Multiple questions: none; there is no multi-question machinery that I could find (I did not read the appendix). Takes for granted that the question is an agent-level resource. Open: the combination of attitudes; anything resembling closure or agglomeration.

**Beddor & Goldstein, "A Question-Sensitive Theory of Intention".** Question: why does intention seem sensitive to the question? Answer: intention is defined relative to a question, but the question is the single global decision problem. Core: definedness on one global problem, with agglomeration (conjunction of intentions) built in by design; a preface-style worry for intention (Goldstein 2016, not read) motivates the question-sensitive account. Multiple questions: avoided by stipulating one global question. This is the clearest case where cross-question coherence is bought by stipulation rather than derived.

**Blumberg & Hawthorne, "Wanting what's not best" (pp. 1-11).** Question: how can one want what isn't best? Answer: desire is alternative-sensitive; "wants p" is true when p holds in some good alternative in the contextual set, quantifying existentially. Core: Weakening (wanting p entails wanting weaker things) holds; strengthening and agglomeration fail. Multiple questions: not addressed in what I read. "A New Hope" not read.

**Quillien, Bramley & Lucas 2025, lossy encoding.** Question: what do belief reports convey? Answer: a report is a lossy encoding of a credence distribution, chosen to minimize KL divergence for a listener who decodes with an indifference decoder. Core: a report picks the message that best reconstructs the distribution. Conjunction: the Linda case is treated as a joint four-cell question. Multiple questions: as I read it, the encoding is for a single joint question. Open: factorization of the decoder. Quillien 2022 not read.

**Dorst & Mandelkern, "Good Guesses" (sampled only).** Their answer-value account of the conjunction fallacy treats conjunctions as guesses at a joint question whose conjuncts are "contextually orthogonal" (fn 29). The sampled passages contain no discussion of Independent Closure.

**Yablo, Aboutness.** Not read; no claim made.

## KEY QUESTION: how do the attitudes relate across questions?

(1) Is there a principle fixing what you believe relative to QR from what you believe relative to Q and R? Not in any source I read. Yalcin does not have one; Hoek says so explicitly in the OUP chapter (fn 9: "Yalcin posits no such constraints"). Hoek's principles run downward only. Harmonic Parts and Closure under Parthood say that a view on QR fixes the views on Q and on R (its parts). Partial Adjunction, the upward-looking one, is restricted to nested questions, so it does not combine two independent questions. The only upward route is the tautological update: ask QR, which costs effort, and your view there is not fixed by your views on Q and R. Beddor & Goldstein get agglomeration by stipulating one global decision problem. Drucker and Schaffer have no multi-question machinery.

(2) Does Hoek's web independently explain Independent Closure failures? Yes, in the sense that it predicts them: closure holds only relative to a single question that has been asked, and independent questions are unconstrained, so failing Independent Closure is a feature, not a bug. Caveat: Hoek explains failure of closure for the agent's total set of views, whereas Fang's concern is a Guessing-specific failure (Filtering applies to each question and yields conflicting verdicts when questions are conjoined). Hoek's web explains why you need not hold the QR-belief; it does not explain why, if you do ask QR, a Filtering-based guess there fails to extend your Q- and R-guesses (that is Fang's argument and it comes from the formal structure of Guessing). I have not verified how Fang's Closure under Parthood interacts with Hoek's.

(3) What does Yalcin say about conjunction and beliefs not about the same subject matter? Conjunction: believing A and believing B relative to fragments does not give believing A-and-B; you get it only when both are visible within one state at one grain. For different subject matters: fragmentation is exactly the allowance for beliefs not integrated across subject matters. I could not find a positive principle. So the literature has a gap: a *positive* account of what coherence across questions requires, short of closure. Hoek's Dutch Q-books give a negative test for nested/overlapping questions only.

## REAL TASK: candidate paper questions

Overall: I am not confident any is strong. Each is at some risk of being EXPECTED.

**A. Does Guessing satisfy Harmonic Parts?** (i) If you guess an answer to a big question, does that settle your guess on the parts of the question? (ii) Thesis: Guessing-style belief violates Hoek's Harmonic Parts, so the web-of-questions framework cannot house it. (iii) Case: Fang's race example; Harmonic Parts would force the conjunctive guess she wants, while Guessing's Optionality allows both A and not-A guesses on different grains. I have only a sketch of the derivation and have not checked it carefully. (iv) Objection: Fang's own Closure under Parthood may already be the same principle, in which case there is no gap. Possibly fatal. (v) Priority check: Fang (Closure under Parthood), Hoek 2025. High risk the point is made, or trivially made, in Fang's section on Parthood. Needs checking before anything else.

**B. The failure of Independent Closure is an artefact of the decoder, not the credence.** (i) When does a belief report about two independent questions fail to be the conjunction of reports about each? (ii) Thesis: it fails because the listener's decoder is not a product, not because belief is non-agglomerative. (iii) KL divergence is additive across independent questions if the decoder factorizes; a flat decoder over QR is not a product. Minimal pair: one listener with a factorized decoder, one without, same credences, different optimal reports. (iv) Objection: the decoder is defined by the model, so this may be just a modelling choice; Fang may reply that guessing is a mental state, not a message. Not fatal but substantive. (v) Priority: Quillien et al. 2025 (Linda as joint question) is close. It is also "apply a known formula to a new case", the failure type your record warns of. Moderately risky.

**C. Acting forces agglomeration.** (i) Do practical contexts force a conjunction of independent questions? (ii) Thesis: belief used in action is closed under conjoined questions even though belief generally is not. (iii) Beddor & Goldstein's single global problem shows agglomeration is available in the practical case. (iv) Objection: it is by stipulation, and Hoek 2022 already links questions to action. Not fatal but probably already covered. (v) Priority: Hoek 2022, Beddor & Goldstein. High risk of being EXPECTED.

**D. "She thinks A will win" reports a stance, not a state.** (i) Do such reports describe what the agent is like, or the position she takes in a conversation? (ii) Thesis: they report a stance. (iii) A minimal pair would be two contexts with the same credences but different reports. (iv) Objection: that is Yalcin's and Dorst & Mandelkern's existing line; no argument I can supply distinguishes it. (v) Priority: Yalcin, Dorst & Mandelkern, Quillien et al. Likely fatal on priority.

**E. Many question-relative beliefs that cannot be jointly represented.** (i) Can one agent be coherent while her beliefs cannot be put in one belief state? (ii) Thesis: coherence across questions is compatibility with a common refinement, not joint representation. (iii) Case: Q and R each guess, but no QR guess consistent with Filtering extends both. (iv) Objection: Hoek's web already says coherence is local. Not fatal if the refinement condition can be shown to differ from Hoek's overlap condition; I did not check. (v) Priority: Yalcin fragmentation, Hoek 2025, Fang. Vague as stated.

My ranking: A (if the Parthood overlap with Fang checks out as a real gap), then E. I would drop D and C. B is the most clearly something new but is closest to the known-formula-to-new-case failure.
