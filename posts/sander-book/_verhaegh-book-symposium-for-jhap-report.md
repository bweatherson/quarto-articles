## Notes

1. **Morris 1935 does not support "in rough order of importance" (L53).** I read the paper. Morris does not rank the schools. On p. 143 he says pragmatism and realism arose "in part against" the domination of absolute idealism, and that "the dominant temper of philosophers is naturalistic rather than idealistic", which cuts against putting idealism first in 1935. He also does not present a three-school scheme as a "simple narrative"; the text names pragmatism, new realism and critical realism as the movements preoccupied with epistemology. Suggested rewording: *Morris describes pragmatism and realism as arising against absolute idealism (143).* The claim that the three were "the largest, not least because they had distinctive sub-schools" has no source in the draft.
2. **Morris overstated at L63.** "How much more comfortable the realists are with mathematical approaches ... than the other schools" is stronger than the paper. Morris says (146) "In general, realism has upheld the basic significance of logical analysis, though the results have to date been somewhat abortive". But on pp. 145–46 he credits the pragmatists Peirce, Lewis and Sheffer with making Harvard "the center of formal logic in the United States", and on p. 148 he says the old rivalry between the logical and bio-social emphases "has tended to diminish". Safer: *Morris already notes that realism "has upheld the basic significance of logical analysis" (146).*
3. **Brightman (L53).** Accurate. The list is on p. 390: "Kantians, pragmatists, naturalists, Thomists, realists, personalists, and idealists". Cite with that page. The survey covers 1939–45 (published 1947), so "post-war survey" is slightly off: it is a wartime survey published after the war.
4. **Ayer quotation (L57).** I could not load the video or the PhilPapers record's content, so I have not checked "very akin to" and cannot give a timestamp. The 1978 date in the new bib entry comes from a search snippet, not the record itself. Please check both.
5. **Davidson at Princeton (L55).** From memory, Hempel came to Princeton in 1955 and Davidson's Princeton appointment was in 1967, so "so was the very non-positivist Donald Davidson" may not describe the same round of hires. I could not check what Verhaegh says; this needs the proofs.
6. **Philosophical Studies subtitle (L47).** "An International Journal for Philosophy in the Analytic Tradition" is the current subtitle. Check that it was on the journal when it was founded (1950), as the sentence implies.
7. **Footnote citation (L61).** I guessed `Weatherson2007-WEADPW` ("Doing Philosophy with Words", your reply to Soames in *Philosophical Studies*). I have only read its abstract, which is about ordinary language philosophy and Ryle, not specifically Austin. Confirm it is the paper you mean and that it says what the footnote claims.
8. **Front matter.** The abstract says "book launch event" while the output file name says "book symposium". The date `09-10-2026` is ambiguous (9 October or 10 September). The abstract is a one-line framing note, not an abstract, if the journal requires one.
9. **Ending.** L79 opens "So I'll end with a question", and L81 is a further closing paragraph. The paper announces its ending twice.
10. **Bibliography.** I appended six entries to `brian-quarto.bib` (see the separate list). I did not touch existing entries, but `Ayer1936` has the publisher as "Gollantz." (should be Gollancz) and a trailing period in the title. `sander-book-refs.bib` is not used by the paper, and its `note` fields could print in the bibliography if it were ever loaded.
