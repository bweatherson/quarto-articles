# Notes: the checking/inquiry grid (for Monday reading)

## Two questions

1. Is checking a form of inquiry?
2. Does checking require losing knowledge (of the checked proposition, while checking)?

## The grid

|                          | Loses knowledge: YES | Loses knowledge: NO |
|--------------------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **Checking is inquiry: YES** | Friedman, *Checking Again* — checker suspends at the moment of re-opening (preprint p. 10), evidence unchanged (n. 19) | BW; Melchior (official view) |
| **Checking is inquiry: NO**  | Lewis, *Elusive Knowledge*? — attending to not-p destroys knowledge whatever the activity is | McGrath (symposium comments, §3) |

All four cells occupied → the two questions are genuinely independent.

## Regimentation (now in replies.qmd)

Inquiry = genus (question-directed epistemic activity). Finding out and checking = species. The Ignorance Norm and the taking-for-granted principle attach to *finding out*, not the genus. McGrath's "checking without inquiring" = checking without finding out.

## Melchior's two levels (resolves the §2.2 vs p. 117 confusion)

- KSAC is a thesis about *intuitions*: in checking contexts we *tend to think* insensitive methods can't yield knowledge. Part II of the book explicitly leaves open whether the intuitions are right.
- His own first-order view (p. 117, Replies): checking does NOT preclude knowing; the felt loss is a mistaken transfer of checking's sensitivity standard onto knowledge.
- So: same cell as BW, plus an error theory about the pull of Friedman's cell.
- He grants checking is a form of inquiry at p. 122 (Replies, contra a narrow reading), distinguished within inquiry by the specific intention + sensitivity requirement.
- He weakened KSAC to sufficient-conditions-only (Replies pp. 104, 115) — cite the 2023 version, not KC 142-3.

## Three-way contrast on the loss-intuition

- Friedman: veridical (checker really suspends, so really stops knowing).
- Melchior: error through and through.
- BW: veridical about finding out, error about checking; the intuition is stable because checks really do sometimes slide into finding out (mind opens), and those losses are real.
